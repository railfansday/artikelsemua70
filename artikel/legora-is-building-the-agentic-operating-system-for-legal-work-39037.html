Founded in Stockholm in 2023, Legora⁠(opens in a new window) is building what CEO and co-founder Max Junestrand calls “the agentic operating system for legal work”: bringing AI into the workflows lawyers rely on every day.

The company started with the insight that legal teams are unusually time crunched, meaning some valuable work doesn’t get done. Think of the junior lawyer told to review an impossible number of contracts overnight and only able to focus on a fraction.

Legora can provide those services “on tap”, which is why Junestrand’s ambition is that every piece of legal work in the world should run on his platform. OpenAI has been part of that journey from the beginning: the release of GPT‑3.5 was the moment many of the legal use cases the founding team had been experimenting with became possible. Today, OpenAI is a key model provider for Legora, powering core workflows across its legal AI platform, from agentic legal work to large-scale document review. Legora builds on upcoming OpenAI model capabilities as they become available, translating advances in intelligence into what lawyers can accomplish with its product.

In a recent development, legora’s use of AI goes beyond the product it sells, and has expanded its use of ChatGPT Work, Codex and dots across its own teams. OpenAI’s technology is becoming part of both the product Legora builds for its customers as well as shaping how the firm itself operates. “It’s incredibly crucial to me that we stay as AI-native as possible,” confirms Junestrand.

For Jacob Lauritzen, CTO at Legora, coding agents have changed how quickly the engineering team can develop new ideas. Instead of researching one possible solution and committing engineering time before knowing whether it’s the right path, teams can prototype many approaches at once. “We can explore 20 solutions at the same time and, within days, pick the one we know will be the best.”.

The same approach changes how Legora responds when something goes wrong. Previously, diagnosing a product problem could mean several engineers investigating different possible causes over a number of hours. Coding agents can now search millions of lines of logs and pursue many possible explanations simultaneously, sometimes taking Legora from bug report to working fix in around five minutes.

It has been revealed that customers, of course, don’t see the engineering efficiency itself. But they do experience the results: problems can be resolved faster, feedback can reach product teams sooner, and upcoming ideas can be tested with customers earlier in the development cycle.

Legora’s own legal team experiences the same transformation its product is designed to create for customers. General Counsel Emma Kadri Bergström points to the example of NDAs. Her team analysed the agreements they had historically signed and built a playbook that allows Legora to take a first pass at incoming NDAs, highlighting terms that fall outside acceptable parameters.

Before the workflow was introduced, Kadri Bergström estimates that reviewing these “boring documents” consumed around 20% of the legal team’s capacity. Today, it’s under 1%.

Sources indicate that that reclaimed time matters because the company itself is scaling rapidly. In the past year, Kadri Bergström claims the business has expanded from three offices to 18, completed three financing rounds and made five acquisitions. The legal team has had to support that growth while remaining closely connected to the business.

In a recent development, AI has also allowed the team to take on work that might previously have been outsourced or narrowed because of limited capacity. In M&A diligence, for example, agents can begin reviewing contracts and surfacing information across the deal set, with lawyers reviewing and interpreting the output.

It has been revealed that “AI doesn’t just make you faster. It also increases quality.”.

For Legora’s customers, the opportunity isn’t simply to complete the same work faster. AI has also increased the scope of work that can be taken on.

Recent findings suggest that alex Fortescue-Webb, Global Head of Legal Engineering, points to due diligence as an example. In a traditional review, time and cost constraints can force teams to concentrate deeply on only the most valuable contracts. Legora’s Tabular Review workflow can analyse thousands of contracts across multiple questions in minutes, allowing lawyers to apply detailed scrutiny much more broadly.

It has been revealed that that can alter the quality of the final work. Risks that might previously have sat in contracts outside the manually reviewed subset can now be surfaced for a lawyer to assess.

Sources indicate that the results are already measurable. Junestrand cites one Fortune 100 customer that has:.

reduced contract turnaround time by 90%.

saved more than $20 million on external litigation and diligence.

returned 9.5 hours per lawyer each week for higher-value work.

Through the Legora plugin for ChatGPT⁠, legal teams can also use Legora’s capabilities within their ChatGPT conversations.

Build a network of champions. Internal advocates help turn experimentation into sustained adoption.

Be ready to rebuild. As models improve, yesterday’s product assumptions can quickly become outdated.

According to recent reports, for Legora, the next frontier isn’t just making today’s legal workflows faster. It’s expanding the kinds of work AI can take on, while making collaboration between lawyers and AI increasingly natural.

It has been revealed that jacob Lauritzen sees two advances happening in parallel. First, agents will be able to tackle longer-running tasks that require far greater context and autonomy. He imagines due diligence spanning millions of pages running overnight, with the analysis ready for a lawyer to review when they arrive the next morning.

At the same time, increasingly capable models are becoming faster. That opens up a different kind of experience: real-time collaboration where a lawyer and AI can work back and forth on a contract, analysis or piece of advice rather than treating AI as a tool that receives a task and returns an answer.

“When you blend human and AI, that’s when the output comes out best.”.

That moving frontier means Legora’s roadmap can’t be static. “Model improvements force me to reconsider the roadmap on a biweekly basis,” notes Lauritzen. Engineers, product managers, designers and sales teams all frequently ask him what’s the latest thing that’s become possible for customers.

For Bergström, it’s about changing the perception of legal teams. “Every legal department is worried about becoming a bottleneck. That’s when the business stops coming to you,” she states. AI is transforming legal teams from bottleneck to business partner, from the people you avoid to the people you want in the room alongside you.

If Legora succeeds, the outcome won’t simply be lawyers completing today’s workload more efficiently. It will be legal teams able to take on work that capacity once made impossible, with more time for judgement, strategy and the high-value decisions that still require a human.

As Junestrand puts it: “It’s the best time in history to be a lawyer.”.
