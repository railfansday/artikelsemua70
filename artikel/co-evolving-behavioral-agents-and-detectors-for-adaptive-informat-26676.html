Co-Evolving Behavioral Agents and Detectors for Adaptive Information Operations

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (292 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.27156v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Detectors of state-sponsored information operations (IOs) are trained on campaigns that platforms have already taken down. They lose much of their accuracy on an actor they have not seen, even though no operator in the archived data is trying to avoid them. A real operator wants two things, to reach an audience and to avoid being caught. We present IO-AI, a framework that gives simulated agents these two capabilities, engagement and evasion, and tests what each one does to detection. The agents are built from the posting behavior of real campaigns and choose the emotion, type, and timing of each post. An agent and a detector then train against each other in turn. We rank the trained agents by how hard they are to detect and train one detector on them from easiest to hardest. On 20 Twitter IO campaigns, this detector reduces missed detections of adaptive agents by 65% relative to a detector trained only on the archived campaigns, at a similar false-alarm rate. On actors it has never seen it reaches an F1 of 0.81, against 0.62. We find that an agent trained to seek engagement becomes easier to catch, because it learns the fast, emotional retweeting that archived campaigns were caught producing. An agent that was trained for engagement before the game keeps its engagement lead after it learns to evade. We also introduce a behavioral distance metric that compares two groups of accounts using only their unlabeled posting histories. The larger the distance between two groups, the worse a detector trained on one does on the other (Spearman rho = -0.58), so the metric can be used to choose which fresh groups to test first.
