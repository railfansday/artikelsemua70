Ahead of global elections, we’re helping people access information, supporting cyber defenders, and increasing AI transparency.

Sources indicate that ahead of the U.S. midterm elections, we’re continuing our work to help people access reliable voting information and supporting election defenders. Here are some updates on that work:.

Helping people access reliable information.

Industry observers note that our Democracy Works⁠(opens in a new window) integration is now live in the U.S. across languages, bringing information about registration, deadlines, voting locations and other election requirements directly into ChatGPT. Democracy Works compiles this information with state and local election officials and monitors changes throughout the election period. Responses also include links to official election sources so people can check the details for themselves.

According to recent reports, our integration with The Associated Press⁠(opens in a latest window) brings live vote counts into ChatGPT for elections in the U.S. and Brazil, after polls close. Election officials remain the authority on voting rules and certified results.

It has been revealed that we now apply image stylization to requests for images depicting election interference or that may discourage voting. These outputs produce stylized – also known as cartoonified – illustrations, rather than photorealistic images, to help reduce the risk that an imagined scene is mistaken for documentary evidence. This approach preserves room for illustrations and social commentary. Our policies prohibiting election interference and demobilization always apply, even if that misuse involves cartoonified imagery.

According to recent reports, through our Daybreak⁠ program, we’ve offered voting-system manufacturers registered with the U.S. Election Assistance Commission and state election authorities special access to models for cyber defense at no cost, to help them identify weaknesses and harden election infrastructure. We are also working with elections services vendors.

We continue to prohibit political ads from running on our platform.

We also continue to prohibit using our products to create or distribute scaled campaign messaging, including images and video, that advocate for or against a candidate, political party or ballot measure. Campaigns can use our tools for responsible, human-directed work⁠(opens in a latest window), including internal briefings, planning, everyday writing, translation, compliance and administrative tasks.

2026 is the world’s second major election year since generative AI became widely available, and we’re continuing to build on the foundation we laid in 2024 to help protect elections in countries and territories around the world.

Our focus is to build and responsibly deploy groundbreaking products in ways that:.

Surface reliable information about voting and results.

Support cyber infrastructure defenders.

Increase transparency around AI-generated content.

Monitor bias in our models to keep ChatGPT’s responses politically neutral.

People already use ChatGPT to ask practical questions in their preferred languages about civic events: how to register, where to vote, what deadlines apply, what’s happening with a developing news event, or where to find official election results.

Building on our efforts in 2024, we are working with partners to direct people to reliable sources of information about voting. Beginning this fall in the United States and Brazil, OpenAI will provide live vote counts from The Associated Press⁠(opens in a upcoming window) as results roll in on election night. In the US, we will also partner with Democracy Works⁠(opens in a new window) to display reliable information about voting and registration processes, including voting locations and other election logistics, when people ask about those topics. Globally, we will continue to refine the way web search surfaces helpful information with source links.

In a recent development, since 2024, we have continued to improve the quality of information people get when they ask ChatGPT about election topics and breaking news. ChatGPT can search the web to provide stronger answers with source links so people can go deeper.

In a recent development, this is an vital moment for cyber defenders across industries, and we believe AI plays a critical role in hardening digital infrastructure—including systems that support elections. OpenAI is committed to building resilience across the infrastructure stack, including in ways that support election execution.

We recently confirmed Daybreak⁠, our effort to change the way software is built and defended. Daybreak includes a number of programs intended to make software safer and more resilient, including Codex Security, which automatically identifies, validates, and helps remediate vulnerabilities in developers’ code. For more advanced defense, our Trusted Access for Cyber ⁠(TAC) program provides verified individuals access to frontier models for cyber defense. We have offered Codex Security as well as TAC access to registered voting system manufacturers⁠(opens in a new window) in the US, and we are engaging the National Association of Secretaries of State (NASS) and the National Association of State Election Directors (NASED) to ensure state election authorities are briefed on the latest cyber capabilities and our tools for defenders.

Recent findings suggest that people are increasingly using AI tools to create content they then share on social media, messaging apps, and the web. To help combat misleading “deepfakes”, we are investing in a multi-layered provenance approach that will equip people to verify whether content they’re seeing has been created or modified with AI.

Last week, we confirmed a partnership to bring SynthID ⁠digital watermarks to images generated through ChatGPT⁠, Codex, or the OpenAI API. This builds on our ongoing commitment to using the C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) standard, which uses metadata and cryptographic signatures to help information about an image securely travel with the content itself. These two approaches are complementary: SynthID embeds an invisible watermarking layer that can hold up through screenshots and other transformations, while metadata can provide more information than a watermark alone.

Recent findings suggest that we’re also previewing a public verification tool⁠ that lets people check whether an image they encounter off-platform was generated using OpenAI tools. The site can detect whether a SynthID watermark originating from OpenAI is present, as well as surface C2PA metadata when it is found.

Provenance tools aren’t a complete solution to election-related deception, but they are an notable part of a broader integrity framework. And since most people encounter AI content on social media and messaging platforms, we are happy to partner with social media companies as they consider steps to protect elections by using provenance markers as a relevant signal in deciding which civic content they recommend and distribute in people’s feed.

And we are excited to support legislation that would advance transparency:.

According to recent reports, protect Elections from Deceptive AI Act (S. 1213)⁠(opens in a latest window) would add new prohibitions from knowingly distributing materially deceptive AI-generated content involving federal candidates when used for federal election activity.

Preparing Election Administrators for AI Act (S. 2346)⁠(opens in a newly introduced window) would require the Election Assistance Commission to issue a report with voluntary guidelines for election offices on the use and risks of AI technologies in election administration. These guidelines are especially important given increased cyber capabilities of frontier models.

OpenAI will continue to do our part to combat election interference. We enforce Usage Policies⁠ prohibiting users from deploying our tools for election interference, demobilization, or deception about the origin of AI-generated content.

Our detection and enforcement systems have continued to improve since 2024. We monitor and enforce⁠ policies with privacy safeguards in place and clear review processes. And we act when someone tries to use our tools to interfere with the election process, discourage participation, or mislead people about the origin of AI-generated content, including as part of coordinated deceptive activity. When we identify abuse, we take enforcement action, including restricting or terminating access to our services.

Our Intelligence and Investigations, Safety, Security, and Integrity teams work to detect misuse patterns and investigate attempts to use our tools for deceptive or election-related abuse, including covert influence operations. Since February 2024⁠, we’ve regularly⁠ published⁠ reports⁠ with⁠ our⁠ findings⁠ and will continue to do so.

Sources indicate that while the public, policymakers, campaigns, and companies work to develop norms for AI use in politics, we continue to prohibit the use of our products to create or distribute scaled campaign messaging for or against a candidate, political party, or ballot measure. We do think political campaigns should be able to use our tools for responsible, human-directed work, like drafting internal briefings, planning, everyday writing, translation work, compliance, and administrative tasks. These use cases are allowed under our usage policies.

In a recent development, AI tools offer real opportunities for people to engage others in newly introduced ways that expand and deepen civic communities by expanding access across languages, education-levels, and backgrounds. We believe it’s key that people can use AI systems to learn about, explore, and discuss political issues, and we will continue to enable that, while avoiding misuse by bad actors.

According to recent reports, consistent with our rules against using our services for scaled campaign advocacy, we will not allow advertisers to run political ads on our platform this cycle. You can read more about how we are approaching advertising on ChatGPT⁠.

We know people come to ChatGPT for more than election logistics. They use ChatGPT to explore ideas, test arguments, and gain more understanding of issues they care about. That’s why we’re committed to combatting political bias in the way ChatGPT responds to questions.

Our Model Spec principle Seeking the Truth Together⁠(opens in a new window) outlines our commitment to keeping ChatGPT objective by default, with the user in control, including when owners engage in political conversations. ChatGPT is a tool for people to engage with big ideas and develop their own perspective on issues. So the model is designed to avoid behavior that would manipulate a user or to conceal relevant facts or viewpoints to nudge them in a particular direction. Last fall, we announced⁠ a new political bias evaluation we use to test our models’ ability to remain objective. This notable work has made OpenAI a leader in political bias evaluations that test how models respond to political topics.

According to recent reports, we will continue to learn from partners, update our safeguards, and adapt. Our goal is to support people’s ability to participate freely in elections and make their own decisions—with reliable information, transparency, and effective safeguards. We look forward to deepening the ways we can support our consumers, civil society, and voters as they participate in elections this year and beyond.
