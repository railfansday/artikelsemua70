Whether you have one Era 100 speaker and are looking to expand your Sonos system or you have 12 speakers and can’t resist another, it’s a great time to add another Sonos speaker to your home. Several of the organization’s speakers are on sale for Black Friday, from individual ultra-portable Bluetooth/Wi-Fi hybrid speakers to entire home theater sets.

All S2 Sonos speakers received an AI-powered patch with the arrival of Sonos 27voice, the company’s take on a context-aware, more intelligent voice assistant. Significant app and player updates have recently been implemented, including the ability to control your system with Claude or ChatGPT, more intuitive volume controls, speaker and volume presets, detailed system health updates, and more, all available in free updates.

I test Sonos and other speakers all year, and these are my favorite deals so far. These deals will be available until Nov. 30, according to Sonos.

Sources indicate that the Era 100 is a great place to start building a larger Sonos system, thanks to its unassuming stature and a full suite of Sonos’ best features, including voice assistant support, AirPlay compatibility, and access to Sonos’ vast music streaming platform. If you don’t want a battery-powered speaker and instead want one that stays fixed to a single outlet in your house, start with the Era 100.

I keep an Era 100 on my kitchen counter, and I often play music, YouTube videos, or podcasts on it so I can hear clearly while I’m cooking. If you don’t need access to a voice assistant, you can get a microphone-less Era 100 SL for $149, which is also $40 off.

The hourglass shape is a reflection of the spatial audio theme. / Maria Diaz/ZDNET.

Recent findings suggest that sonos’ Era 300 is the more advanced speaker in the Era lineup, with 6 Class-D amplifiers powering four tweeters and two woofers. Of the four tweeters, one forward-firing mid-tweeter handles center audio, while two side-firing tweeters and one up-firing tweeter create separation and height, critical for directional audio in Dolby Atmos playback.

Also: Sonos Era 300 vs. Denon Home 400.

The Era 300 works amazingly in a pair, connected to a powerful soundbar like the Arc Ultra. However, you have the freedom to set up one or multiple around your home as standalone speakers. The Era 300 is also fitted with a microphone for voice assistant support and Trueplay room acoustic tuning, though you can toggle the mic off at any time via a physical switch.

A pair of Era 300 speakers is also on sale for $798, a $200 discount.

According to recent reports, the Arc Ultra is Sonos’ latest and most capable flagship soundbar, with 15 Class-D amplifiers powering nine tweeters and six midwoofers. Two tweeters fire upward for immersive audio height channels, while the others are angled along the Arc Ultra’s horizontal frame for dialogue and high frequencies. The Arc Ultra’s most amazing feature is its built-in woofer, which delivers a clearly felt and heard bass response. You could hold off on a Sonos Sub for a while if you go with the Arc Ultra.

Sources indicate that also: Sonos Beam Ultra vs. Sonos Arc Ultra.

According to recent reports, the Arc Ultra also has intelligent features, including AI-powered Speech Enhancement, Night Sound, and TV Audio Swap with Sonos headphones. The Arc Ultra only supports HDMI eARC and Optical, though you need eARC to watch Dolby Atmos formats. So, if you need more ports, look elsewhere.

Sources indicate that the Sub 4’s price certainly isn’t anything to sneeze at, but its performance justifies it. Sonos fitted the Sub 4 with dual Class-D amplifiers to power two opposite-facing, force-canceling 5-inch-by-8-inch elliptical woofers. Their opposite placement eliminates distortion and keeps the cabinet completely still, even at high volumes. Additionally, the Sub 4 is one of the best-looking subwoofers I’ve seen, straying from the typical boxed design, so that’s a plus for design-focused folks who don’t want an eyesore of a sub in their living room.

In a recent development, I have a Sub 4 paired with an Arc Ultra in my living room, and the Sub 4’s takeover of low frequencies helps the soundbar truly shine. The Sub 4 is very powerful, so if you’re not pairing it with a first-gen Arc or Arc Ultra, you could get by with a Sub Mini, which is also on sale for $399. However, if you want two Sub Minis with a flagship soundbar, you might as well get a Sub 4.

The Sonos Play is the company’s latest Wi-Fi/Bluetooth hybrid speaker, blending the Roam’s portability with the Move 2’s room-filling sound. The Play sits on a charging base when it needs juice, and its 24-hour battery life lets you go on adventures beyond your home’s Wi-Fi network. With an IP67 durability rating, the Play is dustproof and waterproof. I took it to the beach this summer, and though its narrow base made it a challenge on the uneven sand, its durability and battery life were the last of my concerns.

According to recent reports, when you take the Play back home, you can pair two of them with a soundbar to create portable rear speakers, which is great for people who have a soundbar in a room but don’t want to invest in rear speakers, especially if the room isn’t very significant. The Play’s versatility is its best feature, which is why I awarded it an Editors’ Choice award back in March.

It has been revealed that the Roam 2 is Sonos’ most portable speaker, weighing just under 1 pound and easily fitting in your hand. This speaker is also a Wi-Fi/Bluetooth hybrid, meaning you can use Wi-Fi features like AirPlay and Sonos voice27 while at home, and rely on Bluetooth connectivity when you’re on the move.

Industry observers note that the Roam 2 sports a 10-hour battery and an IP67 durability rating. Despite its small size, I actually recommend it only to the most dedicated Sonos fans, as there’s little return on investment if you don’t already have several Sonos speakers. The Roam 2 isn’t a speaker you build a system around.

The Move 2 is the most advanced Wi-Fi/Bluetooth hybrid speaker in Sonos’ lineup, with three Class-D amplifiers, two tweeters, and one midwoofer to back it up. This speaker’s acoustic structure is identical to that of the Era 100, so if you don’t need the Move 2’s portability, go with the Era 100.

Also: Sonos Play vs. Sonos Move 2.

The Move 2 is humidity-resistant, so it’s not suited for splashes or accidental falls in water. Additionally, it weighs 4.3 pounds, so it’s not the best option for fast-paced adventures. The Move 2 is, however, a very solid choice for someone who wants a capable Sonos speaker they can easily move from room to room, or from the kitchen to the patio.

Sonos doesn’t put its speakers on sale often and generally doesn’t offer the most eye-popping deals, either. However, it’s rare for the entire Sonos catalog to be on sale, so this year’s Black Friday sale is a great time to buy multiple speakers if you want to add a few more components to your system.

According to recent reports, black Friday is always the Friday after Thanksgiving. This year, it falls on Nov. 27.
