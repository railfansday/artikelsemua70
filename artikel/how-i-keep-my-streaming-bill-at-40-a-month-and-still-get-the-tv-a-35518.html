Recent findings suggest that streaming services are the backbone of modern entertainment, with most requiring a paid subscription to access their best features and content. According to Deloitte’s 2026 Digital Media Trends report, 90% of US households have a paid subscription to a video-on-demand service, paying an average of $71 for four services.

Also: I replaced my paid streaming apps with free ones – here’s what surprised me.

Sources indicate that it’s really easy to end up with several subscriptions and a monthly subscription payout that exceeds $100. A 2024 Allconnect report states that the average American household’s cable bill totals $122 per month; new reporting shows subscription growth has stalled as people get sick of rising costs. What’s the point of cable-cutting if your subscriptions cost the same?

According to recent reports, the key to keeping your subscription bills low is to be realistic about your watching and/or listening habits and being able to stomach a few ads here and there. I manage to keep my music and TV subscription bill under $50 per month by signing up for free trials, assessing a platform’s content and how much I see myself using it, and going from there. These four made the cut. Here’s why.

According to recent reports, A music streaming subscription is a must-have for me, as I’ve given up on the radio years ago. Music-on-demand is a nonnegotiable, since I listen to music while working, driving, cooking, and doing all kinds of things in between. I take no qualms with a few ads here and there while watching a YouTube video or a TV show, but nothing is more irritating to me than a 30-second ad interrupting my living room concert, or a free music subscription tier forcing me to listen to my playlists out of order.

Also: I let Apple Music’s new AI tool curate my playlists for 24 hours.

Apple Music raised its prices over the summer, and the Individual plan now costs $12/month, and the Family plan is $20/month, up from $11/month and $17/month, respectively. Apple Music is my music streaming platform of choice, and a huge part of that choice is Dolby Atmos. Tidal also supports Dolby Atmos, but Apple’s catalog is more expansive and discovery-focused, while still offering tracks in up to 24-bit/192 kHz resolution, plus curated playlists, radio demonstrates, artist interviews, and music videos.

It has been revealed that if you want to put your family on a strategy, Apple Music’s multi-person plan is a few dollars cheaper per month than Amazon’s and Spotify’s. Amazon supports Dolby Atmos; Spotify doesn’t. However, Apple Music lacks podcasts and audiobooks, which Amazon and Spotify have. I’m here strictly for music, so I stick with Apple Music.

I spend a lot of time on YouTube. I won’t divulge all of my watching habits here, but I can easily spend hours entertained by my YouTube algorithm. In the last few years, YouTube ads have become weird, incessant, and overall unbearable. If that’s how YouTube tries to encourage people to pay for an ad-free account, it works.

Despite my YouTube obsession, I’ve always been stubborn about giving YouTube my money. But one day, on a 45-minute flight home to Atlanta from Wilmington, NC (a regional flight without Wi-Fi), I let YouTube bill my Google account for a Premium Lite subscription so I could download a video to pass the time. I vowed to cancel once my free trial ended.

In a recent development, now, you may be asking, “If YouTube ads are so bad, why would you purchase a plan that still has ads?” First, there’s a $7 difference between an Individual YouTube Lite and YouTube Premium subscription; I’m not willing to pay $16/month for YouTube. Second, the ads aren’t as prevalent as you’d think. I can watch hours of YouTube videos without ever encountering an ad.

According to recent reports, youTube’s fine print states that music-related content comes with ads, and I can vouch for that. Whenever I’m in the mood to watch BTS videos, whether they’re official music videos or fan-uploaded videos, I’ll run into ads. Fortunately, I pay Apple to access the same music videos ad-free in my Apple Music subscription. So, I go there to watch. Are you seeing my point?

No double-dipping over here in my wallet. That’s how they get you.

Sources indicate that if you want a Peacock subscription, you’ll need to honestly assess your watching habits to ensure you’re not overpaying or underpaying. If you’re a certified Bravo fan like me and want to rewatch all, and I mean all, of the Real Housewives franchises, plus others, you can get away with the Select initiative. This plan also has NBC demonstrates, like “Law & Order” and “Chicago PD.”.

However, I love Peacock’s live sports catalog too, so I go for the Premium plan. There are select live NBA and NFL games here, as well as once-in-a-while events like the World Cup and the Olympics. I watched both major events on Peacock and was so impressed by the coverage. Every Olympic match and sport was available to watch, and I could tap into NBC’s live broadcasts.

Additionally, live sports on Peacock are streamed in Dolby Atmos, which is a major plus. Watching your team lose in spatial audio makes it sting just a little less. The Premium proposal comes with movies and Peacock Originals, though I don’t watch much of either.

The Premium Plus blueprint promises no ads on most content (live sports are exempt) and the ability to download content, though Peacock’s fine print states that there are limitations.

It has been revealed that netflix is another streaming service I want to make sure I’m not spending too much on, and another one you should thoroughly assess before you buy the highest tier. Netflix separates its tiers by ads, resolution, and the number of devices that can simultaneously watch on the same account. If you’re like me — you don’t mind a few ads, your household consists of one or two people, and you’re OK with 1080p resolution, get the Standard with Ads strategy.

On the ad front, Netflix doesn’t bombard me with long ads every 15 to 30 minutes. I notice I’ll get 60- to 90-second ads every hour or so, which I can stomach. Considering an ad-free Netflix subscription is an additional $11 per month, I don’t have an concern. Netflix’s sign-up page says the ad-supported tier has “Less [ads] than you might think,” and it’s true.

However, if you have several more people in your home or a child or parent who lives outside of your house whose subscription you want to pay for, you’ll want to upgrade to a more premium tier.

I’m someone who will exchange watching ads for a lower monthly bill for every platform except music streaming. I think streaming platforms leverage ads to coax people into paying more to avoid them, but they’re not an issue for me on platforms like YouTube and Netflix. I use them as a break to check my phone, grab a snack, or snap back into reality after three straight hours on the couch watching TV — a wake-up call, if you will.
