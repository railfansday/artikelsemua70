Founded in 2011, insured.io was created by a team that came from the California MGA market, where they saw first-hand how far the insurance customer experience lagged behind expectations, and where high levels of transient customers meant people were regularly interacting with insurance systems that often felt complicated, dated, and stale.

In a recent development, according to Steve Johnson, Founder and Chief Product Officer at insured.io, that experience stayed with the founders as the firm evolved. After several years of building custom software for insurers, they decided in 2019 to launch a complete customer experience platform that carriers could deploy quickly and easily configure around their needs.

For Johnson, the idea was straightforward – he wanted to give insurers a way to deliver a better customer experience without having to spend years designing and building it themselves, something that could be particularly valuable for mid-sized carriers that didn’t have the technical resources of the largest players.

According to recent reports, insured.io is a complete customer experience platform for insurance carriers. Johnson defines their raison d’etre simply; any digital channel, that a policyholder may need to interact with their insurance carrier, can be deployed as part of a single, integrated solution. This includes a policyholder portal, IVR, text messaging (both inbound and outbound), and conversational AI claims first notice of loss.

Johnson says, “When a policyholder chooses a digital path to access their carrier online, it’s part of our platform. Our platform is designed to be scalable and configurable for any size of insurance carrier and any line of business, and our core tenet is future-proofing your customer experience, where customer expectations consistently outpace the technology that insurance carriers are able to implement.”.

He adds that insured.io has developed a platform that evolves over time, in order to continuously meet and exceed the expectations of policyholders without having to undergo constant waves of new digital transformations.

Industry observers note that being able to set yourself apart from others in the modern InsurTech industry is critical. For insured.io, the watchword here comes around being insurance-first.

In a recent development, johnson explained, “I was an underwriter before I was in tech. Our entire executive team has come from the carrier side, so our platform is built with that mindset: we make technology that works for insurance, instead of making insurance work with our technology.”.

What the insured.io founder makes clear is that the company isn’t a ‘generic, repurposed, no-code platform’ that they decided could work for insurance.

“We’ve constructed everything from the ground-up specifically for this industry, and built for consistency across all channels,” he continued. “While your payment processor may give you a payment portal and a telecom vendor may handle your text messaging, we’re a truly omnichannel, vertical SaaS platform; through a singular integration with a policy management system, we can deploy all of these channels, so they’re all interconnected.”.

Additionally, the insured.io platform puts speed-to-market as a central plank, and Johnson believes this is something that has been lacking in the insurance market for a long time.

He remarked, “When it comes to implementing projects, especially with customer experience in mind, speed-tomarket is paramount because you don’t want to get caught in an infinite development process, where by the time you launch, whatever you’ve done is now out of date. Our goal is to always get introduced within 90 days.”.

A stumbling block for some firms in the InsurTech market has been mistaking digitisation for better CX.

In the view of Johnson, digitisation is a piece of the puzzle, but it isn’t the whole package.

“Customer experience is truly its own discipline, and it has its own unique solutions outside of the scope of just digitisation ,’ he said. “The sole act of digital transformation is not necessarily solving for CX. While solving insurance inefficiency can lead to better CX, you must take a targeted approach when it comes to today’s policyholders.”.

Johnson believes that on this matter, it takes a focus on CX to improve it, and it’s not just a side effect of improving components within the insurance ecosystem.

A key question posed to Johnson is whether insurers are optimising for efficiency at the expense of experience. Does he believe this?

“I think this is true, but it’s not necessarily on purpose,” he said. “It comes from a lack of understanding about what a good user experience for policyholders is. What may be the most efficient internally isn’t necessarily the solution to keep your policyholders satisfied.

“Implementing a chatbot, for example, can be a great example of embracing technology at the expense of the user experience.” said Johnson. “A chatbot should not just be a feather in your technological cap, it must solve real problems for real customers. Otherwise, your new efficiency might inadvertently cost you customers and time.”.

All of these things combined together, Johnson remarks, show how key it is to target customer experience the right way.

It has been revealed that on the question of whether claims experience is still the defining chapter in the insurance CX, Johnson is succinct, “You don’t get your Yelp reviews from the quality of your underwriting or your actuarial skills. They come from how good or bad the claims process is.”.

Johnson added that insurers are much less likely to get a positive review for a good claims experience than they are to get a negative review for a poor one. This, he states, means focusing on improving the claims experience is a huge piece of the puzzle.

He remarked, “One thing that is crucial for this is realtime claim status. Some 30% of calls coming into a claims department are just to see what is going on with their claim, and that’s because many insurance carriers lack the key piece of the claims puzzle, which is claim status tracking – what we call the pizza tracker of claims.”.

When you’re able to see where your pizza is from the moment it goes in the oven to the moment it arrives at your doorstep, insurance has no excuse. Claimants often have no idea what an adjuster is doing at any given time, which causes frustration and increases call volume.

A key question facing the InsurTech industry right now is what happens when customer expectations outpace insurance infrastructure.

Johnson believes this has been a problem since the time the ‘very first insurance policy was ever written’.

According to recent reports, he explains, “Customer expectations will always outpace insurance infrastructure. Insurance has been notoriously slow to adopt new technologies than other verticals. It’s a regulated industry, so it’s certainly easier to implement new technology when you’re selling pizzas than when you’re selling insurance policies.”.

Johnson states his belief that this will always cause challenges internally. Such concerns, he believes, come up around build vs buy, vendor sprawl, and the need to constantly play catch up with customer expectations. All of these issues run alongside the crucial need of still providing high-quality insurance products.

Johnson concluded, “One of the mistakes that is often made is insurance executives trying to design customer experience. It’s never a good idea to design these things by committee, because a focus is on loss ratios, reducing claims, and making sure you collect as much premium as possible may not always align with building a great customer experience. Ultimately, this is why it’s all about finding the right customer facing solutions that can effectively marry all of these concepts together, making the insurance process painless for the consumer from end-to-end.”.
