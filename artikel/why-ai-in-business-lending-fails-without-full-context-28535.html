Why AI in business lending fails without full context

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Banks racing to embed AI across commercial lending may be overlooking a weakness that no product demo will expose: the information their models can actually see.

According to NCino, a single business client can involve several entities and owners, with a relationship that stretches across C&I, CRE and construction lending, and even treasury services.

It has been revealed that serving that client well means understanding financial performance, existing exposure, covenants, collateral and the history of past decisions. Experienced lenders know how to piece this together. AI often does not.

It has been revealed that AI tools are already taking on intelligent document processing at intake, automating financial spreading, answering credit policy questions in natural language and monitoring covenants. The time saved on rekeying data and chasing documents can be redirected towards building relationships.

In a recent development, however, AI works only from the data within its reach. If that is limited to one application, one document set or one system, the model makes its judgement in a silo. A banker in the same position would recognise the gap and look for what is missing. An AI model frequently will not, and its output may still appear complete. The result can be worse than if a person had done the work.

This is why evaluating AI by its capabilities alone is risky. Point solutions can look impressive in a demonstration, but a tool that sees only part of the relationship can only do part of the job. The real question is what lending context sits behind those features. That includes borrower and financial data, exposure, credit policy, documents and decision history.

That context has to live somewhere. A system of record offers a reliable, current view of the borrower, the relationship, credit history and decisions taken. It may be centred in a single lending platform or assembled from connections across multiple systems.

Recent findings suggest that gartner’s Market Guide for Commercial Loan Origination Solutions maps commercial credit across its full lifecycle, from client acquisition and intake through underwriting, approval, booking, servicing and loan management. Along the way it interacts with core banking, CRM, and risk and compliance systems.

Keeping that record complete is a moving target, and Gartner’s recommendations provide a useful framework. First, connectivity: institutions should assess prebuilt integrations, ecosystem breadth and whether APIs follow industry standards. Second, adaptability: a componentised, modular architecture allows banks to add products and adjust workflows without overhauling the entire platform.

Industry observers note that third, explainability: as AI-supported credit decisioning grows, Gartner stresses model governance, transparency, data security and guardrails. Institutions remain accountable for how AI-supported decisions are used.

The stakes are rising. Gartner notes that many CLOS vendors now offer similar AI features, making functionality alone a weak differentiator. Banks that secure the right context first will be best placed to turn AI into strategy rather than just another tool in the stack.
