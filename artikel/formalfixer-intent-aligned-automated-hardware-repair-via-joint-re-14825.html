FormalFixer: Intent-Aligned Automated Hardware Repair via Joint Refinement of Specifications, Assertions, and RTL Implementations

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (167 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24037v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Functional errors in hardware designs can compromise correctness and security, and must be fixed before tape-out. Formal property verification (FPV) provides strong guarantees by proving or refuting assertions, but a failed assertion is not always evidence of an RTL defect: the failure may also come from a buggy assertion or from a specification that is semantically correct but too ambiguous or incomplete for direct formalization. This creates a practical repair problem that existing RTL-centric methods do not directly address: the repair loop must align specification intent, verification intent, and RTL behavior before deciding which artifact to change. \textsc{FormalFixer} addresses this problem with a multi-stage, intent-aligned repair workflow for Verilog designs with assertions. It preserves the original specification as authoritative ground truth and appends confidence-filtered refinements to clarify implicit constraints. \textsc{FormalFixer} uses both intent mismatch and verifier-generated counterexample cone graphs to guide iterative RTL patching. This workflow explicitly checks specification--assertion--RTL correspondence. Results support intent alignment for FPV-based hardware repair.
