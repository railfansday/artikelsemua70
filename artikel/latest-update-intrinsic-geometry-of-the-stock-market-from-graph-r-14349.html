Latest Update: Intrinsic Geometry of the Stock Market from Graph Ricci Flow

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (208 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2510.15942v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We use the discrete Ollivier-Ricci curvature with Ricci flow to examine the intrinsic geometry of financial markets through the empirical correlation graph of the NASDAQ-100 index. A key feature of our approach is that we begin from an entirely ORC-spherical initial graph --- one in which all Ollivier-Ricci curvatures are strictly positive --- in direct analogy with Hamilton's (1982) foundational setting of manifolds of positive Ricci curvature. What happens to such a graph under discrete Ricci flow is an open question: no prior algorithm was designed for or tested on graphs where all curvatures are initially positive and community structure is entirely latent. We introduce ARCUS (Adaptive Ricci Curvature Unsupervised Surgery), which answers this question empirically: the Ricci flow of an ORC-spherical correlation graph develops a discrete neckpinch singularity, the curvature distribution polarising into two sharply separated populations with a self-selecting gap ratio $G \gg 1$. The procedure is genuinely recursive: each subgraph produced by surgery undergoes its own Ricci flow, following the Thurston geometrisation programme. The method is validated against the ten-stock benchmark of Mantegna (1999) and applied to the full NASDAQ-100 correlation graph, producing an eight-cluster hierarchical decomposition consistent with known market structure.
