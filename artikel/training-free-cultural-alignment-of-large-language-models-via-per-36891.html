Training-Free Cultural Alignment of Large Language Models via Persona Disagreement

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (296 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2605.10843v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Large language models now take part in decisions that rest on moral preferences, and those preferences differ by country. A model's default preferences sit closest to one population, so a single deployment serves every country with one country's values. Existing fixes need per-culture training data, a per-country reward model, or access to model internals, which the countries furthest from that default rarely have. We show how far a country can be reached without any of them. Our method, DISCA, keeps the model's weights frozen, sees no country-specific human targets, and reads only the model's score for each answer option. It builds a country as a panel of four persona prompts grounded in World Values Survey microdata. The panel's consensus sets a country-conditioned anchor, a loss-averse refinement beyond that anchor is kept only when two independent runs of it agree, and a running country-level prior pools the decisions already made for the same country. On the 20-country MultiTP benchmark, DISCA lowers misalignment to human preference vectors on all seven frozen backbones we evaluate, by 10 to 24 percent on six of them, and a 14B backbone with DISCA ends closer to the human preferences than a vanilla 70B backbone. DISCA beats every training-free control we test on a shared grid, and on the two backbones where we can replay the exact same model scores it also beats probability averaging, logit consensus and majority vote over the same personas when those rules receive no country prior. Switching each stage off on the logged runs shows that the persona panel and the country-level prior carry most of the gain. Beyond crash dilemmas, DISCA improves social-norm classification on NormAd and moves answers closer to GlobalOpinionQA survey distributions than vanilla decoding does.
