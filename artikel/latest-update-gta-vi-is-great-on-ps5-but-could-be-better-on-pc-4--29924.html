In a recent development, while there is still no word from Rockstar about an official PC port for GTA VI, speculation points to a Q1 2027 release window. Still, it’s hard not to hold out hope for some improvements from the console experience. The biggest complaint from gamers so far is that the Xbox Series X|S and PlayStation 5/Pro lock the game at 30 frames per second and 4K resolution, with reports that even the PS5 Pro struggles with this at times.

Sources indicate that also: Can your PC run GTA VI? The minimum CPU, RAM, and GPU requirements to expect.

Recent findings suggest that it’s likely Rockstar will include menu options for setting frame rate limits, changing the resolution, enabling V-Sync and other VRR technology, and enhancing the visuals beyond what a console is capable of. Despite an absolutely stunning and highly enjoyable out-of-the-box experience on consoles, Rockstar has the potential to create a truly definitive build of the game for PCs that includes features not possible on consoles as well as some tweaks to the framework already in place.

Since many PC gamers like myself have already bought GTA VI on a console, double-dipping is going to be a harder sell unless Rockstar includes a few key features that make buying the full game twice feel worth the cost. Here are the ones I’d wish for.

Industry observers note that one of the more popular talking points online is the goal of getting GTA VI to run at 60fps at 4K resolution on PCs, and that is entirely possible with the right build and in-game settings. Rockstar is very likely going to include menu options that let you create custom parameters that boost your frame rate, as most triple-A titles released on Steam and other PC platforms allow you such control.

These are usually found under the Graphics tab in your game’s settings menu and include sliders or drop-down menus for setting everything from draw distance and lens flare to frame rate caps and input latency.

According to recent reports, even running on high-end hardware like an RTX 5090 graphics card with 32GB of RAM and a 10-core CPU to back it up, the texture- and detail-heavy visuals of GTA VI could give your PC a good run for its money. Reducing graphics quality or adjusting lighting and shadows means more gamers can enjoy the game. Dropping the resolution from 4K to 1440p or 1080p is another quick, simple, and free way to boost your frame rate, as the lower video quality renders faster.

In a recent development, I have to be honest: my decision to buy GTA VI on PC fully rests on whether or not Rockstar implements online crossplay between PC and console players or offers GTA VI Online as a standalone title. If you have GTA V on console, you know that you can purchase the story and online modes separately so you don’t have to waste money on a single-player experience you’ll never touch if all you want to do is run around Los Santos with your friends and cause chaos.

In a recent development, there is no such option for PC players — it’s all or nothing. The online experience for GTA V does not have a crossplay feature, either. This means that if you bought GTA Online for a specific platform, you can only interact with other players on that platform. I understand why servers are kept separate (chiefly, microtransactions), but crossplay is a huge sell for single-platform gamers as well as anyone who hates the idea of buying a full game twice for the same experience.

Recent findings suggest that as I mentioned above, I already have GTA VI for the PlayStation 5. I didn’t spring for any special editions or add-ons, but the base game still ran me a cool $85. If an online mode is tied to a single-player experience again and doesn’t allow crossplay, I’ll be perfectly content to sit back and watch my friends play. At least until I could snag the game at a significant discount.

Back in 2023, Rockstar bought Cfx.ce, the maker of the wildly popular FiveM and RedM multiplayer mods for GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2. FiveM is now the official gateway for GTA Online roleplay (RP) communities, which have exploded in popularity in recent years thanks to streamers on Twitch and YouTube posting highlights of characters trying to live normal lives in the game’s chaotic world.

In a recent development, rockstar’s purchase of the parent firm gave me hope that GTA VI would not only launch on PCs and consoles, but also with Day 1 support for RP communities. Including native support for the FiveM manager and compatible mods would not only make it easy for total newcomers to test the waters, but it would also ease the transition for experienced roleplayers from GTA V Online to the new map and systems of GTA VI.

According to recent reports, the Cfx.ce sale to Rockstar also brought about the launch of an official creator content storefront in Jan. 2026, giving PC gamers playing GTA V/Online an official support network for all manner of gameplay and quality-of-life mods, not just for roleplay communities. With GTA VI Online, it would be very welcome to see an integrated storefront for searching and installing mods, as well as a pathway for creators to share their work with the community either for free or for a reasonable fee to cover the time and effort put in.

In a recent development, currently, the kinds of mods supported are: custom maps and in-game textures, upcoming props and weapons, updated environments, character skins, and various scripts for roleplaying different in-game jobs. Day 1 access to the creator content storefront would give mod managers time to update code for GTA VI compatibility and players the ability to migrate their current mod library with very little fuss.

As it stands at the time of writing GTA VI doesn’t have a difficulty selector to change how gameplay feels for different gamers. It’s permanently locked to what would likely be considered a “Normal” mode, with enemies dealing out moderate damage and taking several strategic shots to neutralize for a pretty run-of-the-mill GTA experience.

There are accessibility options to make gunplay and a few other game mechanics easier for gamers, including a Colorblind Mode, high-contrast path and item markers, different in-world interactive icons, standard subtitles, and aim assist.

It has been revealed that if and when GTA VI comes to PCs, it would be a welcome change to see an actual difficulty selector to either add a layer of challenge for experienced players or a more laid-back and story-driven experience for casual-leaning gamers who are more interested in the story. In lieu of that, it would be very nice to get an expanded set of accessibility tools for refining gunplay and driving mechanics, allowing gamers to tailor the game to their playstyle.

The Rockstar-published game L.A. Noire gives you the ability to skip driving and gunplay sections of the game if you failed repeatedly or simply didn’t enjoy them, and it’s a feature that could see a welcome return with gamers with FOMO who want to blaze through the main story so they can talk about it with friends.
