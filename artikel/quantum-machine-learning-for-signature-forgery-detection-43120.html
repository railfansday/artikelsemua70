Quantum Machine Learning for Signature Forgery Detection

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (202 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.33163v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Offline handwritten signatures remain a primary behavioural biometric in legal and financial documents. Detecting skilled forgeries from a static raster image is hard because pen pressure, timing and stroke order are missing. This work studies a family of quantum machine learning pipelines for offline signature forgery detection on a public Kaggle corpus of images split into two classes. We pair a ZZ feature map quantum kernel with three classical learners: K-Nearest Neighbours, Support Vector Machine and Random Forest. We then propose a deeper hybrid that uses EfficientNetB0 as a frozen feature extractor, reduces the resulting 1280 dimensional representation to a qubit register sized subspace through a quantum compatible principal component projection and decides class membership with a nearest neighbour rule. On a single held out test fold the proposed hybrid reaches 92.62 percent accuracy. On stratified five fold cross validation the same pipeline yields a mean accuracy of 91.26 percent with a standard deviation of 1.13 percent. The pipeline keeps a quantum embedding step that is portable to NISQ hardware. We report a controlled ablation across six quantum variants, confusion matrices, a t SNE projection and per class precision, recall and F1 score.
