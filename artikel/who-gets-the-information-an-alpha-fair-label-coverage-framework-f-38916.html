Who gets the information? An $\alpha$-fair label-coverage framework for multi-label feature selection

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (229 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.32200v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Recent information-theoretic filters for multi-label feature selection disagree on how to distribute relevance over labels: some amplify features that inform a few labels, others balance what every label receives. We show these choices are implicit social welfare functions. Treating labels as agents whose utility is the information they receive, we propose FLIC, which greedily maximises the $\alpha$-fair welfare of per-label information coverage. A single parameter $\alpha$ moves from utilitarian ($\alpha=0$) through proportionally fair ($\alpha=1$) to max-min selection ($\alpha\to\infty$), and a dozen published relevance terms lie on this axis, most as special cases, first-order approximations or limits. The objective is monotone submodular for every $\alpha\ge0$ whenever each label's information is, so greedy selection is a $(1-1/e)$-approximation; this fails for $\alpha<0$, and the max-min limit is inapproximable. Fano's and the Hellman-Raviv inequalities tie raw coverage to the Bayes Hamming loss and normalised coverage to per-label relative error. FLIC uses bias-corrected, label-wise conditional information and has no relevance-redundancy trade-off parameter. On 22 data sets, FLIC is the best of 14 information-theoretic filters on seven of nine metrics, winning 86 of 117 pairwise comparisons significantly and losing none significantly. Among 17 methods it ranks first on ranking loss, coverage and macro-AUC with two classifiers and has the best mean rank (narrowly with ML-kNN), while three embedded methods lead on Hamming loss, one-error and micro-F1.
