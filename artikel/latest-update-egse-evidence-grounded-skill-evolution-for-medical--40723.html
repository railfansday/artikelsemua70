Latest Update: EGSE: Evidence-Grounded Skill Evolution for Medical Reasoning Agents

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (221 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.33909v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Medical reasoning agents combine foundation models with specialized tools to interpret medical images, yet reusing their historical experience remains challenging. A correct final answer can conceal flawed reasoning or tool use, synthesized advice may lack source support, and superficially similar cases may require different diagnostic procedures. In this paper, we propose EGSE, an evidence-grounded skill evolution framework that distills historical dialogues from similar cases into reusable conditional workflows and progressively refines a multi-faceted skill library through supported and validated updates to improve medical agents. EGSE evaluates intermediate reasoning and tool use and contrasts repeated successful and failed rollouts to extract useful practices and evidence-supported avoidance guidance. These experiences are synthesized into workflows with applicability conditions and checks traceable to source dialogues. Library additions and merges require source-grounded review and execution validation. At inference time, EGSE routes queries to skill nodes organized by imaging modality, question task type, and disease category, then retrieves and selects applicable workflows based on their conditions, the current images, and the question to guide evidence acquisition and interpretation. Experiments across four chest X-ray benchmarks show that EGSE outperforms representative medical agents and skill evolution methods in reasoning accuracy while reducing tool calls. It also delivers consistent accuracy gains across seven foundation models and supports skill transfer across datasets.
