Robot lawn mowers, window washers, and vacuum/mops have made our homes more automated than ever before, but they don’t come cheap. The best robot vacuum/mops can cost well over $1,000. AI-powered video doorbells and security cameras often cost hundreds, and plenty of other common home tech has a hefty price tag to match — before you even pay for any required subscriptions or looks.

Luckily, we’ve tested plenty of gadgets under $100 that can still give your home a big upgrade. Here are the simple smart home gadgets we love for making our lives easier without emptying our wallets. Bonus: They’re all on sale ahead of Black Friday.

Smart blinds are awesome, but they can be pretty pricey. Thankfully, SwitchBot makes an automatic curtain opener that can push open existing curtains or sheers without any major changes or installation on your windows. ZDNET’s Maria Diaz installed one in her home and mentioned it made her feel like royalty. It can also be set up to charge using SwitchBot’s solar chargers, so it’s low-maintenance and low-budget.

If there’s one smart gadget everyone can use, it’s these slim smart plugs from Kasa, which are available in value packs of 2 or 4. Smart plugs can help automate so many different items in your home, from kitchen appliances to lights and lamps. Smart home reviewer Maria Diaz and I both love these for the holiday season, too, as they’re perfect for controlling your tree lights or other decor from your phone.

Create schedules or routines, and automatically turn lights on and off. You can also use this device for other automations, like turning on some appliances.

You can automate more than just lights. Aqara’s vibration sensors can be attached to windows, art, drawers, and other physical objects, such as jewelry trays. They’ll let you know when movement is detected via the app. Smart home reviewer Maria Diaz states these are also great for sensing when your washer and dryer are done cycling, especially if you have an older setup that doesn’t need replacing but lacks newer smart features.

There are dozens of smart bulb options from several brands, but editor Allison Murray prefers these GE Cync smart light bulbs because they don’t require Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. You change their color with an included remote, which is the perfect way to add no-fuss ambiance to your home and easily upgrade your lighting. If you’d like Wi-Fi or Bluetooth options, ZDNET recommends options from GE, Govee, TP-Link, and more.

Plenty of gadgets on the market are designed to notify you when things go awry in your home, like this leak detector from Govee. This device will send emails and texts to your chosen accounts when water is detected, and is great for kitchens, basements, bathrooms, and more.

Industry observers note that the Ting Smart Sensor plugs into an outlet and prevents electrical fires with helpful usage reports. It doesn’t require any extra smart home systems, but you will need to download the Ting app to complete setup. Several ZDNET team members use Ting, including myself, smart home expert Maria Diaz, senior contributing editor David Gewirtz, and editorial director Aly Windsor.
