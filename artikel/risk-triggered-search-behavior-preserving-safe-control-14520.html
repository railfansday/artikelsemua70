Risk-Triggered Search: Behavior-Preserving Safe Control

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (251 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24409v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Pre-trained visual world models generate multi-step action plans from current and goal observations, yet terminal-proximity objectives fail to prevent forbidden-zone traversal or rigid-body collision sliding. Purely visual interfaces may lack analytical dynamics, structured states, or explicit collision geometry, requiring internal prediction of action-conditioned process risk. We propose Risk-Triggered Search (RTS) for frozen Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) world models. Rather than relying on static state features that cannot distinguish candidate plans sharing an observation, RTS unrolls action-conditioned future latent prefixes in the frozen world model and trains a lightweight risk head with process-violation supervision. To intervene selectively while preserving safe nominal behavior, RTS uses the same predicted prefix-risk criterion as both the search trigger gate and hard feasibility gate: safe nominal plans execute directly, whereas alarms trigger local Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) search to minimize nominal deviation within the feasible set. To prevent isotropic Euclidean minimal modification from shrinking obstacle-directed actions into zero-amplitude stalling, we introduce an amplitude-preserving metric $d_{\mathrm{AP}}$ that retains tangential progression while maintaining a unique zero at the nominal initiative. Under unified gating and this unique-zero condition, bypassing safe-state search yields closed-loop trajectories strictly identical to every-step same-kernel search. Evaluated under a process-safety benchmark and Safe Arrival Rate (SAR) metric across five tasks, RTS improves SAR over Direct by 12.67--41.67 percentage points on navigation and articulated control and +2.08/+4.17 pp on Cube/PushT boundary cohorts, maintaining 100% action identity with every-step search while cutting controller time by 17.71%--70.41% across 1,968 paired trajectories.
