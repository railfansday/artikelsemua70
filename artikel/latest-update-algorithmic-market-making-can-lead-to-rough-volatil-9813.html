Latest Update: Algorithmic Market Making Can Lead to Rough Volatility Through the Order Book

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (267 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24028v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Existing work suggests that markets can show volatility with paths rougher than those of Brownian motion. In theory this can happen when the order flow is close to instability with memory that decays as a power law. However, it is not fully clear how such order flow could arise from the predominantly algorithmic market making that characterizes order book dynamics. We show one such consistent mechanism. We treat each market maker as a desk that uses a first-order gradient rule to move the distances of its orders from the mid price toward target distances provided by an unknown oracle. We assume that distinct desks do not act synchronously in time but act whenever new information relevant to their respective oracles arrives, which each desk samples over its own characteristic lookback horizon. Across all desks the rule therefore takes the form of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with information-driven (mini-batch) noise. In the continuum limit this upgrade rule makes the volume of the order book follow a conservative stochastic partial differential equation (the Dean--Kawasaki equation) whose fluctuations drive the order flow. The distinct horizons of the desks further induce a persistent memory in the order flow that decays as a power law. Standard scaling arguments then lead to rough volatility. We confirm the key predictions of the model on simulated agents and on six months of Binance data with about 790 million orders. In liquid markets governed mainly by market makers, such as the perpetual futures of Binance, the order books are consistent with how our desks change the book.
