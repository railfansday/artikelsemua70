WePM: Hierarchical Conflict Reconciliation for Unified MLLMs

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (254 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.27365v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Bringing multimodal intelligence from the cloud to consumers' devices keeps sensitive data local and reduces inference cost, but deploying a specialized model per capability duplicates vision-language capacity under tight memory and compute budgets. We formalize heterogeneous capability density (HCD), measuring how broadly and effectively a model serves heterogeneous task families per unit of resident inference memory. High HCD across representation, generation, and decision raises two challenges: these capabilities need visual evidence at different spatial granularities under a fixed token budget, and separately collected task corpora yield conflicting updates to shared parameters, while cross-task paired supervision is costly and ambiguous for open-ended inputs. We present WePM, a family of pocket models unifying representation, generation, and decision within one vision-language backbone. Multi-Granularity Visual Budgeting (MGVB) allocates a fixed visual-token budget across global, regional, and fine-grained banks; Unified Listwise Knowledge Distillation (ULKD) enriches separately collected data with soft teacher guidance, avoiding cross-task paired annotation; Stable and Efficient Multitask Optimization (SEMO) regulates each task's contribution through explicit loss weights and exposure schedules, with sequential gradient accumulation under memory constraints. In the joint representation-generation setting, WePM outperforms all baselines across five evaluation groups, raising the Joint score from 38.15 to 52.74. In single-task evaluation, WePM-4B attains the best average score over 30 embedding, 39 generation, and seven typed-decision benchmarks (72.86, 62.41, 61.11). Against the separate-model stack with the highest HCD, WePM achieves slightly higher balanced performance with 61.9% less resident weight memory, a 2.64-fold higher HCD.
