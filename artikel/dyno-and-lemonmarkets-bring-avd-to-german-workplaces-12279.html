DYNO and lemon.markets bring AVD to German workplaces

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

DYNO, an Offenburg-based digital occupational pension provider, has joined forces with lemon.markets, a Berlin-based Brokerage-as-a-Service FinTech, to add Germany’s new state-subsidised retirement investment account, the Altersvorsorgedepot (AVD), to its platform.

Starting in 2027, staff at the more than 650 businesses that presently use DYNO will be able to manage both an occupational pension (bAV) and an AVD in a single place. lemon.markets will run the brokerage and custody backbone for the AVD, handling everything from opening accounts and safekeeping assets to processing subsidies and tax.

Industry observers note that the move turns DYNO from a provider focused only on occupational pensions into a broader retirement platform. Until now it has offered ETF-based bAV contracts without commission, and the AVD extends its reach into private retirement saving. Its “Pension Intelligence” tool combines the statutory, occupational and private pension pillars in a single view, helping employees understand how to maximise employer contributions and government subsidies.

Recent findings suggest that through its integrated API platform, lemon.markets manages the full investment chain behind the AVD. As an investment firm licensed by BaFin, it supplies a compliant, off-the-shelf private retirement account, meaning DYNO does not need to build its own execution, custody, subsidy or tax functions.

Sources indicate that DYNO retains ownership of its relationships with employers and staff and operates the customer-facing interface. For lemon.markets, the deal represents its first step into occupational pensions and opens up employers as a fresh distribution route.

It has been revealed that DYNO was set up in 2021 by Marc Karkossa and Luis Weber and applies the neobroker approach to workplace pensions. It describes itself as the first digital platform allowing firms and their staff to arrange commission-free, ETF-based pension contracts directly, which it says cuts costs, improves transparency and delivers stronger long-term returns.

lemon.markets gives banks, asset managers and FinTechs access to capital markets via a single API, works with firms such as Pleo, Holvi, Optio and Tomorrow, and has been part of the dwpbank Group since August 2025, which holds more than €2.4 trillion in assets under custody.

Sources indicate that DYNO co-founder and CEO Marc Karkossa said, “Retirement saving tends to work best for people who already know their way around finance. Many employees would benefit from a good retirement product but never get one, simply because nobody guides them.

It has been revealed that “We built DYNO to change that. With the AVD, we now cover a second product and show each employee how to use both to get the most out of employer contributions and state subsidies.”.

lemon.markets founder and CEO Max Linden remarked, “Whether someone gets a good occupational pension should not depend on union membership, a collective agreement or a works council. Most employees in small and mid-sized companies meet none of these conditions. Bringing bAV and AVD together on one platform makes retirement savings easier to manage. That is what DYNO is building for 2027, with lemon.markets as its infrastructure partner.”.
