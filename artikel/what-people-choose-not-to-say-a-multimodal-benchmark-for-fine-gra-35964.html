What People Choose Not to Say: A Multimodal Benchmark for Fine-Grained Information Manipulation in Conversations

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (151 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.31346v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Recent advances in MLLMs have improved social understanding, enabling models to recognize emotions and distinguish truthful from deceptive behaviors. However, existing benchmarks largely focus on coarse-grained emotion recognition or binary truth-deception detection, overlooking the diverse ways people manipulate information in conversations, such as withholding, leakage, fabrication, evasion, and defensive counterclaim. Understanding these behaviors requires reasoning about underlying facts, participant knowledge, and conversational context, making them difficult to evaluate reliably. To address this gap, we introduce InfoCon, a multimodal benchmark for fine-grained information manipulation in conversations. InfoCon identifies six fine-grained strategies and derives a coarse consistency label from their factual and interactional relations. We develop an evidence-grounded multi-agent annotation framework to construct and verify reliable labels from rich contextual evidence. We benchmark representative multimodal models and systematically analyze their fine-grained information manipulation understanding, providing a testbed beyond binary truth-deception judgments.
