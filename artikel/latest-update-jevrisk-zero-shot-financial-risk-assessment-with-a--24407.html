Latest Update: JevRisk: Zero-Shot Financial Risk Assessment with a Language Decision Model

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (244 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.26999v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Banks must monitor every customer's financial risk, such as money laundering and credit default, by interpreting financial activity in the context of the customer's profile. Existing risk monitoring systems of banks are mostly based on expert-designed risk indicators, which apply predefined thresholds to every customer and cannot adapt to their personal situations. Supervised models recognise risk patterns from labelled customers, but each label often needs an expert's review or months of repayment history, which is slow and costly. Financial risk assessment therefore calls for a zero-shot decision system that minimises the need for labels and generalises across customers and risk types. To this end, we present JevRisk, a system for zero-shot financial risk assessment with a language decision model. JevRisk works in two steps. First, following regulatory guidelines in the finance domain, JevRisk computes exact risk indicators with code. Second, the language decision model (e.g., \jev{}) reads these indicators together with the customer's profile and activity, judges the case in context with broad world knowledge, and returns the score of high risk. We evaluate JevRisk on the anti-money laundering task with public synthetic data from Scotiabank and on the credit risk task with real data from Home Credit and Lending Club. On both tasks, JevRisk outperforms rule-based and unsupervised methods, and is on par with or better than supervised methods (e.g., GBDT and TabPFN) trained on about a thousand labelled customers.
