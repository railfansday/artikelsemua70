MPC Sample 1.4 officially opens up the device to modders and third-party developers.

Akai is continuing to open up its various music-making platforms with the MPC Sample 1.4 firmware. My favorite (and the most affordable) of the MPC samplers has become a popular home for vibe-coded custom firmwares and mods. The company has decided, rather than fight it, to embrace the modding community. The latest patch adds several latest developer features, including support for the new SDK and custom Knob Effects plugins.

Recent findings suggest that plugins added to the appropriate folder on your microSD card are now automatically recognized and will appear at the bottom of the list in the Knob Effects menu. There’s also a Developer Mode, which lets you run scripts from the microSD card or access the sampler’s internal filesystem over USB, which will be handy for all those modders vibe-coding their own add-ons for the MPC. There’s also remote screen access for mirroring the display on an external monitor through the MPC Remote Screen Viewer app.

There are a few other nice updates, including flatten pad, which speeds up the resampling process for baking in effects on individual pads, and sequence record autoplay, which adds a looper-like experience. Akai also added a scale option to the 16-levels pad mode, so you’re not limited to 16 chromatic notes, and can instead play melodic one-shots in E minor pentatonic, B major, or whatever key your track happens to be in.

For a look at some of the custom firmwares, effects, and other mods already available for the MPC Sample, check out this video from Loopop.
