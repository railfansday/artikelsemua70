In a recent development, my keychain is the one bit of kit I get asked about most. People are often surprised at the things I carry around. I guess most people just carry their keys on their keychains, while mine is full of multitools, gadgets, and other everyday carry (EDC) essentials.

Recent findings suggest that here’s a tour of what I carry on mine. You don’t need everything on this list, but it might be a good place to start building yours. Remember, the beauty of this approach is that you can customize your EDC to suit your needs.

Recent findings suggest that the KeySmart Air is the core of my keychain. This device serves as both the main key loop and a way to attach an Apple AirTag to my stuff, so I don’t lose my entire keychain.

If I didn’t need the AirTag carry feature, I’d probably go for the KeySmart Mini (yes, I like KeySmart gear), which is 20% off right now. If you are a Tile user, the KeySmart Pro is a good alternative.

This security key from Yubico features both USB-C and NFC, making it suitable for most modern devices, from computers to smartphones and tablets. It’s 15% off right now.

This tiny multitool fits inside the KeySmart Air (and is now 30% off, down to $7). It’s a box opener, a mini pry bar, a mini ruler, a flathead screwdriver, and a Phillips head screwdriver.

According to recent reports, this handy box cutter is made from tough polyamide plastic and is a safe way to open letters and packages. It’s cheap, at only $6.99.

However, I use this device in a different way, and find that it’s a handy pry tool for opening electronic devices without damaging the plastic shell of the device.

These are retractable, portable, and lightweight scissors that are designed to fit into KeySmart accessories. They’re perfect for cutting threads and fingernails, snipping tags, or slicing zip ties. I’ve been using mine for a while now, and they’re still in great condition, and I’ve been cutting things with them that I shouldn’t — such as thin wires and thick plastic.

This stainless steel S-Biner SlideLock has two gates with sliders that lock securely in place. I find these locks perfect for things that need to be easily removed from the keychain, and they’re far simpler to use than split rings.

This tiny rechargeable flashlight has a runtime of 4.5 hours in low-power mode; it’s constructed of tough aircraft-grade aluminum and is water and dust-resistant with an IP68 rating.

Unlike most tiny flashlights that require a myriad of complicated button presses, this device features a twist cap — twist to activate a low-power beam, twist further to activate a high-power beam.

This has lived on my keychain for over a year now, and while the black anodization is showing signs of wear, the flashlight is still going strong.

I also tested the Olight IMINI 2, and found it to be a superb everyday carry flashlight, and I like the fact that the USB-A connector is integrated into the design. It’s 40% off right now.

Recent findings suggest that this glow-in-the-dark tag makes locating keys a lot easier. The tag is waterproof up to 50 meters, dustproof, and weatherproof. You can also use it for night fishing!

This compact multitool keychain packs seven handy functions into a single carabiner. It includes a flathead and slotted screwdriver, seven different hex wrench sizes, a bottle opener, a pry bar, a scraper, a key loop, and a built-in measuring ruler. It’s 21% off for Black Friday.

I regularly need a SIM tray eject tool, so now I carry this one on my keychain — and it’s now down to just $6.
