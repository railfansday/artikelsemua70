Binding Constraints in a Strategic Technology: An Industrial Vulnerability Index for the Foundation Model Industry

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (268 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2510.23421v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Industrial policy directed at artificial intelligence has become substantial. Public investment concentrates on compute, where private capacity expansion is already underway, and is largely absent from grid interconnection and generation. Whether that allocation accurately reflects the most binding constraint in production has not yet been systematically measured. This paper introduces the Artificial Intelligence Industrial Vulnerability Index (AIIVI) as a framework for assessing potential production constraints in frontier AI. We construct a composite index of input vulnerability for the foundation model industry from fourteen concentration and sustainability parameters, aggregated by an O-Ring functional form in which capability is strongly influenced by the most constrained input. AIIVI should not be interpreted as a direct measurement of production constraints. Rather, it provides an index-based approximation derived from observable indicators and weighting assumptions designed to capture relative exposure to input vulnerabilities. Using this transparent and reproducible framework for estimating the relative vulnerability of frontier AI production to disruptions across key input dimensions, we also evaluate the index at 31 August 2026 for the nine developers at the capability frontier. We find values of 0.75 under AI-elicited weights and 0.62 under equal weights. The analysis identifies compute and energy as the principal drivers of input vulnerability. Marginal changes in these dimensions produce substantially larger variations in AIIVI than comparable changes in capital, talent, or data. We highlight three exposures that receive limited attention in existing assessment frameworks: the increasing reliance on external debt for data-centre financing, local opposition as a constraint on data-centre siting, and export controls targeting deployed AI models rather than hardware.
