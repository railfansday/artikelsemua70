📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Arva AI, a company that deploys AI agents to make critical banking decisions, has secured $20m in Series A funding as it looks to push its accuracy-focused technology deeper into the bank back office.

Arthur Ventures led the round, and existing backers Y Combinator and Gradient also took part.

It has been revealed that according to the company, most back-office decisions at banks are still made manually, but this has begun to shift. Over the past year, Arva’s customers started entrusting notable decisions to its agents, with staff setting policies and monitoring outcomes.

During that period the firm rolled out its technology at scale with banks and FinTechs, among them a top-10 US bank, a payments institution listed on the S&P 500 and the global FinTech Wise.

In a recent development, arva argues that accuracy is the deciding factor in how much work a bank can delegate to AI. Areas such as financial crime compliance, onboarding, payment operations and lending generate a constant stream of decisions, most of which are still reviewed individually as backlogs grow. For AI to take on this work, banks must be able to trust each result and justify it to regulators. The company says general-purpose models fall short on both counts, lacking the required precision and often being unable to explain their reasoning.

In a recent development, arva’s agents manage critical decisions from beginning to end across compliance, onboarding, payments and lending. The company believes high accuracy depends on its models, agents and learning systems operating in tandem.

In September it set up Arva Research, an in-house lab developing proprietary models for the most accuracy-sensitive stages of a decision. Its agents draw on these models to process each case, while AgentCore, the firm’s continual learning infrastructure, tailors them to a given bank’s operations and policies over time.

Arva Intel, the lab’s debut model, carries out in-depth online research into individuals and businesses, and beat leading general-purpose models on accuracy by 13% in the organization’s testing.

Recent findings suggest that the fresh capital will support Arva Research’s development of further models and the expansion of AgentCore. Arva also intends to extend its agents to additional decision types, such as disputes, payment operations and lending.
