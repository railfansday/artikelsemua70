Latent-in-the-Middle: On the Security of Cross-Model Latent Communication in Multi-Agent Systems

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (249 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.34278v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: As large language models increasingly collaborate in heterogeneous multi-agent systems, latent communication has emerged as an efficient alternative to exchanging natural-language messages. By sharing internal representations, agents can avoid repeatedly processing the same context, reducing communication overhead and prefill computation. One such mechanism is cross-model Key-Value (KV) cache transfer, which uses a learned mapper to translate a source model's KV cache into one a target model can use. However, this handoff means the target model consumes context it did not encode itself, thus calling into question whether its safety alignment still holds. In this work, we investigate the security implications of latent handoffs by introducing Chasm, a framework characterizing cross-model handoff attacks across three fresh attack surfaces: the source model, mapper, and handoff mechanism itself. Our experiments reveal that latent handoffs substantially undermine safety alignment and prompt-injection defenses. Notably, a target model that follows only 0.4% of benchmark prompt injections natively, follows up to 44.2% when transferred through a mapper from a source model. On the other hand, for jailbreak attacks, transfer alone raises attack success by up to 24.6 pp within a model family and zero-shot attacks by up to 69.2 pp, whereas white-box optimization through the mapper boosts the attack success rate to 92.5%, against 37.5% for ordinary GCG on the same target. To address these vulnerabilities, we propose a lightweight target-side cache guard that corrects transferred caches to restore the target model's refusal, considerably mitigating these attacks.
