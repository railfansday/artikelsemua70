Latest Update: Cardiac MRI-informed electrocardiography enables biologically grounded cardiomyopathy detection

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (264 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.23925v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Precise myocardial phenotyping at population scale is limited by the cost and accessibility of cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR), whereas electrocardiography (ECG) is widely available but reflects electrical rather than structural and functional information. Here we develop MyoECG, a cross-modal representation learning framework that distils myocardial structural and functional knowledge from CMR into standard 12-lead ECGs, enabling imaging-informed cardiomyopathy detection and subtype classification from ECGs alone. MyoECG was pretrained on 45,241 paired ECG-CMR examinations from UK Biobank and a tertiary cardiovascular centre, then developed and validated across more than 1.8 million ECGs from seven independent datasets. Across internal and external datasets spanning prevalences of 0.2-54.7%, MyoECG outperformed five pretrained ECG foundation models, with AUROCs of 0.800-0.974 for cardiomyopathy detection. In a multi-reader study of six cardiologists, MyoECG assistance improved mean sensitivity by 0.109 (95% CI, 0.041-0.175; P = 0.009) and specificity by 0.056 (95% CI, 0.004-0.108; P = 0.040). In 41,128 UK Biobank participants, the MyoECG score showed genetic correlations with cardiomyopathy (rg = 0.409) and dilated cardiomyopathy (rg = 0.371), genome-wide associations at four loci, including regions mapped to NFIA and KCNQ1, and associations with 69 circulating proteins related to myocardial stress, cell adhesion, vascular biology and metabolism. The cardiomyopathy correlation attenuated to non-significance after adjustment for 27 measured CMR traits (rg = 0.131; P = 0.143), indicating that the score captures CMR-derived information rather than dataset-specific shortcuts. MyoECG offers a scalable route to CMR-informed cardiomyopathy assessment and a strategy for transferring knowledge from resource-intensive imaging to widely deployable physiological signals.
