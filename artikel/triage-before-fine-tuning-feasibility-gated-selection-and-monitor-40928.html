Triage Before Fine-Tuning: Feasibility-Gated Selection and Monitored Adaptation of Embodied Foundation Models for a Fixed Physical Platform

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (326 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.33772v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Embodied foundation models are increasingly deployed by selection: a practitioner picks a public checkpoint by benchmark rank and fine-tunes it on local data. The platform is fixed before the model is chosen, with little or no local data. We ask what such a checkpoint loses on a fixed platform and which part of the loss local data can buy back. We split the gap by what can repair it. A sensing gap, information the sensors do not capture, cannot be closed with their data; a latency gap, output arriving after the control loop needs it, belongs to the loop, not the weights; only a distribution gap is fine-tuning's to repair. TRIAGE turns this into feasibility-gated selection and monitored adaptation in five stages: gates on the sensor contract and on end-to-end latency that no score can override, probes on ten minutes of local logs, a ranking by target-relevant score among the candidates that pass, and fine-tuning that is monitored and checked in closed loop before release. On driving benchmarks, a deadline below a leading closed-loop model's latency makes it collide on 80 to 90% of routes, and losing field of view moves plans 2.2 to 5.7 times more than losing resolution at equal pixels. Local frames close 75% of a stability gap but raise perturbation sensitivity at small budgets, and picking by score violates a gate in two of four deployment profiles. On a full-scale vehicle, the factory camera failed the sensing gate and an added camera repaired it. The latency gate removed the two highest-scoring candidates, neither of which completed a closed-loop trip. The selected model passed acceptance in 4 of 5 closed-loop trips, and the closed-loop check withheld a fine-tuned model that had improved offline. TRIAGE lets organizations that cannot train foundation models from scratch find out whether a public checkpoint can run on the robot or vehicle they own, and what to fix if not.
