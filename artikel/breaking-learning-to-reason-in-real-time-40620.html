Breaking: Learning to Reason in Real-Time

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (224 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.33513v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: AI agents that operate in the real world must reason correctly and on-time, yet they right now do not account for how time should alter their reasoning. Modern agents learn to do tasks in environments that are entirely synchronous: where the world waits for the agent to act before changing. A real-time environment, by contrast, changes independent of the agent. Existing approaches try to bridge this gap by imposing a ratio of thinking to acting. However, for an agent to succeed in real-time it must learn to act swiftly when delay is costly and to reason longer when the benefit outweighs the missed opportunities. To this end, we introduce real-time reasoning training (RRT) as the class of training techniques for real-time reasoning agents. We develop three standard RRT techniques: (1) using real-time data, (2) decoupling acting and observing, and (3) curriculum learning in time. Together, these simple techniques substantially outperform strong baselines. Across time pressures, we outperform the strongest non-RRT baseline by an average of 33% reward on in-distribution and 55% reward on out-of-distribution tasks in two real-time environments: Frogger and Snake. These results demonstrate that we can teach agents to balance reasoning and responsiveness under real-time constraints, and that RRT opens a broad, new set of capabilities for real-time agents.
