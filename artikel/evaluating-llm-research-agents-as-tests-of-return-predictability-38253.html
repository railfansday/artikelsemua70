Evaluating LLM Research Agents as Tests of Return Predictability

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (214 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.04070v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: How often do research agents built on large language models report that they can forecast returns when the data contain no signal? We score the conclusions of four such agents as statistical decisions on the same simulated monthly return series. Without a prescribed significance level, the agents reported that their forecasts would beat the expanding historical mean in 16-44% of 110 null samples, whereas a Holm-adjusted regression test rejected the absence of predictability in 8% of the same samples. When a weak predictor was present, comparisons at matched false-positive rates did not establish a detection advantage for the three commercial agents. Instructing those agents to report outperformance only if they would reject the absence of predictability at the 5% level, accounting for every specification they evaluated, reduced their false-positive rates to 0-3%. It also reduced detection at a population $R^2$ of 1%: from 74% to 45% for Claude Opus 5.5, from 59% to 6% for GPT-6.1 Sol and from 71% to 15% for GPT-6.1 Terra. Whether the instruction holds the false-positive rate at 5% remains unresolved. For researchers who delegate the search for return predictors to an agent, specifying the final comparison deserves as much attention as choosing the forecasting model.
