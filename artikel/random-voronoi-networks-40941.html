Random Voronoi Networks

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (225 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.33875v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Nearest-neighbor methods are local, simple, and interpretable, but their non-parametric form makes them memory-intensive, expensive at inference, and difficult to integrate into differentiable neural architectures. We introduce Random Voronoi Networks (RVNs), a family of structured three-layer MLPs that express nearest-neighbor geometry through sampled Voronoi comparisons. The first layer computes Random Voronoi Features (RVF) from affine pairwise separators between selected training samples, the second layer aggregates these comparisons into Voronoi cells or top-k neighborhood scores, and the readout layer produces classification or regression predictions. We show that, when all samples and pairwise comparisons are included, step RVNs recover exact Euclidean k-NN, while ReLU RVNs provide a violation-score realization for 1-NN that can be relaxed into a differentiable soft weighting. Subsampling yields approximate RVNs with explicit control over the number of cells and features. We study several unsupervised sampling methods that trade off random coverage, directional diversity, coordinate sparsity, and empirical feature variance. Empirically, RVFs produce a highly effective data encoding strategy, providing performance boosts to downstream methods ranging from linear to modern tabular deep learning models. Finally, we showcase that RVF provides an interpretable feature map due to its k-NN based construction and ties to pairwise local directions. These results position our Random Voronoi formulation as a bridge between nearest-neighbor rules, random features, and modern tabular models.
