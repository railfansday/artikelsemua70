80% of mid-sized FS firms fear falling behind on AI

Mid-sized financial services firms are struggling to keep up with larger rivals on AI, with 80% of their leaders saying they are falling behind, according to research from TXP, a digital transformation business focused on tech consulting, development and resourcing.

The study points to a squeeze between thin in-house expertise and expensive outside partnerships.

The report, titled ‘Partner Dependency: The Hidden Cost of AI Projects’, polled 200 executives shaping AI strategy at mid-market organisations, among them businesses in financial services and insurance. Its central finding is that weak internal know-how, combined with poor handover of knowledge during projects, is locking firms into long-term reliance on external providers.

Industry observers note that some 65% of mid-market FS leaders remarked inadequate knowledge sharing has left them relying on outside partners to run or extend their AI initiatives. A further 56% admitted their staff cannot properly judge whether systems built by partners are working as intended.

Looking ahead, 77% expect this reliance to leave their own teams unable to oversee AI independently, and 79% fear it raises the likelihood that faults in AI systems will slip past unnoticed or prove impossible to resolve internally before they grow into larger problems.

In a recent development, financial pressure is another concern. Of those surveyed, 91% believe dependence on partners could push expenses higher over time, while 56% said spending on implementation partners has exceeded the value AI has delivered so far.

According to recent reports, trust in suppliers also emerged as an glitch. Two-thirds of respondents claimed some partners had overstated their AI capabilities, with firms soon discovering a lack of genuine expertise. In such cases, organisations can face delayed projects or the added expense of hiring a second provider to finish the job.

TXP operates as a digital transformation specialist, offering tech consulting, development and resourcing services.

TXP chief operations officer Tim Hurst said, “Mid-sized financial services firms often have smaller budgets and fewer internal skills than large enterprises. To keep pace with complex, critical technologies such as AI, partnerships and external support are essential. But without effective knowledge transfer, partner support often becomes partner dependence once the initial delivery phase ends. Costs quickly rise as projects extend beyond implementation into ongoing management. The value of AI is eroded and errors become harder to spot and fix, introducing security and operational risk.”.

Hurst added, “AI shouldn’t be the sole domain of larger, well-funded FS firms, and partners play a key role in levelling the playing field by helping the mid-market realise the benefits of AI. Mid-sized businesses should look for partners with strong security and compliance credentials who can integrate AI with their existing systems. Knowledge transfer and a clear plan to reduce long-term dependency should be built into every relationship from the start. Partners must build systems that work, and leave clients able to innovate long after they’ve gone.”.
