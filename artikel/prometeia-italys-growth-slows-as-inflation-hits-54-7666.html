Prometeia: Italy’s growth slows as inflation hits 5.4%

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The global economy has proved more resilient than many expected this year, but Italy is increasingly being left behind, according to the latest November 2026 brief from Italian consultancy and economic research firm Prometeia.

In its analysis of Italy’s position in the global economy, Prometeia found that worldwide growth has weathered both the energy shock and the rise in inflation. The main driver has been the race to invest in artificial intelligence, which is boosting business investment in the US and lifting exports across Asia. As a result, Prometeia has raised its 2026 global GDP forecast to 3%, while its projection for trade growth has climbed to 5%.

Prometeia expects major monetary authorities to keep tightening, with a further 25bp rate rise anticipated in December from both the US Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank. Europe, however, continues to sit on the sidelines of the AI-driven upswing, with regional growth expected to hover around 1.3%.

According to recent reports, italy stands out for the wrong reasons. According to Prometeia, Italian GDP flatlined in the third quarter, recording 0% growth quarter on quarter and bringing an end to four straight quarters of expansion. The firm has trimmed its 2026 growth forecast for the country to 0.8%, down from 0.9%.

Recent findings suggest that prometeia argues that the inflationary shock is now weighing directly on economic activity. The outlook for next year is weaker still, with growth projected to slow to 0.6% in 2027. A key factor is the fading impact of investments under the National Recovery and Resilience Proposal (NRRP), which have supported activity in recent years. The 2027 Budget Law is expected to offset this only in part.

Industry observers note that inflation is the other major pressure point. Prometeia reports that Italian consumer price inflation jumped to 5.4% in October, up from 4.2% in September. The surge was driven largely by regulated electricity tariffs and the cost of unprocessed food. Core inflation, which strips out more volatile components, rose more modestly to 2.0%.

It has been revealed that the firm expects headline inflation to peak at around 5.9% around the turn of the year before easing as energy prices soften. On an annual basis, Prometeia forecasts inflation will average 3.4% in 2026 and 2.7% in 2027.
