Sources indicate that the most anticipated game of the last two decades is finally here, but Rockstar has yet to announce an official PC release date or system requirements for GTA VI. GamesBeat claims to have confirmed that a port will arrive sometime in Q1 2027. Either way, you have plenty of time to make sure your build is ready.

It has been revealed that based on my experience with gaming PCs and the hardware requirements of similar games, I estimate that GTA VI’s minimum PC requirements could include 16GB of RAM, an Nvidia GTX 1060 or AMD Radeon RX 480 GPU, and an Intel Core i7-4770K or AMD Ryzen 5 1500X CPU. GTA VI shares a lot of technological DNA with Red Dead Redemption 2, which I used as a reference to estimate the minimum PC system requirements needed to run GTA VI at reasonable settings.

I’ll walk you through the PS5 and Xbox Series X|S specs, as well as the known requirements for RDR2, to help you decide if an upgrade is needed.

Recent findings suggest that the full download file size for GTA VI tops out at 223GB on PS5, making it more than double the size of GTA V Enhanced on PC (105GB) and roughly 1.5 times bigger than RDR2 (150GB). So if you’ve been trying to scrape by with a 256GB or 512GB storage drive, I strongly advise you to upgrade to at least a 1TB SSD for GTA VI. This not only gives you plenty of breathing room for other key programs and files on your PC, but an SSD will also give you faster loading times than a traditional HDD, which is an absolute requirement for the texture-heavy, sun-drenched tropical visuals of GTA VI.

Both the PS5 and Xbox Series X|S use custom AMD Zen 2 8-core proprietary CPUs and AMD Radeon RDNA 2 graphics processors, which is great news for PC gamers running older rigs. When a game is ported to PC, it has to be optimized for the lowest common denominator, and there are plenty of gamers out there running older 8-core Intel and AMD CPUs.

So, there is hope that the minimum CPU and graphics card requirements will be low enough that most PC builds from the last five years will be able to handle GTA VI on at least medium settings.

Since RDR2 and GTA VI share a lot of the same gameplay systems and game engines, I can get a much better idea of what kind of PC build you’ll need by looking at the requirements listed on RDR2’s Steam store page.

Sources indicate that red Dead Redemption 2 requires at least an Intel i5-2500K or AMD FX-6300 CPU, 8GB of RAM, and either an Nvidia GTX 770 or AMD Radeon R9 280 graphics card. These are fairly old components, so if you’re rocking a build kitted out with them, don’t expect to be able to push your game past baseline specs for frame rate or visuals.

Recent findings suggest that if you want the full experience, you’ll need at least an Intel Core i7 or AMD Ryzen 5 1500 processor, 12GB of RAM, and an Nvidia GTX 1060 or AMD Radeon RX 480 to meet RDR2’s recommended requirements. A lot of gaming PC builds you can buy now are outfitted with at least an RTX 30 series or AMD Radeon 8000 series graphics card and a CPU that was released within the last generation, so if you need a complete overhaul, it’s fairly easy to find a prebuilt rig at a reasonable price.
