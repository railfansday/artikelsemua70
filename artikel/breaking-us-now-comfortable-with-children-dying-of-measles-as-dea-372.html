Breaking: US now "comfortable" with children dying of measles as death toll hits 10

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, health officials in Pennsylvania and Wisconsin confirmed two more measles deaths late last week, bringing the national tally of confirmed measles deaths this year to 10. The total includes the deaths of at least four infants: two in Pennsylvania, one in Newly introduced York, and one in Wisconsin.

Industry observers note that wisconsin health officials said the latest measles death—the second for the state—was in a child. Pennsylvania’s health department did not reveal the age or other details of the latest death—the state’s seventh—besides revealing it was of an unvaccinated resident in Montour County. Overall, the seven deaths in Pennsylvania include two in Jefferson County, three in Lancaster County, and one in Mifflin, in addition to the latest death in Montour. An eighth death was reported in Franklin County, but state health officials have not confirmed it.

Sources indicate that for all the states with deaths so far, health officials have noted that they’re the first deaths in decades. Multiple outbreaks are underway across the country, pushing the national tally of cases to nearly 5,500 across 47 states and jurisdictions, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Pennsylvania’s case count alone is over 1,500, and Wisconsin is reporting nearly 650.

With outbreaks that have been smoldering and flaring since last year, the US has reportedly lost its measles elimination status—as medical and public health experts have widely expected it would. A panel of experts for the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), a regional branch of the World Health Organization, convened last week to review US measles data and assess its elimination status. The PAHO is set to announce its conclusion and the review’s other findings on Tuesday, November 17. But sources broke the news to The Wall Street Journal on Friday that the panel had found that the US had indeed lost its status.

It has been revealed that an elimination status is determined by international health authorities after a country has gone more than 12 months without continuous spread of a virus. The status is lost when epidemiological and laboratory data indicate that a chain of transmission involving a viral strain with an identical genotype and lineage has continued uninterrupted for 12 months or longer.

Recent findings suggest that the US earned its elimination status in 2000 after hard-fought vaccination campaigns over several decades. But US vaccination rates have slipped below protective levels in recent years, fueled by the rise of anti-vaccine rhetoric and vaccine misinformation from fierce anti-vaccine advocates, including current US Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. As vaccination rates slipped, measles cases skyrocketed. The current case tally is the highest seen since 1991.

Recent findings suggest that in reporting the PAHO’s determination, the Journal quoted Noel Brewer, chair of an independent US committee who presented findings about the country’s measles outbreaks to PAHO, on the country’s lamentable status: “Runaway measles outbreaks in the United States, as well as the needless deaths, are all due to low measles vaccination coverage. We’ve gotten comfortable letting children be stopped by a deadly disease and die from it.”.

The US Department of Health and Human Services did not immediately respond to requests to comment about the loss of the elimination status or to answer a question about how anti-vaccine rhetoric and misinformation, including that from Kennedy, has contributed.

It has been revealed that medical and public health experts continue to sharply criticize Kennedy’s and the Trump administration’s poor handling of the ongoing severe outbreaks. The CDC has not held press briefings in efforts to halt the vaccine-preventable illness and promote vaccination. Instead, Kennedy reportedly ordered the CDC to remove measles death data, and the CDC director declined to approve an urgent health alert about the life-threatening risks of measles during pregnancy.
