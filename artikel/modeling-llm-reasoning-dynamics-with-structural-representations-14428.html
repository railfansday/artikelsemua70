Modeling LLM Reasoning Dynamics with Structural Representations

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (188 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.23749v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Language-model reasoning alternates between sustained activities and local operations. We study how this temporal structure predicts continuation and the computation needed to analyze it. T/E/S combines text, local events, and spans of activity. Across reasoning and information-seeking tasks, events and spans improve next-outcome prediction in 70 of 76 evaluation groups for a Transformer and 74 of 76 for Qwen with LoRA. Span duration and preceding events are associated with transitions, and retaining event history across span boundaries improves prediction. We formulate budget-allocation counting and action-conditional continuation queries, prove counting hardness under explicit encodings, and derive conditions for affordable decision certification. In finite-process experiments, bounds that cancel shared continuation mass recover the same optimal actions as full dynamic programming while reducing mean inference CPU time by 77.7%. On 71 held-out HotpotQA and MuSiQue questions, a compact structural observer improves prediction of whether a fixed inference procedure finishes within a CPU allowance: Brier loss falls from 0.0836 to 0.0756. An annotation audit identifies substantial errors in that observer, separating this computational result from semantic validation of its features.
