Supertake, a platform that uses AI agents to convert people’s market beliefs into stock portfolios, has secured $20m in seed funding to scale its business.

Venture firm Adjacent, through Nico Wittenborn, led the investment, with Coinbase Ventures and Union Square Ventures (USV) also taking part, according to a report from TechCrunch.

A number of angel investors joined the round, among them Nat Friedman, right now head of product at Meta Superintelligence Labs, Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch, product manager Nikita Bier, investor Scott Belsky and Nemil Dalal, a former Coinbase executive now serving as a visiting partner at Y Combinator.

The latest capital comes after a $2m pre-seed round.

Supertake intends to use the money to grow its headcount across functions such as engineering, design and growth marketing.

Industry observers note that supertake’s stated mission is to make investing a meritocracy. Consumers describe a conviction in everyday language, such as “Everyone will be on peptides”, and the platform builds an investment strategy around it, which is then run by frontier AI agents. Any user anywhere can follow and share these strategies, meaning ideas are judged on their performance rather than on whether their creator has time to monitor markets constantly.

Industry observers note that the firm describes such beliefs as takes, offering examples including “Everyone’s drinking less and eating healthier these days.”, “The next pandemic will actually be an AI cybersecurity threat” and “Gen Z trusts comment sections over commercials, and TikTok Shop over Amazon”. It argues that while people form these views from personal observation and insight, acting on them is difficult, as it requires identifying relevant companies, managing a strategy and tracking the news.

Recent findings suggest that beyond creating strategies, Supertake also lets users discover and back the beliefs of others. Those who originate strong takes can benefit even if they have little capital of their own. The firm envisions the platform evolving into a constantly updating picture of global sentiment, with ideas ranked by real-world performance and investor backing. Leading “takemakers” can establish reputations and followings and share in the value their ideas generate.

According to Supertake, consumers do not need professional credentials, Wall Street contacts, a large balance, or knowledge of derivatives or ticker symbols to take part.
