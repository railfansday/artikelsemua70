Conformalized Tree-Based Gradient Ensembles for Out-of-Distribution Detection and Plausible Recourse

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (220 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.31319v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Counterfactual recourse provides actionable feature adjustments that reverse unfavourable predictions made by automated decision models. However, when recourse search optimizes purely against classifier confidence or narrow proxy scores over gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDT), search procedures can exploit boundary artifacts and low-density regions---an instance of Goodhart's law---yielding out-of-distribution (OOD) recommendations that appear favourable nominally while lacking manifold plausibility. In this paper, we study conformalized OOD gating coupled with actionability-constrained counterfactual search across two tabular benchmarks: Adult and Give Me Some Credit (GMSC). Calibrating finite-sample anomaly gates (gate_IF, gate_LOF, and the conjunctive gate_ALL) at significance level delta = 0.05 over isolation forest, local outlier factor, and k-nearest-neighbor signals, we audit generated counterfactuals against held-out GMM and OCSVM detectors alongside ablation studies (gen10, gen100, and relax). Across matched recourse pairs where both constrained and unconstrained searches succeed, gate_ALL significantly reduces held-out OCSVM flags on Adult (N=541, Delta flag_OCSVM = -0.0296, p_Holm = 4.0e-4) and lowers both held-out flags on GMSC (N=540, Delta flag_OCSVM = -0.0346, p = 1.0751e-4; Delta flag_GMM = -0.1663, p = 3.5679e-16) while increasing matched recourse cost (Delta cost = +0.3035, p = 1.3365e-5) and narrowing overall feasibility. Our findings formalize the cost--feasibility--plausibility trade-off and demonstrate how conformal OOD gating audits recourse reliability on tabular ensembles.
