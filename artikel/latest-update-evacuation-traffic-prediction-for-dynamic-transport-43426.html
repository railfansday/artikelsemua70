Latest Update: Evacuation Traffic Prediction for Dynamic Transportation Networks over Multiple Hurricanes: A Generalized Graph Learning Approach

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (261 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.34687v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: High traffic demand during hurricane evacuations causes severe congestion on major highways, leading to delays and safety risks for evacuees. Predicting evacuation traffic in real-time can significantly enhance evacuation management efforts. However, evacuation traffic is highly dynamic and uncertain due to evolving hurricane characteristics and diverse population response. Each hurricane has unique characteristics and evacuation contexts, resulting in different spatial-temporal traffic patterns. Moreover, the structure of detector-based transportation networks varies across multiple hurricanes. Hence, a model trained on the data from one hurricane may not generalize well for others. This study proposes a generalized dynamic graph learning framework using traffic detectors and evacuation data from 12 historical hurricanes. Leveraging the dynamic nature of transportation network, we develop a Graph Convolutional Long Short-Term Memory (GCN-LSTM) model that predicts evacuation traffic up to 6 hours in advance. For an unseen hurricane (Milton, 2024), the model shows strong generalizability, achieving an RMSE of 280.6 and R2 of 0.96 for 1-hour prediction, and an RMSE of 459.8 and R2 of 0.89 for overall 6-hours prediction. We also introduce a modular training approach, where we evaluate performance improvements by sequentially incorporating data from multiple hurricanes. The proposed model outperforms state-of-the-art baseline models for overall 6-hour prediction horizons. Ablation experiments show an 8.4% improvement in RMSE for the proposed dynamic GCN-LSTM model compared to its static counterpart. Traffic management officials can use the developed model to understand future traffic conditions during hurricane evacuation in advance and take proactive measures to reduce delays during evacuation.
