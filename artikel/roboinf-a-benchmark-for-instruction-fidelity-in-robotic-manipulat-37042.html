RoboInF: A Benchmark for Instruction Fidelity in Robotic Manipulation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (178 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.30555v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Precise language control requires embodied models to follow instructions governing both task outcomes and execution. Although recent methods train policies to follow increasingly detailed commands, existing benchmarks often assess instruction following alongside perception, planning, and generalization to unfamiliar scenes. Higher task success therefore offers limited evidence of closer adherence to instructions. We introduce RoboInF, a benchmark measuring how reliably manipulation policies adapt their behavior to changes in an instruction. For each of eight tasks, six to twelve expert-validated instruction branches vary the requested goal, procedure, or manipulation detail while holding the initial scene and robot state fixed. Task-specific checkers verify the terminal goal and use recorded trajectories, contacts, and event sequences to assess compliance throughout execution. The accompanying RoboInF-4K dataset pairs 4,242 expert demonstrations with simulator-derived annotations of motion and contact. We evaluate leading manipulation policies ($\pi_{0.5}$ and OpenWAM) and inference-only agents (GPT-6 Astra and Qwen3.8-Flash). All four models exhibit a gap between task completion and faithful execution, indicating substantial room for fine-grained instruction following.
