Breaking: VisionQ: Benchmarking VLM Judges on Criterion-Specific Comparisons of Computer Vision Method Outputs

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (180 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2610.00666v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Qualitative comparison figures in computer vision papers are commonly established to illustrate which method's output is best on a named criterion, such as visual sharpness or surface complexity. VisionQ investigates this key part of computer vision work beyond human judgment towards objective vision-language models (VLMs) as judges. From 1,409 CVPR and ICCV papers, it creates blind multiple-choice questions, each testing one criterion, with the correct answer based on the authors' claimed best result. The best of 21 judges aligns with the authors' claims on 69% of questions, against 32% by chance. Yet it keeps most of its accuracy without the criterion and changes its answers when the options are reordered. Trained on every option order of a larger question set, our VisionQ-Judge raises accuracy on unseen papers from 50% to 61%. The paper highlights the potential of VLM judges to provide objective, criterion-specific comparisons across a broad range of computer vision methods in the past, present, and future. Data, weights and code are available at https://huggingface.co/visionq-review and https://github.com/ReML-AI/visionq.
