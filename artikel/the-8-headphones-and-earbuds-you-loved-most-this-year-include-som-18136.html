Sources indicate that you can’t go wrong with a pair of trusty headphones or earbuds to accompany you through life’s busiest moments. We’ve tested plenty of headphones and earbuds, from budget picks and Android-favored models to AirPods and workplace headphones.

Also: Amazon’s Black Friday sale is live: The best deals.

In a recent development, we’ve spent all year testing headphones, and now it’s time to go over which ones you, our readers, wanted the most this year. Our favorite picks don’t always match your most-purchased picks, so maybe this list will help you find a new favorite.

We compiled this list with aggregate data from our user base — your privacy is protected, and there is no way for us to identify individual people’s purchases. Without further ado, here are the headphones ZDNET readers bought the most from links in our stories this year.

Also: Sony’s WH-1000XM6 headphones just hit their lowest price yet for Black Friday.

Earbud of CMF Buds 2 Plus / Prakhar Khanna / ZDNET.

It has been revealed that the CMF Buds 2 Plus are a solid pair of sub-$100 wireless earbuds, with support for Sony’s hi-res LDAC codec, an IP55 dust- and water-resistance durability rating, and great noise cancellation. If you pair them with a Nothing phone, you’ll unlock Low Latency Mode for mobile gaming and ChatGPT integration.

Our readers have loved these earbuds all year, from January until now. Even if you don’t have a Nothing phone or another Android phone, the CMF Buds 2 Plus have a surprisingly premium feel and look, despite their $59 price. If you need a reliable pair of everyday buds, look no further.

Industry observers note that I love having buttons on my headphones and the CMF Headphone Pro has plenty of them. / Prakhar Khanna/ZDNET.

The CMF Headphone Pro is another banger from the budget brand, with a $79 pair of stylish over-ear headphones with high performance. Like its bud-form sibling, the CMF Headphone Pro has remained popular with our readers from the top of the year until now. These headphones are a solid choice for Android people, with Google Fast Pair, Find Hub, and LDAC support.

Additionally, the CMF Headphone Pro promises 50 hours of playtime, spatial audio support, and effective noise cancellation. On the design front, the CMF Headphone Pro easily camouflages its budget price, with colorful accents and a multifunction dial on the earcup. There are many reasons why we, and you, love the CMF Headphone Pro.

Bose’s QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen) are the organization’s most capable earbuds yet, with an intuitive AI-powered adaptive noise-reduction algorithm, a design that’s highly conducive to effective noise cancellation, great comfort, solid voice pickup, and six hours of continuous playtime.

Review: Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen).

According to recent reports, we like Bose’s latest flagship earbuds, not for their flashy features, but because Bose has perfected the earbuds’ fit. They’re optimized to suit the way your ears respond to their sound and noise-canceling capabilities. We tested the QuietComfort Earbuds (2nd Gen) in our lab, and their noise-reduction performance trailed only Apple’s AirPods Pro 3 and Sony’s WF-1000XM6, averaging -30.6 dB across low, mid, and high frequencies.

In a recent development, sony’s WH-1000XM5 headphones are an oldie but goodie. They’re not the brand’s latest headphones, but they have several of its high-performance hallmarks. You’ll get 30 hours of playtime, excellent noise cancellation, Sony’s hi-res LDAC codec, and several of Sony’s companion app features.

If you have an Android phone, Sony upgraded the XM5 to support Bluetooth LE Audio, which includes Auracast and the LC3 codec.

Also: Your Sony headphones just got a useful Bluetooth upgrade with the latest software patch.

Since this is an older pair, you can usually snag them for around $230 to $250. If you like Sony headphones and want the best the company can offer without paying $460 for the latest WH-1000XM6, try the XM5.

According to recent reports, apple’s latest flagship earbuds are the most acclaimed earbuds we’ve tested, with an Editors’ Choice and Lab Award to back it up. The Pro 3 earbuds sport several iOS-exclusive intelligent features, including Siri Interactions, Head Gestures, and in-ear heart rate monitoring. With Apple’s vast software and promise for long-term support, you’ll get a lot out of the AirPods Pro 3 for a long time.

The Pro 3 buds topped our ANC lab testing, averaging -35.6 dB of noise reduction across low, mid, and high frequencies. The AirPods Pro 3 are my personal daily driver and one of the three headphones I’ve ever recommended buying full price; they’re absolutely worth it, and our readers think so, too.

Earbuds for sleep can be useful for people living in busy cities or in close living spaces, and a pair of traditional noise-canceling earbuds might not cut it. Our sleep-deprived readers chose the Soundcore Sleep A30, which we liked for their comfortable design and helpful sleep features, like alarms and automatic sleep detection.

Headphones for work don’t have to be boring, and one of our (and our readers’) favorite picks is the Logitech Zone Wireless 2 ES. You’ll get 25 hours of talk time and 20 hours of listening time. These headphones also have two noise-canceling mics with AI-powered noise-reduction algorithms for clear voice pickup.

Review: Logitech Zone Wireless 2 ES.

The Zone Wireless 2 ES is also certified for Microsoft Teams, meaning it meets Microsoft’s Teams Premium Microphone for Open Office requirements for hybrid work.

Recent findings suggest that the Jabra Evolve3 75 is our sleeper pick of the year, and our readers agree. We liked its exceptional call quality, hours-long comfort, and suite of office-friendly features. The Evolve3 75 supports remote IT management, allowing IT teams to deploy tech giant-appropriate permissions.

After testing the Evolve3 75 in the real world, we found they held up well outside the office, too. These headphones are a versatile, high-performing option for both workplace and outdoor use.
