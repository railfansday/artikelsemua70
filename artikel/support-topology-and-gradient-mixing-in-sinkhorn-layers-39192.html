Support Topology and Gradient Mixing in Sinkhorn Layers

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (234 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2609.07954v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Sinkhorn attention layers normalize weights over a fixed sparse mask by alternating row and column scaling. When training these layers, sparse masks are usually judged by their edge count, graph diameter, or forward reachability. We show that the sparsity pattern and marginal budgets also govern how gradients flow backward through the scaling steps. Differentiating one row-column cycle on column log-potentials yields an explicit row-stochastic Markov kernel built from the two half-step transport looks, which reduces to a reversible two-step random walk at a balanced fixed point. Because potentials are defined only up to additive constants on each connected component, reverse-mode gradients propagate as zero-sum vectors whose total-variation norm contracts at a rate given by the kernel's Dobrushin coefficient. This coefficient bounds both unrolled backpropagation and implicit-differentiation truncation error. Crucially, graph connectivity alone does not prevent gradient trapping: we prove a face-lattice dichotomy showing that a mask and marginals guarantee score-uniform one-step contraction if and only if every nonempty face of the transportation polytope retains pairwise two-hop column overlap; otherwise, large learned scores can push the contraction rate arbitrarily close to one. We extend this criterion to multi-layer schedules, prove exact one-sweep mixing for coordinate-factorized and butterfly masks, and derive closed-form bounds for expanders, local bands, and global hub tokens. Deterministic numerical checks verify our finite-difference adjoints, canonical sparse masks, adversarial score limits, and spectral formulas.
