Who are you? The Personal Imitation Game

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (162 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.24888v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Large Language Models (LLMs) now perform strongly on Alan Turing's 1950 Imitation Game, so we propose a harder challenge, the Personal Imitation Game, which asks whether a machine can replicate a specific individual in conversation rather than a generic human. Testing with 30 participants, we establish a human baseline and report the identity signals interrogators rely on: stylometric features, personality traits, and biographical facts. To demonstrate the framework, we design an LLM-based system that plays the game for one of the paper's authors, drawing on an autoethnographic dataset spanning ten years of personal digital communication. In a 20-person deception study, interrogators choose our system over the author's own friends in 7 of 10 rounds, and the author over our system in 13 of 20; neither rate is distinguishable from chance at this sample size. We consider design improvements, applications of personal AI agents, and implications for identity and privacy.
