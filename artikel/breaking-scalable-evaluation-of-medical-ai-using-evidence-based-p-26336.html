Breaking: Scalable Evaluation of Medical AI using Evidence-based Practice Recommendations

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (252 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.27007v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Rigorously evaluating medical artificial intelligence (AI) requires testing open-ended clinical management across diverse presentations and stages of care. However, existing benchmarks rely on manual expert authoring, limiting coverage across conditions, conditional care pathways, and evolving guidelines. We develop an automated pipeline that extracts and verifies structured clinical criteria and branching decision trees from practice recommendations to generate scenarios spanning the care continuum, including nuanced presentations and practice variations across regions and guideline editions. Applied to 436 BMJ Best Practice clinical monographs (354 US, 82 International), this yields 3,200 scenarios spanning differential diagnosis, investigations, treatment, and complications, plus targeted suites for regional specificity (731 US/International pairs) and temporal freshness (630 scenarios). To grade responses, we develop a clinician-calibrated autorater that scores management aims on a 4-point rubric stratifying deviations by clinical severity. Across Gemini models, scaling improves adherence, while web search (+0.8 percentage points [pp] on average) and multi-sample synthesis (+2.2 pp) combine additively (+2.8 pp). In context, full monographs consistently trail condensed excerpts. On targeted suites, web search narrows international performance gaps (+4.2 pp) and improves adherence to newly updated guidance (+4.6 pp). In a study with Primary Care Physicians (n = 9) across 200 scenarios, AI assistance raises adherence from 50.5% to 70.0%, driven largely by fewer clinical omissions. Overall, our results demonstrate a scalable evaluation setup using an evidence-based corpus originally written to guide human clinical decision-making, and demonstrate its viability to assess medical AI and its assistance on clinician decision-making.
