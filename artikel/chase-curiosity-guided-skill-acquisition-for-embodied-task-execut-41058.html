CHASE: Curiosity-Guided Skill Acquisition for Embodied Task Execution

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (163 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.34416v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Robots executing tasks in partially observed environments must resolve uncertainty while operating with incomplete skills. Inspired by human curiosity, we investigate how robots can formulate task-relevant hypotheses, seek evidence, and acquire missing skills during task execution. We propose CHASE, a Curiosity-guided Harness for Acquiring Skills from Evidence, which operationalizes curiosity as selecting investigations that resolve task uncertainty and expand executable capabilities. Four modules connect dynamic scene understanding, hypothesis-driven planning, on-demand skill acquisition, and verified execution. The planner balances information value, skill reuse, and acquisition/execution cost. Missing skills trigger bounded exploration, learning from verified segments, and held-out admission during acquisition; separate checks verify physical effects and acquired evidence. Newly admitted skills expand the executable investigation set, closing the loop between resolving uncertainty and acquiring capabilities. Simulation and real-robot experiments evaluate investigation, skill acquisition, and task execution. CHASE achieves 50-80% task success under combined information and capability gaps, with 70-90% evidence resolution.
