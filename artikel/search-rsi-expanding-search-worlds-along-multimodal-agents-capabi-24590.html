Search-RSI: Expanding Search Worlds Along Multimodal Agents' Capability Frontier

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (276 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.27780v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Multimodal search agents answer knowledge intensive visual questions by grounding them in retrieved evidence, and recent recipes train them on multi hop tasks that are synthesized from knowledge graphs and grown online to keep pace with the agent. These recipes, however, treat the searchable world as fixed: a new question can be well formed and verifiable while its evidence is absent from the retrieval backend, so the agent issues the right query, retrieves nothing, and is penalized as if its policy were at fault. Such unreachable negatives accumulate with every round of growth. We propose Search-RSI, a recursive self improvement framework that expands the search world along the agent's capability frontier. In each round, the current policy diagnoses its partially mastered regions of a task space spanned by hop depth, relation rarity, and distractor density; new tasks there are grown together with their evidence; and the same policy decides how they are used: candidates on which its probes disagree feed reinforcement learning, while those beyond its reach become hint guided demonstrations of its own. Keeping the policy inside this loop matters: an improver frozen at the start of training loses 3.2 points. On nine multimodal search benchmarks, Search-RSI keeps improving where static task growth saturates, raising the average accuracy of the recipe it builds on by 9.8 and 9.5 points at 9B and 27B, of which 7.0 points are out of reach for a world that is merely made consistent. The loop uses no external model and only privileged task structure that is removed before training, at 1.27x the compute of static growth.
