FINRA’s fresh outside activities rule rewrites compliance

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

US broker-dealers face a significant overhaul of their compliance frameworks as FINRA’s new Rule 3290, covering outside activities requirements, reshapes how firms assess and supervise the business interests and securities dealings their registered representatives pursue beyond their day jobs.

The rule takes effect on 25 October 2027. While that may appear comfortably distant, the scale of change involved means firms that delay preparation risk being caught short. Rule 3290 replaces two long-standing provisions, Rule 3270 on outside business activities and Rule 3280 on private securities transactions, consolidating them into a single framework with new terminology and definitions that compliance teams will need to absorb.

Sources indicate that to help firms navigate the transition, RegTech specialist RegEd hosted an in-depth discussion on the fresh requirements. The session unpacked the key terminology and definitions underpinning the rule, along with practical considerations for adapting policies, processes and technology to meet the updated standards.

Sources indicate that A central focus was distinctions the rule draws between outside activities, outside securities transactions and transactions involving selling compensation. Understanding where an activity falls within these categories will be critical, as each carries different obligations. Misclassification could expose firms to supervisory gaps and regulatory scrutiny.

The session also examines the broader implications of Rule 3290 across the compliance lifecycle, including disclosure, assessment, approval, supervision and recordkeeping. For many firms, this is likely to mean revisiting how representatives report outside activities, how those disclosures are reviewed and approved, and how ongoing oversight is documented.

Beyond the rulebook itself, RegEd addresses the operational side of implementation. Firms will need to consider updates to written policies, internal workflows, compliance systems and staff training. For organisations still relying on manual or fragmented processes to track outside activities, the latest rule may serve as a catalyst for investing in more automated, technology-driven oversight.

RegEd also shared insights into how its clients are approaching preparation, offering attendees a view of emerging best practice, and outline how the firm can support firms’ compliance programmes as requirements continue to evolve.
