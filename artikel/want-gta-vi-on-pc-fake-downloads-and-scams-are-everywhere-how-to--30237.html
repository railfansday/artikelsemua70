Industry observers note that GTA VI isn’t yet available for PC, but that hasn’t stopped scammers from flooding the web with fake downloads that could put your PC at risk. These range from poorly made GTA VI clones vibe-coded with AI to actively malicious, virus-riddled downloads designed to turn your PC into a crypto mining operation or steal your money and data. Cybersecurity provider Kaspersky reported recording 27,416 detections of GTA VI-related malware and unwanted apps since June.

Also: Can your PC run GTA VI? Minimum requirements to expect.

You can probably spot the majority of GTA VI scams a mile away, but you’re never immune to an official-looking webpage or the promise of a free copy. No matter how official a page seems, unless the news is from Rockstar itself, you’re being lied to. And take any claims of the game code being dumped and cracked for PC with a large grain of salt. The only way you can play on PC is to use the PlayStation Remote Play app to stream from your console to your PC.

Recent findings suggest that rockstar has a history of making PC gamers wait their turn for titles. Red Dead Redemption 2 got its PC port a year after consoles, and GTA 5 came to PCs almost two years after launch. While there is a rumor that Rockstar will be bring GTA VI to PCs early next year, there hasn’t been an official announcement.

According to recent reports, so what if you got fooled into downloading a fake version of GTA VI? Fortunately, cleaning up and protecting your PC is a fairly straightforward process.

Recent findings suggest that alright, so you’ve downloaded a fake version of GTA VI. Your very first move should be to immediately uninstall the program. Getting rid of the software limits a scammer’s or hacker’s options for hijacking your PC. But you can’t stop there.

Sources indicate that the fake GTA VI program itself might be gone now, but it could still be hiding malicious files deep inside your PC and wreaking havoc. Running antivirus software like AVG Avast, even the free version, can help track them all down for quarantine and destruction.

A “cracked” PC program is any software whose copy protection has been circumvented and removed, while a keygen is a program that reverse-engineers the license validation algorithm your PC uses to generate fake but official-looking product keys.

Both processes are heavily involved in the software pirating and sharing community, and you’ll be able to find hundreds of users across dozens of less-than-legal forums claiming to have dumped the source code for GTA VI and cracked it, making it available for PC users and modders.

While a few might actually be trying to distribute the game for free, no strings attached, most will likely have a host of malware hiding inside to hijack your PC.

As my grandfather used to say: If it smells like b.s., it is. The vast majority of GTA VI download scams fall apart under the slightest bit of scrutiny, so don’t be afraid to follow the money, as it were.

Notorious cracker/leaker Cyberleek claimed to have the entire source code for the Xbox build of GTA VI and would release it on Nov. 23 — but only if his scammy crypto coin hit $15 million in market cap — which was then lowered to $6 million in a spectacular failure of self-negotiation. The hacker, or group of hackers, did post a collection of dumped assets from the game online (character models, texture packs, etc.), but there was no executable file needed to run the program.

It has been revealed that today, in a post I won’t be linking for legal reasons, Cyberleek has seemingly made good on their promise and updated their file dump with an executable. But it remains to be seen if the .exe is authentically decompiled so that modders and emulation developers can use it or if it’s another bluff in a long line of overpromises and lackluster deliveries. Either way, what Cyberleek is doing is highly illegal, and I cannot advise you strongly enough not to touch this download.

Even if it is a way to run GTA VI on your PC without a workaround or an official port, it’s also likely hiding all kinds of malicious code that will allow Cyberleek to hijack your PC and turn it into a covert crypto miner to boost their meme coin value before disappearing into the ether like usual. But if you’re gullible enough to fall for this very obvious scam from one of the most infamous online scammers, chalk it up as a hard lesson you needed to learn.
