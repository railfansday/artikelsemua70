CSSF AI questionnaire: what fund managers need to know

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Luxembourg’s investment fund managers have until the end of the year to show the regulator exactly how they use artificial intelligence, and a single answer could determine how much scrutiny follows.

According to Zeidler Group, the latest joint survey from the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) deserves far more attention than its statistical label suggests.

Released on 17 November 2026, the questionnaire must be submitted via eDesk by 31 December 2026. Authorised investment fund managers are among the addressees, alongside banks, investment firms, payment and e-money institutions, specialised and support PFS, crypto-asset service providers and Luxembourg branches.

Zeidler Group notes that while the exercise is framed as a survey, it hands the CSSF a detailed view of each manager’s AI usage, governance arrangements and dependence on third-party providers.

Sources indicate that this is the third AI survey run jointly by the two authorities. The first, held between October 2021 and January 2022, covered only credit, payment and e-money institutions. Fund managers were brought in for the second round in mid-2024, and the findings, published in May 2025, explain the regulator’s interest.

Investment fund managers made up the largest respondent group, at 261 of 461 participants. Roughly half were already using AI or planned to within a year, and they were the only category where most machine learning use cases (57%) depended on third-party vendor solutions. Across all respondents, only 43% had a formally approved AI policy.

According to recent reports, on eDesk, the procedure sits under “Investment funds and vehicles / Investment fund managers”. Zeidler Group highlights that this makes it particularly relevant for UCITS management companies authorised under Chapter 15 of the Law of 17 December 2010 and AIFMs authorised under Chapter 2 of the Law of 12 July 2013.

Industry observers note that there is some ambiguity over whether participation is optional. The CSSF communiqué states the questionnaire “must be completed and submitted” by the deadline, while the user guide calls participation voluntary but “strongly recommends” it. Zeidler Group advises managers to treat it as an expected submission, not least because a non-response will be visible to the supervisor.

It has been revealed that the questionnaire has five sections: General, Digital Strategy, AI technologies, AI third-party providers, and up to ten AI use cases. The pivotal point is question 6, where firms classify their most advanced AI use case as concrete (A), experimental or proof of concept (B), or not planned within 12 months (C). Only option C restricts the exercise to the first two sections.

It has been revealed that zeidler Group warns that AI is often more widespread than firms assume. Staff using GenAI tools such as ChatGPT or Copilot may already fall under options A or B, and AI deployed by delegated portfolio managers, central administrators or other service providers may also be relevant, both for the survey and for delegate oversight.

Firms should check their eDesk dashboard, align answers with their DORA register of information, outsourcing and delegation records and internal AI policies, and be ready to explain material changes since 2024. Conducting officers, IT, compliance and risk teams should agree responses before submission.

Zeidler Group also recommends using the exercise as a gap analysis, particularly around AI policies, rules on public GenAI tools and staff AI literacy, which has been required for deployers under Article 4 of the EU AI Act since 2 February 2025.
