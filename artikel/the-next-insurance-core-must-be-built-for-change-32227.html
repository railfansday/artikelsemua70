The next insurance core must be built for change

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Innovation in insurance will never stop — yet most firms keep locking themselves into technological dead ends that cannot evolve.

Technology is advancing rapidly, and firms are racing to embrace change for fear of being left behind. Nowhere is this more evident than in the surge of interest around AI. In only a few years the technology has leapt forward, and insurers are now working out how to bring it into their infrastructure — as an add-on, or as a replacement for existing systems.

It has been revealed that yet for many, this is simply another turn in a familiar cycle of replacing legacy technology. Insurers build core infrastructure around the latest capabilities, only for it to become outdated within a few years. Once the system becomes too limiting, they replace it — and the cycle repeats. Ledgertech co-founder and CEO Eran Tirer urges firms to break the loop and build their next insurance core with change at its centre.

In a recent development, tirer has spent his career helping companies modernise their infrastructure. He started at 17, building a product to estimate the actuarial value of claims in motor insurance. Around the turn of the millennium he worked in legacy modernisation and transformation, helping financial institutions move from COBOL to Java. He then founded EZSource, a code-analysis platform used by large enterprises — including insurers and banks — to understand and safely change complex core systems; the organization was acquired by IBM in 2016.

Decades spent inside large-enterprise legacy estates and complex transformation programmes convinced Tirer and his co-founder, Omer Caspi, that insurance modernisation needed a different approach. Founded in 2018, the Switzerland-based InsurTech built an AI-driven, cloud-native insurance core that lets insurers embed insurance products anywhere and replace outdated technology in phases rather than all at once. Its no-code platform enables clients to configure, test and launch fully regulated products in weeks, with support across the entire policy lifecycle.

The approach is already proven at scale. The platform runs in production with Tier-1 insurers — including Israel’s largest, with some $180bn in assets under management, which is replacing a 35-year-old core product by product on Ledgertech — and the organization was named Swiss InsurTech of the Year in both 2024 and 2025.

”If you just do a technology replacement, you are not driving any benefits for the business.”.

In a recent development, several factors are driving demand for more flexible insurance infrastructure, but none is more pressing than the rise of AI. The technology promises to revolutionise how insurers work — but it requires a flexible, open foundation.

It has been revealed that to implement AI properly, Tirer explained, it needs access to data. The data structures inside core mainframe platforms were never designed with AI in mind, making it impossible for the technology to use that data and realise its full potential.

That view is gaining traction and will become a major priority for insurers. The topic dominated the International Insurance Society’s annual meeting in Rüschlikon last year, Tirer noted, with speakers across the panels emphasising that improving core legacy platforms is vital to AI’s success.

Tirer added, “Insurers that are slow to adopt AI risk falling behind.” With AI able to improve customer interactions and risk management while reducing operating costs, failing to harness it is a serious handicap — especially heading into a soft market with intense competition.

AI may be the loudest driver of flexible modernisation, but it is not the only one. The older systems become, the greater the risk they pose — unreliable, costly to maintain, and dependent on a shrinking, ageing pool of developers who still understand them, while the technical debt keeps compounding. At the same time, some insurers have been selling stakes in operating companies, leaving them with capital — a large share of which is being directed into digital transformation.

Tirer said, “There are many compelling events that have suddenly been brought together into what I call a perfect storm.”.

Replacing old technology may sound like a familiar story, but the endless cycle of swapping one system for another every several years no longer works. Tirer said, “If you just do a technology replacement, you are not driving any benefits for the business. You’re still running the old technology architecture.”.

As an example, he pointed to Japan, where many insurers built their operations around Hitachi or Fujitsu mainframes. With Fujitsu planning to end mainframe support in 2035, insurers must now decide whether to migrate to another mainframe, as many Hitachi customers did before them, or take a genuinely latest route.

“If you move from a Fujitsu mainframe to an IBM mainframe, you’re just going to pay an arm and a leg to move from one old technology to another old technology that’s still supported. You haven’t changed anything. These companies are collectively spending hundreds of billions of dollars just to keep the lights on — 70% of the IT budget in an insurance manufacturer goes to running the current systems before doing anything that actually brings value.”.

Insurers may bolt AI tools onto these old systems, he stated, but they will never reach the potential of AI implemented on a modern core.

You move from one old legacy to another old legacy.

You move from an old legacy to a slightly less old legacy — the packaged core suites designed in the 2000s.

Or you move to a modern platform that actually brings the business fresh capabilities, like Ledgertech.”.

Recent findings suggest that for insurers building a new insurance core in 2026, some capabilities are non-negotiable — and flexibility sits at the heart of them. Insurers need to be able to build insurance products within days or weeks, and push updates to them within hours. Open, customisable APIs matter just as much, allowing firms to change a product without breaking its connections to other systems across the ecosystem.

Another essential for modern infrastructure is the ability to generate user interfaces automatically — for any mobile device or desktop — while staying consistent with the brand book. Tirer noted, “What a lot of companies did up to now is build separate digital stacks. So you have a core system on the legacy side and a digital stack on the open side, and they somehow link together — and when you make a change here, you need to make a change there, and so on. The cost of ownership doubles, and you don’t have any flexibility.” These isolated environments, he argues, need to be replaced with one merged ecosystem that keeps everything cohesive.

Industry observers note that A unified core also means everyone across the insurance business works from the same information, reducing errors. Whether managing agents, tracking pricing for renewals or monitoring discounts, everything is clearly documented — giving insurers full traceability and auditability of what happens inside the business.

While building the newly introduced insurance core is a top priority, that does not mean doing everything at once — that only invites errors and business disruption. “You can’t do a substantial bang. That was the approach years ago, and it’s just too risky — most likely it will fail.”.

It has been revealed that instead, Tirer argues, the better route is to start small. Ledgertech’s clients typically begin with a single product line running in parallel with their existing core, proving the platform — and all the connectivity around it — in production.
