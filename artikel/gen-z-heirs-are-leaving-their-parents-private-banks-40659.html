Gen Z heirs are leaving their parents’ private banks

🌐 Source: Fintech • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (494 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways

  • Drawing on McKinsey research, fincite highlights that around €2.4tn in private financial assets will pass to the next generation in Europe by 2030.
  • Germany demonstrates the scale of the shift.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Private banks across Europe face a defining test as trillions in family wealth change hands. According to WealthTech firm fincite, the coming years will decide whether inherited assets stay with incumbent institutions or move elsewhere, and the evidence suggests the decision comes far sooner than many firms assume.

Drawing on McKinsey research, fincite highlights that around €2.4tn in private financial assets will pass to the next generation in Europe by 2030. That is close to a fifth of the region’s private financial wealth. For a typical mid-sized wealth manager, fincite notes that 10 to 15% of pre-tax profit could be exposed, largely through outflows after inheritance and shifting client relationships.

Germany demonstrates the scale of the shift. Tax authorities recorded a record €141.1bn in inheritances and gifts in 2025, while the IMK estimates the real annual figure is closer to €400bn.

Recent findings suggest that loyalty is far from guaranteed. Citing a 2026 Capital Group study of 1,200 wealthy heirs, fincite points out that 54% of Gen Z heirs picked a new advisor after inheriting, compared with 38% of Gen X and 28% of Baby Boomers. Speed matters just as much. Some 91% of respondents made their first major financial decision within three months, and in Germany 67% acted within days or weeks, against 43% globally.

According to recent reports, german heirs do offer private banks an opening. According to the data fincite references, 73% fully implement their advisor’s recommendations, compared with 48% globally, and only 61% had the final say on inheritance decisions, versus 82% worldwide. Trust in existing relationships runs deep, but because decisions are made rapidly, fincite argues that relationships with the whole family must be built before any wealth moves.

According to recent reports, the next generation also wants a different kind of service. Some 96% of Gen Z expect up-to-date technology and real-time information, seven in ten used a digital platform after inheriting, and roughly a third put inherited wealth into digital assets or crypto. Human advice still counts: 78% say an existing advisor relationship was the most valuable factor during inheritance.

It has been revealed that fincite argues that current advisory models fall short. Capgemini found that only 17% of high-net-worth clients consider their advice seamless and personalised, while 97% of firms still segment clients mainly by wealth tier. Younger heirs often hold fragmented wealth across neo-brokers, crypto, property and business stakes, and 49% say too many options or conflicting advice is a major challenge.

To compete, fincite recommends five building blocks: a complete view of total wealth, mapping the family as a single unit, digital client access, transparent and traceable advice, and an early start well before the transfer occurs.

Its platform, fincite • cios, is used by more than 9,000 wealth managers across Europe and aggregates internal and external portfolios, including real estate and crypto assets.
