November 16, 2026 · Research · Science.

We’re expanding the ChatGPT for Academic Researchers program to give up to 200,000 researchers worldwide free access to ChatGPT.

According to recent reports, the program began as a pilot in July. Applications for the next round open Monday, November 23. We’ll award 50,000 newly introduced grants to eligible researchers on a first-come, first-served basis. Each grant gives the recipient and up to two collaborators free access to ChatGPT Plus. If you’re already on the waitlist and qualify, we’ll email you before applications open with instructions for activating your account.

Recent findings suggest that participants can use OpenAI’s frontier models and get training on how to use ChatGPT in their research. By default, we don’t use data shared through the program to train our models.

Waitlist signups by country or territory as of November 12, 2026. Darker colors indicate more signups on a logarithmic scale.

20,000 researchers in 33 countries have joined the program since the pilot began in July. Shuqi Li, a researcher at Brigham and Women’s Hospital and Harvard Medical School, looks for upcoming drug targets that could ultimately shorten tuberculosis treatment. He is exploring whether ChatGPT can help link lab and patient data to speed up antibiotic development. Arthur Porto, assistant curator of AI biodiversity at the Florida Museum of Natural History, and his team uses AI tools to turn photographs of museum specimens into searchable databases. They also created a 3D model of a butterfly whose wings change color as you turn it, just like the real specimen.

We’re also publishing Science at the Frontier: How Scientists Use ChatGPT, new research from our Economic Research team examining how more than 1,800 researchers used ChatGPT during the program’s summer pilot. Surveyed participants estimated that, without AI, the same research work at the same quality would have taken nearly twice as long.

We want more researchers to have the tools and support to pursue ambitious questions and make discoveries that improve people’s lives. That is central to our mission of ensuring that AGI benefits all of humanity.

It has been revealed that the program’s summer pilot helped us understand how researchers use ChatGPT and how we can improve their experience. Based on feedback from researchers, we’re moving away from lottery-based selection. For the next round, we’ll provide access to the first 50,000 applications who meet the eligibility requirements.

Sources indicate that the pilot gave researchers access to ChatGPT Pro. When we reviewed pilot usage, we found that most participants would have stayed within ChatGPT Plus usage limits. Based on that finding, we’re offering Plus for new grants so we can extend access to more research teams.

Each grant for this round covers the recipient and up to two collaborators, supporting up to 150,000 researchers. Our goal for the program as a whole is to reach 200,000 researchers, double the goal we announced when the pilot introduced in July.

It has been revealed that we’ll continue learning from participants about the application process, the capabilities they need, and the support that helps them use these tools in their research. This feedback will help us refine the program and shape future rounds.

It has been revealed that different scientific fields will use AI in different ways, and researchers will need different kinds and levels of support. We’re working with scientific communities to understand those needs and shape our tools, training, and programs around them.

Our support also includes helping scientific communities turn AI-generated results into deeper understanding and fresh discoveries. In mathematics, we contributed $5 million to a new Simons Foundation initiative⁠ to help mathematicians examine, understand, and build on results produced by AI.

We’re also providing different ways for researchers and institutions to access our tools. ChatGPT for Academic Researchers is just one way we’re helping researchers access advanced AI tools.

Through ChatGPT Edu⁠(opens in a fresh window), universities can give their campus communities access to ChatGPT for research, teaching, and learning, with tools for analyzing data and administrative controls for managing access.

In a recent development, for researchers working in biology, GPT‑Rosalind⁠ brings together specialized biological reasoning and access to scientific tools and databases. The Rosalind Academic Proposal⁠ helps bring these capabilities to eligible labs at U.S. universities, offering up to 30 seats at no cost for up to 12 months. Access to GPT‑Rosalind requires approval through our trusted-access process.

Visit our researcher access guide to learn more about ChatGPT Edu, GPT‑Rosalind, and ChatGPT for Academic Researchers.

In a recent development, as these efforts grow, researchers’ experiences will help us understand where our tools are useful, where they fall short, and what support is needed. We’ll use that feedback to improve our programs, develop training and learning resources, and create opportunities for researchers to exchange approaches with one another.

Our goal is to give more researchers the tools and support to pursue ambitious questions—and to keep improving that support as their research and our capabilities evolve.
