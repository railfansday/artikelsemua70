ROSWORLD: When Digital Task Competence Meets Physical Execution

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (171 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.27467v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Computer-use agents now solve most desktop tasks through direct software input, but a robot using the same computer must turn GUI knowledge into physical contact with a keyboard and mouse. We introduce ROSWorld, a benchmark that couples a physically simulated robot workstation to a live OSWorld desktop, so that a robot and a desktop agent pursue the same goals under the same official evaluators. The evaluated model receives observations only from the workstation camera views and operates the peripherals through either a single-arm rigid probe or dual-arm dexterous hands. Across five frontier models and 40 tasks in ten applications, models that complete 31-39 tasks on the desktop complete at most 23 with the probe and 28 with the hands. Paired trajectory analysis shows that models adapt their interaction strategies toward keyboard input, yet physical execution frequently fails through missed presses, incorrect targets, and unintended contacts. ROSWorld makes the gap between knowing what to do on a computer and physically doing it measurable.
