Measurement Artifacts in Trustworthiness Evaluations of Quantized Small Language Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (218 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.32848v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Evaluations of quantized language models attribute the change in a benchmark score to quantization. We write the measured change as the true change plus a term contributed by the instrument. Instrument error that is the same at every precision can shrink a paired comparison but cannot create or reverse an effect. Error that interacts with quantization can do both, and it is reported as an effect of quantization. We estimate this interaction for four measurement choices in 8 instruction-tuned models below two billion parameters, at float16, LLM.int8() and NF4, on identical items. Measured against labels from two human annotators, the 4-bit rise in harmful compliance is about half to two-thirds of the rise in non-refusal that a corrected key word detector records. Cloze and lettered multiple-choice scoring disagree about the effect of quantization far more than their noise allows: their estimates correlate at \r{ho} = 0.10, against a ceiling of about 0.77. LLM.int8() answers and generations depend on which items share a batch, and padding alone changes its answers. Rounding activations to 8 bits is enough to produce this dependence. NF4 is batch-invariant. The two largest reported calibration losses are response-bias shifts. We release item-level predictions, option scores and human annotations, and give recommendations for evaluating compressed small models.
