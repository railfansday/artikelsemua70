Gated Concept Adapters: Layer-Wise Concept Bottlenecks for Auditable Text Understanding

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (243 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.32600v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Human-facing text understanding systems are used to infer affect, moderate harmful content, and support socially sensitive decisions. However, Transformer predictions are difficult to audit because they are produced from high-dimensional hidden states that do not provide a compact, inspectable interface for human analysis or intervention. We introduce Gated Concept Adapters (GCA), a layer-wise concept bottleneck framework that attaches gated, low-dimensional concept-producing readout adapters to selected outputs of pretrained Transformers. Token representations are mapped into concept activations, filtered by learned gates, pooled into layer-level summaries, and exposed to the prediction head as a concept-mediated interface. The dimensions can be inspected through usage statistics, signature n-grams, post-hoc concept names, and targeted concept interventions. We evaluate GCA on fine-grained emotion classification, toxicity detection, topic classification, and a controlled verb-cloze stress test. In the affective and safety-relevant classification settings, GCA learns comparatively low-redundancy and intervention-sensitive concept representations while retaining useful predictive performance. On toxicity detection, it remains close to an unconstrained ModernBERT baseline while enabling concept-level inspection, targeted intervention, and lightweight post-hoc control. On GoEmotions, the end-to-end concept model incurs a macro-F1 cost relative to unconstrained RoBERTa, while a task-initialized frozen variant substantially reduces this gap. On AG News, GCA remains competitive with recent concept-based baselines, and the Qwen verb-cloze experiment clarifies the limits of concept-mediated full-vocabulary prediction. Overall, GCA provides a neural concept interface for auditable text understanding, prioritizing transparency, diagnosis, and intervention over unconstrained accuracy maximization.
