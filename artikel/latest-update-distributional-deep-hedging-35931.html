Latest Update: Distributional Deep Hedging

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (173 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.26652v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Deep hedging learns a hedging strategy by optimising a risk preference over a neural-network policy. The preference is fixed before training, so each upcoming preference requires a new policy and, in actor-critic methods, a new critic. We propose distributional deep hedging, in which the distribution of the hedging profit and loss is learned through its quantiles and the risk preference is applied afterwards, as a weight on those quantiles. We characterise the preferences that admit this representation, which include expected shortfall and its mixtures, and show that the entropic utility is computed from the same quantiles. At the current policy, each of these preferences is bounded below by an expected utility with the same gradient. This bound reduces risk-sensitive hedging to a sequence of risk-neutral problems, from which we derive policy-search and actor-critic algorithms. In numerical experiments, the learned hedges approach references computed by dynamic programming, and a single distributional critic trained without a preference can serve preferences chosen after training.
