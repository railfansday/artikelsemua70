On the Challenges of Using LLMs in High-Risk AI Recruitment: Guiding Questions for Legal Conformity with the AI Act

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (245 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.27462v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The EU Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act) imposes detailed obligations on providers of high-risk AI systems but offers limited operational guidance, particularly when these systems integrate third-party General-Purpose AI (GPAI) models. This paper introduces a toolkit that fosters the development of legal-by-design AI-assisted recruitment systems by translating the core legal obligations of the AI Act's conformity assessment---specifically Articles 9--15 and Annex IV---into techno-normative requirements and corresponding guiding questions. While recruitment pipelines may involve components with varying technical complexity and differing levels of algorithmic transparency, this paper focuses on the most challenging case: the integration of third-party GPAI models into critical stages of the selection process, including resume and job description parsing, applicant scoring, and shortlist generation. This scenario presents significant challenges for downstream providers of high-risk AI systems, as it underscores the difficulties in obtaining the technical information needed to demonstrate compliance with the AI Act's techno-normative requirements, both prior to and during market deployment. After introducing the toolkit and illustrating its application through a simplified example, the paper explores informational dependencies between upstream and downstream GPAI model providers. These dependencies may produce informational gaps across the AI supply chain, potentially hindering the collection of evidence necessary for the AI system provider to ensure legal compliance. The resulting lack of technical transparency negatively affects the deployer, who may struggle to maintain meaningful oversight of the system, and ultimately impacts the persons subject to the AI output.
