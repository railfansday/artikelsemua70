Industry observers note that AWS Certificate Manager (ACM) now problems Amazon N2 certificates for mutual TLS (mTLS) and other authentication outside web browsers. ACM public certificates no longer include client authentication, in line with WebPKI requirements, so teams that needed client certificates had to define their own trust governance models and apply them to their private PKI. With Amazon N2 certificates, you can authenticate to partners and other organizations, such as a partner's client calling your API, without building and governing your own CA for them to rely on.

Amazon N2 certificates come from the Amazon N2 certificate authorities (CAs), which are operated and governed by Amazon Trust Services, the same organization that operates the CAs issuing ACM public WebPKI certificates. Your peers add the Amazon N2 roots to their trust stores to verify your certificates. Certificates are valid for up to 395 days, and ACM renews them automatically: AWS integrated services use each renewed certificate, and you can also export them to your own clients and servers.

You pay for each domain name in an ACM-issued Amazon N2 certificate, including when you use it with AWS integrated services. For details, see ACM pricing.

Amazon N2 certificates are available in all commercial AWS Regions where ACM is available. To learn more, see Amazon N2 certificates in the AWS Certificate Manager User Guide.
