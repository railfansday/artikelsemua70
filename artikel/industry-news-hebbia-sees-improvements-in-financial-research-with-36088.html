Before a banker can recommend an acquisition to their client, they need to make sense of financial statements, customer contracts and management presentations. Hebbia’s Matrix helps bring that research together, linking answers to the documents behind them.

Hebbia tested OpenAI’s GPT‑6 Astra to improve two parts of that work: finding the sources a question requires and building a presentation that follows the banker’s brief.

It has been revealed that it could work like this: a banker is helping their client assess a strategic acquisition of an industrial-software company, with roughly 2,000 files to review. What makes its revenue durable? How does it compare with peers? In Hebbia’s hypothetical example, the banker could load the data room into Matrix and examine customer concentration, contract terms and churn in cited cells. Matrix could bring in peer filings and industry news to help develop the deal thesis, then turn that research into a diligence summary and valuation deck in the firm’s template.

In that workflow, GPT‑6 Astra selects documents or Matrix cells to support an answer, then turns the analysis into native PowerPoint slides that follow the user’s structure and guidelines.

“Traceability is really important to our customers because they’re basing millions of dollars of decisions on what the AI actually comes back with,” Kavita says. “Citations help with traceability as well as giving users confidence that the AI found the right answer.”.

Recent findings suggest that in Hebbia’s tests, GPT‑6 Astra combined broader source coverage with highly relevant citations. It scored 13.6 percentage points above the best alternative tested on citing the required types of sources, while 99.5% of the excerpts it cited were relevant to the question.

Industry observers note that hebbia tested a prerelease build in August-September 2026, using the same financial research cases and grading criteria for each model. Domain experts wrote or audited the criteria against source documents.

It has been revealed that “When you’re looking at a data room with thousands of documents, that’s extremely notable. Even a single percentage-point gain can yield a lot of marginal benefit,” Kavita says.

A presentation also has to look like work the banker would send to a client.

“I don’t want to create a deck and send that to my partner that looks like AI made it,” Kavita states. “I want it to be made in my firm’s branding, and I want it to look like a human-made slide.”.

Industry observers note that GPT‑6 Astra led on both following the brief and presentation quality. In Hebbia’s tests, it met 97.9% of the applicable presentation requirements, putting it 14.4 percentage points ahead of the best alternative tested on that measure. Other models traded off polish and adherence to the brief; GPT‑6 Astra ranked first on both.

“In the past, we’ve seen models that really shine on one or the other of these dimensions where they’re really good at craft, but the instruction following and adhering to guidelines is not strong, or vice versa. GPT‑6 Astra is the first model we’ve seen where it’s number one on both of those fronts,” Kavita says.

In Hebbia, owners can open a citation to see the underlying document and highlighted passage; slide footnotes lead back to the source material, too. A review could be as specific as checking a customer-concentration figure against the source table and correcting the reporting period before the deck goes to the investment committee.
