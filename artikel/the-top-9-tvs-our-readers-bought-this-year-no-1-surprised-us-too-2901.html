And while we certainly have had our favorites over the past year, so did our readers: the following TVs were purchased by hundreds of readers via links in ZDNET reviews, buying guides, and other content in 2026 so far. It has been revealed that the most popular TV with ZDNET readers so far in 2026 has been the Amazon Fire TV Omni QLED. Unfortunately, it’s no longer in production and has been replaced by the Ember QLED series TV, which offers the same features and viewing experience but with an updated design and a streamlined operating system.

Keep reading below to find out more about the rest of the most popular TV models that ZDNET readers actually bought this year.

The number-one pick among ZDNET readers is sadly no longer in production. The Amazon Fire TV Omni QLED has been replaced with the updated Ember QLED, which offers the same high-quality picture and audio with more refined smart features and better Alexa integration. The Ember QLED supports Wi-Fi 6 for faster web browsing and fewer buffering stops when streaming, as well as Dolby Vision HDR and adaptive brightness settings for the best viewing experience in almost any lighting.

Review: The Amazon Fire TV Omni QLED is great for gaming, but works just as well with Alexa.

The Bravia 8 II is an excellent OLED TV that continues to prove why Sony is neck and neck with LG for the title of king of TVs. The updated QD-OLED panel is brighter than its predecessor without sacrificing contrast, color accuracy, or detail. And the 120Hz refresh rate means you’ll get smooth motion in everything from live sports and movies to video games and social media videos.

More: Why I’m recommending the Sony Bravia 8 II over competing OLED TVs – especially to enthusiasts.

Most Accurate HDR Color Reproduction.

According to recent reports, the U8 is Hisense’s flagship Mini LED TV, and the winner of our Lab Award for most accurate HDR color reproduction — beating out several other far more expensive brands. This TV is capable of a maximum brightness of 5,000 nits for the best visibility in almost any lighting environment. It also features a 165Hz refresh rate, making it a great choice for sports fans and console gamers, as well as support for AMD FreeSync Premium Pro VRR to prevent screen tearing. Film buffs can take advantage of both IMAX Enhanced and Filmmaker mode for seeing movies as creators meant them to be seen.

Also: We lab-tested 7 TVs for color accuracy – and the winners weren’t Sony or Samsung.

Industry observers note that the Hisense U6 Pro is a solid midrange option that uses a Mini LED panel to create millions of bold, rich colors as well as sharp contrast. It also features an anti-glare display for better visibility, a 144Hz refresh rate, and support for both Dolby Vision HDR and Dolby Atmos virtual surround sound.

Industry observers note that the Hisense U7 splits the difference between premium and midrange, offering crisp 4K resolution, bold colors, and rich sound. It supports both Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos for a more immersive experience, while the 165Hz refresh rate provides ultra-smooth motion. You can also share media from your iOS and Android devices via both AirPlay and Google Cast.

It has been revealed that samsung’s OLED TV options have continuously topped our lists as some of the best TVs you can buy almost since the minute they hit the market. And the S95F is no exception, even though it’s last year’s flagship. It may be a generation behind, but it still gives you plenty of reasons to pick it up, like signature OLED picture quality, object-tracking sound for a more immersive experience without extra speakers, and adaptive sound for the best listening experience in almost any environment.

Review: I tested the Samsung S95F OLED for a week, and I’m ready to give up my living room TV.

According to recent reports, the LG C5 is the flagship OLED of 2025, and it’s a very smart choice for anyone who wants cutting-edge picture and audio quality without having to pay the usual premium to get it. You’ll still get great color accuracy, razor-sharp contrast, and ultra-fine details as well as a host of smart features like preloaded streaming apps, voice controls, and screen sharing.

Review: Why last year’s LG C5 OLED is the smartest TV buy right now.

Sony’s entry-level LED TV offers support for both Dolby Vision HDR and Dolby Atmos, a 120Hz refresh rate, 4K resolution, and a suite of preloaded apps like Disney+ and Netflix so you can get started watching your favorite reveals right out of the box. Bluetooth connectivity lets you set up wireless soundbars and subwoofers to create a fully wireless home theater, while AirPlay and Google Cast let you share media from your iOS and Android devices.

Both myself and former ZDNET editor Chris Bayer got to test out the TCL QM8, and we were impressed by what the company had to offer with its flagship TV. With a peak brightness of 5,000 nits, you’ll get excellent visibility in almost any lighting environment — even direct sunlight — while the 5,000 local dimming zones give you OLED-level contrast for sharper image quality.

The QM8 has a base refresh rate of 120Hz that can be boosted to 144Hz for console gaming, while AMD FreeSync Premium Pro VRR support prevents stuttering and freezing while gaming. The TCL QM8 is available in sizes up to 115 inches, but ZDNET readers most often opt for the 65-, 85-, and 98-inch models for both typical living rooms and dedicated home theaters.
