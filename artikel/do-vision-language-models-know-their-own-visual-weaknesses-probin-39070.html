Do Vision-Language Models Know Their Own Visual Weaknesses? Probing Capability-Level Metacognition

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (250 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.32940v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Metacognition includes knowing one's own strengths and weaknesses across kinds of tasks, not only judging whether a single answer is correct. We test whether vision-language models (VLMs) know in advance which kinds of visual tasks and image conditions are hard for them, across a wide range of open and proprietary models. Because some tasks are hard for every model, we separate what a model knows about this shared difficulty from what it knows about itself. We find that VLMs know little in advance about their visual weaknesses. They misjudge their overall accuracy and do not even know which kinds of tasks are hard for every model, and most misjudge how degraded images affect them. We further train VLMs to predict their own accuracy, which teaches them their own level of accuracy on kinds unseen in training but not which kinds are hard for them. However, information about these weaknesses appears once a model starts solving. On a few unlabeled questions of a kind, the probability a model assigns to its own answers follows weaknesses particular to the model, across kinds of tasks and image conditions. VLMs thus hold information about their own weaknesses that their stated predictions do not yet reflect. By separating what is particular to a model from what holds for every model, our evaluation offers a test for methods that aim to teach VLMs their own weaknesses, and the answer probability offers them a signal to start from.
