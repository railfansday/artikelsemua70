Tech Report: LSEG: AI and minerals pull Asia Pacific M&A away from global slide

🌐 Source: Fintech • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (551 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that while global M&A lost momentum in the third quarter of 2026, Asia Pacific told a very different story. According to new analysis from LSEG Data & Analytics, deal value involving the region stayed broadly flat at $271.1bn in Q3, a 25% rise on the same period last year. Deals targeting Asia Pacific companies performed even better, climbing 8% quarter-on-quarter and 35% year-on-year.

The headline figures mask an important shift, however. LSEG Data & Analytics notes that deal volumes are falling, suggesting the region’s resilience reflects more disciplined, selective dealmaking rather than broad-based growth.

China remained the dominant force. LSEG data demonstrates announced deal value in the country rose 33.3% from Q2, taking its year-to-date total to $249.8bn, or 40.8% of Asia Pacific-targeted activity. Large recapitalisations at major financial institutions bolstered the numbers, technology became China’s biggest target sector, and private equity-backed deals reached a five-year high of $59.6bn, more than twice last year’s level.

Recent findings suggest that australia staged a dramatic recovery. Target M&A reached $64.7bn in Q3, up 116.7% on the previous quarter, lifting the year-to-date figure to $108.6bn, a 129.8% annual increase. Appetite for mining, resources and infrastructure assets, fuelled by interest in critical minerals and the energy transition, drove the surge.

In a recent development, india’s activity was shaped by domestic consolidation, restructuring and overseas ambitions. Target M&A slipped 2.8% to $88.4bn year-to-date, though deals with any Indian involvement edged up 1.3% to $110.3bn. Outbound M&A jumped 24.1% to $21bn as Indian corporates continued to expand abroad.

In a recent development, singapore cemented its role as a regional investment hub. LSEG Data & Analytics reports any-involvement activity nearly doubled, rising 98.2% to $104.3bn, while target M&A grew 88.5% to $39.2bn, its strongest first nine months in five years. Outbound deals hit a record $46.3bn, and high technology made up almost 46% of Singapore-related activity.

According to recent reports, strength was spread across deal sizes. In Asia Pacific excluding Japan, transactions under $1bn rose 16.4% by value and 16.3% by count year-on-year. Mega deals of $5bn or more grew 28% over the first nine months, with Q3 activity in that bracket up 45.5% on Q2.

In a recent development, technology was the leading target sector, reaching $164.1bn, up 97.2% year-on-year and representing 21.7% of regional activity, the second-highest total since LSEG records began in 1980. Investment in AI, data centres and digital infrastructure powered much of this, with transactions involving Anthropic, DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, STT GDC and DayOne Data Centers. Yet materials, financials, industrials and energy & power together accounted for over half of all activity.

Private equity also shone. Sponsor-backed M&A reached $131.4bn over nine months, up 101.4% from $65.2bn, with deal count up 62.2%. Q3 value eased 2.9% to $46.6bn and deal count fell 23%, though this followed a Q2 that was the strongest quarter since Q4 2021. Valuations, financing conditions and geopolitics continue to temper deployment.

It has been revealed that looking ahead, LSEG Data & Analytics concludes that investors are becoming more disciplined but not less active. For dealmakers, success will depend on understanding where each market’s local drivers meet the region’s shared pull towards AI, digital infrastructure and strategic resources.
