Breaking: ReliaGuard: Reconstruction-Aware Watermarking for Source Attribution and Blind Edit Localization

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (251 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.16368v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Generative image editing can reconstruct the background outside the requested edit, weakening spatial watermark evidence even when the source identifier remains recoverable. This creates an ambiguity for blind localization: a weak watermark response may indicate either an intended edit or background reconstruction. We propose ReliaGuard, a reconstruction-aware watermarking framework that addresses this ambiguity through reconstruction-stable evidence writing and coarse-to-fine blind reading. The writer aligns keyed feature responses in the protected image and its variational autoencoder reconstruction while preserving a frozen source-attribution channel. A quantization-aware projection bounds the distortion of the released eight-bit image. The reader combines keyed responses with image context to refine a base localization map, then integrates native-resolution detail with multiscale features to estimate the edit region. It is trained on paired observations before and after image processing and requires only the received image and key at inference. On 300 source images and 900 editing cases from three domains, ReliaGuard achieves intersection-over-union scores of 85.61% for complete editing outputs and 94.90% for locally spliced outputs at thresholds calibrated to a nominal 1% background false-positive rate on complete outputs. The former exceeds the strongest baseline by 45.02 percentage points, while decoded identifier-bit accuracy reaches 100% in both conditions. Across seven image-processing families, the corresponding localization scores remain 81.01% and 86.49%, with identifier-bit accuracies of 92.76% and 92.79%. These results show that reconstruction-aware writing and contextual reading can jointly support source attribution and edit localization under generative editing and subsequent image processing.
