AI raises the stakes as insurers struggle to evidence skills

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Zing365 has found that nearly every senior insurance leader believes their firm satisfies its CPD and regulatory training obligations, yet 41% say proving that learning has taken place is their biggest compliance challenge.

The study arrives as insurers respond to the FCA’s policy statement PS25/21. From 9 December 2025, this scrapped the mandatory 15-hour annual CPD threshold for insurance staff who are not CII qualified and do not work in investment roles. The responsibility for maintaining suitable knowledge and competence still sits with firms, but logged hours can no longer serve as proof of it.

The hours requirement continues to apply to long-term insurance staff, and professional bodies may impose their own rules.

The data comes from The UK Insurance Skills & Competency Index 2026, which underpins Zing365’s white paper, From compliance to competence – How insurance builds measures: and maintains capability in the age of AI.

Censuswide carried out the independent survey of 151 senior managers and insurance brokers with responsibility for training, competence or corporate strategy.

It has been revealed that confidence levels were striking. Some 99% of respondents believe their organisation reliably meets mandatory CPD and regulatory learning requirements, while 84% describe their learning and development programmes as very or extremely effective.

Recent findings suggest that the 41% figure combines four separate answers on the single greatest compliance-learning challenge: measuring effectiveness (14%), tracking progress or completion (11%), recording and reporting CPD (9%) and demonstrating competence (7%). The report notes these are practical difficulties and do not show that firms are unable to prove compliance.

Recent findings suggest that respondents rated coaching and mentoring (51%) and on-the-job learning (48%) as the most impactful training methods. Zing365 believes firms should record how this kind of learning is put into practice, alongside formal course records, to gain a more complete view of capability.

Industry observers note that artificial intelligence features heavily in the findings. A total of 82% say AI is reshaping their business or is being actively deployed, and 40% rank AI and digital skills among the hardest capabilities to build. Almost a third (32%) expect AI skills to demand the largest workforce investment over the next two years, close to triple the next category, cyber security awareness, at 11%.

Zing365 argues that training should test whether staff can spot gaps, challenge unsupported conclusions and justify decisions reached with AI support. The survey did not examine how often employees defer to AI. The FCA regulates AI through existing frameworks such as the Senior Managers and Certification Regime, which places weight on accountability and oversight as latest tools are adopted.

Industry observers note that zing365 managing director James Read said, “Confidence is not the same as evidence. Many firms will have strong learning programmes, but the question is whether they can show what their people are able to do, where they need support and what changed as a result of their training.

“Greater flexibility over CPD makes decisions about what training to deliver, and how to assess it, more crucial. Counting hours was never enough to establish competence. Firms need a clear basis for judging whether learning is helping people carry out their responsibilities.”.

Read added, “An AI-generated summary can look convincing and still omit an vital policy condition that changes the decision. If someone accepts it without checking, completing an AI course tells us very little about whether they were prepared for that responsibility.

“For compliance and training leaders, the notable question is what happens when an employee encounters an answer that looks right but is wrong. Can they recognise the problem, explain their reasoning and know when to seek help? That is the capability learning needs to build and assessment needs to test.”.
