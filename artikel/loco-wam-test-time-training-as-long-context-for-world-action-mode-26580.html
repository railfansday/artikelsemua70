LoCo-WAM: Test-Time Training as Long Context for World-Action Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (193 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.26853v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: World-action models guide robot actions by predicting what happens next, but typically condition on a short observation history. Extending this history increases computation at every decision and can require a separately trained model. We introduce LoCo-WAM, which learns from the outcomes of its actions through test-time training while keeping the same short history length. It uses newly observed frames to update a low-rank adapter in the video expert, whose adapted predictions guide subsequent actions. The updates accumulate within each episode and are reset between episodes, allowing past observations to influence decisions after they leave the input window. LoCo-WAM thus learns from a longer stream of experience without expert action labels, additional data or meta-training. Experiments on two world-action models show improved task success across four simulation benchmarks. On RoboCasa GR-1, a model with 2.4,s of history matches one trained with 9.6,s, while a model with 4.8,s exceeds it by 4.8 percentage points. Our analysis highlights that the updates improve prediction of upcoming frames, affect the action mainly through adaptation of the imagined future, and have an increasing effect on actions as the episode unfolds.
