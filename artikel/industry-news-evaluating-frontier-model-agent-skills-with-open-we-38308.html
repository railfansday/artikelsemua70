Industry News: Evaluating Frontier-Model Agent Skills with Open-Weight Models in a Science Gateway

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (265 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.31480v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Scientific analyses that span many jobs and computing sites depend on workflow management systems to run reliably, but writing a production workflow requires expertise in such a system, which many domain scientists do not have. Coding agents can supply this expertise when they are guided by skills and packaged procedures for recurring tasks. We wrote skills and specialist agents for the Pegasus workflow management system with a frontier model, and the PegasusAI Studio science gateway offers them to scientists through a chat interface. New users without frontier-model credits need an open-weight model, served at no cost on research cyberinfrastructure, but skills written for a frontier model may not work with an open-weight solution. This paper presents a method for adapting such skills. An evaluation harness drives the real gateway as a user would, scores the workflows the agent builds, and feeds recurring failures back to the frontier model that maintains the skills. We evaluated 21 open-weight models on 17 tasks. The five highest-scoring models are close in quality after one trial per task, and their median response times differ twelvefold. In repeated trials with the best of the three open-weight models, we repeated scores of 0.76 on workflow building against 0.82 for Claude. The harness led to five changes to the initial skills. We also identify challenges in evaluating agents within operational systems: evaluation failures can be mistaken for model failures, and agents can circumvent experimental controls within their privilege domain. In a controlled ablation, an initially significant effect disappeared when the restrictions were properly enforced.
