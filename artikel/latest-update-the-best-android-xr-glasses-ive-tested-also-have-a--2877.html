Latest Update: The best Android XR glasses I’ve tested also have a genius ‘hybrid mode’

I’ve finally found smart glasses with the Android XR experience I’ve been looking for. It’s taken some trial and error — I first bought the Samsung Galaxy XR headset, but sold it after a few months because the badly positioned rear strap knob prevented me from sitting back on a plane. For the past week, I’ve been wearing the new Xreal Aura, bringing the full Android XR experience to the glasses form factor.

Also: The best AR and MR glasses in 2026: Expert tested and reviewed.

According to recent reports, xreal has been perfecting its XR glasses for years by extending your connected device to the massive screen. The Aura brings the full Android XR ecosystem to customers via the connected Puck for a starting price of $1,099, while also functioning as standard XR glasses. For context, this is some serious value, especially since Samsung’s Galaxy XR starts at $1,799.

When I first opened the Xreal Aura, I thought it was another pair of the Xreal 1 Pro Plus. The hardware is nearly identical, except for two small cameras in the top corners of the front of the glasses to capture gestures. There is also a center-mounted camera similar to the Eye found on the Xreal Pro series, for capturing content and 6DoF spatial anchoring. Despite the additional cameras, the glasses still weigh only 95 grams — far less than the Samsung Galaxy XR’s 545 grams.

Industry observers note that speaking of gestures, I was pleasantly surprised by how responsive they were. With the Samsung Galaxy XR, I became frustrated quickly with gesture navigation and ended up using a connected Bluetooth mouse. Here, they’re much more usable.

There are physical buttons for control as well. The right arm has three: a small button at the top, one at the bottom, and a longer button behind it, primarily used for volume control. With the Puck connected, the buttons provide basic controls for volume, transparency level, and recentering the display. Audio comes through the slot speakers near the arms of the glasses and sounds great with minimal leakage thanks to the glasses’ partnership with Bose.

The guts of Android XR are contained in the puck that connects to the glasses. It’s about the size of a standard smartphone, but without a display. The top serves as a touchpad for navigation and menu selections in Android XR, if you prefer that method over gestures. The power button doubles as a fingerprint sensor to secure the Puck and unlock it with a simple tap. The longer volume button is just below the power button.

Sources indicate that there are two USB-C ports on the bottom: the left is for charging (mostly), and the right is to connect your Xreal glasses. You can also charge the Puck while using it, so if you are on a long flight, you can connect it to the glasses. The Xreal Aura Puck has a decent advertised battery life of 4-5 hours, compared to Apple Vision Pro and Samsung’s Galaxy XR at up to 2.5 hours.

In a recent development, the Xreal Aura is the first Android XR device to launch with a sleek glasses form factor — exactly what I wanted to see to improve the VR/AR experience for the end user. By contrast, the heavy Apple Vision Pro and Galaxy XR remain fairly niche due to their high prices and very large size.

Android XR brings the full Android experience to the glasses, with up to five displays you can move around in the virtual space, immersive experiences that can take you to other worlds or deep below the ocean surface, and optimal productivity options powered by thousands of Android apps.

With the Puck’s lightweight form factor and belt clip, you can walk around and quickly switch to Follow mode to see the world around you through the glasses, with your Android XR content positioned in a small window off to the side. Just tap the Follow mode button again to return to the full Android XR environment.

It has been revealed that one rather unique option I was unaware of until I read through the manual was the option to connect an external source to the USB-C charge port for a hybrid experience. Xreal calls this mode Wired Mirroring, and includes a shortcut to enable it in the Android XR launcher.

Plugging into a laptop brings your PC or Mac’s display to the glasses, giving the option to mirror or extend from your connected computer and then take full advantage of increasing the size of the display, moving in closer or further away from you, and viewing up to four other Android apps within the Android XR user interface.

I enjoy using this mode when I have something up on my computer to work on, with another piece of content streaming or another app open to gather data from in Android XR. I admit, it’ll take some time for me to get used to this unique mode, but it seems it can increase productivity, especially with the ability to peek below the lenses and see the other display on my computer.

Previously, devices like a Nintendo Switch 2 couldn’t connect to a pair of smart glasses because they’re designed to output video only via a dock. The Xreal Puck functions similarly to the Xreal AR adapter, so it “fools” the Switch 2 into thinking it is docked, then outputs the game video to the Xreal Aura glasses. You don’t need the Xreal AR adapter, as the Puck now serves this role and has its own power, eliminating the need for a USB-C power source.

Since the Xreal Aura glasses looked so much like the Xreal 1 Pro Plus I recently tested, I decided to plug my Apple iPhone Duo into the Xreal Aura, and voila! They performed just like the 1 Pro Plus, including the full on-screen display menu options. I later read in the manual that this is called external display mode.

While you should not purchase the Xreal Aura solely to serve as standard XR glasses, it is fantastic to have this option, since there are times when you may want to use the glasses without the Android XR platform. For example, when I travel for work with just a Surface Pro, I want a substantial ultra-wide screen experience directly from the Surface Pro to the glasses. This lets me travel with just the Xreal Aura and promptly switch to the Android XR experience for other tasks.

Recent findings suggest that apple TV and Netflix are right now unavailable on the Xreal Aura in Android XR, but you can watch them by plugging in your iPhone or Android directly into the glasses without using the Puck. This is why Xreal provides the ultimate flexibility with the Aura at a reasonable price point.

Xreal also sent along the Xreal Game Controller, co-branded with GameSir, as well as the Xreal Keyboard. The Game Controller has a charging stand that plugs into USB-C to charge the controller for hours of gameplay. Since I primarily game with a Nintendo Switch 2, I took off the removable magnet faceplate and swapped out the left directional pad for one that mimics the Switch 2.

After connecting via Bluetooth, I enjoyed an optimal gaming experience on my Switch 2 and the Xreal Aura. Even better, I activated Google Gemini directly from the controller to ask for help when stuck in game areas. As a casual gamer, this dual functionality motivated me to play more games.

Extensive text input is painful to do with just gestures, but voice-to-text is very accurate and fast. However, if you enjoy typing on a keyboard, you either need to connect a computer and use that keyboard while looking under the glasses, or connect a Bluetooth keyboard for mobile computing. The Xreal Keyboard has great key sizing and spacing, along with solid tactile feedback. It would be nice to have a trackpad attached, but finger gestures are fine too if a Bluetooth mouse is not connected to the Aura.

The Xreal Game Controller is available for $99 and the Xreal keyboard is $79, but there are frequently bundle offers that offer discounts.

Xreal provides exactly what I was hoping to see with Android XR with the new Xreal Aura glasses. They have a sleek form factor and offer the full package with a very nice carrying case. The 70-degree FOV is not as expansive as that of full-on heavy headsets, but it still provides an immersive experience that is sure to satisfy most people.

It has been revealed that the additional modes make the Aura an even better value, and it’s clear that this is the Android XR solution you want for maximum flexibility and productivity. I look forward to commuting, traveling, and working in a big-screen environment with this pair of glasses.
