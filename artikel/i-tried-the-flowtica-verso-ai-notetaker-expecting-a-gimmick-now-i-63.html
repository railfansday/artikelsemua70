Industry observers note that AI is often touted as a tool for increasing efficiency, and businesses have grasped its potential benefits with abandon – perhaps, in some cases, far too quickly. But when done right, it can improve productivity.

One company focused on products that automate time-consuming, tedious tasks is Flowtica — makers of the Scribe pen we saw at IFA earlier this year. It was an impressive device, but its next product, the Flowtica Verso, promises more.

Also: Run this 10-step security check on your phone once a year – here’s why.

According to recent reports, the Flowtica Verso is a compact “AI productivity companion” with an e-paper display and a connector that attaches to the back of your smartphone. Instead of managing multiple apps to record meetings, transcribe, or handle your to-do list and calendar, Verso is designed so you don’t need to use your phone at all — everything you need is on the device’s screen, and always available.

Recent findings suggest that your kit comes with the Flowtica Verso itself, a USB-C cable, and a circular, magnetic clip you can use to attach the Verso to the back of your smartphone or any other magnetic surface. The device itself is thin, light, and comfortable to hold. There are just three buttons: one on the left to power it; an orange button for recording, and a secondary right button, which is the “Ask Flowtica” or AI button.

There’s a QR code on the display that lilnks to the app download page, available on Android and iOS. After starting it up for the first time, Verso asks what it should call you, to best describe your role and industry, and what you expect to use your AI companion for.

You then pair your device to your smartphone via Bluetooth, and set up Wi-Fi, which you don’t need to record meetings, but you will need for transcription, since it calls the cloud to process.

The Always-on display is comfortable, and as it is designed without a backlight, its e-paper screen is always available to you in the background, a useful addition for work when you just want to glance at upcoming meetings or calendar events, all of which are displayed on the Verso screensaver.

In a recent development, however, in dim lighting conditions, it’s not as easy to read and could be particularly difficult for anyone with vision impairment. I also found that response times varied when I tapped the screen or swiped across to read content on my Verso, ranging from immediate to taking a few seconds to register.

For my test, I chose to record a conversation from The ZDNET Show on the best products of 2025.

In a recent development, I recorded the discussion between ZDNET experts from approximately five feet away, and I left my TV on to provide some of the background noise you might expect in a busy meeting room. After pressing the record button, I let the Verso get to work.

Sources indicate that while recording, you can simply leave the device on a table and let it record and transcribe for you without supervision. You can also choose to read a live summary of a meeting as it’s generated, or choose to read a transcription, which could be useful if the user is hard of hearing. There’s a small, red light at the bottom of the Verso that flashes when it is recording.

Once you want to finish recording, press the recording button again. Each recording is stored under the Meeting tab, accessible from the main screen. Dates, times, and the length of each meeting is displayed. Tap into it, and you can find meeting notes created from the recording, including a meeting type, executive summary, and an AI-generated summary spanning multiple pages.

While transcriptions are not 100% accurate – I would suggest a ballpark of around 80 – 85% – many of the mistakes are small, grammatical errors or miswordings, such as “zPed” instead of ZDNET, with human oversight, they still take a lot of effort out of transcribing.

Recent findings suggest that there’s plenty of options for exporting your meeting content, too. While you can do this directly from the Verso, I like how every meeting and recording is laid out in the Flowtica app, complete with a “To-do” function for adding follow-up tasks, exporting your notes, sharing, or changing your meeting language. (As the latter is based on AI, I can’t comment on how accurate the translations are.).

Sources indicate that the significant draw with all this data is being able to ask the AI questions about your meetings. You can have a chat with your AI assistant and ask it to perform tasks for you, including setting reminders, creating follow-up tasks, and summarizing meetings. It works rather well and doesn’t take too long to answer, but I think this is a secondary benefit that augments the calendar and transcription options, rather than the other way around.

I asked for a bullet-point summary of my most recent recording, and despite the Verso capturing some strange dialogue from background TV noise, it still understood what I requested and provided a solid answer.

According to recent reports, another notable feature is the ability to set up contact cards. The Verso has NFC connectivity, and business people can set up a business card they can pass to another with a tap. You set up your contact card in the accompanying app and update Verso to store it.

Recent findings suggest that yes. I think it has the best use cases in business, meeting-heavy roles, and for anyone tasked with transcribing conversations, where it could potentially save you many hours. This also expands to journalism, and I intend to use this gadget when I’m next in the field, attending a conference or conducting an interview.

It does take some time to set up and learn, but for those willing to remember to use it for a few days, I think it would become a valuable tool, rather than a device left to gather dust on the shelf. The Ask Flowtica AI feature is a nice touch, but it’s actually the meetings > transcription > AI-assisted summaries functions that can boost productivity. It doesn’t hurt that the gadget is small enough to keep with your tablet or smartphone, either.

Industry observers note that I’m generally wary of AI-first devices that promise productivity, as in many cases they’re just buzzwords and a cool gadget wrapped in an AI bow. However, when it comes to the Flowtica Verso, I don’t mind admitting that this kind of tool will actually help me in my day-to-day workflow and will be invaluable in helping me with transcription — a task I don’t want to spend much time on.
