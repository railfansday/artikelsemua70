Industry News: Risk-Calibrated Intent Routing for Conversational AI via Signed Lexical Evidence

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (244 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2610.00262v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Conversational AI has become widely used as advances in language models let people find information, get writing help, and ask for practical guidance in their own words. When an assistant handles specific tasks, it must infer what the user wants and decide whether to act on that prediction. A sentence classifier's confidence can miss clues in the wording. We combine the gap between the classifier's two highest scores with signed lexical evidence, a score based on words and short character patterns. The score is positive when those patterns favor the predicted intent and negative when they favor another. We use separate calibration data and a statistical upper bound on the error rate to set the minimum confidence required to accept a request. Across ten runs per dataset, our method reduces the area under the risk-coverage curve, which summarizes error as more requests are accepted, by 15.8%, 15.1%, and 11.8% on BANKING77, CLINC150, and HWU64, respectively, compared with a confidence model that uses only sentence classifier scores. At a 5% error target, the share of accepted requests rises by 1.83 percentage points on BANKING77 and 5.14 points on HWU64. CLINC150 already has near-full coverage at that target. At a 2% target, calibration finds a rule that accepts some requests in all 30 runs. Our contribution is a two-feature confidence model that improves routing by measuring how strongly the wording supports or challenges the original intent prediction.
