Weakly Conditioned Diffusion Model Augmentation for Prostate Segmentation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (235 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.34230v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Deep learning models for prostate MRI segmentation often struggle to generalize when trained on limited heterogeneous data, leading to boundary errors and over-segmentation. We present an augmentation framework using mask-conditioned synthetic and diverse T2-weighted MRI volumes to improve boundary precision and reduce over-segmentation. We employ a 2.5D weakly mask-conditioned denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) via channel concatenation. The diffusion model conditions on stacks of adjacent binary masks from training folds. The model generates T2 volumes consistent with the conditioning anatomy while diversifying tissue appearance. We evaluate the proposed approach on two cohorts of data: (i) the LUND probe cohort (n=432) for strategy selection and training nnU-Net from scratch with a 23% synthetic data mix, and (ii) the Medical Segmentation Decathlon (MSD) prostate cohort (n=32) evaluated with a 20% synthetic data mix. For the LUND cohort, we found that training an nnU-Net model from scratch with a 23% synthetic data mix reduced the mean false-positive voxels per case from 11,102 (baseline) to 10,757. On the MSD cohort, the augmented model with a 20% synthetic mix achieved a mean cross-validated Dice of 0.9094, improving over the baseline (0.8935) by 1.59 percentage points and reducing mean false-positive voxels by 23.1% (4,201 to 3,229). Experimental results on both these cohorts suggest that mask-conditioned DDPM augmentation can reduce over-segmentation errors and improve boundary adherence in data-constrained settings.
