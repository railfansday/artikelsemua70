Industry News: RoboARC: Physical Puzzles for Robotic Manipulation under Spatial Constraints

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (313 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.33390v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Many hard manipulation tasks come down to a spatial constraint that only a few motions satisfy, such as passing a part through a gap barely wider than itself or separating two interlocked parts. These motions depend on the exact geometry of each scene, so demonstrations and skills recorded elsewhere rarely transfer. We therefore ask: when manipulation hinges on spatial constraints, can robots reason out the right motions? We introduce RoboARC, a benchmark of 90 tasks in six constraint types over rigid and soft materials, on three robots in MuJoCo. Every task is a frozen scene with a physics-based grader and a verified reference solution that replays bit-identically. Each task comes in a Privileged regime, with the full simulator state and resets, and a Standard regime with real-sensor inputs (camera images and proprioception) and a single attempt without resets. We evaluate seven coding agents and four VLA policies, none trained on these tasks. The strongest agent, GPT-6 Astra in Codex, solves 86% of the tasks in the Privileged regime and 43% in the Standard regime, and the four VLA policies, run zero-shot, solve none of their 18 one-arm puzzles. On 15 puzzles that GPT-6 Astra solved in the Privileged regime and failed in the Standard regime, object poses add 4 points and part geometry 13, but both together add 36, from 24% to 60%. Only 7% of the coding agents' failed runs pass more than half of a task, and 53% of the failed runs in the Standard regime are stuck on a single step. Frontier coding agents already reason out the right motions for many of these tasks once they know where the parts are and what shape they have. From their own sensors, in a single attempt, the strongest solves fewer than half of the tasks. The website can be found at https://roboarc.embodied-first-exam.ai.
