Tech Report: Self-similar Organization of Information in Language

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (223 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.26935v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: How much information relevant to predicting the future of a passage (i.e. predictive information) is carried by the preceding text, and how is this information distributed along that history? These questions are fundamental to understanding both the statistical structure of language and what large language models (LLMs) can learn about it. Indeed, LLMs trained to predict the next token in text must exploit this predictive information, so its organization determines how the structure of language becomes available to the model to learn. We develop a theory for the organization of predictive information based on the scaling behavior of a family of information measures, and the theory predicts that if this organization has the same form at all measured length scales -- i.e., it has a self-similar structure -- then the information measures evaluated at different lengths collapse onto a common form. We observe this scaling collapse in long text documents, showing that predictive information is present at every length scale we measure and has a self-similar structure. Self-similarity is a considerably stronger property than showing the existence of long-range dependencies, and it constrains the process generating the text. More broadly, our results give quantitative support to the empirical observation that conditioning on more distant text continues to reveal new structure in language to LLMs.
