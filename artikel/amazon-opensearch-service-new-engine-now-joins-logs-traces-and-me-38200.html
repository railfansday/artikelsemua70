Amazon OpenSearch Service new engine now joins logs, traces, and metadata

🌐 Source: Amazon • 📅 24 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (266 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Amazon OpenSearch Service now supports distributed joins, high-cardinality aggregations, and OpenSearch query DSL on the new engine.
  • With Piped Processing Language (PPL) or SQL, engineers and agents can query multiple petabytes of data to connect an error log to its trace, and enrich telemetry with service ownership or deployment metadata, in one query.
  • In addition to PPL and SQL, the fresh engine now supports OpenSearch query DSL.

Amazon OpenSearch Service now supports distributed joins, high-cardinality aggregations, and OpenSearch query DSL on the new engine. Observability data often spans logs, traces, service metadata, deployment records, and tenant data. Engineers and their AI agents must connect these datasets to determine what failed, where it failed, and who was affected.

With Piped Processing Language (PPL) or SQL, engineers and agents can query multiple petabytes of data to connect an error log to its trace, and enrich telemetry with service ownership or deployment metadata, in one query. High-cardinality aggregations measure incident impact across millions of distinct hosts, users, or tenants. The new engine spreads aggregations and joins across data nodes, so you don't need to copy related data into a single index first. With routing key support, you can keep related documents on the same shard, so lookups and joins on that key stay fast.

In addition to PPL and SQL, the fresh engine now supports OpenSearch query DSL. You get the engine's cost savings and performance improvements for supported analytics workloads without rewriting queries in PPL or SQL. Applications, alerts, and integrations that send query DSL keep working.

In a recent development, these features are available on OpenSearch 3.9, at no additional charge, in the following AWS Regions: US East (N. Virginia), US East (Ohio), US West (Oregon), Canada (Central), Asia Pacific (Mumbai), Asia Pacific (Singapore), Asia Pacific (Sydney), Asia Pacific (Tokyo), Europe (Frankfurt), Europe (Ireland), Europe (London), and Europe (Spain). To get started, create or upgrade a domain to OpenSearch 3.9 with the latest engine. To learn more, see the documentation.
