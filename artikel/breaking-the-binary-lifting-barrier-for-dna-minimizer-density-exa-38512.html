Breaking the Binary-Lifting Barrier for DNA Minimizer Density: Exact Optimization of a Native 4-ary Order at (w, k) = (12, 5)

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (255 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.32159v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: Minimizer schemes sample at least one $k$-mer from every window of $w$ consecutive $k$-mers via a total order $\pi$ on $\Sigma^k$. For DNA ($\sigma = 4$), state-of-the-art lookup schemes such as GreedyMini and OptMini optimize a binary order ($2^k$ ranks) and lift it to $\Sigma^k$ by ranking one bit-plane primarily and breaking $2^k$-way ties lexicographically on the other bit-plane. At $(w, k) = (12, 5)$, this projection locks sets of $32$ DNA $5$-mers into contiguous blocks and restricts the active universal hitting set (UHS) prefix to $448$ of $1024$ $5$-mers, giving exact expected DNA density $2246214544 / 2^{34} \approx 0.1307468945$ for OptMini (k5-w12-13.txt) and $2248978620 / 2^{34} \approx 0.1309077849$ for GreedyMini (w12_k5.gm). We bypass binary lifting and optimize directly over native $4$-ary permutations ($|\Sigma^k| = 1024$) from scratch via: (i) an exact de Bruijn endpoint transfer-matrix dynamic program over $B(4, 4)$ ($256$ states), (ii) a weighted set-cover ratio initializer (SetCover4) producing a compact $U = 314$ UHS prefix, (iii) bitmask-sparse simulated annealing preserving the zero-cost $w$-walk-free tail invariant, and (iv) two-pass $O(N)$ exact best-insertion sweeps with large-neighborhood ruin-and-recreate search. The resulting explicit $4$-ary order achieves exact expected DNA density $2234289615 / 2^{34} \approx 0.130052772292$ (density factor $1.690686$), cutting charged windows by $11924929$ contexts ($-0.530890\%$ relative) below OrensteinLab/Minimizers and $14689005$ ($-0.653141\%$) below w12_k5.gm at $8200$-byte lookup storage ($1280$ bytes bit-packed). Warm-start relaxation stalls at $U = 379$ ($0.1301728789$), whereas native $4$-ary initialization breaks $32/32$ high-bit blocks across all $2+2$ nucleotide partitions.
