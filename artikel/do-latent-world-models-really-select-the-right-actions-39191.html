Do Latent World Models Really Select the Right Actions?

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (193 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2609.05461v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: This paper highlights that the planning success of a latent world model does not reveal how well its score selects actions. Latent world models score candidate action sequences by the distance between their predicted embeddings and the embedding of a goal image, and their strong planning results are naturally read as evidence that this score picks good actions. Our study is simple: we take two released models, JEPA-WM and DINO-WM, execute the action sequences their planners consider, and compare what the score prefers with what actually happens. We make two observations. First, the score is informative but unreliable: it picks better actions than chance, yet it often passes over a successful sequence that the search has already found. Second, planning still succeeds, helped by a wider search and by replanning from fresh observations. With the released search budget, PointMaze planning succeeds in 91% of episodes, yet the top-scored candidate at the end of a search is no more likely to succeed than a random one. These observations suggest that the score should be measured directly, next to the success rate.
