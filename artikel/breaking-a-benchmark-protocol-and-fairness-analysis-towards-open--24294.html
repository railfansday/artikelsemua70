Breaking: A Benchmark Protocol and Fairness Analysis Towards Open-Set EEG Seizure Type Classification

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (258 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.27783v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Automated EEG seizure type classification systems are typically trained and evaluated under the closed-set assumption, where all seizure types encountered at deployment are seen during training. In clinical practice, this assumption fails: patients present with rare or atypical seizure types that fall outside the training label space, and a system that forces a wrong label is worse than one that abstains. We introduce the first open-set recognition protocol for EEG seizure type classification on the Temple University Hospital Seizure Corpus (TUSZ v2.0.3), defining known and unknown classes using the clinical taxonomy itself: seizure types with sufficient subject representation (at least 12 subjects) form the known set, and rare types form the unknown set. We evaluate seven post-hoc open-set scorers (MSP, MaxLogit, energy, Mahalanobis, kNN, GHOST, and COSTARR) applied to a partially fine-tuned EEG Foundation Model (EFM), using a subject-disjoint leave-one-class-out (LOCO) protocol over three seeds that tests whether methods can detect seizure types excluded from fine-tuning. On true unknown classes, the best scorer (Mahalanobis) reaches a mean AUROC of 0.66, and differences among the top scorers are within seed-to-seed variation. Held-out known seizure types from unseen subjects are detected only slightly above chance (AUROC 0.54-0.58 for all scorers). Per-subject rejection rates are highly uneven for all scorers, and GHOST does not significantly improve per-subject fairness over energy scoring (two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank, p >= 0.25). We release the protocol, subject splits, and code as an initial baseline; our results indicate that open-set seizure type recognition remains a hard, open problem.
