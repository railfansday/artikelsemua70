📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, with the EU’s Anti-Money Laundering Regulation (AMLR 2027 / Regulation EU 2024/1624) set to replace a patchwork of national directives with one directly applicable rulebook, regulated firms face a narrowing window to get their compliance operations in order. RegTech firm KYCP believes it has already done much of the heavy lifting for its clients.

In a recent development, according to KYCP, a comprehensive capability assessment of its platform against the incoming regulation found that its Client Lifecycle Management (CLM) engine natively meets the regulatory benchmarks across all key operational areas. For the tech giant, this is less a one-off milestone than a reflection of its broader product strategy, which treats regulatory alignment as an ongoing pursuit built on adaptability, architectural flexibility and continuous refinement.

It has been revealed that the stakes are significant. The EU AML Package introduces strict operational requirements intended to eliminate regulatory arbitrage and standardise compliance across every Member State. KYCP claims its platform addresses these mandates out of the box across five core pillars.

Sources indicate that on customer due diligence and identity data (Articles 19–22 and 28), KYCP supports risk-based CDD triggers, configurable fields, mandatory data capture and verification workflows for individuals, corporates, beneficial owners and representatives. Where CDD cannot be completed, the platform automatically applies hold, block or termination statuses and records every decision in an audit log.

According to recent reports, beneficial ownership is another pressure point. KYCP’s platform maps complex, multi-tiered corporate structures and non-standard ownership models such as trusts and foundations (Articles 20 and 51–62). Configurable scripts calculate combined ownership across layered chains and flag cases where sanctioned ownership breaches thresholds, including the 50% rule under Articles 20 and 26.

For risk scoring, review cycles and sanctions, KYCP’s Scores Module and Rules Engine generate continuous risk scores based on custom parameters. This automates review cycles, enforcing a maximum one-year refresh for higher-risk clients and up to five years for standard risks.

It has been revealed that enhanced due diligence is also automated. KYCP triggers EDD for politically exposed persons, their family members and close associates, as well as high-net-worth personalised service thresholds, escalating high-risk relationships for senior management approval and four-eyes review (Articles 34–45).

Finally, KYCP highlights governance and auditability (Articles 9–10, 21, 69 and 76–77). The platform keeps immutable, timestamped logs of data changes, workflow executions and compliance decisions, while built-in escalation paths support internal controls, suspicious activity reporting, periodic information refreshes and third-party reliance management (Articles 48–50).

For the regulated organisations across more than 25 jurisdictions that use KYCP for onboarding, ongoing monitoring and risk orchestration, the message is one of reassurance: their technology partner is treating future compliance with the same rigour as day-to-day execution.

Uncertainty remains, however. The Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA) is still finalising its Regulatory Technical Standards ahead of the July 2027 enforcement deadline. KYCP indicates it will keep evolving its CLM engine accordingly, aiming to give clients the confidence, clarity and control to operate securely, maintain full auditability and stay ahead of shifting global regulation.
