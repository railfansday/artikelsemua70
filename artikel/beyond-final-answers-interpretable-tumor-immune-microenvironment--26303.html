Beyond Final Answers: Interpretable Tumor Immune Microenvironment Reasoning from H&E and mIF

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (294 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.26794v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Readouts of the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), such as CD8+ T-cell density, intra-epithelial infiltration and the Ki67 index, combine tissue architecture from H&E with cell phenotypes from multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF). Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) could automate these readouts with an explanation, but two gaps remain. Pathology MLLMs read H&E alone, while these readouts need the cell phenotypes that mIF measures. And their reasoning has no step-level truth to be checked against, so R1-style reinforcement learning rewards only the final answer and leaves the reasoning unverified. Same-section H&E and mIF provide what both gaps lack: mIF measures every cell without manual annotation, which gives cell-level ground truth for each step that clinical guidelines prescribe, from delineating a tissue compartment to counting and normalizing the marker-positive cells within it. We therefore present HERMES (H&E-mIF Reasoning with Multimodal Evidence Supervision), an R1-style MLLM that reasons jointly over paired H&E and mIF in guideline steps and is trained with a process reward that checks every stated quantity against this truth. Across four field-level TIME tasks in colorectal cancer, HERMES (4B) reaches an average accuracy of 84.5%, compared with 74.7% for the strongest of twelve proprietary, general, medical and pathology MLLMs, and a higher share of its intermediate quantities is correct than for the strongest open-weight baseline. Without the process reward, reinforcement learning barely improves on supervised fine-tuning (80.0% vs. 78.8%) and fewer stated quantities are correct; editing a decisive stated quantity changes the answer as implied in all 885 test questions. Without retraining, it keeps the highest average accuracy on an external cohort imaged with CODEX (76.4% vs. 67.3%) and on head and neck cancer, a cancer type absent from training (76.7% vs. 72.5%).
