Latest Update: Corma hits $1bn valuation with $125m Series A

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Corma, an AI research lab building foundation models for defensive cybersecurity, has secured $125m in Series A funding at a $1bn valuation, only three months after revealing its seed round.

Recent findings suggest that NEA led the investment, with participation from returning backers Sequoia Capital, Khosla Ventures and Coatue. The latest raise takes the company’s total funding to $185m.

Corma’s $60m seed round, led by Sequoia, was declared in August.

The funding arrives as large organisations move promptly to integrate Corma’s model into their security operations amid mounting concern over AI-driven attacks. Since the organization debuted publicly, interest has climbed among Fortune 100, Fortune 500 and Fortune Global 500 businesses, which are handing a growing share of their security workload to Corma’s autonomous AI workforce. To keep pace, the firm has expanded its server capacity 24-fold.

At its August launch, Corma cautioned that AI was advancing far faster on the offensive side than the defensive one. That concern was borne out in October, when a series of serious vulnerabilities and breaches affecting widely used software, nicknamed “Black October,” left security teams under pressure and prompted warnings from governments in Washington, Tokyo and elsewhere.

According to Corma, the imbalance stems from the industry’s focus on coding. As leading labs pour resources into models that write software, those same models become more adept at spotting flaws, since vulnerabilities are effectively bugs. When combined with agents, they can now carry out exploits from start to finish. Defensive work outside code security, however, calls for different skills, such as sifting through large amounts of audit logs, security events and network flows, linking faint signals across systems over extended periods, and remaining consistent through long chains of decisions. Coding models were not designed for these tasks.

Corma trains foundation models specifically for this defensive work and deploys them as an AI workforce that sits alongside human security teams, operating within their existing tools and handling a broad range of security tasks.

Corma co-founder and CEO Alon Pluda mentioned, “Cybersecurity is now a battle of intelligence against intelligence, and Black October made that impossible to ignore. Since we came out of stealth, demand from the world’s largest organizations has been overwhelming, and one of our hardest problems has been securing capacity fast enough to keep up with the demand.

NEA partner Aaron Jacobson remarked, “Corma is building the intelligence layer that defensive security needs. As AI gives attackers an advantage, Corma’s combination of frontier AI research and deep cybersecurity expertise has the potential to help organizations defend against these threats at scale. Corma is the only manufacturer taking this on in a way that is scalable across organizations to prevent the next major cyberattack.”.
