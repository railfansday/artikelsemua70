Industry observers note that 2026 was a huge year for the laptop industry. The MacBook Neo completely disrupted the budget PC market, while Microsoft’s Surface Laptop Ultra and Nvidia’s RTX Spark laptops redefined agentic AI in Windows. But as we round out the year, I always love looking back to see which of the best laptops stood out among our readers. These are the 10 most popular laptops of 2026 based on aggregated data from our retail partners and our readers.

In a recent development, the following entries are listed in order of units sold through links clicked from ZDNET articles. (Don’t worry, your privacy is protected. ZDNET only has access to aggregate data from our specific user base. There’s no way for us to identify individuals.).

Also: Best Black Friday laptop deals.

Recent findings suggest that from lightweight laptops to the best laptops for Linux, we’ve tested dozens of laptops in real-world environments to determine which ones are worth your hard-earned money. Here are ZDNET readers’ favorite laptops for 2026, based on their actual purchases (and many of these laptops are on sale right now for Black Friday.).

More top-purchased by our readers: Top overall | SSDs | Portable power stations | Smartwatches | TVs | Headphones.

Our top-selling laptop this year was the latest MacBook Air M5, Apple’s thinnest and lightest MacBook to date, powered by the M5 chip for up to 18 hours of battery life. With a standard 16GB of unified memory, the Air is more than powerful enough to take on demanding workflows.

Read our review: Apple MacBook Air M5.

Recent findings suggest that the ThinkPad T14S is a battery-efficient, powerful workhorse with extensive ports and memory customizations. Armed with a Snapdragon or AMD Ryzen chipset, this laptop prioritizes function over form, despite having one of the best keyboard and trackpad combos around.

Read our review: Lenovo ThinkPad T14S.

If you’re looking for a beast of a gaming rig, the Alienware 18 Area-51 is up there with the best. It comes with an Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX processor, up to 64GB of RAM, 2TB of storage, and an Nvidia GeForce RTX 5070Ti, capable of running the latest triple-A gaming titles without breaking a sweat.

Read our review: Alienware 18 Area-51.

Earning our Editors’ Choice award for its superior repairability, the 14th-gen ThinkPad X1 Carbon is as light as it is powerful, with a redesigned double-sided motherboard and an excellent battery. This is the ultimate ThinkPad: upgradeable, repairable, utilitarian, and with premium touches like a gorgeous 2.8K OLED display, haptic touchpad and 10MP webcam.

Read our review: ThinkPad X1 Carbon (Gen 14) Aura Edition.

Asus’ Vivobook 16 is a great laptop for everyday people looking for a big display, full-sized keyboard, and lots of ports. The Snapdragon X processor on board allows for good battery life, but the 8GB of RAM is best suited for students and everyday users working with standard productivity software.

Looking for a serious deal? This IdeaPad has all the basics for a very affordable $329. It comes with 8GB of RAM and 256GB of storage, which is not a whole lot, but part of the reason for its ultra-competitive price point. I’d recommend this laptop for people looking for a laptop for checking emails, surfing the web, and basic work tasks without the need for a lot of custom apps.

It has been revealed that acer’s Predator Helios lineup packs a ton of power at an accessible price point. This model comes with an Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX processor and 16GB of RAM with a GeForce RTX 5060 graphics card, enabling top-tier gaming performance.

Recent findings suggest that apple’s 15-inch MacBook Air M5 offers significant hardware upgrades over its predecessor while retaining its sleek, lightweight design in the larger form factor. The combination of its Liquid Retina display, 18 hours of battery life, and 12MP webcam ensures a top-notch experience whether you’re editing photos or hopping on a video call, and the 15-inch size offers an excellent “Goldilocks” size that’s not too substantial, not too small, while still being 0.4 inches thick.

Read our review: Apple MacBook Air M5.

Industry observers note that the 2026 refresh of the Acer Swift 16 AI holds the line as Acer’s premium 16-inch, but to give it a unique identity, it got a few different design elements pushing it further into “made-for-creatives” territory — most notably, the football field-sized trackpad that also serves as a drawing surface with the included stylus. The other headliner is the 16-inch OLED display. Just like last year’s model, it’s crisp, bright, and vibrant, with an edge-to-edge glass construction at WQXGA+ (2880×1800) resolution and a standard 120Hz refresh rate.

Read our review: Acer Swift 16 AI (2026).

Microsoft Surface Pro (13-inch) / Kyle Kucharski/ZDNET.

Industry observers note that rounding out the list is Microsoft’s 2026 Surface Pro, one of the most premium convertible tablets we’ve tested, particularly with its versatile form factor. For users looking for a tablet/laptop hybrid that doesn’t feel fragile, the Surface Pro nails the flexibility with excellent hardware and a long list of configurations. The top-selling configuration and the one I recommend most comes with 16GB of RAM and the Snapdragon X Plus processor for snappy, lightweight performance and over 10 hours of battery life on one charge.

Read our review: Microsoft Surface Pro 13-inch (2026).
