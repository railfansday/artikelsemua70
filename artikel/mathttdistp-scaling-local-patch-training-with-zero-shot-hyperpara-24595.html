$\mathtt{DistP}$: Scaling Local-Patch Training with Zero-Shot Hyperparameter Transfer

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (219 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.27808v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Local-update methods offer a communication-efficient alternative to Distributed Data Parallelism ($\mathtt{DDP}$), but introduce additional hyperparameters that require costly tuning. For $\mathtt{DDP}$, zero-shot hyperparameter transfer reduces this cost by reusing settings tuned on smaller models, but its applicability to local-update training remains unclear. We propose $\mathtt{DistP}$, a theoretical framework extending existing transfer rules from $\mathtt{DDP}$ to local-update training with varying worker counts, local steps, and outer optimizers. With averaging as the outer optimizer, $\mathtt{DistP}$ supports learning-rate transfer across width and depth while improving model quality. The tuned inner learning rate also directly transfers across synchronization intervals, but follows two regimes when increasing the global batch size: square-root learning-rate scaling for larger local batches and a fixed reference rate for additional workers. We then generalize width and depth transfer to other outer optimizers, enabling stable scaling with $\mathtt{Adam}$. However, with momentum in the outer optimizer, changing batch size or local steps can require inner--outer learning-rate tuning, consistent with prior work. In language-modeling experiments at $823$M parameters, $\mathtt{DistP}$ reduces validation loss by $8.1\%$ and improves mean downstream accuracy by $10\%$ relative to baselines without retuning at the larger scale. Fitted scaling laws suggest that this advantage grows with the compute budget, reinforcing the benefits of $\mathtt{DistP}$ for communication-efficient training.
