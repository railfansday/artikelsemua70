CALVIN-MAX: When Does a Mid-Execution Change Hurt a Robot Policy?

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (300 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.31868v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Robots must often keep working after the world changes, as when a block moves or the camera shifts while the robot still acts on what it saw before. Recent work highlighted that one change during a task lowers success for every policy tested, but did not separate the effect of its timing from the cost of the changed world, or measure what it costs afterwards. We introduce CALVIN-MAX, a benchmark of 7,828 cases on CALVIN's five-instruction chains, each with one change to the objects, the camera, the robot, or the instruction. Each case compares three runs that are identical until the affected instruction begins: one without the change, one with it in place from that moment, and one in which it arrives while the robot is moving. The second run against the first gives the cost of the changed world, and the third against the second the effect of meeting it mid-motion. On 1,526 held-out cases per policy, with hypotheses fixed in advance, each of ten released policies loses 4.4 to 9.9 percentage points on the affected instruction over the eight core changes. Whether timing matters depends on the change: one that alters what the robot sees costs 5.4 points less mid-motion than from the start, whereas a change to the scene itself or to the robot highlights no detectable effect. Rewinding the robot to the start of the instruction removes the benefit for every policy; the stricter test we fixed in advance, that the rewound run then matches the second run, passes for three. The cost also outlasts the affected instruction: the change stays in place, and a policy that still completes that instruction is 5.0 to 16.8 points less likely to complete the next one.
