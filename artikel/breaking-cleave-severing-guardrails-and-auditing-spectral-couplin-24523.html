Breaking: Cleave: Severing Guardrails and Auditing Spectral Coupling in Open-Weight LLMs

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (207 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.27426v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Open-weight Large Language Models (LLMs) are widely downloaded for their strong coding and reasoning capabilities and deployed locally beyond the provider's control. To prevent misuse, developers embed safety guardrails into these models before release. However, we find that these guardrails rely on the residual writing weights to trigger refusals. Based on this insight, we propose Cleave, a training-free attack that directly edits these weights to remove safety restrictions while preserving general capabilities. With Cleave, malicious users can either remove all guardrails or selectively lift only cybersecurity restrictions. Because this selective removal retains up to a 99% refusal rate on other harmful requests and avoids weight rank collapse, it evades both behavioral spot-checks and static weight audits. Across five open-weight LLMs from 7B to 320B parameters, Cleave achieves up to a 67.97% attack success rate on seven safety benchmarks, substantially outperforming existing white-box baselines. On 356 cybersecurity tasks and 15 live vulnerable services, it further boosts executable exploit generation by up to 24.0 times and compromises up to 66.7% of the targets. To counter this threat, we propose a dual-stage defense that combines pre-release subspace diagnostics with post-release weight-coupling audits, achieving 100% detection recall at a 1% false-positive rate.
