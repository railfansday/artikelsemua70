AI is becoming part of how Singapore Airlines serves customers, empowers employees, and builds technology. The airline uses the OpenAI API for customer-facing experiences and OpenAI models inside Jarvis, its governed internal AI platform for employees. It also uses OpenAI models in its software development workflows to deliver more personalized service, improve productivity, and help teams build more efficiently.

Across these deployments, Singapore Airlines pairs OpenAI models with orchestration, internal data controls, validation, and human review, giving teams a consistent way to bring AI into production workflows responsibly.

One of Singapore Airlines’ most visible AI applications is Kris, its customer-facing chatbot. After moving Kris from a traditional dialog-flow system to an LLM-powered conversational experience using the OpenAI API, Singapore Airlines saw a 2x increase in customer satisfaction.

Alongside that, the airline deployed AI-assisted tools for customer service representatives, including OpenAI API-powered call summarization, so that representatives spend less time on after-call work and more time on the customer. Singapore Airlines saw an approximately 10% reduction in average handling time for its customer service representatives.

According to recent reports, in the earlier dialog-flow based chatbot design, every topic had to be mapped manually, and every flow had to be written in advance. That made coverage difficult to scale. If an intent was missing, the conversation was more likely to end up requiring support from a customer service representative, even when the customer’s request could have been handled through self-service.

Sources indicate that singapore Airlines moved Kris to a fully LLM-powered conversational experience using the OpenAI API. Instead of depending on predefined flows, the chatbot can interpret customer intent in natural language, ground answers in a knowledge base, manage multi-topic conversations, and hand over to a customer service representative with the relevant context when needed.

Today, customers use Kris for general queries, special requests, real-time flight disruption information, and selected transactional workflows. Singapore Airlines is continuing to expand its capabilities to include booking changes, baggage transactions, and more complex agentic interactions.

Flight Recommender lets customers describe the trip they want in natural language instead of repeatedly adjusting filters, dates, and destinations. Usage has grown nearly 3x over the past year.

Industry observers note that built on the OpenAI API, Flight Recommender uses an orchestration layer connected to fare and availability data. It interprets customer intent, calls the relevant tools, and returns personalized travel recommendations in natural language.

The team has also implemented guardrails for input and output validation, malicious or inappropriate query handling, hallucination reduction, PII handling, and authenticated access to only the context required for each query. Singapore Airlines is preparing to expand the experience into a more conversational chatbot interaction, where customers can keep refining travel ideas rather than restarting a search.

In a recent development, beyond customer-facing experiences, Singapore Airlines is also using OpenAI models in parts of the software development lifecycle to help teams plan, refine, test, and review work more efficiently. Across selected software development lifecycle activities, teams have achieved productivity gains of up to 20%.

AI now runs through the entire delivery lifecycle and not just coding. Teams use generative AI models to support requirements refinement, stakeholder-session analysis, product specifications, edge-case identification, test-case creation, architecture review, and the breakdown of complex work into smaller, more testable tasks.

Industry observers note that for one commercial feature, the team used the OpenAI API to challenge assumptions, identify ambiguous requirements and surface edge cases before development begins. Once those were well-defined, AI helped translate them into design options, implementation plans, test cases and then code generation.

During deployment and beyond, AI also helps to interpret how the system is behaving in production, and flagging challenges that might otherwise go unnoticed. With AI active at every step of the workflow, team members have shifted from producing every artifact themselves, to directing, reviewing and validating what AI produces throughout the delivery lifecycle. This means they can focus on work that truly requires human judgement, such as prioritization, architecture, governance and stakeholder outcomes.

More broadly, OpenAI models are helping Singapore Airlines improve requirements quality, test coverage, and conformance with internal standards. Teams use these workflows to generate edge cases for testing, review architecture plans, and support human validation before work moves further through the development lifecycle.

Sources indicate that beyond engineering teams, Singapore Airlines is making AI accessible across the business through Jarvis, its internal AI platform. Powered by OpenAI models, Jarvis gives employees a governed way to use AI in their day-to-day work, from summarizing documents to creating custom workflows.

According to recent reports, employees use Jarvis to work across PDFs, Word documents, Excel files, PowerPoint decks, and other file types in a secure environment, while creating custom workflows and lightweight knowledge bases for their day-to-day work. Today, more than 60% of Singapore Airlines employees are using Jarvis monthly with adoption continuing to increase.

Recent findings suggest that singapore Airlines is continuing to expand AI across customer and employee workflows. For Kris, the next phase is deeper self-service, with more transactional capabilities that help customers resolve requests directly in chat. Flight Recommender is moving toward a more conversational travel discovery experience, where customers can refine ideas over multiple turns rather than restarting a search.

With Jarvis available across the business, Singapore Airlines is focused on helping more employees put AI to work in their day-to-day roles. Together with its customer-facing innovations, this is helping the airline build a future where AI makes travel more seamless, employees more productive, and the business more responsive to changing needs.
