Recent findings suggest that revolut⁠(opens in a latest window) was built to challenge the way people interact with money. That ambition now stretches across more than 40 markets, 800,000 businesses and a consumer base that’s grown by 23% in the last 12 months.

Add to that a firm culture that expects small, lean teams to move swiftly and it’s no surprise that ChatGPT and Codex have proven a natural fit.

“The perfect description for a high-caliber Revolut person is someone who can identify a problem really rapidly, deduce the solution, design four scenarios, identify the best one of those four then take it to market,” indicates Kim Maniovich, Revolut’s Global Head of Business Marketing.

“Partnering with OpenAI enables us to build a lot of these solutions in a way that’s incredibly lean, cost-efficient and really tailored to the unique way that we work.”.

But moving faster is only part of the opportunity. For Revolut, the bigger question is what becomes possible when talented people spend less time on the mechanics of getting things done—and more time tackling problems that could fundamentally change financial services.

Sources indicate that as Revolut grows, so does the complexity of protecting its customers. According to one report⁠(opens in a new window), over half of adults worldwide reported a scam encounter in 2025.

Industry observers note that for Revolut, every transaction creates another opportunity to identify suspicious activity, investigate potential risks and implement robust and tailored protections for its customers. But the sheer volume of those transactions far outstrips the human resources available to any bank.

Recent findings suggest that “The biggest problem we can tackle with AI today is the scaling of humans versus the demand that we get,” says AI Lead David Abitbol. That’s why one of Revolut’s largest AI applications is a FinCrime agent powered by OpenAI models.

Rather than requiring investigators to work through every eligible case manually, the agent can examine information, apply Revolut’s rules and complete the defined process automatically.

It has been revealed that the release of GPT‑5.5 was a turning point. It was then, says Abitbol, that the models had become capable and cost-effective enough for Revolut to delegate screening and investigation workflows entirely to the agent. The result is greater capacity to monitor transactions without increasing investigator headcount at the same rate as demand.

That, adds Information Security Engineer Mo Amiri, “translates directly into stronger protection and a more resilient system our customers can trust.”.

‘Move fast but build trust’ could easily be Revolut’s internal engineering slogan. While Abitbol points out that Codex has delivered a 30-60% reduction in coding time, he’s also clear that certain guardrails are non-negotiable.

Sources indicate that “We have a systematic framework where anyone in the company who’s building anything with AI has the same set of standards across development, A/B testing, and business metric prediction,” he states. “So every engineer is held to the same standard of quality across the company. We started to get enough intelligence for reasonable costs where we could increasingly delegate initial investigations and screenings to the agent.”.

According to recent reports, for Senior Platform Engineer Dmitry Pitik, that 30-60% reduction in coding time became tangible while he was building an internal code-review service. He designed the system, broke it into features and initially assumed he’d need a development team to implement it.

“I realised I don’t need a whole team of developers to implement this,” he recalls. “I can just launch different agents and assign each of them a particular task.” Codex became his implementation team, while Dmitry spent more time designing the system, planning the work and reviewing what came back.

Sources indicate that this is a pattern that Abitbol has seen across Revolut. As Codex returns capacity to engineers they’re using that reclaimed time “to spend more time discussing design and the best solution. So we spend more time designing instead of implementing.”.

According to recent reports, that doesn’t remove the need for engineering expertise, it just changes where that expertise is applied. “Every developer is responsible for the code they produce, and we don’t blindly trust AI,” Pitik states. The result is a different division of work: AI can help with implementation, while engineers devote more attention to the decisions that determine whether the software is useful, reliable and worth building.

Industry observers note that that overhead is worth it. As Codex works on one task, Pitik can work in parallel on another. That kind of multitasking is essential in a firm where, as Kim Maniovich explains, the average employee has “three different jobs.” Using Codex creates a virtuous (if challenging) flywheel where “month on month the targets we’re setting get more aggressive, but also more attainable. And so the bar keeps moving up. AI is making that possible.”.

Examples of what Revolut has been able to do with this new-found capacity include developing a tool to build web UIs for internal apps and external-facing products.

Recent findings suggest that or turning seven years of design documentation into AI-readable context in a single day, creating a foundation for tools intended to help designers work more effectively. The exercise illustrates how AI can make accumulated organisational knowledge more accessible, reducing the effort required to work with complex systems. “We can shift focus from the things which are vital but hard to do without AI to the things which matter for customers,” claims Senior Designer Dmytro Pavlov.

It has been revealed that mo Amiri’s job is to make sure all of this speed is matched by security—not just for customers but for Revolut’s own employees. That gives him two views of AI at once: how it can help Revolut investigate a problem, and what teams need before they can deploy it safely.

In a recent development, in a phishing investigation, for example, an analyst once had to gather an email, inspect its links and attachments, trace the sender and assemble the evidence manually. “With stronger AI tools, we can uncover those connections instantly, and dig deeper into the questions that really matter,” he indicates.

While AI does the legwork and presents an investigator with a structured case, it’s still down to the individual to review the original records, decide whether the explanation makes sense and approve any action.

In a recent development, amiri applies the same thinking when teams bring upcoming AI applications into Revolut. He follows the complete chain: what information the system sees, where that information goes, which tools it can reach, what it can do with that access and what happens when something fails. He also tests whether apparently convincing conclusions are actually supported by the evidence.

Industry observers note that “The most critical decision is always about accountability,” he says. “If an AI tool suggests a code change, a human engineer needs to understand that change well enough to stand by it. That directly drives how we design the review process, giving developers the context they need without cluttering their everyday workflow.”.

For Revolut, safeguards aren’t separate from innovation. They help establish the conditions under which teams can confidently adopt more capable technology. “That’s how you earn real confidence in AI,” he explains, “by making thoughtful decisions upfront and testing as you go.”.

Sources indicate that use speed to raise ambition, not lower standards. Time saved on coding and investigation creates room for better design, harder questions and more consequential work.

Industry observers note that keep people on the hook. AI can gather evidence, write code and recommend action. A person still needs to understand the work well enough to stand behind it.

Industry observers note that make your knowledge usable. AI works better when it can reach clear, well-organised context. The same exercise often demonstrates where the organisation itself needs to be clearer.

Revolut has already shown how AI can increase the amount and complexity of work its teams can attempt. Its next opportunity is to carry that combination of capability, judgment and design into the way customers interact with their money.

Abitbol imagines a more conversational interface in which customers can ask Revolut to help with everyday financial tasks: blocking a card, managing a subscription or finding ways to save for a trip. Pavlov sees the design challenge underneath that experience—turning complexity into something consistent, familiar and easy to use.

Kim Maniovich connects that future to the culture driving the work. “I hope that we can take the values that we operate by every day—this unapologetic ambition, the desire to never settle—and enable people to apply that to their own lives through the products that we build for them.”.

It has been revealed that as AI makes it easier to build, the differentiator becomes knowing what to build—and maintaining the judgment, standards and customer understanding to do it well.
