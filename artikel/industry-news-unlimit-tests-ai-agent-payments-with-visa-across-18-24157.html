📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Unlimit, a worldwide provider of financial infrastructure, has signed up to Visa’s Agentic Ready Program for Acceptance, putting its payment network to the test as AI agents prepare to make purchases on behalf of consumers.

According to recent reports, under the initiative, Unlimit and Visa will examine whether the tech giant’s systems can handle transactions triggered by AI agents rather than people. The trials will cover how credentials and authentication information are processed securely, full end-to-end testing, and what happens after a purchase, including refunds, chargebacks and reconciliation.

The aim is to confirm that merchants and platforms on Unlimit’s network can accept agent-led payments for their customers while relying on existing card networks and their safeguards.

Visa’s broadened programme unites acquirers, payment service providers, merchants and platforms in an effort to get the wider ecosystem ready for AI-initiated payments on a global scale.

In a recent development, unlimit operates a proprietary payments network reaching over 180 countries. The firm holds licences across 97 jurisdictions, supports in excess of 1,000 local payment methods, offers payouts in more than 150 currencies and connects directly with domestic payment systems. This blend of international coverage and local integration gives the firm a foundation to serve agentic commerce across varied payment rails, regulatory regimes and shopper habits.

Through its participation, Unlimit is contributing to a shared framework for agentic commerce, one designed to build trust into emerging purchasing experiences while extending the infrastructure that businesses already depend on.

Industry observers note that unlimit CEO of global payments Irene Skrynova said, “AI agents are becoming a new type of customer. They will increasingly search, compare and purchase on behalf of people, fundamentally changing how businesses sell and compete. This will create an entirely new wave of demand, and companies that adapt early will be best positioned to capture it. Our work with Visa is an important step towards building the infrastructure that will power the next generation of global commerce.”.
