Breaking: Cleaning correlation matrices with external information: the Maximum Variance estimator

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (236 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.30843v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Rotationally invariant estimators clean the eigenvalues of an empirical correlation matrix but keep its noisy eigenvectors. We study an estimator that corrects the eigenvectors by using external information, namely a partition of the assets into groups such as asset classes or regions. The Maximum Variance (MaxVar) estimator restricts the leading eigenvectors to linear combinations of the maximum-variance portfolios of the groups and replaces the rest of the spectrum by a single eigenvalue. It is a factor model with about n parameters instead of n2/2, and it is positive de nite even when the number of observations is smaller than the number of assets n. We apply it to the correlation matrix of 47 futures on stock indices, bonds and currencies, with the three asset classes as groups. In an illustrative Monte Carlo test, in which the reference matrix has the structure assumed by the estimator, the distance to the reference is reduced by one third with respect to the empirical matrix, against less than 10% for the rotationally invariant estimator. The decisive test is the performance of a portfolio on real data: over 1993-2018, the Sharpe ratio of the risk parity portfolio, which involves the inverse of the matrix, is 1.28 with the MaxVar estimator, 1.24 with the rotationally invariant estimator and 1.17 with the empirical matrix, and the advantage increases when the estimation window is shortened.
