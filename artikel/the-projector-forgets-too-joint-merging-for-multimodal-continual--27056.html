The Projector Forgets Too: Joint Merging for Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (240 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2604.14016v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning (MCIT) is essential for sequential task adaptation of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) but is severely restricted by catastrophic forgetting. While existing literature focuses on the reasoning language backbone, in this work, we find that forgetting can arise from changes in both the cross-modal projector and the language model's LoRA adapters. In component restoration experiments, restoring an earlier task's LoRA adapter while retaining the final-task projector can leave an accuracy gap relative to restoring both components. To address these two sources of forgetting, we present \textbf{JoMe} (\textbf{Jo}int \textbf{Me}rging), which combines \textbf{C}ross-modal \textbf{P}rojection \textbf{M}erging (\textbf{CPM}) and \textbf{L}ow-rank \textbf{P}arameter \textbf{M}erging (\textbf{LPM}). Specifically, CPM recovers perceptual alignment by adaptively merging cross-modal visual representations via visual-prototype guidance during inference. Simultaneously, LPM recursively merges task-specific LoRA updates to mitigate interference across tasks. By leveraging recursive least squares, LPM yields a closed-form solution to the layer-wise least-squares objective on fixed inputs. Notably, JoMe operates as a training-free paradigm that achieves knowledge merging via efficient CPU-based algebraic operations, eliminating additional gradient-based optimization beyond initial tuning. Our extensive evaluations confirm the superior performance and robustness of JoMe across multiple MLLMs and benchmarks. Specifically, on the UCIT benchmark, JoMe achieves significant leads of up to 5.37\% and 1.72\% in final average accuracy over state-of-the-art methods across two different MLLMs, respectively. \footnote{Our code and data are included in the supplementary material for examination.}.
