Tech Report: OpenAI is offering small businesses free AI support – and up to $8K/mo to experts who help them

It has been revealed that if you run a small business, OpenAI would like to give you free expert assistance through its OpenWorks blueprint. If you’re an AI expert and are willing to help out local businesses in your community adopt AI successfully, OpenAI may give you up to $8,000 per month.

Also: The best free AI courses and certificates for upskilling.

Yesterday, OpenAI introduced OpenWorks, which the company describes as “a nationwide network of OpenWorks Partners to provide free, hands-on AI support to small businesses.”.

The offering is straightforward. OpenAI is reaching out to small businesses that can benefit from better integration of AI technology, providing them with experienced guidance and support from vetted experts, all at no charge to those that qualify.

Right now, the pilot program is available in 20 communities. According to a tweet by OpenAI’s Jake Ottiger, OpenWorks is open only to communities in the US “for now”. As you can see from the map below, substantial gaps remain across the Mountain West and rural America. Right now, the program appears to be in-person focused. The partner application specifically asks, “Can you work in person?” and “Tell us where you can help in person.”.

Still unknown is whether a virtual assistance program is planned for those small businesses located outside of urban centers.

It has been revealed that as an educator and an AI expert, I’d enjoy helping small businesses in my area, but I live in a tiny farm town in a state that doesn’t have a single community listed on the map. For the record, though, because I cover OpenAI aggressively, it probably wouldn’t be appropriate for me to participate, even if such a program were in my community.

In a tweet, OpenAI president and cofounder Greg Brockman described OpenWorks as a program designed to fund local AI experts to help small businesses grow with AI. He said, “AI is accelerating small business, lowering the barriers to entry (I’ve been increasingly hearing from people who left their job to start a small business because they have AI tools to support them) and helping them earn more.”.

I don’t think he’s suggesting you just quit your job to use AI to start a business, but as the owner of a two-person family business, I have found that AI has been an enormous force multiplier for all sorts of projects, many of which I’ve discussed here on ZDNET.

It looks like OpenWorks is open to most small businesses; yours doesn’t have to be a tech-centric tech giant.

If you run an independently owned small business in the US, you can apply to be included in the program. If accepted, you’ll get one-on-one support from a partner expert, help with the tools you’re already using, and three free months of ChatGPT Business.

On the other hand, OpenAI notes, “Software and SaaS companies, venture-backed businesses, and private equity-owned businesses are not eligible.”.

In the announcement, OpenAI spotlighted four businesses it’s been working with through this program.

In a recent development, tiny Things is a tiny shop packed with, well, tiny things; the company sells small gifts. With OpenWorks’ help, Tiny Things built a custom inventory dashboard that connects existing tools to help it keep its most popular products in stock.

According to recent reports, ghost Grow 843 is a microgreens and lettuce grower. OpenAI helped the supplier target restaurant prospects and gain new restaurant customers.

In a recent development, sign Badgers is a custom sign business. AI helped automate inventory tracking and managing vendor price checks. The company claims OpenWorks’ help saved them 2-5 hours each week. For a small business, those savings aren’t about money. They’re about whether you get to have lunch or go home before the current workday starts to slide into the next workday.

Black Paper Party is a gift wrap supplier. With OpenWorks’ help, it matched purchase orders with shipping records, discovered if it had billing errors, and avoided thousands of dollars in potential chargebacks.

Sources indicate that for those interested in becoming paid partners, OpenAI asks you to be technically capable, collaborative, and motivated to give back to your community. The firm also asks, “Can you commit four full days per month to providing free, hands-on AI help to local small businesses, plus one monthly workshop?”.

Recent findings suggest that the company also asks you to propose ideas for what you’d work on for your first community day and what you’d work on together with small businesses. OpenAI asks you to list one or two client testimonials to show that you’re able to collaboratively work with small businesses.

Recent findings suggest that “We’re looking for technically skilled and curious individuals and small teams committed to helping local businesses thrive,” OpenAI notes. “Software and AI consultancies are encouraged to apply alongside their existing client work.”.

Sources indicate that as for the $8,000 per month, the company doesn’t specify how that number is arrived upon or how much individual partners will be paid. They just say “up to.” I still think it’s a very interesting opportunity for the right consultants and educators.

It has been revealed that this kind of market development program with subsidized business assistance is nothing latest. It’s a tried-and-true way to grow technology adoption beyond the “gee whiz” phase and into the proven, reliable deployment phase.

Also: Stanford’s free online course takes you far beyond ChatGPT.

In 1990, IBM set up a small business division that offered training and incentives for businesses who wanted to adopt PC technology. The tech giant even set up pop-up stores inside of the old Sears retail chain.

In 1996, Microsoft created a small business technology partnership board. The firm set up a $5 million fund in cash and software to “Help connect hundreds of small-business organizations across the country to the Internet.” This was back in the early days when internet adoption was still latest to small businesses.

Recent findings suggest that one example of Google funding a subsidized business assistant program goes back almost 10 years. In 2017, the firm set up a free workshop program for small businesses to help them learn how to use Google’s tools for digital marketing.

In a recent development, companies set up these business assistance plans for a variety of reasons, but they’re all grounded in sound business development strategies. A significant part of this approach is to convert enthusiasm and pilot experiments into long, sustainable parts of a business strategy.

According to recent reports, with a high-yield technology like generative AI, the difference between success and failure isn’t always about the tech itself, but about how it’s applied. By funding expert help, partners can help companies succeed and thrive with new innovations.

Such programs help convert curiosity into working systems, help gain “design-ins” and become the platform businesses choose, reduce the cost of acquiring long-term customers, build a knowledge and add-on distribution network, and create success stories and business proof that can be references across the sales process.

Industry observers note that when executed correctly and participants chosen fairly, plans like OpenWorks are wins for everyone. The small businesses gain support and practical expertise, the partners gain opportunities that help them grow their portfolios, and the sponsors gain customers, grateful reference accounts, and champions who will tell their stories to other small businesses.

Recent findings suggest that I’ve often criticized OpenAI for its lack of traditional product marketing discipline, but this sort of program is right out of the professional product marketing and business development playbook. And that’s a good thing for everyone involved.
