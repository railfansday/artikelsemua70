Incomplete-market CAPM equilibria in continuous time

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (200 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2408.03134v4 Announce Type: replace Abstract: We study continuous-time CAPM equilibria with general mean-variance preferences of the form $U(\mu,\sigma)$, where $U$ is strictly quasiconcave, increasing in the mean $\mu$ and decreasing in the volatility $\sigma$ of terminal wealth. Our framework accommodates both financial and productive assets with nontradeable endowments. We allow for equilibria driven by general semimartingales in the case of linear mean-variance preferences and by continuous semimartingales in the case of general mean-variance preferences. The key insight is a connection between mean-variance efficiency and quadratic utility maximisation. We first characterise quadratic equilibria through an explicit family of equilibrium price processes indexed by the aggregate risk tolerance. We then show that each agent's optimal mean-variance strategy corresponds to a quadratic optimum with an endogenously determined risk tolerance. This correspondence reduces the mean-variance equilibrium problem to a one-dimensional fixed-point problem, whose solvability we establish using fine properties of (dynamic) mean-variance hedging and a novel stability result for quadratic BSDEs with BMO growth at the origin. Finally, examples illustrate how unspanned stochastic-volatility risk interacts with aggregate endowments to affect equilibrium asset prices, and how nonlinear mean-variance preferences influence equilibrium risk tolerance and asset-price comparative statics.
