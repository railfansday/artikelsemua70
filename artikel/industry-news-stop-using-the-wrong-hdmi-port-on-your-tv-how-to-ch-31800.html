Industry observers note that not all HDMI ports are created equal. Most newer TVs come with three or four HDMI ports; on the surface, they look identical. They all have the same shape and work with the same HDMI cable, but not every port supports the same features. Choosing the wrong one can leave soundbar silent, your PS5 stuck at 60Hz, or your PC unable to output at a high refresh rate.

Here’s the good news: It’s easy to learn what each HDMI port does and how to match your devices to the right one.

Industry observers note that all HDMI ports carry video and audio, but they support different bandwidth levels, and that determines which devices you should use with them. If you happen to plug into the wrong HDMI port, nothing will break, but you will not get full performance or sound.

Industry observers note that for example, an HDMI 2.0 port supports 4K at 60Hz, HDR, and ARC audio, making it good for streaming media players and cable boxes. Meanwhile, an HDMI 2.1 port supports 4K at 120Hz (or 8K at 60Hz), VRR, ALLM, and eARC, which is great for next-generation consoles like the PS5. HDMI 2.1 can sometimes reach 4K at 240Hz with Display Stream Compression (DSC).

Then there is usually an ARC / eARC port, or audio return channel. It looks like an HDMI port, but it can send audio back out to a soundbar or AV receiver. ARC supports standard Dolby Digital 5.1, while eARC supports uncompressed Dolby Atmos and DTS:X.

Your TV likely has labeled HDMI ports, so take a look at the back to see for yourself.

Recent findings suggest that usually, they’re called HDMI 1, HDMI 2, and HDMI 3, and then you’ll see ARC or eARC. Some manufacturers print descriptions under the labels. You might see an HDMI 2.1 port described as 4K120, VRR, Game, or Enhanced, or it might even say the exact resolution and frame rate it supports. If there’s no description for an HDMI port, assume it’s standard HDMI 2.0 — except the one showing ARC.
