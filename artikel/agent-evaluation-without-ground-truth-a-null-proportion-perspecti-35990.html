Agent Evaluation Without Ground Truth: A Null Proportion Perspective

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (194 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.31539v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: The correctness of actions taken by agents is typically evaluated against known, externally verified ground truth labels, such as whether a proof is correct or whether code compiles and passes unit tests. However, as coding and science agents are entrusted with more end-to-end autonomy -- proposing interventions, running experiments, and deciding what to deploy and investigate next -- whether an agent's actions genuinely improve an outcome must be inferred from noisy, often inconclusive experiments. To evaluate agents in this setting, we define agent quality as a weighted sum of correct decisions along its trajectory, using sequences of $e$-values as statistical evidence regarding correctness in place of ground-truth labels. Our framework accommodates arbitrarily dependent decisions, data-dependent trajectory termination, dynamically generated hypotheses, and quality measures defined after the trajectory concludes. We construct upper and lower bound estimators and confidence sets for agent quality with statistical guarantees that hold uniformly over agent trajectory distributions and establish instance-wise sharpness of our estimator class. We demonstrate our methods with a case study comparing the success of Claude Opus and Claude Haiku in improving the runtime of an open-source Python package.
