Beyond Linear Decodability: Understanding and Improving Instruction Following in World Action Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (223 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.27401v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: World action models (WAMs) exhibit strong manipulation capabilities yet often fail to follow test-time language instructions. Prior work reveals that non-following (NF) WAMs encode information about the instructed target, but this information does not control their behavior. By comparing NF WAM with its instruction-following (IF) counterpart, we find that target information is substantially more linearly decodable in IF WAMs, despite being encoded in both. Motivated by this observation, we introduce linear decodability supervision (LDS), a simple yet effective auxiliary training objective that promotes the linear decodability of the instructed target information. On LIBERO-CF, InternW0-$\Delta$ with LDS achieves a success rate of 74.6% and a grounding rate of 89.2%, surpassing the strongest baseline by 26.8 and 42.9 percentage points, respectively. The effectiveness of LDS challenges the prevailing interpretation that encoded target information does not contribute to WAMs' behavior. To reconcile this apparent contradiction, we propose a prior-instruction competition mechanism, which explains NF and IF behavior as arising from the dominance of prior and instruction signals, respectively. We provide causal evidence for this mechanism by evaluating two training-free interventions derived from it. We reinterpret LDS through this mechanism, showing that it acts as a form of instruction signal enhancement. Finally, more comprehensive evaluations across diverse manipulation tasks and visual domains demonstrate the consistent effectiveness of LDS.
