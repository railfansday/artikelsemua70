Recoverable Symbols, Unstable Execution: Auditing Internal Pathways and Decision Identity in Visual State Tracking

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (228 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.27715v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Visual features can retain every symbol a task needs while a model built on them still changes its answer when irrelevant parts of the image change. We study this gap in visual state tracking, where a recurrent executor reads a sequence of symbol tiles through a frozen ViT encoder and decides whether a displayed candidate equals the final state. Our diagnostic pairs each input with a donor view that preserves every queried tile, checks with a symbol-supervised decoder that the full symbol chain is recoverable from both feature sequences, and swaps individual internal channels of the same fitted executor in both directions. On 3,526 pairs in which every symbol is recovered in both views, changing the surrounding context lowers the balanced accuracy of a rotary recurrent head by 17.13 points. The loss runs mainly through the input-dependent rotation phase: restoring the original phase repairs 351 of 391 new errors and brings accuracy back to 88.54%, yet 142 individual predictions still differ from the original ones. A locked test on 192 unseen program families reproduces the pathway (28 of 29 new errors repaired; 382 of 384 predictions unchanged), and the effect partly carries over to single-image canvases. Consistency objectives derived from this analysis reduce the internal discrepancy they target but do not outperform ordinary data augmentation.
