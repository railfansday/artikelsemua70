Latest Update: Error analysis for the numerical approximation of the quantile hedging problem

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (203 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.26673v1 Announce Type: newly introduced Abstract: We analyse numerical approximations of quantile hedging in complete linear markets with Markovian diffusion dynamics. The problem is formulated as stochastic control for an augmented state $(X,P)$, where the probability martingale $P$ is constrained to $[0,1]$ and controlled through an unbounded volatility coefficient. We combine control truncation with piecewise-constant policy time-stepping (PCPT), stopping controls continuously upon boundary hitting to preserve the state constraint. Using one-step dynamic programming principles and regularisation, we derive a generator inequality and explicit value-function error bounds. Under standard coefficient regularity and a Lipschitz payoff, optimising the truncation and regularisation parameters yields an error exponent arbitrarily close to $1/8$ from below, uniformly in the probability variable. For controls bounded from the outset, we obtain exponents arbitrarily close to $1/4$ from below, approaching the exponent established by Jakobsen, Picarelli and Reisinger without state constraints. We also implement a fully discrete scheme with neural-network controls. Grid-time projection onto $[0,1]$ introduces a martingale defect and a pricing bias. We quantify this bias in a toy problem and mitigate it using a state-dependent control bound. Experiments in Black--Scholes and Lipschitz local-volatility models illustrate the correction and the decrease in pricing errors under grid refinement.
