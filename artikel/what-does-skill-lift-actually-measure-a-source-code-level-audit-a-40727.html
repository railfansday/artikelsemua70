What Does Skill Lift Actually Measure? A Source-Code-Level Audit and Noise-Aware Reanalysis of SkillsBench

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (273 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.33943v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: Agent skills are directories of instructions, reference materials, and scripts loaded on demand by coding agents. According to SkillsBench, although human-curated skills can raise the passing rate by about 16.6 percentage points (pp) on average, they have actually reduced the results for some tasks. Later research has started to gate or eliminate skills according to the above per-task gains. We have audited the above benchmark at the source-code level and analysed 7,554 public trials to form 1,421 paired "(configuration, task) cells". Source-code audit identified two controlled-verified cases of grader dependence and one operational definition gap; the average lift on these three tasks was 74.6 pp, and the overall average lift for the full set was 18.7 pp. The audit also found that four tasks contained placeholder skill directories, and a certain execution harness additionally added 48 built-in skills in most runs. No task-level reduction achieved statistical significance in the pooled reanalysis with multiple-testing correction. As there were at most three runs per condition for each cell, the observed negative differences are very susceptible to run-sampling noise, and arithmetically, 58% of the cells could not have shown a decrease. Therefore, the raw numbers of negative cells cannot show that skills cause harm. A gating strategy based on the skill-free score seems to improve by 2-3 pp in the logs; however, even after shuffling condition labels to remove any skill effect, it still achieves an increase of 4-7 pp. On the held-out runs not used for fitting and decision-making, there was no statistically significant improvement. The audit ledger and analysis scripts have been published by us.
