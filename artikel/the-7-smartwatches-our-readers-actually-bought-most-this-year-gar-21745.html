In a recent development, our no. 1 most-purchased smartwatch is a screenless fitness tracker with a seven-day battery life and a discreet look that blends in with whatever you wear. It’s an easy wearable to keep on, thanks to its week-long battery life and the fact that it looks more like a bracelet than a health tracker.

The Fitbit Air is comfortable to work out with and sleep with, something I can’t say about many smartwatches that do the same thing (for double the price). Google’s Health app is one of my favorites, with an engaging interface that displays weekly cardio capacity, sleep score, readiness, and other metrics right at the top, where I want them. The Fitbit Air is $100 without any promotion tacked on — a good deal already on a near-perfect product.

Sources indicate that even though the Apple Watch Series 11 is a year old, it’s still an excellent buy. In fact, this year’s Series 12 has the exact same battery life as the Series 11 (24 hours), and most of the upgrades to the Series 12 come through latest Audio Intelligence features and sensor upgrades. The sensor upgrades advance the health-monitoring tech under the hood, but I don’t know if this makes the greatest difference to the average customer who is using their watch to track general workouts and communicate without their phone.

Ditch the latest model, snag a deal on the Series 11, and wear a more-than-capable smartwatch that gets almost all of the same software updates as the Series 12.

This is the final boss of smartwatches. It’s often hailed as one of the best rugged smartwatches on the market, next to only the Garmin Fenix 9 Pro, which launched earlier this year. With built-in satellite communication technology, it’s “the premier sports watch for those who want connectivity everywhere, even if they do not pack a phone,” ZDNET’s Matt Miller writes in his review.

In a recent development, one of my favorite features on the Galaxy Watch 8 is one that runners will love most. The smartwatch offers a Running Coach feature that assesses your running level, creates a training plan, and helps you prep for your first or next race. It also has a sensor that assesses your antioxidant level: all you do is press your thumb on the sensor below the screen. Unlike Google’s bulbous smartwatches that are round and chunky, the Samsung Galaxy Watch 8 is slim and flat on top, so it feels a little bit lighter and less clunky.

According to recent reports, the Garmin Epix Pro 2 is a few years old, but there’s still lots of interest in this multisport smartwatch. It’s got a bright AMOLED display for easy viewing in any environment, and it’s durable, with a scratch-resistant sapphire lens. One review said it may be overkill for casual users, and at $600, I’d agree. This is a great alternative to the Garmin Fenix 8 Pro or 9 Pro, but not the right option as a first smartwatch. Consider an Apple Watch or a cheaper choice first.

Industry observers note that forerunners are the marathon-training smartwatch of choice for novice and experienced runners, and this one is a bit smaller, perfect for those with small wrists. The Garmin Forerunner 165 is a budget-friendly option for first-time runners. It’s got an 11-day battery life, an AMOLED display, and several runner-friendly features and metrics to help people along their jogging journey.

Sources indicate that this smartwatch could last 18 days without a charge. It’s got a durable design alongside Garmin’s signature built-in flashlight. ZDNET’s Matt Miller, who reviewed the watch, said it was a “rugged, chunky watch that can take a beating and keep on going.”.
