Chain-of-Thought Hidden States Reveal Task-Solving Directions for Direct Answers

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (216 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.24598v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting improves the performance of Large Language Models by eliciting intermediate reasoning steps. However, CoT is far from perfect: on some tasks, models perform worse than with direct-answer prompting, and on others they generate a few hundred tokens to reach the correct answer. We start by labelling each CoT hidden state as recovering if patching it into the direct prompt yields the correct answer, and failed otherwise. A linear probe shows that the model encodes these two classes differently. We then show that the representation space of the direct prompt holds a direction for each question that leads the model, in a short generation, to the correct answer more often than CoT does. Specifically, we rotate the direct prompt's last hidden state once, before generation, towards a direction built for each question from its own recovering and failed states. Across 25 tasks, these directions raise the direct prompt's mean accuracy from $46\%$ to $85\%$ on LLaMA 3.1 8B (CoT: $69\%$) and from $52\%$ to $86\%$ on Qwen 2.5 7B (CoT: $77\%$), with a tenth of CoT's tokens. CoT is thus not the shortest route to this accuracy, and these directions can be used in future work to train models to align their states with them.
