Recent findings suggest that as a smart home reviewer, I’m especially fond of robot vacuums. I enjoy unboxing, setting up, and testing these devices, and I love seeing new technologies emerge each year. Even when robot vacuums look similar, each one is actually quite different, which means I’m constantly learning new things about these devices.

In a recent development, we test dozens of robot vacuums each year at ZDNET, both in our lab and at home. As a result, I’m always writing reviews of different models and updating our choices to reflect the best units you can buy.

Also: Best early Black Friday deals.

Our team also compiles data on the top-selling robot vacuums across various retailers each year. The following are listed in order of units sold, based on the links clicked from ZDNET stories thus far in 2026. (A note: Your privacy is protected — we only have access to aggregate data from our user base, and there is no way for us to identify individual people’s purchases.).

The Roborock Saros 10R was ZDNET’s pick for the best robot vacuum and mop of 2025, and the winner of a ZDNET Editors’ Choice award. It features industry-leading 22,000Pa of suction and a slim 3.14-inch-tall design that allows it to navigate under low furniture. It also has Roborock’s VertiBeam Lateral Obstacle Avoidance technology to prevent it from getting entangled in cords and other small obstacles on your floors.

The Ecovacs Deebot X11 OmniCyclone is a powerful robot vacuum with a superb self-emptying mechanism and a unique charging feature. The dock uses a dust canister instead of a replaceable dust bag, so you don’t have to worry about buying replacements every few months. The robot itself has the longest battery life I’ve tested, thanks to GaN charging. Every time the robot goes to the dock to auto-empty or wash its mop, the dock uses fast charging to top off the battery, extending its life.

In a recent development, the Roborock Q7 M5+ is a surprisingly effective budget-friendly robot vacuum. It can handle pet hair in your home with its powerful 10,000Pa of suction for only $200 (for a limited time). This is a budget-friendly robot that doesn’t promise more than it can deliver. As a result, you can rest assured that it will complete the basics of vacuuming and light mopping done well.

The Mova P50 Pro Ultra is the top performer against pet hair out of all the robot vacuums I’ve tried — and I’ll happily die on this hill. It has an anti-tangle CleanChop brush and, while most robots claim to have anti-tangle brushes, this one actually cuts hair as it rolls, so it never tangles. This robot reliably keeps my floors hair-free, even with a medium-length-haired dog who sheds like crazy.

To boot, the Mova P50 Pro Ultra also has a self-washing mop and auto-emptying dustbin for hands-free cleaning.

Sources indicate that the Eufy Omni S1 Pro is one of the best robot vacuum and mop combos I’ve ever tested. As a reviewer, I get a lot of these devices and often don’t have room to keep them — but the S1 Pro is the one I’ve kept the longest in my home. It features precise cleaning and obstacle avoidance, and its mop roller is the best one I’ve seen in a robot of this type.

The successor to the S1 Pro is now available and even more impressive. You can read my experience with the Eufy Omni S2 here.

Sources indicate that one of the top performers among affordable robot vacuums, I wasn’t surprised to see the 3i G10+ on the list of most purchased models. Newly introduced units are no longer available for purchase on Amazon, though you can find it used for about $100. Since the G10+ doesn’t have a self-emptying dustbin, it features a dustbin compression system that lets you go up to 60 days before emptying — completely bag-free.

The mop roller doesn't only mop with clean water, but it also extends along the edge of a wall or furniture to clean. / Maria Diaz/ZDNET.

Sources indicate that the Ecovacs X8 Pro Omni is the recipient of the ZDNET Lab Award for best pickup performance in a robot vacuum, which means it was the most effective at vacuuming debris in our lab tests. With 18,000Pa of suction and effective navigation, this robot cleans every inch of my floors. I love the roller mop on it, which extends to clean along wall edges, and always mops with clean water.

Sources indicate that the Mova Mobius 60 features interchangeable mop pads that automatically swap based on the area or mess the robot is cleaning. If it’s going to clean the kitchen, the Mobius 60 goes back to the dock to “grab” the ThermoHold Mop that is good for oily messes. When it needs to clean hardwood and delicate surfaces, it uses the Plush Mop. The HyperClean Mop is for all areas, comprised of composite fibers.

The RV20 Max is a beginner-friendly robot vacuum for customers who don’t like the bulky base stations taking up space at home. Priced at $199, the RV20 Max works surprisingly well, with 5,300Pa of suction power and navigation that doesn’t rely on bump-and-run. The trade-off is that this robot doesn’t have the best obstacle-avoidance capabilities, so it may get stuck on cables and small items.

Unfortunately, this product is being discontinued, so it’s out of stock at Amazon at this point.

The Eufy C10 is a super-slim robot vacuum that is only 2.85 inches tall, perfect for fitting under low-clearance furniture. Even at this low profile, the C10 features LiDAR navigation and smart mapping. You can also go up to 8 weeks without worrying about replacing the dust bag at the dock. As a robot vacuum only, the Eufy C10 is best for mostly carpeted homes or those where you prefer to do your own mopping.
