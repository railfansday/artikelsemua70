EviCraft: Can Minecraft Agents Find What They Cannot See?

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (245 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.32765v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: In Minecraft, a common testbed for embodied agents, semantic tools such as mineBlock pick targets from the simulator's loaded world state without a visibility test, so a buried ore the agent has never seen is one call away, and scores cannot show whether the agent searched. We formalize the missing requirement as evidence closure: every decision of an agent system, including those inside its tools, may depend on the world only through facts for which the agent holds evidence. EviCraft is a runtime and benchmark that enforces closure below the agent and makes the admission rule an experimental variable: a perception-grounded regime (G) admits only what is in line of sight or returned by the agent's own actions, and a loaded-state regime (L) reproduces standard tool stacks, with agents, tools, and worlds held fixed. On Survival, the regime effect exceeds the strongest agent's lead: under G, three mid-sized agents reach the diamond in 32%-40% of runs, against 76%-94% under L, and the strongest agent under G scores below each of them under L. In two models, removing only the line-of-sight test closes 93%-96% of the score gap, and loading state only inside tools recovers 70%-98%, so safeguards on observations and queries leave most of the leak open. In open-ended acquisition, the gain from L concentrates in items that depend on buried resources or mobs. Code and benchmark are available at https://github.com/AllenReder/EviCraft.
