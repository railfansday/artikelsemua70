I normally don’t preorder or buy games on Day One, but c’mon, it’s GTA VI. I was the first in my friend group to play on launch day; I was like a kid on Christmas morning. But I didn’t want to keep it all to myself, so I made plans to stream the game via Discord for a private watch party with my friends.

Using both the PS Remote Play app and Discord’s built-in screen sharing, I created an imperfect-but-workable solution — and got to have a bonkers good time with my friends despite GTA VI being a single-player experience.

In a recent development, the first few hours of GTA VI are absolutely Florida Man Simulator, and what better way to honor that than to find a jerry-rigged solution for sharing gameplay? The PS Remote Play app lets you stream gameplay to your smart TV or PC from your PS5 console, and then I simply used Discord’s screen-sharing feature to show the app to my friends.

I was most worried that both programs might compress the video feed into a pixelated, bitcrushed mess, but frequent check-ins from the peanut gallery assured me that everything was running smoothly and looking crisp.

Using PS Remote Play did add a significant amount of input lag to my GTA VI gameplay, and while it’s easy enough to compensate for, it does make gunplay and driving slightly frustrating experiences.

Sources indicate that as someone who is more used to real-world driving simulators like American Truck Simulator and Assetto Corsa Competizione, the driving already feels a touch too floaty and slippery, and the lag means you have to plan every turn and stop well in advance to avoid sliding into the environment or slamming into the back of an NPC vehicle and getting the cops called on you. The gunplay lag is much easier to compensate for, especially if you keep your focus buffed and the meter filled to pull off trick shots and down multiple enemies without wasting ammo.

Streaming GTA VI to my friends also helped spark some interesting conversations about where we could see different influences from other Rockstar games, like the Heat system working very similarly to Red Dead Redemption 2’s Wanted mechanic: If you get caught committing a crime but manage to evade the police, that area of the map holds “heat,” so police and NPCs will be more vigilant and quicker to respond to reported crimes.

We also took plenty of time to just poke around Leonida and get a feel for the game world.
