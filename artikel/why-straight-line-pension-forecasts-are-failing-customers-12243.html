Why straight-line pension forecasts are failing customers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Financial institutions are not short of technology. Core systems run accounts, policies and payments, while portals and adviser tools sit at the front.

According to Kidbrooke, the problem comes when firms try to add something fresh, such as a digital pension forecast or an investment advice journey, without destabilising the systems they already depend on, inflating costs or eroding customer trust in the results.

Kidbrooke argues that the financial planning engine behind these experiences is where the real decision lies. It combines customer data, product rules and market assumptions to show what different choices could mean for someone’s finances. The WealthTech firm states three qualities should guide selection: speed, flexibility and model quality.

In a recent development, on speed, Kidbrooke highlights two dimensions: how quickly a service can launch and how fast it responds once live. Because the analytics within its KidbrookeONE platform are already built and running in production, firms can start small and scale. Swedish pension provider Skandia released a digital pension advisory solution in around four months and has since reused the same calculation engine across five customer journeys. In the UAE, HAYAH Insurance rolled out three digital savings and pension planning journeys in weeks.

Recent findings suggest that responsiveness matters just as much. In Skandia’s journeys, KidbrookeONE recalculates 5,000 market scenarios across 720 time steps every time a customer changes an input. Kidbrooke separates fast-moving customer data from slower economic scenarios, which are refreshed through monthly market calibration, allowing detailed modelling without delays.

According to recent reports, regulation is adding urgency. Since 6 April 2026, UK firms with FCA permission have been able to offer ready-made suggestions to groups of customers with shared characteristics under the Targeted Support regime. Kidbrooke notes that building these journeys within existing digital channels is a clear practical advantage.

It has been revealed that flexibility is the second pillar. KidbrookeONE uses a modular, API-first architecture, so a pension provider could plug in Forecasting and Gap Analysis APIs without replacing wider systems. Ruth Asset Management, meanwhile, has used APIs to consolidate market and ESG data from multiple providers and automate manual processes.

Kidbrooke’s Checkup API can flag when a portfolio drifts from its recommendation or a fund leaves the available range, an approach Skandia uses to keep in touch with customers after their first purchase.

Kidbrooke warns that without a shared foundation, fee rules and forecast calculations can be maintained separately across portals, adviser tools and partner platforms, causing answers to drift apart over time.

Sources indicate that the third pillar, model quality, may carry the greatest consequence. A calculator assuming a fixed return draws a comforting smooth line but hides real risk. KidbrookeONE’s economic scenario generator uses Monte Carlo simulation, recalibrated monthly across hundreds of risk factors, to show a range of outcomes and the likelihood of meeting a goal. Kidbrooke stresses this is critical near retirement, where retiring into a downturn can produce a very different result from retiring into a rising market.

For Targeted Support, scenario testing can reveal where a suggestion suits one customer but harms another, such as someone without a cash buffer facing early market falls.
