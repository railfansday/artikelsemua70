GPT‑6 Astra tops Retell’s voice agent benchmark, while Codex helps it reach a $1.6M annual revenue run rate per employee.

Annual revenue run rate per employee.

Industry observers note that retell AI⁠(opens in a new window) is a voice AI platform for contact center operations, helping businesses build agents that can answer questions, take action, and connect a caller with a person when needed. Its platform now serves more than 4,000 businesses, with monthly call volume growing from 30 million in early 2026 to more than 100 million by September.

According to recent reports, as Retell serves more enterprises with complex workflows, it uses OpenAI models to keep call quality and reliability high. Today, OpenAI models generate responses for 80% of calls on Retell, with customers achieving resolution rates above 80%. Retell has also surpassed 1 trillion tokens consumed across OpenAI models, reflecting the scale of that adoption.

Retell’s engineers use Codex throughout development, from researching unfamiliar code to building features and debugging complications, and GPT‑6 Astra to test front-end interfaces.

Recent findings suggest that enterprise voice agents need to follow company policies and stay within their assigned roles, even as conversations change. Retell built its vCX benchmark to compare dozens of models using difficult, multi-turn conversations drawn from real production scenarios.

It tests how well models follow instructions, stay grounded in the information they are given, call the right tools, and use natural conversational language. In Retell’s October 13, 2026 benchmark, GPT‑6 Astra ranked first for instruction following at 89.7%.

Source: bench.retellai.com · October 13, 2026 · All providers · “None/low” reasoning · Fast variants excluded.

It has been revealed that retell also shared evaluations conducted before the launch of the GPT‑6 Astra model that indicated GPT‑5.5 ranking highest among models on both non-hallucination rate (88.0%) and tool-call correctness (88.3%) with reasoning enabled, outperforming Gemini‑3.1‑Pro and Claude‑4.6 on both measures. It also led on non-hallucination rate with reasoning off.

Retell notes new customers who choose OpenAI models continue using them. Customers also have access to OpenAI Realtime models through the Realtime API and OpenAI text-to-speech as a voice engine. Retell makes new OpenAI models available the day they launch and reruns its benchmark as models improve.

Contact center teams use Retell to define their agents’ business context, goals, and rules. Developers can further configure workflows and integrations.

Sources indicate that during a call, Retell’s harness runs the audio models, tracks the conversation, and manages when the agent speaks, listens, or responds to interruptions. OpenAI models, including GPT‑6.1 Sol, GPT‑6 Astra, and GPT‑5.5, use the customer’s instructions and conversation context to generate responses and request tool calls. Retell executes those calls through integrations with the customer’s systems and can write outcomes back to a customer record.

Recent findings suggest that when human judgment is needed, the voice agent briefs the representative with context, answers their follow-up questions, and confirms they are ready before connecting the customer. After a call, Retell uses GPT‑5.6 Terra to analyze the transcript and extract outcomes and other data.

In a recent development, with this approach, Retell reports customer resolution rates above 80%. At electronics organization Anker, agents handle Tier‑1 post-sales support in the US and UK, including order tracking, warranty checks, and troubleshooting. Anker reported an 80.4% case resolution rate and a Net Promoter Score (NPS) of 63. Its team also noted the agents’ tone, pacing, and warmth met the standard they expected of a human agent.

In a recent development, at Pine Park Health, Retell agents handle appointment reminders and proactive scheduling and record the outcomes in patients’ medical charts. The team recovered medical-assistant time equivalent to 2.1 full-time roles and redirected it to patient care. Its scheduling agent achieved a 55.7% booking rate when it reached a patient.

Daytime traffic on Retell’s platform can reach more than 50 times its overnight low. To help keep calls responsive even at peak demand, Retell uses dedicated servers with OpenAI, shared across its customers. In Retell’s October 13, 2026 benchmark, GPT‑6 Astra and GPT‑6 Sol each recorded median first-sentence latency of 883 milliseconds, with GPT‑5.6 Luna ranking highest at 497 milliseconds.

OpenAI’s regional API endpoints allow developers to route traffic to specific geographic regions, which helps Retell reduce network travel time and meet customers’ data residency requirements in industries such as healthcare and financial services.

According to recent reports, retell also builds redundancy into each layer of the service. It runs its own telephony stack with a second stack ready to take over, along with backups for voice and model services, to help keep calls running when a service fails.

In a recent development, retell reaches OpenAI models through Amazon Bedrock, OpenAI’s API, and Azure. Retell built its platform to answer callers quickly, so it routes requests among the three providers based on latency. Bedrock has the lowest latency and currently receives the most traffic.

Sources indicate that as of November 2026, OpenAI models responded through Bedrock in about half the time they took through the other two providers. Retell can route every OpenAI model on its platform through Bedrock, in every agent workflow.

It has been revealed that as Retell’s enterprise growth brings more requests for custom workflows and integrations, its engineers often need to work in unfamiliar parts of the codebase. Before writing code, they give Codex a high-level requirement. Codex surveys the code, checks it against actual data, and explains what needs to change. One engineer stated Codex did this research better and much faster than he could.

Industry observers note that during implementation, engineers say Codex often catches details they had not considered. For debugging, Codex cross-references logs and database records with the code to investigate what is happening. Retell’s engineers also use Codex in their work with customers building on OpenAI models to speed up tasks such as writing the instructions that guide how a model responds in a customer’s application.

To test changes to its web interface, Retell relies on GPT‑6 Astra’s computer use. When an engineer opens a front-end pull request, GPT‑6 Astra opens the interface, clicks through it, tests flows, tries to break them, and reports the issues it finds. Retell notes earlier models could not perform this work reliably.

Retell continues to hire, but its engineering team hasn’t needed to grow at the same pace as the business. Retell reports $1.6 million in annual revenue run rate per employee.

Every business knows how it wants to serve its customers. Retell’s goal is to let the people closest to that work apply their expertise directly, using advanced voice AI and a platform that lets them build, test, and improve their own agents. Next, Retell prepares to expand quality assurance and simulation, support more languages, and develop knowledge base auto-learning.

As OpenAI models continue to advance, Retell expects its customers to deliver world-class service to millions of callers, on their own terms.

“No one knows a organization’s customers better than the company itself. We give them the power to act on that expertise and improve AI agents directly. The goal is for them to build an in-house team of internally deployed engineers who own and run it all,” claims Bing.
