Tech Report: PACE: Policy Augmentation and Compensation With Explicit Disturbance Estimation for Robust Locomotion

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (247 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.27061v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Reinforcement learning has made quadrupedal locomotion remarkably robust. To handle unmodeled dynamics and external disturbances, domain randomization is usually employed to make a policy hedge against every condition it might meet. However, maintaining accurate tracking across varying dynamics and generalizing beyond the sampled range remain challenging. Here we show that explicitly estimating the disturbance and feeding the resulting estimation back into both the policy and the controller can relax this compromise. We introduce Policy Augmentation and Compensation with Explicit disturbance estimation (PACE). The framework lumps payload variations, center-of-mass (CoM) shifts, actuator degradation, and external forces into one joint-space disturbance. A nonlinear observer subsequently recovers this disturbance online without requiring joint acceleration measurements. The disturbance estimation provides feedforward torque compensation as the foundational control pathway and further augments the policy observation to enable disturbance-aware action generation. Considering the learning difficulty of fast-varying contact effects, we further provide an analytical contact estimation approach from joint and inertial measurements, without demanding force sensors. Across eleven simulated dynamics conditions, PACE reduces velocity-tracking error by 18.1% to 57.0% against state-of-the-art baselines, and maintains improved tracking under out-of-distribution (OOD) commands. Notably, in real-world experiments, the deployed real robot carried an 11.5 kg payload, nearly equal to its own mass. It also negotiated unseen 16 cm step-downs and crossed five outdoor terrains. The policy was trained exclusively on flat ground in simulation with at most 3 kg of added mass.
