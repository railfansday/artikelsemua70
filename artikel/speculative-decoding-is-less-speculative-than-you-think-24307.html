Speculative Decoding Is Less Speculative Than You Think

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (173 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.27891v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Speculative decoding accelerates language-model inference by preparing candidate continuations before the target model generates them. Its efficiency depends on how well limited drafting computation anticipates the path the target will actually follow. Anticipating this path involves two sources of uncertainty: the target model's predictions and the randomness of sampling. Yet the former requires a target-model forward pass, while the latter can be fixed before drafting. Building on this observation, we introduce DICE (Draw-Informed Candidate Selection), which pre-samples and synchronizes verification randomness across drafting and verification. By combining these known draws with predictions from an existing drafter, DICE allocates computation toward continuations more likely to be used in the current sampling run. This principle guides node selection throughout draft-tree construction and extends to asynchronous prefetching, where DICE predicts which next-round drafts to prepare. Without additional drafter training or changes to the target sampling distribution, DICE achieves state-of-the-art decoding throughput across diverse settings, with speedups of up to 24.6pp over strong baselines.
