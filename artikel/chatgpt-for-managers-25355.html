Prepare for conversations and manage team work more effectively with ChatGPT.

People management is a series of high-stakes moments: 1:1s, feedback, hiring decisions, performance cycles, team updates, and hard conversations. Much of the work is preparation and follow-through—capturing what you heard, deciding what to do next, and communicating clearly.

ChatGPT can help with the time-consuming, repetitive parts such as organizing notes, drafting first-pass messages, and creating reusable templates for recurring tasks like 1:1 agendas, interview kits, onboarding plans, and performance documentation.

It doesn’t replace your judgment or responsibility to follow HR or legal policy, but it helps you get past the blank page and move faster.

Prepare for conversations without overthinking them. You know what needs to be addressed, but planning how to approach the conversation takes time—how to be direct, which examples to use, and what success looks like. ChatGPT can turn your notes into a simple initiative with talking points, questions to ask, and a clear next step so you can walk in prepared instead of improvising.

Make your writing clearer, more consistent, and easier to act on. A lot of manager writing is repetitive and sensitive such as feedback, expectations, role clarity, performance notes, hiring feedback, and team updates. ChatGPT can draft a first version in a neutral tone with specifics and structure, so you spend your time reviewing for accuracy and fairness.

Keep “manager basics” repeatable across the year. Manager tasks repeat throughout the year, including 1:1s, goal setting, onboarding, and performance cycles. ChatGPT helps standardize templates and checklists so nothing falls through the cracks and your team has a consistent experience.

ChatGPT is most effective when you bring in real context such as notes from 1:1s, project updates, survey themes, or role expectations.

Industry observers note that with concrete input, it can help structure thinking, clarify decisions, and produce stronger outputs. Over time, it reduces the overhead of managerial work so you can focus more on coaching, judgment, and decision-making.

Projects: Keep multi-step work organized over time.

Skills: Standardize work you do repeatedly.

Data analysis: Spot patterns, surface risks early, and turn spreadsheets or raw data into decisions.

Image generation: Create and refine visual content to make materials more engaging.

To evaluate the impact of ChatGPT in management workflows, look at both efficiency and effectiveness.

Useful signals include time saved on recurring tasks like preparing 1:1 agendas, summarizing updates, writing feedback, and consolidating planning materials, as well as improved consistency across communications.

You can also look at downstream team outcomes, such as more regular coaching, clearer goals, better-prepared reviews, faster onboarding, and stronger follow-through.

For leaders, the most meaningful measure is whether managers are spending less time on administrative work and more time on high-value activities like coaching, strategy, hiring, and team development.

In a recent development, create an agenda for a 45-minute team meeting on [topic]. Include the goal, pre-read, timeboxes, the decisions we need to make, and how we will capture owners and due dates. Add 3 prompts to keep the discussion focused.

Draft a team update about [topic/change]. Include what is changing, why it is changing, what is not changing, what I need from the team, and where people can ask questions. Keep it straightforward and avoid corporate buzzwords.

Clarify expectations for a role.

Help me write a role expectations summary for [role] on my team. Include what “good” looks like in 3 to 5 responsibilities, how we measure it, and the working norms that matter most. Make it concrete and easy to refer back to.

Follow-up note after a sensitive discussion.

Draft a follow-up message to [person] after a conversation about [topic]. Summarize what we agreed on, what will change, who owns which actions, and when we will check in again. Keep it short, clear, and non-accusatory.

Conversation plan for a tough topic.

I need to have a difficult conversation with [person] about [topic]. Using these facts: [paste], create a short blueprint: opening, key points, questions to ask, how to respond if they disagree, and the specific outcome I should aim for. Keep it direct and respectful.

Help me write feedback to [person] about [situation]. Here are the facts and examples: [paste]. Draft a message that (1) states the impact, (2) references specific examples, (3) sets the expectation going forward, and (4) ends with an invitation to discuss. Keep the tone neutral and avoid loaded language.

I want to coach [person] on improving [skill]. Based on their current role and these examples: [paste], propose a 4-week coaching plan with weekly focus areas, practice exercises, and what evidence would show improvement. Ask me for any missing context instead of guessing.

Help me draft a one-page set of team working norms for [team]. Include communication expectations, meeting rules, response times, escalation paths, and how we handle disagreements. Keep it short and written like something a team would reference, not a policy document.
