Sources indicate that for the manufacturer, generative AI is more than a productivity layer: it is a fresh computing interface. That commitment takes shape across three areas—transforming the business, creating new customer interfaces, and building AI-native products.

When generative AI arrived, Localiza took a view that defined everything that followed: this was more than another piece of software in the toolkit; it was a change in the way the company operates.

Industry observers note that rather than pursuing isolated use cases, the manufacturer organized its collaboration with OpenAI around three strategic pillars: transforming the business through experimentation at scale, creating latest customer interfaces, and building AI-native products. What connects all three is the decision to redesign processes around intelligence, context, and speed.

The principle guiding internal adoption is straightforward: scale enables learning, and learning creates better products.

Recent findings suggest that in practice, that meant distributing ChatGPT licenses as a resource for experimentation and productivity, rather than simply as an individual benefit. Teams across Localiza, from finance to day-to-day operations, maintain active experiments with custom GPTs, agents, and Codex.

Industry observers note that codex first arrived in the technology department—software engineers, product managers, designers, and data scientists. But adoption did not stop there: analysts, managers, PMOs, lawyers, and other business specialists began building their own prototypes and automations to test hypotheses within their areas of expertise.

This was an effect the organization had not anticipated—a redefinition of the stages before development, with multidisciplinary teams prototyping solutions before they enter a development queue.

The second area starts with an observation about behavior: people have begun describing what they need in natural language, creating a fresh entry point for mobility services—at the very moment they express their intent.

Localiza’s internal data confirms this shift: traffic originating from LLMs has been growing rapidly since 2024, with ChatGPT emerging as the main channel.

The firm chose to establish a presence in this space early. In March 2026, it became Brazil’s first mobility platform—and one of the first in Latin America—to offer an app integrated directly into ChatGPT. Customers can explore rental, subscription, and used-car options and begin their journey in the conversation, before completing the transaction in Localiza’s secure environment.

Internally, Localiza’s app in ChatGPT is treated as what it is: an experiment designed to keep the company close to the frontier of customer behavior, accelerate learning from real interactions, and explore how AI complements the existing ecosystem.

The manufacturer’s clearest example of an AI-native product is Liza, a conversational assistant that has become an established success story in its operations.

The difference from a traditional chatbot lies in the architecture, not the interface. Rather than following a fixed decision tree, Liza was built around conversation, intent, and context: it combines OpenAI models, Localiza services, and specialized tools to support key parts of the experience, from understanding the initial request to assisting with key stages of the rental journey.

The ambition is to handle repetitive journeys with quality and free teams for complex, higher-value interactions.

But success is not measured by the volume of automation.

Localiza was already mapping and measuring its customer service journeys before it had access to this type of technology—and that foundation made it possible to decide clearly where an agent should step in and where a human needs to intervene.

Industry observers note that liza also serves as a continuous learning loop: aggregated, monitored signals from interactions help identify needs, points of friction, and service opportunities. Combined with voice capabilities—voice-to-text and text-to-voice—these signals support its evolution from a transactional chatbot into an AI-native customer interface that can learn and scale across different mobility journeys.

The partnership with OpenAI began early: the first version of Liza still ran on GPT‑3.5.
