Industry News: Trust and Lottery-Related Anomalies

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (193 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.27020v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: We examine whether social trust shapes the pricing of lottery-like stocks by influencing investors' beliefs regarding optimistic information associated with extreme future payoffs. We argue that higher social trust reduces investors' skepticism toward optimistic information associated with extreme future payoffs, increasing demand for lottery-type stocks and leading to overpricing and lower subsequent returns. Using a sample of 8,875 firms across 49 US states over the period 1996-2018 and MAX as a proxy for lottery-like characteristics, we show that the MAX effect is concentrated in regions with relatively high trust and is weak or absent in regions with relatively low trust. We further find that this trust conditioning is stronger in regions with lower education or income levels, consistent with social trust playing a relatively more notable role when investors have more limited access to private information. The results are robust to alternative lottery proxies and a range of robustness checks. Our findings identify social trust as a novel determinant of the pricing of lottery-like stocks and provide evidence that social capital may amplify belief-driven mispricing in assets with highly skewed payoffs.
