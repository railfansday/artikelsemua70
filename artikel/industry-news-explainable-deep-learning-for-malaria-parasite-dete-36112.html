Industry News: Explainable Deep Learning for Malaria Parasite Detection and Species Differentiation in Thick Blood Smear Images: A Pilot Feasibility Study

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (246 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.31855v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Species-level differentiation of Plasmodium falciparum (PF) and P. vivax (PV) determines treatment, yet deep learning detectors rarely test whether their explanations are reliable. In this pilot feasibility study of TRACE-Mal, a research prototype combining YOLOv8n-seg detection and a six-model soft-voting ensemble, we examined whether agreement among three post-hoc explanation methods (Grad-CAM, LIME and SHAP) is associated with the correctness of species calls. the study analysed 2,847 parasite crops from 250 thick smears within publicly available Lacuna Malaria Datasets. The model achieved an mAP50 score of 0.79 and the ensemble had a macro F1 of 0.84 (PF recall 0.86; PV recall 0.74). Unadjusted analyses of crops revealed that predictions explained by greather than or equal to 2 methods were more accurate (86.2% vs 78.9%; difference 7.3 percentage points; 95% CI 3.5-11.1) compared to those explained by less than 2 methods. As the number of explanatory methods increased so did prediction accuracy (from 73.8% for no methods to 89.5% for all three methods). When performing a cluster-bootstrap analysis of 60 test images, again there was a positive consensus difference (7.3 percentage points; 95% CI 2.9-11.8). Further, incorporating explanation information into a model's prediction confidence enhanced its error differentiation ability (AUROC 0.024; 95% CI 0.008-0.041). PF and PV parasite crops have distinct morphologies. Although based on one unvalidated public data source, these findings demonstrate proof-of-concept and should not be taken as a measure of diagnostic capability.
