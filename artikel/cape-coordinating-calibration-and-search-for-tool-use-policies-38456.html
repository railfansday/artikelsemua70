CAPE: Coordinating Calibration and Search for Tool-Use Policies

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (232 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.31938v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A tool call can fail even when the operation behind it is correct, because the receiving tool parses delimiters, dates or record layouts under a configuration the agent cannot inspect. Compiling a tool-use policy for such a tool requires both calibration, which evaluates candidate calls on candidate configurations, and search over the evaluated calls. The two share one compilation budget: calibrating everything first postpones any improved policy until the end, while re-solving every partially known model delays the next useful query. A call becomes usable as soon as its effects are known on the configurations that reach it. We present CAPE, a training-free compiler built on this observation. It maintains an executable policy at all times, directs calibration to the calls that can improve this policy, and tightens bounds only where doing so ends calibration early. Returned policies preserve every configuration the initial policy completes, and CAPE certifies optimality when its executable and optimistic values meet. On CSV and JSON workflows built from 64 requests, CAPE reaches the independently computed optimum in 502 of 504 compilations while taking 22.0% less compilation time than eager exact search. On larger CSV families it returns policies with higher completion at similar compilation time, and it raises completion by 15.38 to 34.66 percentage points over an LLM that revises its serialization from error feedback.
