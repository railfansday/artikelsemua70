Instagram’s latest tool for creators targets unauthorized reposts and AI-modified videos

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Instagram is introducing a latest tool to help creators protect their original videos, the Meta-owned firm announced Tuesday. The feature, called “content protection,” is already available on Facebook and is designed to detect when creators’ original videos are being used without their permission. The company is also expanding the tool to address AI content, not just conventional reposting.

The announcement comes in response to creators’ concerns over how easily original content can be copied and redistributed across social media platforms, especially considering how generative AI has made it easier to manipulate existing videos.

Recent findings suggest that instagram states the content protection tool continuously scans Instagram for unauthorized uses of creators’ protected videos. When Instagram detects a match for one of a creator’s Reels uploaded by another user, it sends the creator an alert. Creators can then choose to block the video from being viewed on both Instagram and Facebook to hide the matched content. They can also choose to add an “original by” label on the content that links viewers of the matching video back to their original video.

Additionally, creators can choose to keep the match visible while monitoring its performance or release their claim on the reel, allowing it to remain visible on Meta’s platforms.

The tool will also begin detecting AI-generated videos that reuse audio from creators’ original content, even when the visuals have been altered. This revision will initially be tested with a small group of creators in the coming weeks. The manufacturer plans to expand access to this feature to creators who enroll in content protection on both Instagram and Facebook.

Instagram says it’s exploring ways to limit the reach of accounts and content that repeatedly use AI tools to repost others’ work.

Creators who are eligible for content protection will see a notification in their activity feed. Once they enroll, every original video they post to Instagram going forward is automatically protected. They can also choose to retroactively protect eligible videos they’ve already posted by manually selecting them.

If creators have permitted certain profiles to use their work, such as their second profile or a brand, they can add them to their allow list so their videos won’t be flagged as matches.

Tuesday’s announcement marks another step in Instagram’s ongoing efforts to give creators greater control over their content and protect the originality of their work.
