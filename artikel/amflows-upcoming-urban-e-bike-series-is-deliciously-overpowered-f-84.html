After shaking up the mountain bike world, DJI spinoff Amflow is hitting city streets with the UE Carbon and UE Carbon Pro — bringing 125Nm of torque, 82 miles of range, and Find My tracking — starting at $3,500.

DJI spinoff Amflow has spent the last couple of years shaking up the electric mountain bike (eMTB) market with ridiculously high-torque, lightweight models. Today, it’s bringing that same overengineered energy to pavement with the UE Series, its first urban commuter line.

It has been revealed that the series consists of two models: the UE Carbon and UE Carbon Pro. Both pack 125Nm of torque, 1,100W of peak power, and an 8x max assist ratio capable of climbing 30 percent grades — all while operating quietly under 45dBA (quieter than a household refrigerator). A swappable 600Wh battery provides up to 132km (about 82 miles) of range and fast-charges from zero to 100 percent in 2 hours and 23 minutes.

Recent findings suggest that while most city e-bikes deliver their power modestly, Amflow is strapping a mountain-bike-grade motor onto a 20.9 kg (46 pounds) upright step-through frame — and pricing it slightly below its flagship trail bikes. The UE Carbon starts at €3,099 (about $3,500 USD), while the Pro will sell for €3,499 ($3,900 USD).

But this isn’t just another commuter bike. With its proprietary Avinox drivetrain, Amflow has been ahead of the curve in blending consumer gadget UX — features like wireless phone charging, Apple Watch proximity unlock, Find My integration, and camera controls — with serious hardware specs. That’s one of the reasons why The Verge’s own Thomas Ricker has been so bullish on the brand for the past few years.

It has been revealed that with 125Nm of max torque and 1,100W of peak power, the Avinox M2 in the UE Series is slightly more dialed back from Amflow’s 150Nm flagship eMTBs. That noted, 125Nm is still massive overkill for flat city streets — but it won’t hurt to have it when encountering any grades over 30 percent. A new Auto Boost mode automatically dumps extra power when starting at a traffic light or hitting a steep hill. And traction control will help prevent any spin-outs on wet asphalt.

It has been revealed that the 600Wh removable battery is good for up 82 miles in Eco mode — and likely less than that while relying on higher levels of assist. It’s worth noting that the 1,100W peak power and 8x assist ratios will likely be software-capped depending on where you live and what regulations allow.

Recent findings suggest that as a brand within the larger DJI ecosystem, Amflow loves to integrate a lot of tech into its bikes. A 1.4 inch touchscreen is integrated into the handlebars alongside a 15W wireless phone charger — plus a 20W USB-C charger, if you prefer. The cockpit also features plenty of Apple tie-ins, like the aforementioned Find My tracker and Apple Watch proximity unlocking. The UE can also pair with a heart rate monitor, allowing riders to set a target heart rate zone that will dictate how much pedal assist the bike provides. And, of course, there’s direct recording controls for DJI Osmo cameras, in case you like to film your rides.

In a recent development, additional security is provided by a built-in 10mm hardened steel wheel lock keyed to match the removable battery. And if the bike is paired with “Lost Mode” in the Amflow app, the user can remotely brick the drive system if the bike ever gets stolen.

There’s plenty of commuter-friendly touches, like a 65mm suspension fork, suspension dropper seatpost, and heavy-duty racks rated for 20kg (44 pounds) on the front and 27kg (60 pounds) on the rear.

The bikes are on sale globally starting today via Amflow’s website and retail partners. The organization states a smaller XS frame size is coming in 2027 for shorter riders.
