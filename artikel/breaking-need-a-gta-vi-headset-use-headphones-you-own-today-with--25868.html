Industry observers note that you don’t need to buy a gaming headset to play GTA VI on your PS5 or Xbox Series X|S. You can use headphones you already own by connecting them to your controller with a compatible audio cable and adjusting a few settings.

It’s been a long 13-year wait for GTA VI, but with the holiday gift-buying season so close, you may be strapped for cash to buy a gaming headset, especially after spending $80 to $100 on the game. The good thing is that GTA VI is strictly single-player right now, so you don’t need a headset with a mic to enjoy it. You can also easily turn a pair of headphones you already have into a makeshift gaming headset.

Sources indicate that however, since your consumer headphones aren’t primed for console gaming, you’ll need to tweak some audio settings. Here’s what you need to know.

Whether playing on a PS5 or an Xbox Series X|S, you’ll need to wire your headphones to your controller. Neither console supports standard Bluetooth audio.

Your everyday consumer headphones have a different sound profile than gaming headphones.

Sources indicate that gaming headphones tend to offer clearer directional cues, so you can hear an enemy’s footsteps approaching. Consumer headphones are generally more bass-heavy, which can mask more intricate sounds of faraway dialogue, footsteps, or ambient sounds.

With a bass-boosted EQ, the low end of explosions, gunshots, and crashes may be too intense, and less prominent highs could make it harder to hear vital cues like car doors full of a vengeful gang shutting, an NPC asking for a favor in the distance, or a scorned Leonidan whispering your location.

If you’re adamant about going wireless with your everyday headphones, you might try to use a Bluetooth dongle or a workaround with your TV. If you must use a dongle, it should support the same codecs as your headphones. If there’s a mismatch, the devices may default to the lower-quality SBC codec, potentially delivering poorer sound and higher latency.

Sources indicate that if you connect your console to your TV and wirelessly connect your headphones to your TV, you may get a significant lag between your in-game actions and the audio in your headphones. I don’t recommend this either.

It has been revealed that I also don’t recommend using high-impedance studio headphones, as your controller may not have enough power to drive them adequately. Finally, I don’t recommend using your headphones’ built-in spatial audio rendering at all, especially when it’s on top of your console’s rendering. Double spatial audio rendering can make your audio sound strange, and you may lose the directional cues spatial audio is supposed to enhance.

If you want spatial audio, configure these settings in your console’s audio settings.

Using your everyday headphones with your gaming console isn’t a long-term solution, but it can work in a pinch, especially if you want to play late into the night without disturbing your housemates. With some tweaking to your headphones’ and console’s audio settings, you can make it work, but before GTA VI Online comes out, put a solid pair of gaming headphones on your holiday wish list.
