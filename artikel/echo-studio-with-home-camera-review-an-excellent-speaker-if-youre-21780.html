Echo Studio with Home Camera review: An excellent speaker – if you’re OK with being tracked

The Echo Studio, Amazon’s flagship smart speaker, just got an upgrade that includes a Home Camera. It’s not just for watching what’s going on in your house while you’re away; the camera also tracks your location to adjust audio volume depending on where you are. Is this a privacy nightmare or the speaker of your dreams? The answer is not quite so simple.

Also: Apple HomeView review: The deep ecosystem integration wins again.

Recent findings suggest that I tested the latest Echo Studio with Home Camera to see how it performs in my smart home. I was (and still am) skeptical of the camera, but after a few days, I grew increasingly pleased with the audio quality and tracking features the more I used it and compared it with other speakers. Ultimately, it’s these things that make the speaker stand out for me.

The Echo Studio supports Wi-Fi 6E and Bluetooth 5.3, and serves as a smart home hub for Alexa with Zigbee, Matter, and Thread compatibility, and can also serve as an Eero extender. If you have a home theater setup or a Fire TV, the Echo Studio can also be integrated into your existing system, syncing up to five Echo Studio or Echo Dot Max speakers — all of one type; you can’t mix and match.

Personally, I don’t keep indoor security cameras except for one to watch my dog while I’m out, and that’s only on when no one else is at home. I’m the type to cover all the cameras on my Echo Show devices and laptops, because I don’t like the idea of having a connected device that can record in the privacy of my home, the only place where my kids and family are safe from being unwillingly displayed online.

Because of this, I didn’t quite see the need for an Echo Studio with a Home Camera. However, I admit there are plenty of uses for it, and many smart homes across the US and around the world already have indoor security cameras, so there’s a definite market for this speaker.

The Echo Studio lets you “drop in” to the camera whenever you want to see what’s happening in the room it’s in (and when the camera is on, of course). This is a useful feature if you’re away from home and want to check in on your empty house.

But it’s not a full-fledged security camera. You can’t remotely move the Echo Studio to adjust the angle, for example. So you won’t be able to face it toward the wall for privacy, as you can with a pan/tilt indoor security camera. If you do decide to have your camera face away, you’d have to move the entire Echo Studio speaker, which would lose much of the speaker’s front-firing audio depth.

Industry observers note that the Echo Studio has a button to enable or disable the microphone and camera simultaneously, but you can’t turn one off or on without the other. In other words, you can’t just record audio or just video. The camera also has a dedicated status LED so you can see when it’s running, even if it’s not recording.

The Echo Studio is an excellent speaker for medium and large rooms, as well as open spaces in the home. The Home Camera is meant to complete an immersive sound environment that tracks movement and adjusts volume to match your position in the room. The feature that matches the volume to your location as you move proved to be perfect for listening to music and audiobooks while I cleaned. I moved around picking up clutter, dusting, and vacuuming to test it, and the sound tracking was pretty satisfying.

Industry observers note that the sound from the Echo Studio is rich, full, and crisp. It keeps the vocals clear and the bass deep without rattling, and I’d say it’s comparable to the audio from the Sonos Era 100, but with stronger bass.

According to recent reports, music is one thing, but listening to audiobooks and podcasts on a speaker while you move around can make you miss parts when you get too far along. The Echo Studio with Home Camera solved this problem.

Recent findings suggest that you can also use the camera to leverage Alexa+’s visual intelligence features by showing it something and asking Alexa to tell you more about it. I wasn’t very successful with this feature, but it might be because the lighting in my office is atrocious (Amazon does say you need good lighting to do this).

The Echo Studio with Home Camera is perfect for consumers who appreciate a powerful speaker that doubles as a smart home hub, especially if you have an Eero system or a Fire TV.

When it comes down to it, Amazon built a very capable speaker for Alexa-centric homes in the Echo Studio, and added what could be a leg up with the Home Camera — if you’re into that sort of thing. The camera can be a fun feature for audio tracking and to check in on your pets while you’re out, but there are caveats.
