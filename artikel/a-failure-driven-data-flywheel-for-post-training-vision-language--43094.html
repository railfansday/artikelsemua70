A Failure-Driven Data Flywheel for Post-Training Vision-Language-Action Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (281 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.34698v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models rarely satisfy all behavioral requirements of a new platform, and teams without a large deployed fleet lack the data to address the resulting failures. We propose a failure-driven data flywheel for post-training such models. It constructs target scenarios absent from the available data by inserting task-relevant entities into reconstructed real-world scenes, and evaluates the model against each behavioral requirement of the target task. Failure rates form a failure map across task conditions and difficulty levels, which is re-evaluated after each round and assigns larger data generation quotas to conditions where the model still fails. We instantiate the framework in autonomous driving for occluded-pedestrian emergence and yielding at stop-sign-controlled intersections, and evaluate it on two VLA models and one non-VLA planner in simulation and on a real vehicle. For occluded-pedestrian emergence, five rounds of post-training improve the critical-phase collision-free rate on the sealed test set from 0.261 to 0.579 for AutoVLA and from 0.390 to 0.765 for ReCogDrive, while the unnecessary braking rate in paired control scenarios never exceeds 0.013. On a full-scale vehicle without real-world training, post-trained ReCogDrive achieves 4 of 5 safe passages, versus 1 of 5 for the base model. Fine-grained evaluation reveals a pattern obscured by the collision-free rate alone: both VLA models learn anticipatory deceleration before reaching the occluding vehicle, while braking after pedestrian emergence remains limited. For stop-sign yielding, the stop-before-line rate rises from 0.100 to 0.897 for AutoVLA and from 0.000 to 0.667 for ReCogDrive. With sequential training from the pedestrian-capability models, it reaches 0.599 and 0.358, respectively, while the pedestrian collision-free rate decreases by at most 0.032.
