Open models like Gemma 4 from Google DeepMind are a great option to keep your data local. But how can a local AI model integrate with on-premises data?

Industry observers note that alloyDB Omni, Google’s 100% PostgreSQL-compatible database that runs anywhere, now integrates with locally hosted large language models (LLMs). With AlloyDB Omni, you can build agentic experiences on your own infrastructure and on your own terms. Whether it’s in the cloud, in your datacenter, or even on your laptop, you can have data plus AI anywhere.

In a recent development, alloyDB Omni is a downloadable, run-anywhere edition of AlloyDB that easily deploys to your own infrastructure, including on-premises. AlloyDB Omni packs powerful, built-in AI, including the same ScaNN indexing technology powering Google Search. When you pair AlloyDB Omni with a model, you can store vector embeddings at scale; search them semantically; and even query the database using natural language. You can use virtually any model you like, whether that’s a frontier Cloud-hosted model like Gemini or a local model. And with locally hosted models you maintain complete control over your data environment.

Recent findings suggest that you can now connect AlloyDB Omni to local models like Gemma 4 and Qwen3.8. No matter which model you choose, the architecture remains the same.

AlloyDB Omni calls a local model server directly from SQL — keeping prompts, data, and responses inside your environment.

According to recent reports, this setup enables entirely upcoming use cases for environments that were previously blocked from adopting agentic capabilities. If you operate an air-gapped network or have data locality requirements, you can still build intelligent applications. You can analyze local logs, generate summaries of proprietary internal documents, and power localized chatbots—all while maintaining strict data governance.

Keep data on-premises: Because the database and the model sit in the same local environment, your data never crosses the public internet.

Reduce latency: Processing inference requests locally minimizes network transit time.
