Tech Report: From Shared Patterns to Individual Evidence: Two-Stage Reasoning for Emerging Entities in Temporal Knowledge Graphs

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (237 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.34708v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Temporal knowledge graphs (TKGs) continually introduce emerging entities whose interaction histories evolve from zero-shot debuts into sparse, bursty few-shot trajectories. However, current paradigms are fragmented: pattern-transfer methods lack catalog-wide event memory, whereas online memory models suffer from endpoint-role asymmetry that degrades unseen-answer ranking. Furthermore, sparse early interactions exhibit both long-range dispersion and local redundancy under skewed distribution. To address these challenges, we propose INSPIRE (Integrating Shared Patterns and Individual Evidence for Reasoning), a two-stage forecasting framework over a known catalog. In Stage I, INSPIRE learns an availability-aware shared-pattern predictor that fuses event-updated memory across the full candidate catalog while applying coordinated query- and catalog-side masking. Subsequently, Stage II freezes this predictor and optimizes a lightweight individual-evidence adapter (EA) combining textual semantic priors with correlation-weighted long-range event statistics under support- and role-balanced co-answer supervision with prior-odds debiasing and soft residual alignment. In addition, we decompose evaluation by endpoint roles and track longitudinal emerging-answer cohorts via query-micro and answer-entity macro metrics. Across four benchmarks based on the ICEWS and GDELT event datasets, INSPIRE improves Emerging MRR by 16.73%-88.83% over the strongest baselines. Relative to paired Stage I predictors, EA further increases Emerging MRR by 8.28%-32.63%, Answer-new MRR by 7.81%-41.24%, and cohort macro MRR by 9.64%-39.94%, gains attributed to semantic priors and candidate-level score competition suppressing mismatched established competitors without zero-support prior shortcuts. The official code is publicly available at https://github.com/KcAcoZhang/INSPIRE.
