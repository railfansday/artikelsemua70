In a recent development, I’ve covered a lot of power banks, from ones that are thinner than your phone to huge ones that can power laptops. But I can say — hand on heart — that I’ve never seen a power bank that can connect to a decentralized communication network.

Also: Stop putting your phone in rice – I can confirm this Dryout bag works better.

Sources indicate that yes, that’s right. When push comes to shove, and the lights go out or there’s no cell tower for miles, this power bank can take over from your phone if the network goes down and keep you connected. This is the Heltec Automation 5,000mAh power bank.

Looking at this from the perspective of a power bank, it’s a 5,000mAh unit with a 15W Qi2 wireless charging pad on the front and an input/output USB-C port on the bottom delivering up to 20W.

And yes, all the ports do what it notes on the box they’ll do. It measures 3.9 x 2.9 x 0.4 inches and weighs a hair under 5 ounces. There are three buttons on the power bank; one is the usual power button (marked USER on this unit), and the other two are to do with the communication feature built into the unit.

Also, on the side is a magnetic pogo-pin connector that I initially thought was the charging port, but it’s thankfully only needed to flash fresh firmware onto the device.

There’s also a very neat 2.13-inch E Ink display that’s perfect because not only is it totally readable under the brightest sunlight, but it also uses very little power, drawing on the battery only when the screen updates.

The E Ink display is used in combination with the buttons marked USR and RST to control the unit.

Looking at this as a power bank, it’s a small but decent unit, but if it is just portability you’re after, you’ll have no problems finding a smaller, lighter device. It’s not exactly the thinnest around, but that’s because other power banks don’t have built-in comms.

OK, time to talk about the comms. This is no ordinary walkie-talkie or cell phone in disguise. It uses a system called Meshtastic, which is like combining a walkie-talkie with SMS.

The idea is that you use Bluetooth to pair the unit with a smartphone (some Meshtastic devices have a built-in keyboard, so you don’t need to do this, but this one needs a smartphone), and you use the Meshtastic app to send short text messages to other consumers.

It has been revealed that the radio uses LoRa, a long-range, low-power technology designed to transmit small amounts of data over long distances. And that data is text only. No voice, no photos, no internet access. And by short, I’m talking about a couple of hundred characters.

It has been revealed that how far? As with most things, it depends, and you’ll come across some absolutely outrageous claims online. Yes, the ground-to-ground record is 205 miles, but a typical user is going to get anywhere from a few hundred yards to up to about a mile in a town or among trees, or about 3 to 6 miles over open ground.

But Meshtastic has a trick up its sleeve, and the clue is in the name: mesh. Each Meshtastic device is its own node, and most nodes help pass on — or relay — messages by default, boosting the range of an individual radio. So, the effectiveness of Meshtastic relies on having plenty of owners with devices, as well as others putting up permanent router nodes on rooftops and hilltops to help to boost Meshtastic transmission ranges for everyone.

I’m not going to bog you down with all the details, but if you want to dive down that rabbit hole, the Meshtastic website is the best place to start. But let me warn you, it’s a deep one!

A magnetic power bank is the perfect form factor for a Meshtastic device because it needs power, and, as I said earlier, you need a smartphone to create your messages and send them via Bluetooth to the Meshtastic radio for beaming out into the world.

So, it’s always attached to your phone and ready. And if you’re worried about the wireless charging pad draining the battery, a double-press of the CTRL button turns that off. Battery life when running the Meshtastic radio is excellent, easily good for a few days between recharges.

Not everyone needs a Meshtastic power bank (heck, most people won’t even know what Meshtastic is, or that this technology exists). But I see this catching the eye of two types of people — outdoor enthusiasts looking for a decentralized comms system that doesn’t rely on any infrastructure outside their control, and those who want a radio-based hobby but don’t want to mess about with the FCC or get any licenses.

And for $69, this Heltec Automation Meshtastic power bank a cheap way to get into the Meshtastic world — you might get hooked, and before you know it, you’ll be putting up solar-powered relay nodes and telling everyone they need to get into LoRa radio too.
