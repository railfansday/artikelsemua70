GENSTRAT: Toward a Science of Strategic Reasoning in Large Language Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (247 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2605.23238v3 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Fixed game suites for evaluating strategic reasoning in large language models (LLMs) saturate and are hard to keep free of contamination. We introduce GENSTRAT, a system for evaluating the strategic capabilities of LLMs driven by procedural generation of two-player zero-sum imperfect-information betting games, allowing an evaluator to draw novel, fresh games on demand. Each game is characterized on a scale of five complexity axes (state space, temporal contingency, opponent dependence, private information, precision) and measured by Monte Carlo simulation. Using this generator, we construct a 50-game benchmark and hold a tournament of twelve frontier and open-weight models, gathering data from over 40,000 LLM vs. LLM matches. We further construct a composite complexity axis, and find that, on the eight hardest games, the gap between the best and worst model is 1.7x its value across the whole set of games (1.6x with held-out weights or with hard games chosen by the outcome-free temporal axis alone, and 1.35x with payoffs scaled by their random-play standard deviation). Ablations show that a higher thinking budget, disclosing the opponent's strategy, and higher reasoning matched to a game's specific demands (for private information and planning depth) were associated with higher margins for the LLM player. On 27 solved games, frontier models play at the value of the equilibrium while weaker models lose to it, and the leaderboard predicts strength on fresh games and on six new games, even after controlling for a general-capability leaderboard.
