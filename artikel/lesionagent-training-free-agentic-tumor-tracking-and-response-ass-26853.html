LesionAgent: Training-Free Agentic Tumor Tracking and Response Assessment in Longitudinal CT

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (273 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.27876v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Every few months, a cancer patient's CT scans decide whether treatment continues. 3D foundation models can now detect, segment, register and track lesions, yet none of them reads the scans as a radiologist does. A radiologist decides which findings are lesions, which are the same lesion months later and whether a new spot means progression, and checks each decision against the extensive rules of a clinical guideline. We introduce LesionAgent, an LLM agent that performs this read end to end without task-specific training. It operates frozen 3D models as tools, acts in 3D through coordinate-labelled views, remembers every lesion across visits in a registry, verifies every model output it relies on, and leaves every number to a deterministic guideline engine. On four public longitudinal CT datasets, LesionAgent tracks more lesions and measures them more accurately than every automatic baseline, and where the same 3D models chained by fixed rules call progression falsely at 33 of 56 follow-ups, it does so twice. On clinical follow-up CTs, it agrees with the radiologists' response at 20 of 23 follow-ups, including every change in response, and without training on DeepLesion, it retrieves more of its lesions than every tracker trained on it (89.0% vs. 87.4%). Its harness, not its LLM, makes the difference: given the same LLM and 3D models but not the harness, Claude Code reaches a detection F1 of 41% and LesionAgent 78%, and an open-weight LLM that can run inside the hospital rises from 6% to 73%. LesionAgent and a napari reading app for any LLM are available at https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/LesionAgent.
