Supervaluationism about vagueness and indeterminacy: A judgment-aggregation perspective

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (165 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.27463v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Supervaluationism about vagueness and indeterminacy is based on the idea that some sentences, or predicates within them, have multiple competing interpretations or specifications. A sentence is deemed determinately true if and only if it is true according to all specifications; and determinately false if and only if it is false according to all specifications. In all other cases, the sentence is assigned no determinate truth-value. Supervaluationism, however, is only one way of taking into account the verdicts of different specifications -- in technical terms, "aggregating" those verdicts. In this paper, we introduce insights from the theory of judgment aggregation to study a broad class of different aggregation functions. This allows us to offer an axiomatic characterization of supervaluationism, which, in turn, locates it precisely within a larger logical space of possible approaches to vagueness and indeterminacy. Among other results, we axiomatically compare supervaluationism with subvaluationism, the epistemic theory of vagueness, and Strong Kleene and LP logics.
