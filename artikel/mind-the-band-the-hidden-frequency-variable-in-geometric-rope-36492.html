Mind the Band: The Hidden Frequency Variable in Geometric RoPE

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (275 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.32171v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Geometric rotary position encodings (RoPE) incorporate 3D camera geometry into multi-view attention by rotating token features according to viewing rays, depths, and reprojected image coordinates. Each coordinate is rotated across a progression of frequencies--its frequency band--yet existing implementations leave this band to inherited defaults, either retaining standard 2D RoPE's top frequency or letting the frequency span grow exponentially with the attention head dimension. We show that this unstandardized setting can reverse benchmark rankings. In novel view synthesis on RealEstate10K, RayRoPE matches the strongest baseline in the 47M model (144-channel heads), yet trails by 1.2 dB in the 25M model (48-channel heads), and exhibits the same reversal when varying head size alone at fixed model width. Populating a 243x range with four frequencies instead of the 3x default at 48 channels recovers 0.9 dB of that gap. Across view synthesis and two-view depth estimation, overly narrow bands consistently blur correspondence, whereas all three projection-based encodings perform reliably when their top frequency lies within 0.3--3 rotations per 8-pixel patch (10--90 rotations across 256x256 RealEstate10K). At the opposite extreme, overly wide bands degrade RayRoPE through a forward-backward asymmetry: averaging rotations over a predicted depth interval damps high frequencies in the forward pass, yet leaves their gradients with respect to predicted depth undamped. Blocking only those high-frequency depth gradients recovers 90--93% of the lost PSNR, showing that forward smoothing does not guarantee backward stability. Guided by this analysis, we distill practical guidelines--setting bands in image coordinates, populating spans in small heads, and avoiding unconditional same-view damping--and advocate reporting and matching frequency bands in future multi-view benchmarks.
