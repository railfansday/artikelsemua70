In a recent development, GTA VI isn’t officially available for PC, and downloading a supposed PC version could put your computer at risk of malware. Rockstar has a history of making PC gamers wait their turn for titles. Red Dead Redemption 2 got its PC port a year after consoles, and GTA 5 came to PCs almost two years after launch. While there is a somewhat credible rumor that Rockstar will be bringing GTA VI to PCs early next year, there hasn’t been an official announcement from the studio.

The only way you can play on PC right now is to use the free PlayStation Remote Play app to stream the game from your console to your computer.

In a recent development, in the meantime, scammers and opportunists have wasted no time flooding the market with everything from poorly made GTA VI clones vibe-coded with AI to actively malicious, virus-riddled downloads designed to turn your PC into a crypto mining operation or steal your money and personal data. Cybersecurity provider Kaspersky reported recording 27,416 detections of GTA VI-related malware and unwanted apps since June.

Also: PlayStation Remote Play lets you stream GTA VI to your PC or smart TV.

You’ll probably be able to spot the majority of GTA VI scams a mile away, but you’re never immune to an official-looking web page or the promise of a free copy of the game. No matter how official a download page seems, unless the news is coming from Rockstar itself, you’re being lied to. And definitely take any and all claims of the game code being dumped and cracked for PC with a very large grain of salt.

But all is not lost if you got fooled into downloading a fake version of GTA VI — cleaning up and protecting your PC is a fairly straightforward process.

Alright, so you’ve downloaded a fake version of GTA VI. What now? Your very first move should be to immediately uninstall the program. Getting rid of the software limits a scammer’s or hacker’s options for hijacking your PC. But you can’t stop there.

The fake GTA VI program itself might be gone now, but it could still be hiding malicious files deep inside your PC and wreaking havoc. Running antivirus software like AVG Avast, even the free version, can help track them all down for quarantine and destruction.

Recent findings suggest that A “cracked” PC program is any software whose copy protection has been circumvented and removed. A keygen is a program that reverse-engineers the license validation algorithm your PC uses to generate fake but official-looking product keys. Both processes are heavily involved in the software pirating and sharing community, and you’ll be able to find hundreds of consumers across dozens of less-than-legal forums claiming to have dumped the source code for GTA VI and cracked it, making it available for PC users and modders.

While a few might actually be trying to distribute the game for free, no strings attached, most will likely have a host of malware hiding inside to hijack your PC.

Sources indicate that as my grandfather used to say: If it smells like b.s., it is. The vast majority of GTA VI download scams fall apart under the slightest bit of scrutiny, so don’t be afraid to follow the money, as it were.

It has been revealed that notorious software cracker/leaker Cyberleek is claiming to have the entire source code for the Xbox build of GTA VI and will release it on Nov. 23 — but only if his scammy crypto coin hits $15 million in market cap. And while this is an obvious money-making scheme, others might not be so open about their goals. The best policy is to block, report, and keep scrolling.
