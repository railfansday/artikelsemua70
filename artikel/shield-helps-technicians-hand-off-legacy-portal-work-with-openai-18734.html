📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

With GPT‑6 Astra, the agents in Shield’s Forge platform navigate legacy portals so technicians can focus on issues that need their attention.

For many small and medium-sized businesses, an outsourced IT services provider handles everything from an employee locked out of their laptop to a misconfigured firewall that took the office offline.

It has been revealed that “If a customer is locked out of their computer or there’s a phishing attack, speed is paramount,” indicates Jain. “Whether you’re going to solve their problem or you’re going to escalate to a human, you want to do either as fast as possible.”.

Industry observers note that before using GPT‑6 Astra, Forge’s agents could get stuck at systems they could not access. Customers then had to wait for a technician to complete that step.

In a recent development, shield tried computer use, where an AI model reads the screen, clicks, and types, with earlier models. Jain claims they were not reliable or fast enough. “Usually, a system that doesn’t have an API is an old-school system that also doesn’t look very nice and is very hard to use, even by a human,” Jain says.

Industry observers note that technicians spend much of their day in portals that manage tickets, devices and software licenses. Many of those systems lack APIs, so an agent needs to navigate the screen much as a person would.

Industry observers note that forge’s AI Engineer assigns the work to a computer-use agent, and GPT‑6 Astra navigates the interface to complete supported tasks. Technicians decide which actions it can take on its own and where they need to review or approve the work.

Shield indicates these browser workflows are live, with thousands of runs each month across its IT providers. Phone calls, on-site work and remote-desktop tasks still need human involvement.

“GPT‑6 Astra allows our agents to embody what a technician does by logging into portals and legacy websites that might not have API access and performing tasks that only a human could six months ago,” says Mayank Jain, Head of Technology at Shield.

Jain indicates GPT‑6 Astra was 35-40% faster on average than the legacy models Shield had used for computer-use tasks. He also says customer satisfaction is higher on tickets where AI is used, with speed of resolution being a critical factor.

Shield reports faster execution, fewer failures and fewer timeouts. Its teams also spend less time writing detailed instructions to an agent or helping it through a task.

Shield is testing remote-desktop workflows that would let agents perform more complex actions directly on a computer’s desktop.

Jain notes, “With GPT‑6 Astra, Forge is now truly an extension of our human technicians.”.
