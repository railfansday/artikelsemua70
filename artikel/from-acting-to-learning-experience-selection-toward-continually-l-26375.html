From Acting to Learning: Experience Selection toward Continually Learning Agents

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (180 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.27159v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Successful agent interactions provide training data, but success alone does not determine which records a student should learn from. We examine experience selection for a 4B language agent as a component of continual agent learning. Nine selectors are compared across two fixed experience pools, eight initialization seeds, and two sampling seeds, yielding 288 trained students. Each student is evaluated on 128 development task groups with a common executor. With equal global selection freedom, 256 distinct contents, and 768 training presentations, optimizer-aware gradient selection reaches 81.45\% and 73.19\% success in the two pools. Its gains over global random selection are 8.25 and 5.96 percentage points; gains over global high-NLL selection are 4.10 and 4.05 points. All eight initialization-level contrasts are positive against both baselines in each pool. Relaxing coverage constraints improves gradient selection but reduces random-selection performance, showing that the ranking rule and selection space must be considered together. A separately frozen replication on 128 reserved task groups is complete; its equal-source gradient-minus-random and gradient-minus-high-NLL differences are +7.03 and +5.03 points, respectively.
