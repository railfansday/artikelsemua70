REBEAL: Human References Are No Longer the Best Translation, and Metrics Do Not Notice

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (191 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.32623v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Reference-based MT evaluation assumes the human reference is correct and better than the systems it scores. Both assumptions now fail, since the human reference ranks first in none of the 2024 WMT MQM campaigns and carries annotated errors in 19% to 87% of its segments. What a metric does with a flawed reference is a question about the metric, and it would have been worth asking before reference quality came into doubt. We introduce REBEAL (Reference Bias EvALuation), a benchmark that probes how MT metrics respond to seven types of controlled reference contamination, drawn from the error categories those campaigns record. Across eight widely used metrics and three language pairs, recent learned metrics (xCOMET, MetricX) prove more robust than their predecessors, but each family fails in its own way. Surface metrics show no length sensitivity, so contamination hides in longer segments, while learned metrics accept references in unrelated languages as nearly correct. The failure they share is named-entity contamination, which no metric detects and which flips up to 8 of 36 system pairs when ranking nine MT systems.
