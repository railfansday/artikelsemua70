FACET - Facet Aware Contrastive Embeddings for Traits- Substantial Five Estimation from Life Narratives

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (239 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.23560v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Personality is only weakly and inconsistently signaled by written language, and existing text-based methods typically combine language-model and psycholinguistic representations at the input or decision level. It remains open whether a more deliberate combination of the two views, and a decomposition of the prediction target, can recover more of the signal. We present a three-phase framework for estimating Substantial Five personality domain scores from life-narrative interview text. It encodes text and psycholinguistic features separately and combines them by concatenation or by a learned weighting, decomposes each domain into contrastively trained facet embeddings instead of predicting the domain directly, and pools the facet embeddings by attention into a domain-level prediction. We compare end-to-end with staged training and ablate the input modalities, their combination, and the facet-pooling strategy. On 1,332 participants from the St. Louis Personality and Aging Network (SPAN) study, facet embeddings reach AUC up to 0.740, and domain-level R2 reaches 0.328, highest for Openness. End-to-end training gives lower error than staged training in every domain, and attention pooling gives the lowest error in every domain. Combining the two views lowers error relative to text alone for Conscientiousness and Agreeableness, whereas a learned weighting of the views demonstrates no consistent advantage over concatenation. The framework provides facet- level predictions that a domain-only model cannot, and it improves modestly on a regression from the raw features in four of the five domains.
