Attack Policy Selection can Mislead Monitor Evaluators

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (261 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.34934v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: AI monitors, classifiers that flag dangerous behaviour by AI agents, are often evaluated against transcripts generated by attack policies acting as proxies for dangerous behaviour in deployment. We evaluate an ex-production OpenAI monitor as a case study and find that measured false negative rate is highly sensitive to both the method used to generate attack policies and the level of optimisation applied within that method. This rate ranged from 0.5\% to 20.8\% across optimisation levels of a single method (reinforcement learning), and from 6.4\% to 23.0\% across methods at their best observed optimisation level (optimised prompting vs.\ transcript editing). Every attack policy was developed against proxy monitors only: all reported results show attacks transferring to the held-out GPT-5.4 target monitor. Our results suggest that monitor evasion evaluations can easily mislead: an evaluation using only basic attack methods may give false confidence that a monitor is strong. The sensitivity to attack-policy choice raises a problem: interpreting any particular result as evidence about deployment performance requires justification. The transcripts produced by attack policies are proxies for the dangerous behaviour a monitor must flag in deployment. These proxies can introduce visible artefacts, such as reasoning about a ``side task'' absent from the instructions shown to the monitor, that affect monitor judgements. Given the sensitivity above, the choice of proxy can change an evaluation's conclusion. Reading these results as evidence about deployment therefore requires a separate legitimacy argument for why the proxies are informative, including any justification of conservativeness. Making convincing arguments remains an open problem.
