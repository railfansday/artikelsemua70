Singapore and China expand RMB facility as financial ties deepen

Singapore has moved to widen access to Renminbi (RMB) financing for regional businesses, with the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS), the city-state’s central bank and financial regulator, unveiling a package of measures to deepen monetary and market links with China.

At the centre of the announcement is a revamp of the MAS RMB Facility, which draws on the bilateral currency swap arrangement between MAS and the People’s Bank of China (PBC).

Recent findings suggest that the facility will be split into two parts. An RMB Corporate Facility will allow eligible financial institutions to fund a wider set of activities, including trade financing with third countries, working capital and capital expenditure, with several tenor choices and the option of intra-group funding.

A separate RMB Liquidity Facility will keep providing backstop funding to eligible institutions in Singapore, helping to protect stability in the city’s offshore RMB market.

Collateral rules are also being loosened. Alongside Singapore Dollar cash, both facilities will now accept Singapore Government Securities and MAS Bills, while MAS and the PBC are assessing whether China Government Bonds could also qualify.

Beyond the facility, the two countries proposal closer work on payments, capital markets and supervision. MAS and the PBC are studying a link between their fast payment systems to handle small-value transfers.

MAS and the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) will help companies list and raise funds in each other’s markets, and will back new indices and additional ETF products through cooperation between Singapore Exchange (SGX) and the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock exchanges.

MAS and the National Financial Regulatory Administration will step up banking and insurance oversight dialogue through regular exchanges and training, while MAS and the CSRC will expand staff exchanges covering securities and futures regulation.

Recent findings suggest that commercial players are already acting on the closer ties. DBS Bank, which serves as Singapore’s second RMB clearing bank, has started offering clearing services to support RMB use across the region. Bank of China’s Singapore branch has been named an MAS Primary Dealer, giving it a larger role in the local government securities market. Under an over-the-counter bond arrangement, both Bank of China and DBS have brought on clients to trade and hold selected onshore RMB bonds in Singapore.

SGX and the China Foreign Exchange Trade System will also work together on Panda Bond listings, and ICBC Singapore and CGS International Securities have introduced the CGS-ICBC-CFETS Belt and Road Bond Index ETF on SGX. The meeting was jointly led by Singapore Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong and Chinese Vice Premier Ding Xuexiang.

MAS deputy chairman and minister for national development Chee Hong Tat said, “Singapore and China have made tangible progress to strengthen cooperation and markets connectivity. This year’s measures build on this progress, by exploring new areas of cooperation, alongside stronger commercial partnerships between our financial institutions. Together, these efforts will facilitate growing financial flows and support trade and investment linkages between our two markets.”.
