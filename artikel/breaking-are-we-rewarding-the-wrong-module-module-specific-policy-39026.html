Breaking: Are We Rewarding the Wrong Module? Module-Specific Policy Optimization for Multimodal Reinforcement Learning

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (215 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.32687v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has been widely adopted to enhance the multimodal reasoning of multimodal large language models (MLLMs). However, existing methods share a common limitation of Module-Level Credit Entanglement, in which the vision encoder, projector, and LLM modules are jointly optimized using the same advantage signals even when perceptual correctness and final-answer correctness differ. To address this limitation, we propose Module-Specific Policy Optimization (MSPO), a framework that separates perceptual and outcome-based learning signals across model modules. Specifically, MSPO uses responses to perception checklists comprising auxiliary questions about visual information to derive an advantage for training the vision encoder. From these responses, MSPO computes the Confidence-aware Perception Reward (CaPR), using a perception sign obtained by thresholding checklist accuracy to determine reward direction and posterior confidence from a Beta distribution over latent perception accuracy to scale reward magnitude. We then derive the vision advantage through group-relative normalization of CaPR and scale it using main-task success rates. This advantage guides updates to the vision encoder, while the projector and LLM retain outcome-based GRPO updates. Extensive experiments on seven mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that MSPO outperforms existing RL-tuned models on average at both the 3B and 7B scales.
