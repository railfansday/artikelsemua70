Latest Update: XRL4T1D: A Post-hoc Explainability Framework for Reinforcement Learning in Type 1 Diabetes Management

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (242 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.31498v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Type 1 diabetes (T1D) requires lifelong insulin therapy, and reinforcement learning (RL) has shown promise for automated dosing in the UVA/Padova simulator. However, the decisions of RL controllers remain difficult to interpret. We present XRL4T1D, a post-hoc explainability framework for evaluating pretrained insulin-dosing policies without retraining. Each policy uses 60 minutes of continuous glucose monitor (CGM) and administered-insulin history, sampled every 5 minutes, to recommend insulin. We use SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) to compare the importance of CGM and insulin-history inputs and summarize insulin-history attributions over three 20-minute windows motivated by insulin-on-board. We assess whether these attributions correspond to policy-output sensitivity through targeted input replacement and compare temporal rankings with TimeSHAP. In all 80 patient-policy-mask-size cases, replacing the highest-attribution inputs changes the normalized insulin recommendation more than replacing the lowest-attribution or random inputs. The highest-attribution insulin-history window is also the most output-sensitive in 115 of 120 patient-policy-scenario configurations, and KernelSHAP and TimeSHAP select the same top window in 111 of 120. Applying XRL4T1D to PPO and G2P2C across 20 virtual patients, we find that both controllers rely predominantly on CGM history, especially G2P2C. However, the distribution of insulin-history importance across recent, middle, and older windows varies by cohort, explainer, and analysis settings, without a consistent separation between controllers. Global feature-attribution rankings remain stable under the tested observation corruptions. These results show why attribution fidelity, temporal reproducibility, and controller-comparison robustness should be assessed separately.
