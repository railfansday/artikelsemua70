Reading, Writing and Repairing Game-Theoretic Policies in Weight Space

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (189 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.31979v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Can a language model work in the weight space of a policy network for a repeated game? We put four questions to a mid-size open model, on recurrent networks of 1{,}218 weights with exactly checkable answers. Given a population of opponents described in English, write a policy that is optimal: its value is a finite computation over the agent's information state, so a submitted network has an exact gap from the optimum. Given a network's weights, report its action after each of fifteen opponent histories; recover the state machine it implements, judged on every opponent sequence to depth ten; or repair a network whose output was altered in one place and say which round first goes wrong. The model recovers the program in 62 of 66 replies, reports all fifteen behavioral entries correctly in 34 of 36, and attains the optimum in 20 of 20 when it states a policy the evaluator compiles. Repair is the exception: 4 of 18 repaired, 5 of 18 diagnosed, with a median patch covering one of the two entries needed.
