Conditional What-If Generation for Radio Access Network Energy under Rare and Unseen Contexts

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (236 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.24208v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Energy-saving mechanisms in RANs must balance energy reduction and network performance under varying traffic conditions. Conditional generative models support what-if analysis by generating plausible power-consumption trajectories for radio configurations and operating scenarios, helping operators evaluate energy-saving policies before deployment. However, representative measurements are costly and operationally constrained, so historical data cover only limited configurations and conditions, leaving some contexts poorly represented or unseen during training. In this paper, we study conditional generation of RAN power-consumption trajectories under decreasing training coverage, from well-represented to rare and unseen configurations. We first introduce a context-normalized conditional generator combining temporally aligned conditioning with configuration-dependent target normalization. It improves generation for well-represented configurations but becomes less reliable as training exposure decreases because target consumption is harder to estimate. We therefore introduce a prediction-based generative framework that decomposes power consumption into global prediction, residual level and scale correction, and temporal residual generation. Experiments on a commercial 5G RAN and a public dataset show that hardware metadata provides transferable information about residual scale, while limited target observations at inference improve generation through residual-level correction without retraining. An ablation study confirms that residual-level correction provides most of the gain. Both approaches outperform state-of-the-art methods for well-represented configurations, while the prediction-based approach also generalizes to rare and unseen contexts and, with one target observation, reduces median MAE by about 50% compared with TimeWeaver.
