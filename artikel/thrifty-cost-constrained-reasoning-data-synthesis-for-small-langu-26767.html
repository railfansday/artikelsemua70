THRIFTY: Cost-Constrained Reasoning-Data Synthesis for Small Language Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (211 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.27436v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Small language models (SLMs) are increasingly attractive for efficient, specialized deployment, and are typically trained on reasoning trajectories distilled from a larger teacher LLM. Generating this synthetic data is a major cost of the pipeline, especially when the teacher is a frontier model. Existing collection methods, however, are largely cost-unaware, or at best spread a fixed sampling budget uniformly across queries. Such allocation ignores the wide variation in problem difficulty and the strategic over- or under-sampling it invites. Thus, we propose THRIFTY, a Thompson-sampling bandit controller that spends a fixed generation-token budget to collect diverse correct reasoning trajectories, casting the task as a bandits-with-knapsacks problem with a novel diversity-aware objective. It tracks each problem's success probability with an online Beta posterior and spends each token where it expects the most new distinct correct solutions. Because repeated solutions add progressively less value, THRIFTY adaptively stops resampling saturated problems, concentrates budget on under-covered ones, and retires the unsolvable at the budget's opportunity cost. On math (AIME, MATH-500) and code (LiveCodeBench) benchmarks, THRIFTY gathers up to 22% more diverse correct trajectories per generation token than both the standard uniform-sampling recipe and the strongest adaptive baseline, with the largest gains in the scarce-budget and hard-problem regimes.
