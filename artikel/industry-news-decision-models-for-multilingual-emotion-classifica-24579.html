Industry News: Decision Models for Multilingual Emotion Classification: A Comparative Study Against LLMs and Encoders

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (315 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.27721v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Decision models receive a question with a closed list of answers and return a probability for each answer instead of free text. They promise low latency and robustness to prompt wording, but no study has compared several of them with fine-tuned encoders across languages. We ask whether systems used without training on labelled data (zero-shot) can replace fine-tuning, and what the decision format adds over a general-purpose large language model (LLM). We compare three decision models (Jev, OpenAI Decisions, Strands Decider 2B) and two LLMs (GPT-6 Luna, Qwen3.5-2B), given Italian instructions, with two fine-tuned multilingual encoders (tabularisai, XLM-EMO) on four emotions (joy, sadness, anger, fear) in 10 social-media datasets in five languages, with paired difference and equivalence tests. The three best zero-shot systems (two decision models and one LLM) score on average about one macro-F1 point below an encoder fine-tuned on the target dataset; our test sets cannot exclude a gap of 4-5 points, and on single datasets either approach wins by up to 22 points. They beat encoders fine-tuned on another dataset by 8-15 points, though against XLM-EMO this may not extend to new datasets, and instructions in each text's language change their results by under 1 point. Exploratorily, they beat the strongest public emotion model used as is by about 6 points. OpenAI's decision endpoint matches GPT-6 Luna, the LLM it runs on, at a fifth of its median latency, and a 2-billion-parameter decision model beats a same-size general LLM by 10 points, owing to its training, not the decision format. The best zero-shot systems, the LLM included, have an SD of about 1 point across prompts. Without labelled data they are a strong default, and decision models add lower latency and, unlike generative LLM use, per-option probabilities. Fine-tuning won by a wide margin only on the largest dataset, GoEmotions.
