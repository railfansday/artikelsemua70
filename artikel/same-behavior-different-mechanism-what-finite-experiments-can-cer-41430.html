Same behavior, different mechanism: what finite experiments can certify about how a network computes

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (158 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.08469v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Interpretability research, AI audits and assessments of machine consciousness make claims about how a system computes, not what it outputs. We give such claims a measurement theory. A claim is certifiable from finite interventions exactly when the mechanism lies at positive information distance from those that falsify it; it then costs at least log(1/alpha) over that distance, and an adaptive experiment attains this price asymptotically. Access, geometry and the logical form of the claim set the distance. In solvable networks, behavior carries no evidence about integrated information, and quantities that select one thing and report another have cliffs beside which cost grows as the inverse square of distance, whatever the margin. Equally accurate networks implement different circuits. In language models of up to 7.6 billion parameters, 220 of 223 audited claims about circuits, and a refusal mechanism, were certified on live interventions, 89% within the cost range registered beforehand.
