It has been revealed that the Nintendo Switch 2 features a larger screen than its predecessor, sporting a 7.9-inch LCD capable of a 120Hz refresh rate for smoother on-the-go gaming. It gives you much more storage space than before, with 256GB for large game downloads and transferring your Switch 1 digital titles. The bundle not only includes a Switch 2 console, two Joy-Con 2 controllers, and a voucher for Mario Kart World; it also includes 3 months of Nintendo Online for access to multiplayer features, Nintendo’s virtual console libraries (including the fresh GameCube virtual console), and exclusive member discounts. If you want to continue your membership after the free trial ends, a single-profile membership starts at $19.99 per year, and a family plan starts at $34.99 per year and covers up to 8 profiles.

It’s notable to note that you’re not actually saving any cash on the console itself with this offer. But considering that the digital edition of Mario Kart World lists for $80 on its own, this is still a great opportunity to lock in some savings if you already know it’s a game that you’ll want in your library.

Sources indicate that black Friday is the day after Thanksgiving in the US, and this year it falls on Nov. 27. Cyber Monday kicks off the week after Thanksgiving, and falls on Nov. 30, though you’ll still have plenty of shopping days left after to check everyone off your holiday gift list.

The holiday shopping season is about the only time during the year, outside of special announcements and Nintendo Direct presentations, when you’ll see any sort of discount on all things Nintendo. It is incredibly rare to see a meaningful markdown on a Nintendo console, game, or officially branded accessory, even years after release. So while the deals might not be super impressive, Nintendo is such a hot brand that even a rollback as small as $10 on a console or a few dollars on a newly introduced game title is enough to have stock flying off the shelves even faster than usual.

Parents and collectors can breathe a sigh of relief knowing that the Switch 2 console has broad backward compatibility with physical games released for the original Switch system. You can check whether your favorite game is compatible with both systems using the search function on Nintendo’s website; each game’s web page includes information on compatibility with both Switch systems. If the game you want isn’t compatible, you can download a Switch 2 “upgrade” that makes your already-purchased physical and digital games work with your fresh console — for a fee, of course.
