SPARC: Evidence-guided adaptive model composition for materials discovery

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (234 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.19050v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Closed-loop experimentation requires more than selecting the next experiment. It must also determine which existing measurements and model-derived relationships are sufficiently supported to influence that decision. Here we introduce SPARC (Sparse Predictive Aggregation of Regression Components), a framework that continually revises this support as experimental evidence accumulates. Competing model families are evaluated through out-of-fold predictions, so that each model is tested on observations not used in its corresponding fit. Sparse posterior composition assigns model influence according to predictive contributions supported by the out-of-fold evidence, retaining complementary contributions from several model families. Candidate-dependent predictive dispersion and residual variability define trust, which modulates exploration alongside the predicted response. Independent diagnostics also prioritize existing measurements for prospective verification, with model-derived trust reported alongside them. Across eight published materials datasets, SPARC achieved higher $R^2$ than the corresponding literature-reported values in six, with relative gains of up to 77% without the density-functional-theory- or experiment-derived descriptors used by several published pipelines. In an automated alkaline oxygen-evolution-reaction campaign, 8 of 11 SPARC-selected experiments reached overpotentials below 300 mV, with a best value of 242 mV, compared with 18 of 125 initial experiments. Measurements prioritized for verification before repetition subsequently deviated by 76 mV on average, compared with 27 mV for matched controls. SPARC therefore turns experimental evidence into a basis for revising model influence, directing exploration and identifying measurements that warrant verification.
