How Much Effort Does an AI Code Reviewer Need? Evidence from Claude 5.5 Models on Plausible-but-Wrong Patches

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (259 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.33639v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: AI coding agents write code faster than people can review it, so teams add AI reviewers. Reasoning models let such a reviewer trade cost for diligence through an effort setting. We asked how much effort a reviewer needs to catch plausible-but-wrong patches, which pass a benchmark's tests but fail stronger ones. Claude Opus 5.5 (preregistered), Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 (exploratory) each reviewed the same 90 agent-written Django patches from SWE-bench Verified at low, medium and high effort. By strengthened SWE-ABS tests, 55 patches were wrong and 35 correct. In every model, high effort accepted fewer wrong patches than low (false success .45 against .67 for Opus, .49 against .65 for Sonnet, .51 against .78 for Haiku), a supported difference for Opus and Haiku. For Opus, medium was non-inferior to high within 15 percentage points (difference .018, 95% CI [-.065, .108]) and cost 20% less, short of the preregistered 30%. For Sonnet and Haiku, medium cost 39% and 51% less than high but was not shown non-inferior. Opus at medium or high agreed best with the labels; Haiku at high cost about 8% as much per review as Opus at medium, accuracy difference unresolved. Exploratory coding demonstrated that most rejections named the tested defect and that effort added such rejections. No setting is a strong gate: each accepted at least 45% of wrong patches. For 32 of the 55 wrong patches the issue may not require what the failing test checks; without them, neither accuracy hypothesis holds for any model.
