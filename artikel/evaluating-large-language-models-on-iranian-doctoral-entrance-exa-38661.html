Evaluating Large Language Models on Iranian Doctoral Entrance Exams in Electrical and Computer Engineering

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (278 words, 📖 Standard)

In a recent development, arXiv:2611.31263v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We evaluate four large language models (LLMs) on multiple-choice questions from the 2024 and 2025 Iranian national Ph.D. entrance examinations in Electrical Engineering (EE) and Computer Engineering (CE). Of the 440 questions in the four official booklets, 7 were excluded because the question statement or its figure was ambiguous, leaving 433 questions (186 EE, 247 CE), of which 171 (39.5\%) depend on a figure. Each question was presented in the original Persian and in an English translation, giving 3,464 scored responses. GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5 and Grok 4 were queried zero-shot, with reasoning enabled, through a single commercial web interface (Perplexity) in October 2025, using an automated script. Because every model answers the same items, all inferences use paired tests or question-clustered models. Accuracy separates into two tiers: GPT-5 (79.9\%, 95\% CI 76.6--83.1) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (78.4\%, 74.8--81.9) are statistically indistinguishable, and both outperform Grok 4 (71.7\%) and Claude Sonnet 4.5 (71.3\%) by 6.7--8.7 percentage points (Holm-adjusted $p < 0.001$). The English--Persian accuracy difference is small for every model ($-0.2$ to $+2.5$ points; exact McNemar $p \ge 0.28$). For three models the 90\% confidence interval lies within $\pm 5$ points, so differences larger than this can be excluded. Questions with figures are much harder: the odds ratio is 0.30 (95\% CI 0.21--0.43) after adjusting for discipline. The apparent EE deficit of two models disappears once the larger share of figure-based EE items is accounted for. We describe the dataset, the protocol and its limitations, including the uncontrolled web-retrieval and decoding settings of the interface, and release item-level responses and analysis code.
