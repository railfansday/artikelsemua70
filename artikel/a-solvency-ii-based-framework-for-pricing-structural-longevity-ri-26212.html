A Solvency II-Based Framework for Pricing Structural Longevity Risk under an Affine Mortality Model

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (231 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.26670v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: This paper develops a Solvency II-based pricing framework for structural longevity risk in incomplete longevity markets. The valuation scheme relies on the Cost-of-Capital principle underlying the Risk Margin and combines it with a continuous-time affine mortality model. Mortality dynamics are specified in calendar time, with age-dependent mortality patterns linked to a latent longevity factor driving long-term mortality improvements. The affine structure preserves closed-form survival probabilities and provides a tractable basis for repeated Best Estimate, Solvency Capital Requirement and Risk Margin calculations. We derive the conditional distribution of one-year discounted Best Estimate variations, obtaining analytical expressions for their asymmetry and Value-at-Risk under the Gaussian affine specification. Within this setting, we introduce a stress-based assessment by applying adverse shocks to the deterministic parameters governing the longevity trend. The trend-based stress is compared with a mortality-level stress and with a Value-at-Risk-based capital assessment. The methodology links stochastic mortality modelling, prudential capital valuation and longevity-linked payoff assessment within a unified actuarial framework. A numerical application based on Belgian mortality data illustrates the implementation and shows that the mortality-level stress produces substantially larger scenario-based capital-cost measures than the trend-based stress. These findings highlight the sensitivity of longevity risk valuation to the specification of adverse mortality scenarios. The approach offers a transparent and operational basis for assessing and pricing structural longevity risk under a Solvency II Cost-of-Capital perspective.
