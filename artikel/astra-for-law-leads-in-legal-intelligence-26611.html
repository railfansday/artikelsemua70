It has been revealed that harvey’s Legal Agent Benchmark (LAB) tests whether a model can take a legal assignment from start to finish. LAB-AA⁠(opens in a latest window) is Artificial Analysis’s version of that test. It tests the models on 120 assignments developed by Harvey across 24 areas of law – everything from drafting deposition outlines to reviewing commercial leases to strategizing ownership changes in acquisitions. To earn full credit, an answer must meet every requirement of an assignment without hallucinating, i.e., making a material claim that is not supported by the source documents.

Astra for Law ranked first, earning full credit on 20.8% of assignments, ahead of GPT‑6.2 Astra at 15.0% and Muse Wonder Full at 13.1%.

Recent findings suggest that hallucinations are unacceptable in legal work. A single invented citation can cost a lawyer a client’s trust, credibility in court, and a reputation built over years. That’s why reducing hallucinations has been a priority for Astra for Law. In Artificial Analysis’s latest LAB-AA comparison, Astra for Law tied (with GPT‑6) for the lowest rate of material hallucinations: 0.03 per assignment, compared with 0.59 for Claude Opus 5.5 (the next lowest non-OpenAI model). Notably, Astra for Law's performance on this eval included zero material hallucinations for tasks that met the all-pass criteria.

Recent findings suggest that astra for Law averaged 0.03 material and 0.06 minor hallucinations per assignment. Claude Opus 5.5 averaged 0.59 material and 0.51 minor hallucinations per assignment, about 20 times as many material hallucinations and 8.5 times as many minor ones.

Every OpenAI configuration tested had fewer hallucinations than Claude Opus 5.5, the best non-OpenAI result on this measure. Opus 5.5 averaged 0.59 material hallucinations per assignment, about 20 times as many as Astra for Law.
