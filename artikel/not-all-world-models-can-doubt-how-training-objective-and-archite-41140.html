Not all world models can doubt: how training objective and architecture shape epistemic uncertainty in JEPA and RSSM world models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (296 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.34830v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: World models predict how an environment responds to actions, so that candidate actions can be evaluated before execution. Executed actions, or decisions, taken in this manner are only as reliable as the model's predictive capabilities, and predictions in turn are only reliable within the model's experience. Epistemic uncertainty (EU) is the signal that marks where that experience ends. In this work we offer an extensive empirical study on how training objectives and architectures shape epistemic uncertainty of world models. More precisely, we present a controlled comparison of post-hoc EU (last-layer Laplace and ensembles) between two dominant models of different paradigms: 1) LeWorldModel (LeWM): A joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA), which predicts the next embedding without a decoder, and 2) DreamerV3: A recurrent state-space model (RSSM), which reconstructs the observation from a recurrent latent state. In ten simulated visual-control environments, we follow a controlled state perturbation of the environment state through every stage of the models up to the final EU estimate. We find that the RSSM attenuates the state perturbation through two mechanisms before it reaches an estimator on the predictor feature. Its recurrent enhancement averages over context, so a change in a single frame reaches the state at a fraction of its size and recovers only as the change persists. Its reconstruction objective weights an entity by the pixels it covers, so a small object can be lost due to the training objective. The JEPA has neither mechanism and passes the change through all stages to the uncertainty. We further show that this translates to gaps in five uncertainty related downstream tasks, from unfamiliar-state detection to uncertainty-penalised planning: the RSSM uncertainty misses deviations that its own posterior registers, whereas the JEPA uncertainty reports what its model sees.
