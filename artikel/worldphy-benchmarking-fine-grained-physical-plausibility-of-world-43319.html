WorldPhy: Benchmarking Fine-Grained Physical Plausibility of World Models in Robotics

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (195 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.34130v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: World models are increasingly used in robotics to improve the performance of embodied models. Compared with general-purpose video generation, robotics places more stringent requirements on world models. Generated videos must satisfy fine-grained physical constraints on robot motion and object interactions. However, existing world model benchmarks can overlook robotics specific physical details and struggle to assess them accurately. To address this problem, we introduce WorldPhy, a world model benchmark for accurate, fine-grained evaluation of physical plausibility in robotics. Our benchmark contains 300 tasks, each consisting of an initial-frame image and a task instruction, covering diverse robotics scenes, task categories, viewpoints, and robot configurations. We further collect over 12K high-quality human annotations, and use them to train WorldPhyReward to accurately provide scores and reasons across six fine-grained physical dimensions and an instruction-following dimension. Comparisons with general VLMs and existing reward models show that WorldPhyReward better aligns with human ratings. We system- atically evaluate 13 frontier open and closed-source world models. The results reveal that current world models struggle to consistently maintain fine-grained physical plausibility across different robotics scenarios. Our code is available at https://github.com/chuziyuan139/ WorldPhy.
