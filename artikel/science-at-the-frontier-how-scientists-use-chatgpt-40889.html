How more than 1,800 scientists used ChatGPT—and what they expect AI to mean for scientific discovery. Explore the findings in our latest report.

Scientific progress depends on researchers asking upcoming questions and finding ways to answer them. AI is becoming part of that work, but we’re still learning where it helps and what it could mean for discovery.

OpenAI’s Economic Research team studied more than 580,000 scientific messages from 1,819 scientists in the ChatGPT for Academic Researchers summer pilot and surveyed 251 participants.

Industry observers note that scientists used ChatGPT across the research process, from reviewing literature to analyzing evidence and developing ideas. Surveyed participants estimated that the same work, at the same quality, would have taken 1.9 times as long without AI. And 98% of those who answered our question about discovery timelines expected AI to accelerate discovery in their field.

By default, OpenAI does not use participants’ data from the ChatGPT for Academic Researchers program to train its models. To protect researchers’ privacy, we used tools called automated classifiers to sort messages by the type of research activity, such as reviewing literature, writing code, or analyzing data. The research team could see those categories, but did not read the underlying messages or ChatGPT’s responses.

Sources indicate that scientists in this study used ChatGPT to explore ideas, analyze evidence, write code, and communicate their findings. Their responses also point to ambitions beyond saving time: finding more promising questions and pursuing a wider range of research.

Understanding whether those expectations translate into faster discovery will take more research. Progress will also depend on the funding, staff, and experimental capacity scientists need to test their ideas.

Recent findings suggest that we want to empower more scientists to explore what AI can make possible in their work. Alongside this research, we’re expanding ChatGPT for Academic Researchers⁠, with free access for more than 200,000 researchers around the world. Helping scientists turn their ideas into discoveries that improve lives is central to our mission of ensuring that AGI benefits all of humanity.
