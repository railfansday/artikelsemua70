An Information-Theoretic Analysis of Information Hiding in Semantic Communication

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (240 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.34550v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Semantic communication shifts the transmission goal from the accurate recovery of syntactic symbols to the reliable conveyance of semantic content, yet a systematic information-theoretic characterization of information hiding at the semantic layer is still missing. Building on the semantic information theory of Niu-Zhang and the classical information hiding theory of Moulin-O'Sullivan, this paper establishes an information-theoretic framework for information hiding under semantic communication. The classical hiding model is extended to a two-layer structure (semantic host, syntactic host, syntactic composite signal, syntactic received, semantic received) bridged by synonymous mappings f_s, f_x and the inverse mapping f_y^{-1}, subject to four distortion constraints: syntactic transparency, semantic fidelity, syntactic perturbation, and semantic destruction. The capacity is cast as a Stackelberg game with the hider moving first and the payoff J(Q,A) = I(U~; Y~ | K) - I(U~; S~ | K), yielding the single-letter maximin C^s = max_Q min_A J(Q,A), whose achievability and converse follow from random coding and joint typicality decoding. Relative to the classical capacity, comparison payoffs built from the up/down semantic mutual information give the endpoint sandwich C^s_end(0,1) <= C^s <= C^s_end(1,0) with the classical value C in the same interval, while closing semantic tolerance yields C^s_no-tol <= C_cl under a fair comparison. These results clarify that semantic hiding is not unconditionally superior to classical hiding. Binary, ternary, and Gaussian examples confirm the degeneration under one-to-one mappings and the capacity loss under coarsening.
