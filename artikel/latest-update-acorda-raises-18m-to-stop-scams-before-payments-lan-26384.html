Acorda, a London-based anti-fraud startup set up by three former Monzo employees, has secured £1.8m in a pre-seed round as it builds a network that lets banks and payment providers pool scam intelligence.

In a recent development, according to DealRoom, Eileen Burbidge has backed the round. The startup will put the capital towards bringing banks and payment firms onto its data-sharing network.

The platform gives financial institutions a secure way to exchange scam risk indicators linked to the person or business receiving a payment, in the moments before the funds leave the sender’s account.

According to recent reports, with this information, the sending bank can judge how risky the recipient is before releasing the transfer. Acorda’s aim is to reduce the number of scams that succeed while also lowering the volume of legitimate payments wrongly flagged as fraudulent.

The raise arrives as UK payment companies come under increasing pressure to tackle authorised push payment (APP) scams, in which victims are tricked into sending money themselves. Firms also now carry financial responsibility for compensating those who lose money to these schemes, raising the cost of failing to intervene.

Acorda operates a fraud data-sharing network designed for banks and payment providers, focused on preventing scams at the point of payment rather than identifying losses once money has already moved.

The organization was founded by Robert Parrish, Jhamat Mahbubani and Sam Kelsey, who between them spent over three years developing fraud prevention systems at Monzo. At the digital bank, Parrish headed product for the authorised fraud team, Mahbubani ran machine learning and Kelsey oversaw engineering.
