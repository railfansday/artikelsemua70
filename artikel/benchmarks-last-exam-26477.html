Benchmarks' Last Exam

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (257 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2611.27622v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Public benchmarks guide model selection, post-training rewards and claims of progress in artificial intelligence, yet leaderboards report aggregate scores without verifying whether each task's prompt, environment and grader agree on what counts as success. How often that agreement fails in contemporary evaluations, and in which direction confirmed defects move scores, has remained unmeasured across grading designs. Here we synthesize task-level quality reviews of 749 tasks across eight frontier benchmarks and 767 public model evaluations spanning closed-form question answering, document rubrics and agentic execution to assess whether prompts, environments and graders align. Task validity stratifies sharply by grading architecture. Closed-form benchmarks revised after public error reporting reach 92.5% verified validity, compared with 29.3% across six multi-step suites that nevertheless already supply 161 verified complex tasks. Rubric suites fail through mismatches between prompts and reference checklists, whereas execution harnesses add incomplete state assertions, shared-channel leaks and grading-time collisions. Across confirmed defects, 71.7% withhold credit from compliant solutions while 28.3% permit shortcut passes, indicating that raw leaderboard ceilings understate frontier capability on many practical tasks. Grading committed patches inside a fresh container cuts confirmed runtime bypasses from 14.7% in shared containers to 2.7% (a 5.5x drop, and 0% in read-only or static suites), showing how targeted maintenance restores existing benchmarks at low cost. As the initial milestone of the long-term Benchmarks' Last Exam initiative, this synthesis launches sustained expansion across wider benchmark coverage and deeper evaluation-quality dimensions so that pursuing leaderboard gains directly advances genuine model capability.
