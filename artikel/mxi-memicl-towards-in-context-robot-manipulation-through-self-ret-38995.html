MXI-MemICL: Towards In-Context Robot Manipulation through Self-Retrieved Interaction Memory

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (193 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.32564v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: In-context learning (ICL) offers robots a way to acquire latest skills from a single demonstration without parameter updates. However, existing robotic ICL methods either rely on large-scale pretraining constrained by scarce, costly robot interaction data or require additional annotations yet struggle to generalize across tasks. To bridge this gap, we propose MXI-MemICL, a framework for memory-guided ICL in vision-language-action (VLA) models. By treating demonstrations as retrievable memory, MXI-MemICL enables in-context manipulation without additional large-scale pretraining or specialized annotations. Specifically, a visual-action causal encoder turns actions and their observed effects in demonstrations and completed interactions into reusable memory to guide execution. To retrieve experience suited to upcoming execution, an intention predictor forecasts the next stage's intent and uses it to query memory. The retrieved experience conditions the language backbone through experience-token prefixes and gated cross-attention, guiding action generation with a learned gate that regulates its influence. MXI-MemICL improves success over the baseline by +8.2% and +14.4% on RoboCasa365 and RoboTwin 2.0. Real-robot experiments show a +9.0% gain, with demonstrations adding +5.0% on seen and +16.7% on unseen tasks. The code will be made publicly available.
