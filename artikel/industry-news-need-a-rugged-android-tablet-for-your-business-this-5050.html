For me, Oukitel is synonymous with ruggedness. Every product I’ve ever been sent from them is an exercise in durability. Just picking up one of the brand’s tablets or phones makes you realize how light and delicate other brands’ devices actually are.

The RT10 tablet is no exception. It’s thick, heavy, and sturdy, but it’s no gimmick.

We tested this tablet in our lab under 105°F conditions, and its battery life lasted 32.07 hours, so it’s built to last.

It has been revealed that this is one chubby tablet. / Manufacturer or Full Name/ZDNET.

This heavy-duty tablet can take a beating and continue running for days.

The RT10 weighs in at a chonky 1255g. To put that into perspective, the 13-inch M4 iPad Pro weighs a scant 523g. Given the RT10 is an 11-inch tablet, the 11-inch version of the same iPad only weighs 444g. The RT10 weighs nearly three times the iPad.

Of course, the iPad would shudder if you took it into the conditions the RT10 is built for.

According to recent reports, with the form factor out of the way, let’s talk about how I fared with the Oukitel RT10. After unboxing the tablet, I connected Android to my Google account and set up the usual features (fingerprint, etc). As with any Android device, it was incredibly easy.

I then went about my usual testing. The first thing I do with tablets and phones is see how responsive the UI is. In the case of the RT10, the Android UI was a bit sluggish.

Also: The best rugged tablets you can buy.

That aroused my curiosity. I decided to install a different homescreen launcher. To be specific, I went with O Launcher. Why did I do this? Primarily because O Launcher uses fewer system resources than most, I figured it might solve the tablet’s sluggishness.

Industry observers note that lo and behold, using O Launcher as my default solved that glitch. Now the RT10 runs like an absolute champ.

With speed no longer an matter, I put the tablet through the usual paces. Here are my conclusions:.

The big problem some users might have is the weight. The RT10 is as heavy as a tablet can be. What you have to remember, however, is that’s part of what makes Oukitel’s products so unique.

In a recent development, the RT10 is specifically made to thrive in tough conditions. Take this tablet to the beach, on a hike, to a construction site… it doesn’t matter because it’s got the oomph to stand up to just about anything you can throw at it.

Industry observers note that another thing I really appreciate about the Oukitel tablets is that most of them ship with a handy (and very sturdy) kickstand. The kickstand has to be attached, and it adds to the weight, but it’s very much worth using.

Gotta love these beefy kickstands.

According to recent reports, you also get a hand strap you can attach. Both of these accessories are attached by way of screws, so they are rock solid. Lastly, there’s a carrying strap you can attach to the kickstand to make carrying the RT10 around easier.

Industry observers note that the Oukitel line of devices appeals to a certain cross-section of society. If you drive a Ford F150 to work and you come home caked in dirt or construction dust, this tablet is for you. If you’re a hiker or camper, ditto. As well, if you want a tablet with an absolutely massive battery, you could do a whole lot worse than the RT10.

If, on the other hand, you prefer a tablet that weighs nothing, looks like every other tablet being carried around, and you don’t like Android, this tablet is most certainly not for you.
