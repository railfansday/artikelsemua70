Retirement Planning with Borrowing Constraints and Income Support

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (195 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.33603v1 Announce Type: fresh Abstract: We study how pension income, its pledgeability, and government support that supplements effective consumption shape an irreversible retirement decision. In a continuous-time life-cycle model, a worker chooses consumption, risky investment, and the timing of retirement, subject to a tighter borrowing limit after retirement. When retirement reduces the utility value of a given level of effective consumption, immediate retirement can be optimal on a bounded wealth interval: a worker below the interval retires when wealth rises to its lower boundary, while a worker above it retires when wealth falls to its upper boundary. Two scalar boundary equations characterize these distinct entry points. Risky investment drops discretely upon retirement at either interior boundary, even when it rises with wealth along the low-wealth working branch. In our two-boundary baseline, greater income support lowers the lower retirement threshold and raises the upper one, whereas greater pension pledgeability shifts both thresholds downward. Finally, because the residuals of the boundary equations represent weighted tail integrals of the instantaneous gain from postponing retirement, we completely characterize the optimal retirement region across all levels of post-retirement quality of life.
