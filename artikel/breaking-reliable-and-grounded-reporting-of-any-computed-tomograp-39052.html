Breaking: Reliable and grounded reporting of any computed tomography scan with FORA

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (263 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.32823v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Artificial intelligence systems for computed tomography (CT) remain largely restricted to specific anatomical regions, examination types or predefined abnormalities, limiting their ability to provide comprehensive and spatially grounded interpretations. Here we present FORA, a general-purpose CT model that recognizes and spatially localizes 1,028 hierarchically organized radiological concepts and generates reports across the 28 CT protocols represented in our cohort. Trained on 304,962 routine CT examinations supervised by their accompanying reports, FORA learns to localize abnormalities without manually annotated lesions. Its architecture combines multiple-instance learning with an interpretable concept bottleneck that connects spatial evidence, radiological findings and report generation. We first developed and benchmarked the architecture on chest radiographs, achieving state-of-the-art performance, before training a separate model on three-dimensional CT examinations. On 36,619 held-out CT examinations, FORA achieved a macro area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.918 for radiological concept recognition, exceeding the strongest of 18 existing CT models by 0.21. FORA also ranked first on three public benchmarks, outperforming Merlin on its own test set without training on that institution's data (AUROC 0.907 versus 0.826). On independent localization benchmarks for pulmonary emboli, renal masses and rib fractures, FORA's strongest spatial evidence fell within annotated lesions at rates up to 348 times greater than chance. FORA achieved more than threefold higher macro-averaged finding recall than the strongest existing report generator. These results show that a single model can combine broad abnormality recognition, spatial localization and report generation across 12 CT examination types, using routine reports without lesion-level supervision.
