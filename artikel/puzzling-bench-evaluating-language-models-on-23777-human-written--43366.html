Puzzling-Bench: Evaluating Language Models on 23,777 Human-Written Puzzles with Community-Verified Answers

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (244 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.34331v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: We introduce Puzzling-Bench, a benchmark of 23,777 puzzles with community-verified answers drawn from the Puzzling Stack Exchange archive (2014-2026): riddles, ciphers, logic grids, lateral-thinking problems, wordplay and recreational mathematics, a third of them with images. Every puzzle carries the answer accepted by its author, a short reference answer taken from it (LLM-extracted for the evaluation sets and checked on a 50-puzzle human review), the author's own hints, community tags and a creation date, so that the benchmark supports hint ablations, category analysis and a rolling post-cutoff slice (254 puzzles posted after the training cutoff of six of the nine models we evaluate, including the best one) as a contamination check. We evaluate nine frontier and open models with a two-tier scorer (normalized exact match, then an LLM judge) and validate the judge with three judges from two model families: they agree on 95-97% of verdicts (kappa 0.82-0.96), move no model by more than 1.6 points, and show no own-family preference. The best model, GPT-6.1, reaches 74.8% on a shared 255-puzzle set; a hard core of 17 puzzles defeats all nine models; model failures are strongly correlated, and the second-best model contributes a single puzzle to a greedy ensemble, yet an oracle over all nine reaches 91%. On a 100-puzzle multimodal pilot, withholding the question's image costs the two best models 24 and 35 points. We release the dataset, the scoring pipeline and all per-row provenance.
