PowerFix: Physics-Guided Iterative Repair for LLM-Assisted Power Converter Design

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (201 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.33712v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly being explored for automated engineering design. However, repairing power-converter designs that fail engineering validation remains challenging: the system must identify physical causes, locate responsible design variables, and resolve violations without compromising other constraints. We introduce PowerFix, a physics-guided iterative repair framework that converts validation failures into structured diagnoses and executable patches while preserving topology and design requirements. PowerDiagGraph links failed checks to possible physical causes and constrains repair routes. An LLM prioritizes these routes, while a deterministic compiler translates selected repairs into authorized field-level edits. Candidates are validated on isolated design copies and committed only after all required engineering checks pass; failed validation can guide replanning. On our constructed 96-task flyback repair benchmark, comprising injected single faults, coupled faults, and held-out natural failures from our design generator, PowerFix achieves 78.7% verified repair success rate (VRSR), compared with 30.1% for the strongest of three LLM baselines. Three additional replanning rounds increase VRSR to 88.4%. These offline results provide pilot-stage evidence within a simulation-based design workflow, with hardware prototype validation and transfer to a second converter topology as the next steps toward deployment.
