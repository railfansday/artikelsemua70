Hybrid AI for AI-Native RAN Control: A Functional Perspective

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (228 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.33035v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: AI-native radio access network (RAN) control involves interdependent decisions across network entities, protocol layers, and timescales. Understanding how AI and conventional methods can jointly support these decisions requires a common basis for comparing their functional roles. This survey introduces a technique-agnostic taxonomy of RAN decision-making organized around four complementary capabilities: Representation and Reasoning, Prediction and Simulation, Adaptive Control, and Decision Synthesis. These capabilities are defined by their roles in the control process, independently of the techniques used to implement them. Through this perspective, we examine representative AI and model-based approaches, including foundation models, world models, reinforcement learning, and optimization. We then apply the taxonomy to representative RAN control solutions across slow, medium, fast, and cross-timescale applications to identify which capabilities they implement and how these capabilities are combined in hybrid AI systems. We also review datasets, benchmarks, simulators, digital twins, and experimental platforms according to their support for the decision-making process and reproducible evaluation. The review highlights a gap between the availability of resources for closed-loop experimentation and the standardization of tasks, metrics, and evaluation protocols needed for meaningful comparisons.Finally, we identify research directions for hybrid AI in AI-native RAN, including capability integration, multi-agent and cross-timescale coordination, constraint handling, and reproducible closed-loop evaluation. A curated list of surveyed works and evaluation resources is available at https://github.com/pegahani/Hybrid-AI-RAN-Survey.
