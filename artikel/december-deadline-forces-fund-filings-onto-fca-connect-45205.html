The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) is overhauling how fund managers submit authorisation applications and regulatory notifications, and the window for adapting is closing promptly.

According to Zeidler, the regulator is replacing its long-standing email-based process with a fully digital system built around FCA Connect, a move that will reshape the administration of both UK-authorised funds and foreign-recognised funds.

It has been revealed that zeidler notes that the FCA intends to roll out the change in stages over two years. The regulator’s stated aims are better data quality, stronger oversight and governance, and greater clarity for management companies and investment managers about what the FCA expects and how it reaches decisions. In practice, though, firms will need to rethink internal workflows around authorisation and ongoing fund administration.

The first phase came into force on 2 November 2026. From that date, a defined set of fund authorisation applications and notifications can only be submitted through Connect, with email submissions no longer accepted. Zeidler highlights that this covers newly introduced scheme authorisation forms, including Form 12 for investment companies with variable capital (ICVCs), Form 242 for authorised unit trusts (AUTs) and Form 261C for authorised contractual schemes (ACSs).

In a recent development, forms to amend or wind up authorised funds are also caught. These include Form 21 for ICVC amendments under the OEIC Regulations, Form 251 for AUT changes and Form 261Q for ACS changes under FSMA, as well as Form 283A for UCITS amendments, Form 9 for domestic UCITS mergers and the UCITS feeder form used to stop operating as a feeder. Final notifications confirming that a change has been made, submitted via Form FN, must likewise go through the portal along with all supporting documents.

It has been revealed that the second phase, scheduled for 1 December 2026, is likely to have a broader operational impact. Zeidler explains that the FCA looks to move scheme document filings for UK-authorised funds, and for recognised funds under the Overseas Fund Regime (OFR) and Section 272 of FSMA, onto Connect.

For cross-border managers running recognised funds such as Irish and Luxembourg UCITS, this is significant. Prospectus updates, annual and semi-annual reports and UCITS KIDs will all need to be filed through the FCA portal rather than by email. The same will apply to the Product Summary document, which is set to replace UCITS KIDs under the UK’s fresh Consumer Composite Investments (CCI) regime.

The transition will not end there. Zeidler points out that the FCA intends to extend Connect submissions to certain Alternative Investment Fund Managers Directive (AIFMD) applications and notifications in early 2027, with further reforms expected during that year.

As Connect becomes the central hub for UK fund authorisation and notification activity, Zeidler stresses that funds and their advisers should prioritise familiarity with the platform now.
