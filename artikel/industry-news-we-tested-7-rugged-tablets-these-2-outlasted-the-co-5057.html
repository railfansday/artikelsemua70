For some, a tablet is a lightweight e-reader, or a more portable way to browse the web, take notes, and stream movies. For others, a tablet is a capable and durable device that needs to withstand outdoor conditions like fieldwork, camping, or construction sites. In these instances, you’ll need a rugged tablet.

Industry observers note that rugged tablets differ from regular tablets in that they are usually military-grade certified for durability and can operate in more extreme temperatures. Since their build and intent are different from regular tablets, we decided to develop a upcoming test to compare rugged tablets and their performance capabilities.

ZDNET’s lab in Louisville, KY, tested seven tablets from brands like Samsung, Dell, and more, by putting them in frigid-cold and sweltering-hot conditions to see how their battery life would hold up. Based on our extensive testing, the Samsung Galaxy Tab Active 5 Pro won our cold test, and the Oukitel RT10 won our hot test.

Sources indicate that to test these tablets, we first needed to test their baseline battery life. Gianmarco Chumbe, our lab’s product testing manager, battery-tested these tablets at room temperature (72°F) with the following configurations: screens set to ~500 nits of brightness, Airplane Mode on, a looped playback of the same 1080p H.264 video file (no audio track) in VLC, and the system audio muted. Each tablet started out at 100% battery.

To document battery degradation, a lightweight Python script ran on each tablet throughout the test and logged the battery level every minute. For Windows tablets — like the Dell Pro Rugged models — it read the battery status via the psutil library. Android tablets like the Samsung Galaxy Tab Active 5 Pro used the Termux app to read the battery status via the Termux API. Each tablet plays the looped video, with logging running, until playback stops. We found that Windows tablets were set to shut down at their critical battery level, and Android tablets entered a dormant state at about 1%.

According to recent reports, after getting the baseline results above, Chumbe focused on our cold test, which consisted of placing each tablet in a consumer freezer set to -6°F and running the same configuration as above to simulate a harsh winter environment. Battery life drops were documented once per minute — the same as the baseline test.

The heat test required a more complex setup: Chumbe created a “tablet sauna” using a 40 × 20 × 20-inch enclosure with walls made of half-inch PVC board and a quarter-inch acrylic front panel for viewing the tablets during testing. A 400 Watt fan-forced PTC heater sat at one end of the chamber, while a 20 × 12-in aluminum baffle, positioned 10 inches in front of the heater, blocked its direct airflow, so no tablet was directly heated by it. A circulation fan ran continuously to keep the air evenly mixed.

The thermostat was set between 104 and 106°F, with the heater cycling roughly every 2 minutes (30 seconds on and 90 seconds off). A second, independent thermostat cut power to the heater if the air reached 115°F, protecting the units if the main thermostat failed. Finally, a single 1 ⅛-in. port, sealed around the cables, carried the power and sensor cords. Again, the same configurations and documentation process were used.

In a recent development, some caveats to keep in mind about our testing: the test temperatures reflect the surrounding air, not the device temperature, and while every tablet ran the same video at the same measured brightness, the screen size, panel, processor, and battery capacity differ for each tablet tested. Runtimes reflect each tablet as sold and aren’t normalized for battery size.

As you can see from Fig. 1 above, the Samsung Galaxy Tab Active 5 Pro won our cold test, lasting 12 hours and 47 minutes at -6°F. An honorable mention goes to the Oukitel RT10, which remained at 1% battery for one hour.

Read the reviews: Samsung Galaxy Tab Active 5 Pro | Oukitel RT10.

Sources indicate that an interesting result we had was that neither of the Dell Rugged Tablets completed our cold test. The Dell Pro Rugged 12 Tablet tapped out at 5.10 hours at 78% battery, and the Dell Pro Rugged 10 Tablet stopped working after 6.23 hours at 67% battery. Both tablets displayed “Extreme cold T detected, shutting down for safety.”.

In a recent development, according to Dell’s official operating manual, both tablets should be able to withstand temperatures as low as -20.2°F. Dell states in its manual that performance can be reduced once temperatures reach 5°F, but the whole point of rugged tablets is to withstand harsh elements without needing to move to a warmer area for them to work.

ZDNET reached out to Dell to comment on these results, and we will patch this story once they respond.

In our heat test, the tablet that lasted the longest in our tablet sauna was the Oukitel RT10, which persisted for 32.07 hours. And even though the Dell Pro Rugged tablet didn’t complete our cold test, it came in second for our heat test and lasted 21.65 hours.

As you can see from both Fig. 1 and Fig. 2, the DT Research Rugged Tablet DT312RP had the worst-performing battery out of both tests; however, it wasn’t far off from its original baseline result of 4.43 hours, and even outperformed that baseline number in the heat test (4.65 hours).

Our test results might differ from your own experiences with these tablets, especially depending on the temperature at which you use them and the applications/connections you have running. However, as a benchmark, our testing provides real-world results on how different rugged tablets can handle extreme conditions.
