Scoring Recovery-Time Agent Security: Proposals, Effects, and Retry Instructions

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (246 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.31318v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: A timed-out tool write may be absent, committed, or still in flight. Evaluating an agent only on whether its goal exists can hide harmful recovery actions: auditing 12 supplied traces changes apparent completion from 10/12 to 8/12. We introduce an executable evaluation that separately records tool proposals, runtime decisions, and committed effects. In 3,456 model episodes, the original task wording produces only 39/576 valid recoveries when the initial write is absent; a combined clarification produces 576/576. A further 1,584 episodes test stronger attacks and separate the effect definition from the recovery recipe with three rollouts per absent-state cell. Definition alone yields 276/288 valid recoveries; recipe alone yields 288/288. For Luna, malicious text is associated with 11.1 percentage points fewer valid recoveries under both original and definition-only wording, an availability signal limited by shared tasks and templates. The stronger attacks produce no attempted unauthorized or changed-key action and no unsafe effect. A trajectory audit finds six original-key retries after observing pending status: five denied and one deduplicated. Bounded checks distinguish atomic key enforcement from a delayed-commit race; stale negative reads also invalidate the evidence gate. A separate Claude Code program adds 720 successful episodes: 288 under the original attacks and 432 stronger-attack calibration episodes across Opus, Sonnet, and Haiku 5.5, with no observed proposal or effect deviations. The contribution is a measurement design and a diagnosis of availability, specification, and contract sensitivity, with no demonstrated general defense efficacy.
