Bringing AI Assistance to the Beznau Nuclear Power Plant: A Hierarchical System of LLM Agents for Reactor Monitoring

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (221 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.27556v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Beznau Unit 1 in Switzerland, in operation since 1969, is the most experienced nuclear reactor still running worldwide. Five decades of incremental modernization have left its monitoring systems, alarm logs, variable catalogs, technical specifications and investigation reports in separate silos that were never designed to be queried together. As a result, investigating a core instrumentation anomaly means slow, manual gathering of knowledge scattered across databases, documents, and experienced colleagues. We present a hierarchical multi-agent assistant built on large language models (LLMs) to support these investigations and reduce the need for tedious manual searches in SAP systems. An orchestrating agent breaks down an engineer's question and hands the parts to specialized sub-agents, each with read-only access to one source. Domain expertise is written as natural-language procedures that plant engineers can read, review and revise. The system is strictly advisory, with every answer traced back to its sources. We also report organizational lessons from introducing agentic AI into a culture built on safety, traceability and conservative change. Beyond the technical architecture, the Beznau case study examines how an assistant can be introduced into a setting where evidence must be traceable, changes carefully reviewed, and operational decisions retained by plant personnel, paving the way for the responsible use of agentic AI in industrial plant monitoring.
