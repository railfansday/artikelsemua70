AI investment fuels Asia Pacific’s $302bn equity surge

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, asia Pacific’s capital markets went into the final quarter of 2026 in strong form, according to new analysis from LSEG Data & Analytics. Equity and equity-linked issuance across Asia Pacific excluding Japan hit $302bn in the first nine months, up 57.2% year-on-year and the highest level since 2021. Globally, ECM activity reached $870.7bn over the same period, a rise of 62%.

The quarterly figures tell a more interesting story. LSEG Data & Analytics found that global ECM issuance fell 26% in the third quarter compared with Q2. Asia Pacific excluding Japan went the other way, with proceeds climbing 121% quarter-on-quarter. This happened even though the number of offerings fell 2.1% over nine months, which suggests that a few very large deals drove the gains.

Technology was at the centre of the rally. High Technology raised $123.9bn, or 41% of regional ECM proceeds, an increase of 269.4% on the same period in 2025. The largest deals included SK Hynix’s $26.5bn NASDAQ ADR offering and Alibaba’s $10.2bn Hong Kong placement, the largest primary follow-on ever by a HK-listed tech giant. They also included CXMT’s $9.8bn Shanghai IPO and a $7.8bn follow-on from Zhongji Innolight.

Recent findings suggest that LSEG Deals Intelligence analyst Vianca Sanchez said, “AI is increasingly becoming a capital markets story as much as a technology story. Building out the infrastructure to support AI requires enormous investment in semiconductors, computing capacity, data centres and connectivity, creating financing needs that extend well beyond the technology sector itself.”.

Industry observers note that the pressure goes beyond equity. LSEG Data & Analytics reports that debt issuers raised a record $4.16tn in the first nine months, up 3.7% year-on-year. Government and Agency borrowers accounted for 45.2% of proceeds and Financials for 35.1%. China’s deep domestic bond market and large refinancing needs underpinned much of this activity. High Technology bond issuance also climbed 19% to $30.3bn, a sign that AI-related funding needs are spreading into debt markets.

Industry observers note that growth was uneven across the region. China accounted for 52.8% of ECM proceeds, raising $159.4bn, up 69.9%. South Korea’s proceeds rose 189% and Taiwan’s rose 291%. Singapore reached $7.4bn, up 28.2%, and its Q3 total of $3.9bn was more than four times the previous quarter. India’s total slipped 1.7% to $40.8bn, but its IPO proceeds rose 11.3% to $12.5bn, the highest first-nine-month figure since records began in 1980.

Industry observers note that sanchez said, “The overall picture is one of strong capital-markets activity, but the detail matters. Momentum differs across markets and sectors, and the mix of issuance continues to evolve. What stands out is the breadth of capital raising we have seen across the region through the first nine months of the year.”.

China International Capital Co led the ECM underwriting league table with $23.9bn in proceeds and a 7.9% share. Morgan Stanley followed at $22.6bn, with Goldman Sachs & Co third at $20.9bn. In debt, CITIC led with $264.6bn, ahead of China Securities Co and Guotai Haitong Securities.
