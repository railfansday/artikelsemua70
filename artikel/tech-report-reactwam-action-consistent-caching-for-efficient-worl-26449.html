Tech Report: ReActWAM: Action-Consistent Caching for Efficient World-Action Models

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (179 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.27372v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: World-action models (WAMs) often generate future visual states alongside robot actions, introducing substantial computational overhead during inference. During closed-loop execution, consecutive inference cycles often predict similar action sequences, leading to redundant computation. However, reducing this redundancy requires distinguishing consistent actions from changes that demand fresh computation. To address these challenges, we propose ReActWAM, a training-free framework that combines action-intent-guided prefix skipping with inner-chunk prediction reuse. A coarse-to-fine assessment evaluates motion changes, visual consistency, and action prediction conflicts. For eligible chunks, we draw on first-step differences to adapt the cached latent and network features to the current condition, providing an estimate at a later denoising step that bypasses the redundant prefix. Subsequent inference refines this estimate while reusing stable denoising predictions within the chunk. Experiments across LingBot-VA, FastWAM and the Cosmos family on LIBERO-Long and RoboTwin 2.0 demonstrate $1.41\times$--$5.44\times$ GPU inference speedup, with minimal degradation in task success rates. Real-world experiments on a Franka Research 3 (FR3) robot further achieve $1.46\times$ end-to-end speedup while maintaining success rate.
