Tech Report: Almost $1bn raised in FinTech deals in strong week for investment

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Across the 17 deals reported on by FinTech Global this week, there was a total of $991m raised – showcasing a continuous strength in firms securing investment.

This was up on last week, where total of $602m was raised across 14 FinTech deals, a small increase on the previous week where $454.4m was raised across 12 deals.

Two of the top deals this week came from Bulgarian PayTech firm Payhawk and Model ML, a personal AI platform for financial and professional services.

It has been revealed that geographically, the US secured the most deals this week with 8 in total. Trailing in second was Canada with three, whilst Israel, Egypt, Spain, Georgia, the UK and Bulgaria pulled in one.

Market-wise, financial infrastructure firms made up the majority of this week’s deals with four. In joint second were WealthTech and CyberTech firms on three, PayTech, InsurTech and Blockchain firms secured two.

Recent findings suggest that this week, research from FinTech Global showcased findings of a powerful performance for Global FinTech in this year’s third quarter. The Global FinTech market attracted $27.5bn across 1,275 deals in Q3 2026. Funding climbed 35% from $20.3bn in Q3 2025 but fell 11% from $30.9bn in Q2 2026.

Deal volume fell 2% from 1,306 transactions in Q3 2025 and rose 5% from 1,220 in Q2 2026.

Payhawk raises $280m to build AI-era Finance OS.

Industry observers note that payhawk, the Sofia-founded spend management and finance platform, has closed a $280m Series C round led by Growth Equity at Goldman Sachs Alternatives. The deal values the tech giant at $2bn.

Industry observers note that the raise takes Payhawk’s total funding past $515m. Every existing backer returned for the round, among them QED, Greenoaks, Lightspeed, Hedosophia and Bek Ventures.

They were joined by first-time investors Galdana Ventures, Cogito Capital, Inveready and Invus, as well as European founders Tomas Okmanas and Didac Lee.

Model ML bags $250m to give every banker an AI agent.

Model ML, a London-based personal AI platform for financial and professional services, has secured $250m in a Series B round that values the company at $1.5bn.

It has been revealed that general Atlantic and QED Investors co-led the investment. Evolution Equity Partners, Airtree, Forestay Capital, Y Combinator, FT Partners, Latitude, Inception Fund and 13Books Capital also took part. The deal lifts the total capital raised by Model ML to $350m.

Industry observers note that the raise comes after a period of rapid expansion. Over the third quarter of 2026, annual recurring revenue at the firm increased twelvefold compared with the same period a year earlier, while daily active owners rose 26-fold.

Blue J lands $176m to put AI agents on tax desks.

Blue J, a tax research and advisory platform, has secured $176m in a Series E round that values the business at above $700m, as it moves beyond answering tax questions to handing the actual workload over to AI agents.

JMI Equity, a growth equity investor, led the raise. Existing backers Oak HC/FT and Sapphire Ventures returned for the round, joined by Intrepid Growth Partners. The valuation also exceeds C$1bn. As part of the deal, JMI Equity principal Jack Duane will take a seat on the Blue J board.

The company plans to put the capital behind Workflows, its agentic AI product for tax practitioners, and to push further into the United Kingdom and other international markets.

Corma hits $1bn valuation with $125m Series A.

Corma, an AI research lab building foundation models for defensive cybersecurity, has secured $125m in Series A funding at a $1bn valuation, only three months after revealing its seed round.

Recent findings suggest that NEA led the investment, with participation from returning backers Sequoia Capital, Khosla Ventures and Coatue. The latest raise takes the firm’s total funding to $185m.

Corma’s $60m seed round, led by Sequoia, was announced in August.

TBC Georgia secures $51m as ADB and IFC back trade finance.

TBC Georgia, a leading Georgian financial institution and member of the London-listed TBC Bank Group, has raised $51m from international commercial banks to widen the availability of trade finance for businesses across Georgia.

The capital comes with guarantee backing from the Asian Development Bank (ADB) and the International Finance Corporation (IFC).

In a recent development, it is channelled through two established schemes: the ADB’s Trade and Supply Chain Finance Program and the IFC’s Global Trade Finance Program. Because both institutions stand behind the facility, commercial lenders can extend capital to the Georgian market with added assurance.

Codex lands $30m Series A to scale stablecoin FX.

Codex, a platform that provides foreign exchange infrastructure for stablecoins and fiat currencies, has secured $30m in Series A funding to expand its cross-border payments business.

In a recent development, pantera headed the investment, which also drew backing from Dragonfly, Coinbase Ventures, Tenet, Reciprocal, Fin Capital, LeadBlock and additional investors. The deal puts the tech giant’s post-money valuation at $130m.

Both Dragonfly and Coinbase Ventures have backed Codex before, having taken part in its seed round.

Arva AI lands $20m Series A for AI banking agents.

Arva AI, a firm that deploys AI agents to make critical banking decisions, has secured $20m in Series A funding as it looks to push its accuracy-focused technology deeper into the bank back office.

It has been revealed that according to the tech giant, most back-office decisions at banks are still made manually, but this has begun to shift. Over the past year, Arva’s customers started entrusting important decisions to its agents, with staff setting policies and monitoring outcomes.

During that period the firm rolled out its technology at scale with banks and FinTechs, among them a top-10 US bank, a payments institution listed on the S&P 500 and the global FinTech Wise.

StepSecurity bags $20m as supply chain attacks escalate.

In a recent development, elephant led the round, and existing backers Runtime Ventures, Inner Loop Capital and SaaS Ventures also took part. Together with a $3m seed round raised earlier, the deal lifts StepSecurity’s total funding to $23m.

The organization prepares to use the money to speed up development and wider rollout of its platform, which is designed to stop malicious packages at every checkpoint of the agentic software pipeline.

Vero Security raises $11m as AI security jobs go unstaffed.

Vero Security, a start-up developing a platform for AI Security Engineering, has closed an $11m seed round as large enterprises struggle to staff a responsibility that most still treat as a side duty.

Sources indicate that TLV Partners led the investment, joined by GTM Capital, Maple Capital and a group of angel backers. Vero was established in 2025 and has already signed several Fortune 500 businesses as customers.

Alongside the raise, it is introducing AISecurityEngineering.org, an open community and knowledge hub offering practical field guides, workshops and the ASE credential track for practitioners shaping this newly introduced specialism.

Roadzen raises $10m for India arm at $300m valuation.

Roadzen, which applies AI to insurance and mobility, has secured roughly $10m (₹94 crore) for its Indian subsidiary, Roadzen AI Limited, which is now valued at about $300m post-money on a stand-alone basis.

Recent findings suggest that the round is being raised entirely as common equity. It carries no warrants or preferred rights, and the newly introduced shares rank equally with those held by the existing shareholder, Roadzen.

Because the capital goes into the subsidiary, shareholders in the Nasdaq-listed parent will not be diluted. Family offices and capital markets investors anchored the deal. Several of them had backed the firm’s earlier India subsidiary round and raised their commitments this time.

Valeria bags €5.5m seed to scale AI payroll engine.

Valeria, a Barcelona-headquartered payroll and workforce management platform built on artificial intelligence, has secured €5.5m in seed funding to expand its technology for businesses that see frequent staff changes.

In a recent development, the round was led by BlackFin, with returning backers VentureFriends and Fortino also participating. GoHub Ventures came on board as a newly introduced investor in the tech giant.

The seed raise follows a €1.7m pre-seed round completed in February 2026, meaning Valeria has secured over €7m in total during 2026.

WealthTech finny raises Rs 5.3 Cr angel round.

finny, a WealthTech start-up offering personalised financial advice, has reportedly secured Rs 5.3 Cr (around $560,000) through an angel round, according to a report from BW Disrupt.

Industry observers note that A group of high-profile backers joined the round. Among them were Ramanathan RV, who founded both Hyperface and Juspay, and Anil Goel, who serves as Global CTO at Nielsen. Uday Sodhi, previously an executive at Sony Pictures, also took part, as did Aditya Pittie, MD and CEO of Pittie Group. Angel syndicate Zorvian LLP was among the other participants.

Industry observers note that the tech giant intends to split the new money across three priorities. A portion will go towards R&D, with an emphasis on building out its AI-driven products.

Pretxt raises $5m to keep AI agents within the rules.

Pretxt, a newly launched enterprise AI company focused on runtime oversight of AI agents, has stepped out of stealth mode with $5m in seed capital to build out a technology category it calls AI Supervision.

Deciens Capital, Howdy Partners, Service Provider Capital and NSV Wolf Capital backed the round. Pretxt intends to use the capital to bring its supervision layer to enterprises that want AI agents to handle higher-stakes work without drifting from approved procedures.

Sources indicate that AI agents no longer simply produce text. They now speak with customers, connect to other systems, handle transactions and run business processes. According to Pretxt, these agents do not always behave as instructed.

Verlo raises $4.5m to cut admin work for wealth firms.

Verlo, a provider of an AI operating system built for wealth management firms, has secured $4.5m CAD in seed funding to help Registered Investment Advisors (RIAs) cut back on time-consuming administrative tasks.

Sources indicate that staircase Ventures led the investment, while Pelorus VC came back to back the organization again. They were joined by several angel investors, among them founders and senior executives from Verafin, the Newfoundland financial crime software business that Nasdaq bought in 2020 for $2.7bn.

Global Kiwi Labs raises $4m for TheHUB.

Global Kiwi Labs, the developer behind digital asset colocation network TheHUB, has closed a $4m seed round as it looks to bring the equal-access trading architecture used by major traditional exchanges to digital asset markets.

Valor Capital Group led the round. Adderall Capital, Keyrock and Staking Facilities also invested, alongside a small number of angel backers.

TheHUB’s first site, which is located within the Equinix TY15 data centre in Tokyo and is now live and bringing on exchanges, trading firms and validators. The manufacturer will also use the money to add engineers to its team and move into further regions.

Connect Money raises $3.5m to enter Saudi Arabia and Morocco.

Connect Money, an Egypt-based FinTech offering embedded finance and white-label card services, has secured $3.5m in a pre-Series A round as it gears up to take its platform into Saudi Arabia and Morocco.

The round was led by returning backers Mohamed Farouk, Algebra Ventures and DisrupTech Ventures, according to a report from Wamda.

Sources indicate that the deal blends latest funding with the sale of secondary shares, giving a number of the startup’s early backers a partial exit. Connect Money has not revealed what portion of the $3.5m is new capital.

Eight by Zero lands $3m seed to fix insurance integration.

Eight by Zero, a Montréal-based InsurTech offering a universal interoperability platform for the insurance sector, has secured $3m in seed funding to expand its connectivity technology across more lines of coverage.

The round was led by Diagram, with Canada Life joining as a strategic investor.

Alongside the raise, the firm, also known as 8/0, has named Munich Re Life US, part of the Munich Re Group, as its reinsurance partner. The two businesses are collaborating to tackle the difficulties group and disability insurance clients face in keeping employee data connected.
