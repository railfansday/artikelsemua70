According to recent reports, the latest iPad Mini with the A20 Pro has become my go-to tablet for the past few weeks, and one thing is clear: it’s the most dramatic Mini upgrade in years. The OLED display is a meaningful upgrade from the LCD, and water resistance is a first for the iPad — and a win for durability.

Recent findings suggest that I’ve tested other iPad Minis and have always loved how Apple packs a lot of power into such a portable device. As soon as you turn on the 2026 iPad Mini, however, the difference in the display quality is immediately evident. While someone upcoming to iPads may not notice how much better the OLED is on the Mini, anyone with older models will.

The colors are much richer, with deeper blacks and vibrant contrast. This makes the move from LCD to OLED a very successful choice for Apple, with the latest display making rich content more impressive to view. It also makes it feel like a more premium product, despite its diminutive size. The price matches, starting at $699 — $100 more than the previous Mini model at launch.

Sources indicate that the 2026 iPad Mini is ever so slightly larger, with an 8.5-inch display, about 0.2 inches larger than the previous model. The extra real estate matters, giving you more screen space to view content and work, which I particularly appreciated when I did graphic design work with the Apple Pencil Pro.

In a recent development, the second biggest upgrade is the iPad Mini’s durability. This is the first water-resistant iPad, the first to feature an IP67 rating. Many buyers purchase the iPad Mini for the younger crowd, often for older kids and teenagers, who are also the rowdiest tablet users.

Industry observers note that having water resistance gives you peace of mind not only when kids are using it as their tablet but also when you use it by the pool, in the kitchen, outdoors, or in workplaces where larger tablets are less convenient. At some point, your iPad will come into contact with liquid, and the latest Mini will have an easier time of it.

Sources indicate that buying smaller tablets often means compromising performance, but that’s not the case with the latest iPad Mini generation, as evidenced in our benchmarking tests. In fact, the gulf between the Mini and the iPad Pro (M5) is no longer so dramatic.

The A20 Pro is a substantial upgrade from the previous model’s A17 Pro, and in benchmark scores, the iPad Mini was 80% faster than the A17 Pro in single-core CPU performance and 91% faster in multi-core performance.

In a recent development, the GPU performance was 191% faster than its predecessor and 15% faster than the iPad Air (M4). The smaller, cheaper iPad Mini is no longer a compromise — it’s a worthy choice for a more compact form factor.

Overall, the newest iPad Mini performed much closer to the iPad Air and iPad Pro, though M-series processors remain dominant in multi-core workloads. For creative work, like exporting video, processing large images, and running demanding apps, the M-series chips unsurprisingly outperform the iPad Mini.

Sources indicate that the biggest downside of the iPad Mini is its price, and the fact that it’s now only $80 cheaper than the M4 iPad Air, making buyers wonder whether it’s worth the cost. Right now, for example, you can get an 11-inch iPad Air with the M4 processor for only $649, which is $20 cheaper than the 8.5-inch iPad Mini.

The Air is probably the closest iPad to the Mini in terms of performance and form factor, but it comes down to what you’re looking for in a tablet. The Air is larger, but it doesn’t feature the Mini’s OLED display, which is a little crisper and more vibrant. The Air also scored higher on our multi-core benchmarking, meaning it’s likely a little snappier for multitasking across multiple apps.

Sources indicate that the iPad Mini (2026) has a defined purpose: instead of being simply a smaller iPad, Apple is selling a premium portable tablet that goes with you where other tablets can’t. Smaller than many paperback books, the Mini can easily fit in your bag or purse without weighing you down.

After a back fracture earlier this year, I particularly appreciate a powerful tablet that only weighs 9 ounces, as it couldn’t be easier to carry around. The iPad Mini is the lightest and thinnest iPad ever, and it packs the performance of tablets nearly twice its size.
