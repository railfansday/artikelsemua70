Mechanism Parity for Simulation-Based Validation of Autonomous-Driving Perception

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (240 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.27447v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Simulation is central to intelligent-vehicle validation, but visual realism or output agreement between a real scene and its synthetic counterpart does not prove that a perception system used the same evidence in both domains. This paper addresses that mechanism-parity gap for paired real-synthetic autonomous-driving perception. It unifies Decisive-Feature Fidelity, which compares decisive evidence maps in a frozen system under test (SUT), with Concept-Decisive Feature Fidelity, which projects decisive evidence into semantic scene concepts for diagnosis. The framework is a validation diagnostic rather than a standalone safety certificate: it asks whether output-consistent simulation also preserves the mechanism by which the perception model reaches that output. The empirical evidence comprises ten retrained diffusion ControlNet generators evaluated on a deterministic exact-geometry KITTI-Virtual KITTI 2 subset. Generated counterparts use a 1248-by-376-pixel padded inference canvas and are cropped back to the exact KITTI dimensions before evaluation. The final benchmark reports target-image, output-consistency, and mechanism metrics recomputed from a single audited output set, then adds metric-disagreement cases, bootstrap uncertainty, threshold sensitivity, and concept/cache integrity audits. Across the benchmark, output and appearance fidelity are at most weakly and inconsistently rank-correlated with decisive-mechanism distance for all three frozen SUTs, and a constructed positive control highlights the estimator detects a known planted mechanism that output agreement misses, supporting decisive-evidence diagnostics for intelligent-vehicle simulation validation. All numerical claims are restricted to the declared exact-geometry benchmark, ten generators, and three frozen SUTs.
