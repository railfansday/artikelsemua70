This synchronized collapse has persisted despite extensive pro-natalist financial interventions, revealing a structural limitation in conventional linear demographic models. This paper proposes that the ubiquity of mobile internet and Artificial Intelligence acts as a synthetic mating disruptor, precipitating a non-linear phase transition in human pair-bonding dynamics. Treating the marriage market as a complex system governed by thermodynamic constraints, we present a Fermi-Dirac selection model, using national income distributions as the input spectrum and fitted with Japanese longitudinal data to illustrate how digital hyper-reality generates an increasingly prohibitive Marriage Energy Barrier. Our model quantifies a Tech Factor that decouples female mating expectations from local economic realities during initial courtship, a distortion we predict will be severely exacerbated as AI evolves into an emotional substitute. Projections indicate that this substitution effect is accelerating; without intervention, the world post 2040 risks entering an irreversible demographic contraction, as the sudden surge in TFR required for recovery is biologically impossible. Ultimately, this framework explains why traditional financial subsidies fail to reverse falling birth rates and suggests that digital convenience may represent a behavioral Great Filter that erodes the imperative for species persistence.
