LUDIUS: A Benchmark for Embodied Social Intelligence in Everyday Human-Robot Interactions

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (213 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.34328v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Social intelligence in a robot requires interpreting a situation from the perspectives of the people involved and using that understanding to decide when and how to act. We introduce LUDIUS, a benchmark for evaluating how vision-language models exercise this judgment through a robot's verbal responses and actions, organized around 15 social intelligence skills drawn from social psychology, sociology, and cognitive science. LUDIUS comprises six editions, each a collection of episodes spanning one simulated week. Three editions each cover all 15 skills in a different household. The other three focus on interactions involving multiple people, safety, and context-specific norms, respectively. We evaluate frontier models and a smaller model finetuned on social-interaction data we generated, using a common evaluation harness. The harness supplies each model with the robot's camera views and residents' reactions, retrieves relevant interaction history, and converts the model's tool calls into robot verbal responses and actions. Models tend to perform worse when they must reason about what a resident saw or heard earlier, reconcile competing interests, or fulfill a request within safety constraints and household rules. Beyond the home setting, LUDIUS's design pipeline offers a way to construct social intelligence benchmarks by translating social intelligence skills into context-specific judgments and observable robot behavior.
