AI and the Division of Labor in Public Sector Jobs and Teams

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (234 words, 📖 Standard)

According to recent reports, arXiv:2512.05659v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: How artificial intelligence (AI) affects an organization depends not only on which jobs are exposed, but on how more (versus less) exposed tasks are combined within roles. Using 283,484 UK Civil Service vacancies, we extract 2.7 million tasks and estimate their automation potential with an ensemble of eight foundation models. We find that differences among tasks within roles account for 65.3 percent of total exposure variance, while profession and grade together explain only 4.0 percent, suggesting that seemingly similar roles often differ substantially in their exposure and potential for redesign. Combining roles' mean exposure with their within-role dispersion distinguishes where workforce adjustment, job redesign, targeted task automation, or limited intervention may be most relevant. Taking account of which tasks can change as AI expands into a role, we simulate redesign at the role and team level. Role redesign cuts administration from 30.0 to 24.6 percent of working time and raises assurance tasks from 8.7 to 14.5 percent. When conducted at the team level, redesign merges overlapping roles, cutting the number of distinct roles by 17.7 percent while headcount falls by only 6.4 percent; the posts removed come from junior and middle grades, so restructured teams become weighted toward senior staff. We contribute to the automation, augmentation and productivity debates and advance our understanding of job redesign in the age of AI.
