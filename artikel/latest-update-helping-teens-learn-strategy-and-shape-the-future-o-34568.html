College Planner is coming to ChatGPT for teens, alongside a fresh teen AI council and more tools for learning.

Since we introduced ChatGPT for Teens—the default experience automatically applied to accounts identified as under 18—we’ve seen young people use it to learn, explore ideas and solve everyday problems. Today, we’re sharing early progress on ChatGPT for Teens, including how teens are using its learning tools, along with latest ways for teens to study and prepare for college.

ChatGPT’s 1.2 billion consumers span many countries, communities and backgrounds. That reach makes it especially important to build tools that help young people with different resources and opportunities learn and prepare for their futures.

ChatGPT for Teens is designed to help young people learn, think critically and deepen their understanding. As ChatGPT for Teens became available to more teens globally, those with access sent about 2.7 million more learning-related messages on average than those who didn’t have it yet.

Once enrolled, teens use ChatGPT for Teens’ learning tools at scale. The most selected conversation starter prompts are “ask questions to develop my ideas,” “give feedback on my draft,” and “help me check my evidence.” In one week, nearly 1.2 million teens used Learning Visualizations to better understand math and science concepts, and more than 180,000 used Study Mode for step-by-step learning support.

ChatGPT for Teens includes built-in reminders to encourage teens to take breaks. On average, teens spend under 15 minutes a day on ChatGPT. While less than 2% of teens spend more than three consecutive hours on ChatGPT, in almost half of teen conversations with a break reminder, teens took a break or ended their conversation within five minutes. These usage patterns remain well below the publicly reported⁠(opens in a upcoming window) average of 5 hours for teen social media usage, and reflect a different kind of experience: ChatGPT is designed for learning, building, and creativity, rather than passive scrolling. Among teens with sessions longer than three consecutive hours, over 80% had at least one prompt related to learning.

These early signs are encouraging and we’ll continue to evaluate the experience and learn from teens, families and educators to improve ChatGPT for Teens.

It has been revealed that hundreds of thousands of US teens use ChatGPT each week to blueprint for college. They’re using it to plan applications, track deadlines, research schools and understand financial aid.

Sources indicate that that’s why we’ll soon be adding College Planner to ChatGPT for Teens. We’re designing it to help students manage the practical steps of applying to college, without taking over the personal work of figuring out where they want to apply. Students have different levels of access to support with college applications. Our goal is to make help more accessible, regardless of financial circumstances, alongside guidance from counselors, advisers, and families.

College Planner brings together application requirements, deadlines, tasks, and financial-aid steps for schools on a student’s list in one plan. Students applying to several colleges can return to it to enhancement their progress, use the timeline to plan ahead and see what’s left to do. They can also ask ChatGPT questions along the way, from what a requirement means to how to get started. By bringing these steps together, we aim to save students and families time and make a stressful process feel more manageable.

Industry observers note that we’re designing the initial US experience for high-school students in grades 10–12 who are planning to attend a four-year college. We plan to bring College Planner to students in more countries and expand support to two-year colleges, technical colleges, trade schools and more over time.

College Planner also helps students figure out how to pay for college. They can get help understanding financial aid requirements, track each school’s aid deadlines, and explore scholarships and fee waivers.

Recent findings suggest that keeping track of an application is only one part of preparing for college. Many students also need someone to help them make sense of their options. Alongside College Planner, we’re supporting College Advising Corps⁠(opens in a latest window), a nonprofit that places recent college graduates as advisers in under-resourced high schools to help students navigate the path to higher education. OpenAI’s contribution will help the organization expand into new states and create an AI fellowship program. Through the fellowship, advisers across participating states will test and share practical ways to use AI to help more students navigate the path to college. AI can help students manage application and enrollment tasks. Advisers can help them consider their options and decide what to do next.

In a recent development, when we introduced ChatGPT for Teens, we committed to keep improving the experience. We’re supporting Boston Children’s Hospital’s Digital Wellness Lab⁠(opens in a newly introduced window) and its independent Student Advisory Council⁠(opens in a new window) over the next three years to learn directly from young people about how AI fits into their lives and to give teenagers a meaningful stake at the table as AI is being designed.

For the 2026–27 school year, the Lab will bring together ~22 students across two tracks: ~12 students ages 14–18 working on digital wellbeing projects in their communities and ~10 students ages 16–20, focused on building AI chatbots and emerging technology. These students will work alongside researchers and clinicians on digital wellbeing and AI projects. Students in the AI track will test tools and recommend changes based on how they use AI in learning and their personal lives. We expect to seek feedback on teen safety defaults, parental controls and notifications and AI literacy.

Teens should play a central role in developing the tools that will shape their future. Teens will test tools and develop recommendations for the people building them. Our aim is to bring those recommendations into product and model decisions early and to report back to students on what we change and why. This advisory board will give them a direct voice in that work and share their insights and recommendations to help the wider industry build better tools for young people.

We heard from students that photographing and uploading their notes to ChatGPT can take hours. We’re making it easier for students to turn their notes into study materials like flashcards and quizzes.

With continuous, multi-photo capture, students can photograph multiple pages of notes and automatically combine them into a single PDF, ready to upload to ChatGPT. This is available on iOS, and we’re working to bring it to Android.

We’re also adding flashcards to help teens practice what they’re learning. A student preparing for a biology test, for example, can upload class notes or choose a topic, and ChatGPT can turn the material into cards to review. They can mark the cards they know, return to the ones they missed or shuffle the deck for another round. Decks are saved in a teen’s Library, so it’s easy to pick up where they left off. Students can also ask ChatGPT to pull up the flashcards and create a scheduled task to practice them.

In a recent development, alongside flashcards, we’ve made it easier to create Quizzes in ChatGPT. Students can now upload their notes to turn them into interactive quizzes, and ChatGPT is better at recognizing when you want a quiz and creating one directly in chat.

We’ll keep learning from teens, families and educators, checking how protections work in practice and sharing what we learn as ChatGPT for Teens evolves.
