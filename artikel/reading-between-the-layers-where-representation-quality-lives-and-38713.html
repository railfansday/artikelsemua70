Reading Between the Layers: Where Representation Quality Lives, and What Geometry Reveals

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (234 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.31496v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Representation quality in pretrained text foundation models is not concentrated at the final layer, yet practitioners routinely probe it there because layer selection without labels has remained an open problem. We present a large-scale empirical study of where layer quality lives across 25 models spanning four architectural families and four pretraining strategies, evaluated on 13 datasets across classification, pair classification, semantic textual similarity, and multiple-choice question answering. Peak probing layers depend systematically on pretraining strategy and architecture: masked encoders peak in late-middle layers, sequence-to-sequence models shift their peak with task type, contrastive sentence encoders concentrate signal at the final layer under CLS and last-token readouts but peak early under mean pooling, and decoder-only models exhibit distinct phase structure tied to depth. We distill these findings into the Stable Geometry Index (SGI), a closed-form, fully label-free selector with no fitted coefficients combining power-law fit quality, effective-rank capacity, spectral stability, and a weak depth prior. Across 383 evaluation groups SGI improves over the last-layer baseline and lifts Hit@3 from 0.39 to 0.52, recovering 40% of the gap to the oracle without using any labels, and exceeds a supervised ELMo-style layer mix on the matched subset despite using strictly less information. Our ablations also reverse a common assumption in the spectral-decay literature: power-law fit quality is a stronger predictor of layer quality than the exponent's proximity to one.
