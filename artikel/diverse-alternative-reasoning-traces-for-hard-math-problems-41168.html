Diverse Alternative Reasoning Traces for Hard Math Problems

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (174 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.34928v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Post-training large language models (LLMs) for mathematical reasoning often relies on accepted traces whose final answers pass a verifier. For hard math problems, however, such traces can be rare under independent Monte Carlo (MC) sampling. To address this, we propose Diverse Alternative Reasoning Traces (DART), a trace-mining method that starts from a rejected trace, decomposes it into reasoning steps, and ranks them by position-weighted uncertainty. It then generates deliberately distinct alternative candidates for highly ranked steps using reference-answer guidance and a meta-thought before completing the resulting traces. On greedy-unsolved DeepMath-103K problems, DART recovers 37% more problems than MC sampling at a matched number of generated traces. Its estimated generation cost per recovered problem is nearly identical, while its cost per accepted trace is 30% lower. Post-training Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on traces mined by DART achieves the highest point estimate in five of six evaluations across MATH-500 and AIME benchmarks. These results demonstrate that revisiting rejected traces with DART can expand problem coverage and provide effective post-training data.
