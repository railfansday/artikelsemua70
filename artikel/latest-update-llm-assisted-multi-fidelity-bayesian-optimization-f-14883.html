Latest Update: LLM-Assisted Multi-Fidelity Bayesian Optimization: Fusing Expert Knowledge with Numerical Data

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (246 words, 📖 Standard)

It has been revealed that arXiv:2611.24377v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization (MFBO) reduces engineering design cost by combining inexpensive low-fidelity (LF) models with a few accurate high-fidelity (HF) evaluations and sequentially selecting designs to evaluate. However, when HF data are scarce, the LF-HF discrepancy is difficult to learn from numerical data alone, especially when physics omitted in the LF model makes it design-dependent. Yet even without sufficient data, domain experts often know which physics the LF model omits and under which conditions its effect grows. Existing multi-fidelity (MF) models cannot use such qualitative knowledge directly, but large language models (LLMs) can interpret it and compare designs accordingly. The proposed LLM-Assisted MFBO adds a knowledge basis to the conventional MF model: from an expert description of the omitted physics, an LLM judges which of two designs requires the larger signed residual correction, and a pairwise Gaussian process converts these judgments into the basis. HF data calibrate its contribution and sign, so the knowledge is not trusted blindly. On a lithium-ion battery design problem, the proposed method reduced the regret area under the curve of conventional MFBO by 61%. Knowledge quality, rather than the choice of LLM, determined whether the search was accelerated, as shown by a controlled study that varied the two independently. A knowledge-level ablation further quantified the contribution of each knowledge statement. These results show that LLM-interpreted expert knowledge can complement numerical data as an information source in MFBO.
