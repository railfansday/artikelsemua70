Why we’re supporting a common signal that lets website operators communicate their choices to multiple AI providers at once.

Sources indicate that today, we’re announcing that OpenAI will honor a common signal⁠(opens in a fresh window) in robots.txt, the file that lets websites express their preferences about the use of their content for foundational generative AI training. This voluntary effort makes it easier for publishers and website operators to communicate the same preference to multiple participating providers at once.

Recent findings suggest that previously, website operators would identify individual crawlers and maintain separate rules for each and every provider. Having a shared signal across multiple providers reduces that work for operators.

It has been revealed that we aim to implement the signal in the coming months, and will share another enhancement with details about our implementation when we do so. This upcoming signal will not replace the controls we already provide through robots.txt. Our existing crawler controls⁠(opens in a new window), which provide web publishers flexibility and control over their pages, will continue to apply and be respected.

This draws on the ongoing work⁠(opens in a newly introduced window) of the Internet Engineering Task Force’s (IETF) AI Preferences working group. We’re joining other participants in the working group in today’s announcement of this shared signal, and believe this strengthens our continued participation in the IETF standards discussions. OpenAI will continue to adapt its practices as those conversations and the landscape continue to evolve in view of appropriate controls and fairness.

Recent findings suggest that we believe this is a practical step toward an open web where people can publish and discover information, and website owners have clearer, simpler ways to communicate their choices about how their content is used. We encourage other AI providers to honor the signal.
