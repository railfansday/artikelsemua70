Bottlenecked: The Capital Cost of Protected DeFi Lending

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (282 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.26948v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Securing hundreds of billions of dollars in decentralized finance (DeFi) requires both cryptographically secured collateral and economic capital to absorb losses that collateral alone cannot cover. Composability makes both the amount and location of this protection matter: Kelp's nearly \$300 million April 2026 bridge exploit showed how an attacker could borrow against stolen assets across lending markets. We study how to allocate junior capital that absorbs losses before consumers incur losses in lending protocols. The main object we study is a tripartite graph that records user assets, their covered liabilities, and the junior capital permitted to pay them. Cuts in this graph determine what protection is missing, while a static security game on the graph defines how much such protection should cost. We call this cost the adversarial default premium (ADP), which can be viewed as the excess cost of insurance in composable financial systems. We then study the joint cost of funding loans and protecting their creditors via this insurance. In a tripartite graph with $N$ borrowers and $M$ lenders, we show that existing systems such as Aave and Morpho have an insurance cost that is $\Theta(N)$. Under mild assumptions, we show that the optimal graph has an insurance cost that is $\Theta(\sqrt{N})$ with small constants. We apply our model to numerous 2026 DeFi cyber attacks and estimate ADP and the performance of optimized graphs. We find practical capital cost savings of up to 34.5% for our optimized design versus designs that resemble Aave and Morpho. Our results suggest that new lending protocols can improve capital efficiency by treating junior capital as a first-class constraint in protocol design.
