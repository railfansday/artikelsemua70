Breaking: Fast Bayesian Methods for Uncertainty Quantification in Sparse AI Evaluation

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (201 words, 📖 Standard)

Sources indicate that arXiv:2611.24159v1 Announce Type: upcoming Abstract: Estimating how an AI system will perform on a new benchmark need not require another large model. We use public evaluation counts to choose a weak Beta prior, then revision it exactly from at most 31 revealed outcomes. The predictor uses no item semantics, pretrained weights, or inference API. Its single online evaluation in the AIMS competition returned a team-reported Brier ALC of 0.188477, a 24.6% reduction from the constant-0.5 reference. The complete submission is 7,754 bytes; a server microbenchmark measures 12.71 microseconds per prediction with 31 matching labels. We describe how source evaluations are compressed into 301 prior means and show why a concentration of two lets new evidence rapidly determine the prediction. The resulting posterior gives exact uncertainty about the modelled success rate without numerical integration or neural inference. A public evaluation covers 79 system-benchmark pairs and eight seeds, reaching Brier ALC 0.200250 for the submitted method. Accompanying code and sufficient statistics reconstruct the fitted prior byte for byte and replay all 632 evaluation trajectories. The result demonstrates how much predictive value a compact Bayesian model can extract from sparse target feedback at very low online cost.
