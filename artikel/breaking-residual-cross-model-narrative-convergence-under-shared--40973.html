Breaking: Residual Cross-Model Narrative Convergence under Shared Prompts: Auditing Prompt Controls and Narrative Measurement

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (158 words, 📖 Standard)

Recent findings suggest that arXiv:2611.34015v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Stories created by LLMs based on the same prompts can be narratively similar to one another. This similarity may reflect constraints imposed by the prompts as well as the models' writing tendencies. The current research analyzes 51,240 stories created by five models in the StoryScope corpus based on 10,248 common prompts. It uses 213 narrative features in seven dimensions to compare cross-model distances between stories generated from the same prompts with distances between stories generated from different but semantically matched prompts. To assess the relationship between narrative features and prompt content, a semantic relevance mask was evaluated against 420 author-annotated prompt-feature judgments (F1 = .813; near-threshold accuracy = .621). The equally weighted mean Residual Convergence Index (RCI) was .409 without masking and .406 with masking. These findings indicate measurable cross-model narrative convergence relative to a semantically matched-prompt baseline, with little change following semantic relevance masking.
