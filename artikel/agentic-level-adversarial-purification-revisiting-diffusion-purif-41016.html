Agentic-Level Adversarial Purification: Revisiting Diffusion Purifiers on Native-Resolution Agentic Inputs

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (243 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2611.34181v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Vision-language agents act on native-resolution renders of multi-element pages, where one perturbed photo or a few altered text strokes can redirect the decision. Any content provider can craft such a perturbation on the open model behind the agent, so a plug-in purifier is the natural first line of defense. Yet diffusion purifiers were designed and evaluated at the fixed resolution of the diffusion model on classifier or natural-image benchmarks, and defense evaluations for agents test JPEG, blur and noise but no diffusion purifier. We present the first study of adversarial purification at the agentic level, for an agent built on Qwen3-VL-8B: purifiers run at native resolution on multi-element inputs, face grey-box attackers who know the agent's model but not the defense, and are compared at matched clean cost. We show that treating agentic inputs as classifier inputs misleads both evaluation and design. Resizing the input to the resolution of the diffusion model, as existing purifiers do, raises the TextVQA clean cost of the Divide-and-Conquer purifier (DC) from 4.0 to 35.7 points. On web-shop pages, DC keeps 85.0% grey-box robust accuracy against web-hardened perturbations, where cheap input transformations at matched clean cost keep at most 40.0%, and this robustness comes from the global noise level: input-estimated masks and noise levels add none. These findings hold on a disjoint held-out set and on a second VLM and yield three requirements for defenses, we release the protocol.
