Financial crime could stall the agentic economy, warns Tieto

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The agentic economy is no longer a distant prospect, and according to Tieto, fraudsters have already arrived. As consumers grow more comfortable handing everyday tasks to AI agents and agent-to-agent interactions multiply, the financial sector faces a pressing question: can it protect this new ecosystem before criminals undermine it?

Øystein Namtvedt, head of engineering in Tieto Banktech’s financial crime prevention team, argues that without robust safeguards, the shift towards agentic commerce could stall entirely, or suffer far worse consequences.

In a recent development, tieto stresses that banks and FinTechs must recognise agents as a present reality rather than a futuristic concept. Citing Eurostat figures, Tieto notes that agent usage among European SMEs has surged by more than 800% over the past six months. Consumer sentiment is shifting too, with over half of European shoppers now comfortable using agents to compare prices and hunt for deals.

In a recent development, yet this momentum brings risk. Tieto Banktech’s Defence Center, which tracks global fraud patterns on behalf of clients and their customers, has observed criminal networks becoming increasingly sophisticated and using new technologies to scale their operations. Social manipulation fraud alone rose by more than a third last year, as criminals convincingly faked identities to bypass authentication systems.

Tieto believes this calls for a fundamental change in approach. Fraud teams, it argues, need to fight fire with fire, deploying agentic AI to counter emerging forms of identity theft, fraud and money laundering. With fresh regulation on the horizon, advanced machine learning may be the only viable way to curb fraud while containing costs.

The financial stakes are considerable. LexisNexis research suggests every dollar lost to fraud in 2026 will cost financial services firms $5.47 in overheads, split between operational costs (52%) and reimbursement (48%). Separate analysis from the American Bankers Association indicates these costs have more than doubled in just two years.

Regulatory pressure is set to intensify. From 2027, the EU’s latest AML-R regulation will tighten requirements across numerous categories, extending AML checks to additional merchant categories and transaction types.

Sources indicate that according to Tieto, the combination of stricter rules and increasingly advanced criminal tactics makes adaptive, self-regulating agentic models essential. The tech giant warns of two dangers if banks fail to adapt: malicious agents repeatedly defeating authentication systems, and an unsustainable rise in the time and money spent detecting fraud and money laundering. European banks already spend tens of billions annually on financial crime prevention and compliance, a burden Tieto claims will become unmanageable if legacy approaches persist.

To address this, Tieto has developed AML Explore, a modernised AML solution powered by its proprietary ATLAS AI model, a self-supervised Large Financial Model. The technology is designed to identify emerging fraud vectors and suspicious transactions while reducing the volume of false positives requiring manual review.

According to recent reports, tieto’s internal data shows the approach cuts false positives by 71%, tackling one of the largest cost drivers in AML defence. The tech giant indicates the combined offering delivers a low-cost, highly effective fraud defence system, already used by core customers across the Nordics and beyond.
