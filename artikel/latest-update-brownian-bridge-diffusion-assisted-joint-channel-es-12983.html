Latest Update: Brownian Bridge Diffusion-Assisted Joint Channel Estimation and Data Detection for Jamming-Resilient OFDM Receivers

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (255 words, 📖 Standard)

arXiv:2606.28778v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: In next-generation wireless networks, the growing density of devices and limited spectrum resources pose severe jamming challenges to fragile legitimate communication links in the wireless electromagnetic environment. Crucially, when jamming overlaps with pilot and data symbols in both time and frequency domains, it inflicts a severe bottleneck on receiver-side joint estimation and detection. Existing schemes often lack an effective framework to combat such jamming contamination, thereby failing to guarantee reliable transmission. To address this glitch, we propose a Brownian bridge diffusion-assisted joint channel estimation and data detection (BBD-JCED) framework for jamming-resilient orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) receivers. The proposed framework consists of a jamming suppression module and a Brownian bridge-joint recovery module. The former identifies jamming-contaminated bins in the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) domain and suppresses the dominant jamming components through spectral notching. The latter establishes a Brownian bridge-based probabilistic graph to model the probabilistic transition process from the suppressed OFDM observation to the coded bits, and performs a reverse recovery process to enable enhanced joint channel estimation and data detection. Moreover, we derive a variational lower bound (VLB) to reformulate the intractable bit estimate problem into a tractable training objective, providing a probabilistic foundation for the loss function design. Furthermore, we develop a two-stage training algorithm to separately optimize the jamming suppression and Brownian bridge-joint recovery modules. Simulation results under representative jamming types demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves superior jamming suppression and bit recovery performance over the considered baseline schemes while maintaining competitive inference complexity.
