Crucible: Evaluating LLM Agents in Executable Chemistry Research Environments

🌐 Source: Arxiv • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (219 words, 📖 Standard)

Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.34254v1 Announce Type: latest Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) perform well on chemistry knowledge benchmarks, which mainly assess question answering and isolated tasks. Chemical research, however, requires using specialized tools and interpreting their outputs to obtain chemically valid results. To address this gap, we introduce Crucible Bench to evaluate this ability across all four stages of the design-make-test-analyze (DMTA) cycle. The benchmark contains 1,078 tasks in 12 task families curated from 18 published datasets, covering molecular design, synthesis planning, experimental execution, closed-loop optimization, simulation, and analytical characterization. Agents access 75 domain-specific tools through the Model Context Protocol in seven isolated environments. Sandboxed graders verify the resulting synthesis routes, experimental protocols, and other outputs. Laboratory tasks use robotic simulators or previously measured experimental data. We evaluate 14 LLMs as agents and find that the best achieves an overall score of 0.545, with synthesis planning and closed-loop optimization emerging as the hardest domains. Overall performance does not predict optimization performance well: the model ranked twelfth overall achieves the highest score in closed-loop optimization. Error analysis also reveals that agents often submit chemically invalid results despite running their tools successfully. Stronger models benefit more from tool access, but reliable use of chemical software remains a challenge that Crucible Bench can track as models improve.
