After spending nearly half a billion dollars to thwart Democrats, Musk tries to distance himself from MAGA.

A little less than two years ago, Elon Musk stood onstage at the Conservative Political Action Conference, sunglasses on, black MAGA hat perched atop his head, wielding a chainsaw. Last night, Musk called in to The New York Times DealBook Summit and distanced himself from President Donald Trump’s movement.

“I think I’m ideologically quite moderate. I don’t think I have any kind of extreme political views,” Musk told DealBook editor Andrew Ross Sorkin. “You’ve known me for a long time. Can you think of an extreme view that I have?”.

I can certainly think of a few.

Let’s start with the great replacement. For those who are blissfully unaware, the great replacement is a white nationalist conspiracy theory whose adherents believe that a cabal of powerful people is conspiring to replace white Americans and Europeans by promoting mass migration from the developing world. (The most hardcore great replacement types believe this is a grand Jewish conspiracy; the more politically palatable Fox News types blame Democrats.) As recently as this year, Musk was promoting the notion that white men would be “slaughtered” if they became a minority in the United States.

It has been revealed that musk is X’s most prolific promoter of anti-immigrant conspiracy theories, according to a Bloomberg analysis. At a 2024 campaign rally with Trump, Musk claimed that Democrats want to “take away your right to vote effectively.” His racist fearmongering extends beyond the US. At a rally for Germany’s far-right party last year, Musk encouraged Germans to not lose their national values “in some sort of multiculturalism that dilutes everything.” He encouraged Germans to be proud of their heritage, saying there was “frankly too much of a focus on past guilt and we need to move beyond that.” Musk posted about race nearly every single day in the month of January. “Whites,” he wrote, “are a rapidly dying minority.” It’s a claim he’s made multiple times. “If current trends continue, Whites will go from being a small minority of world population today to virtually extinct!” he mentioned in December 2025. Musk, a birth rate obsessive, is doing his part to keep the white race alive. He has fathered at least 14 children with four different women.

Recent findings suggest that in fact, fears of replacement animated Musk’s support for Republicans in the midterm elections, to the tune of nearly half a billion dollars. (At the DealBook Summit, Musk stated he now feels “pretty foolish” for throwing so much money at Republican campaigns “and not seeing much for it.”) Musk himself said that if Democrats return to power, they’ll “open the borders and legalize everyone living in America illegally to cement a permanent majority.” You could ignore the dogwhistles and argue he meant a permanent political majority, but that would require you to ignore basically every other thing Musk has said about race, migration, and politics over the past four or so years. In 2024, for example, Musk amplified the viral far-right lie that Haitian immigrants in Springfield, Ohio, were eating people’s pets. “Vote for Kamala if you want this to happen to your neighborhood,” Musk posted on X.

The only thing that rivals Musk’s obsession with white birth rates is his fixation on trans people. Musk has repeatedly asserted that his daughter Vivian Wilson, from whom he is estranged, was “killed by the woke mind virus.” (Wilson, who is very much alive, was reportedly cut off from Musk’s health insurance after coming out as transgender.) Shortly after taking over X, Musk decided that the platform would consider the words “cis” and “cisgender” slurs. After J.K. Rowling equated trans activists’ rhetoric to Hitler’s musings on propaganda, Musk reposted her on X, adding, “Precisely.”.

Sorkin feebly gestured at Musk’s comments about “some of the things happening in Europe and around immigration that people did think were far or extreme,” to say nothing of his MAGA affiliation. Musk countered that he doesn’t think these things are extreme, and also that he’s “not MAGA.”.

“I’m not sure what MAGA means,” he continued.

Industry observers note that an interesting thing for the architect of a key part of the MAGA agenda to say! The dizzying pace of news may make it hard to remember just how much damage Musk did to the federal government — and to the world. The chainsaw Musk brandished onstage at CPAC was meant to represent his now-defunct Department of Government Efficiency, which he unofficially led as a government employee under Trump. Musk’s views weren’t an anomaly in DOGE. Marko Elez, a former DOGE staffer — who followed Musk there from SpaceX — resigned after reporters uncovered an old social media account where he urged the US to repeal the Civil Rights Act and reinstate a “eugenic immigration policy.” Elez was subsequently rehired by the Social Security Administration. Though it was never an actual government department, DOGE gutted the federal workforce, infiltrated the Treasury Department and the Social Security Administration, and dismantled USAID, resulting in hundreds of thousands of deaths around the world.

Sources indicate that shortly after Trump’s return to office, Musk called USAID — which, until he and his DOGE staffers destroyed it, provided people across the developing world with lifesaving food and medical aid — a “criminal organization.” Earlier this year, amid mass opposition to ICE’s Operation Metro Surge in Minneapolis — during which federal agents killed two US citizens — Musk defended DHS against its critics. “It is pure evil for people to stop the arrest of child predators,” he wrote on X.

It has been revealed that musk is a creature of the internet, and as such he’s left a pretty extensive paper trail of his beliefs. As recently as October, he was endorsing a reposted screenshot of a 4chan post arguing that women shouldn’t participate in democracy because they “can’t defend themselves physically,” saying it “sounds correct.” Charles Cornish-Dale, the pseudonymous X user behind the post, who goes by Raw Egg Nationalist, is a far-right British bodybuilder who edits the magazine Man’s World.

Given the on-again, off-again nature of their relationship, it’s surprising that Musk’s comments about MAGA were so well received by Trump.

“Oh, I don’t know. He’s a friend of mine. What can I tell you? I mean, you have to ask — there are lots of definitions out there,” Trump told reporters. “MAGA, to me, means something very specific, and when people veer from it, they don’t do very well. But Elon is — Elon’s got very good intentions. I mean, he was with me the whole — he traveled with me.”.

The public has broadly rejected the chaos wrought by MAGA 2.0, and Musk is trying to distance himself from the fallout. More cynically, he’s probably also trying to prevent Democrats in Congress and in Texas from investigating him come 2027. Whatever his motivation, Musk knows the winds have shifted, though his pivot is even less convincing than Alex Karp’s. Though, to be fair, there is earlier video evidence of Musk saying he isn’t MAGA.

“I’m not just MAGA,” he stated at a 2024 Trump rally in Madison Square Garden. “I’m dark, gothic MAGA.”.
