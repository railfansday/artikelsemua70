Industry observers note that arXiv:2611.22962v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Pixel-space diffusion learns visual representations and image generation jointly in a single end-to-end training stage, without a separately pretrained visual encoder. However, plain pixel-space Transformers still trail leading two-stage latent diffusion models in generation quality and training efficiency. We hypothesize that part of this gap arises from retaining one token per patch throughout the backbone, which does not explicitly encourage a visual hierarchy that summarizes related content at progressively coarser levels. To address this, we introduce Hierarchical Diffusion Transformers (HiT), which progressively group related tokens and represent each group with a single token. The main Transformer bottleneck processes these compact group tokens, and ungrouping restores features to their original image positions. A compression budget regulates the average compression ratio across the batch, allowing group memberships and counts to vary across images and diffusion timesteps during both training and generation. The model exhibits emergent semantic structure, learning to group coherent objects and object parts without segmentation labels. In text-to-image generation, HiT further learns to group caption words with image patches depicting the same concepts, without region annotations. HiT achieves 1.48 FID on ImageNet $256\times256$ and reaches comparable generation quality to JiT using approximately 40\% of its cumulative training FLOPs. For text-to-image generation, HiT exceeds UniLiP's average score across five benchmarks with roughly $6\times$ fewer denoiser parameters and $3\times$ fewer forward FLOPs. These results suggest that learning a visual hierarchy within the generator breaks down the barrier to stronger image generation without external visual pretraining.

arXiv:2611.22926v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Typed decision models combine natural-language understanding with a structured interface that returns a selected action and an option-level probability distribution without generating lengthy textual responses. Their low inference cost and response latency make them attractive for transportation problems with predefined alternatives. This paper investigates their effectiveness, robustness, and efficiency in two representative transportation tasks: individual travel-mode choice prediction and sequential traffic signal control. We systematically evaluate TypeSafe Jev and GPT-6 Luna Decisions against conventional transportation methods, reinforcement learning algorithms, and four chat-based large language models. arXiv:2611.22888v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Vulnerability exploitation tests whether language model agents can develop working exploits from known software flaws. Sequential test-time scaling can improve exploitation performance, but larger budgets do not ensure sustained exploration: agents often stop with budget remaining. Yet what happens when execution continues beyond stopping remains underexplored. We investigate this gap on ExploitBench using DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 and MiMo-V2.6-Pro. We find additional verified gains after the first stop response, but gains diminish over longer execution. These findings show that stopping need not mark an agent's exploitation limit, and that continued execution with context management can elicit substantial further capability.

Although such generated futures can be visually plausible, they preserve many driving-irrelevant appearance details, i.e., only a small portion of the tokens directly encode critical planning-relevant factors, such as agent geometry, motion dynamics, and interactions. This introduces a significant performance bottleneck. An important question thus arises: what world state should a world VLA model reason over to support planning beyond pixel-level prediction? Each token is dynamically grounded to a surrounding agent associated with its object's state, such as semantic identity, 3D geometry, motion, and interaction context. AgentWorld learns these tokens through three key processes: explicit geometric grounding, reasoning-oriented supervised fine-tuning, and agent-oriented reinforcement learning. Extensive open-loop and closed-loop experiments under diverse driving scenarios show that AgentWorld enhances planning capabilities, fully exploits visual evidence and agent dynamics, and yields interpretable agent-centric world states.
