  • Also: The best AR and MR glasses in 2026: Expert tested and reviewed.
  • Industry observers note that xreal has been perfecting its XR glasses for years by extending your connected device to the big screen.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

I’ve finally found smart glasses with the Android XR experience I’ve been looking for. It’s taken some trial and error — I first bought the Samsung Galaxy XR headset, but sold it after a few months because the badly positioned rear strap knob prevented me from sitting back on a plane. For the past week, I’ve been wearing the new Xreal Aura, bringing the full Android XR experience to the glasses form factor.

Also: The best AR and MR glasses in 2026: Expert tested and reviewed.

Industry observers note that xreal has been perfecting its XR glasses for years by extending your connected device to the big screen. The Aura brings the full Android XR ecosystem to users via the connected Puck for a starting price of $1,099, while also functioning as standard XR glasses. For context, this is some serious value, especially since Samsung’s Galaxy XR starts at $1,799.

When I first opened the Xreal Aura, I thought it was another pair of the Xreal 1 Pro Plus. The hardware is nearly identical, except for two small cameras in the top corners of the front of the glasses to capture gestures. There is also a center-mounted camera similar to the Eye found on the Xreal Pro series, for capturing content and 6DoF spatial anchoring. Despite the additional cameras, the glasses still weigh only 95 grams — far less than the Samsung Galaxy XR’s 545 grams.

It has been revealed that speaking of gestures, I was pleasantly surprised by how responsive they were. With the Samsung Galaxy XR, I became frustrated swiftly with gesture navigation and ended up using a connected Bluetooth mouse. Here, they’re much more usable.

There are physical buttons for control as well. The right arm has three: a small button at the top, one at the bottom, and a longer button behind it, primarily used for volume control. With the Puck connected, the buttons provide basic controls for volume, transparency level, and recentering the display. Audio comes through the slot speakers near the arms of the glasses and sounds great with minimal leakage thanks to the glasses’ partnership with Bose.

Sources indicate that the guts of Android XR are contained in the puck that connects to the glasses. It’s about the size of a standard smartphone, but without a display. The top serves as a touchpad for navigation and menu selections in Android XR, if you prefer that method over gestures. The power button doubles as a fingerprint sensor to secure the Puck and unlock it with a simple tap. The longer volume button is just below the power button.

Recent findings suggest that there are two USB-C ports on the bottom: the left is for charging (mostly), and the right is to connect your Xreal glasses. You can also charge the Puck while using it, so if you are on a long flight, you can connect it to the glasses. The Xreal Aura Puck has a decent advertised battery life of 4-5 hours, compared to Apple Vision Pro and Samsung’s Galaxy XR at up to 2.5 hours.

According to recent reports, the Xreal Aura is the first Android XR device to launch with a sleek glasses form factor — exactly what I wanted to see to improve the VR/AR experience for the end user. By contrast, the heavy Apple Vision Pro and Galaxy XR remain fairly niche due to their high prices and very large size.

Android XR brings the full Android experience to the glasses, with up to five displays you can move around in the virtual space, immersive experiences that can take you to other worlds or deep below the ocean surface, and optimal productivity options powered by thousands of Android apps.

Also: I compared the 3 best XR display glasses right now – the winner was not the most expensive.

With the Puck’s lightweight form factor and belt clip, you can walk around and rapidly switch to Follow mode to see the world around you through the glasses, with your Android XR content positioned in a small window off to the side. Just tap the Follow mode button again to return to the full Android XR environment.

One rather unique option I was unaware of until I read through the manual was the option to connect an external source to the USB-C charge port for a hybrid experience. Xreal calls this mode Wired Mirroring, and includes a shortcut to enable it in the Android XR launcher.

Plugging into a laptop brings your PC or Mac’s display to the glasses, giving the option to mirror or extend from your connected computer and then take full advantage of increasing the size of the display, moving in closer or further away from you, and viewing up to four other Android apps within the Android XR user interface.

It has been revealed that I enjoy using this mode when I have something up on my computer to work on, with another piece of content streaming or another app open to gather data from in Android XR. I admit, it’ll take some time for me to get used to this unique mode, but it seems it can increase productivity, especially with the ability to peek below the lenses and see the other display on my computer.

Industry observers note that previously, devices like a Nintendo Switch 2 couldn’t connect to a pair of smart glasses because they’re designed to output video only via a dock. The Xreal Puck functions similarly to the Xreal AR adapter, so it “fools” the Switch 2 into thinking it is docked, then outputs the game video to the Xreal Aura glasses. You don’t need the Xreal AR adapter, as the Puck now serves this role and has its own power, eliminating the need for a USB-C power source.

Since the Xreal Aura glasses looked so much like the Xreal 1 Pro Plus I recently tested, I decided to plug my Apple iPhone Duo into the Xreal Aura, and voila! They performed just like the 1 Pro Plus, including the full on-screen display menu options. I later read in the manual that this is called external display mode.

Also: I invested in Samsung’s $1,800 XR headset to replace my dual monitors – and it’s paying off big time.

Industry observers note that while you should not purchase the Xreal Aura solely to serve as standard XR glasses, it is fantastic to have this option, since there are times when you may want to use the glasses without the Android XR platform. For example, when I travel for work with just a Surface Pro, I want a big ultra-wide screen experience directly from the Surface Pro to the glasses. This lets me travel with just the Xreal Aura and rapidly switch to the Android XR experience for other tasks.

Apple TV and Netflix are at the moment unavailable on the Xreal Aura in Android XR, but you can watch them by plugging in your iPhone or Android directly into the glasses without using the Puck. This is why Xreal provides the ultimate flexibility with the Aura at a reasonable price point.

According to recent reports, xreal also sent along the Xreal Game Controller, co-branded with GameSir, as well as the Xreal Keyboard. The Game Controller has a charging stand that plugs into USB-C to charge the controller for hours of gameplay. Since I primarily game with a Nintendo Switch 2, I took off the removable magnet faceplate and swapped out the left directional pad for one that mimics the Switch 2.

According to recent reports, after connecting via Bluetooth, I enjoyed an optimal gaming experience on my Switch 2 and the Xreal Aura. Even better, I activated Google Gemini directly from the controller to ask for help when stuck in game areas. As a casual gamer, this dual functionality motivated me to play more games.

Recent findings suggest that extensive text input is painful to do with just gestures, but voice-to-text is very accurate and fast. However, if you enjoy typing on a keyboard, you either need to connect a computer and use that keyboard while looking under the glasses, or connect a Bluetooth keyboard for mobile computing. The Xreal Keyboard has great key sizing and spacing, along with solid tactile feedback. It would be nice to have a trackpad attached, but finger gestures are fine too if a Bluetooth mouse is not connected to the Aura.

Also: I wear XR glasses every day on my commute – Why Xreal’s latest pair is my new main driver.

The Xreal Game Controller is available for $99 and the Xreal keyboard is $79, but there are frequently bundle offers that offer discounts.

Xreal provides exactly what I was hoping to see with Android XR with the fresh Xreal Aura glasses. They have a sleek form factor and offer the full package with a very nice carrying case. The 70-degree FOV is not as expansive as that of full-on heavy headsets, but it still provides an immersive experience that is sure to satisfy most people.

Recent findings suggest that the additional modes make the Aura an even better value, and it’s clear that this is the Android XR solution you want for maximum flexibility and productivity. I look forward to commuting, traveling, and working in a major-screen environment with this pair of glasses.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On Monday, large trucks began circling the streets of Washington, DC, carrying billboards urging Congress to investigate the Trump administration’s ties with Nvidia’s disgraced CEO Jensen Huang.

“Jensen was in bed with a spy from China,” the billboards created by the Tech Oversight Project (TOP) alleged. “Trump was in bed with Jensen.” TOP is a nonprofit that describes itself as “dedicated to reining in Significant Tech.”.

Currently, Trump is supporting a campaign to bring back Huang as Nvidia’s CEO—despite many Republicans calling it a “horrible idea.” Breaking his silence, Huang issued a statement Tuesday saying he would be “honored” to return, but the board said reinstatement is not under consideration as an internal investigation into US spying claims remains ongoing. Huang used the moment to say that his interests align with the US’s, saying:.

I came to the United States 53 years ago, and becoming an American citizen is one of the greatest gifts and proudest moments of my life. Nvidia has been my life’s work. Building it into the company it is today alongside my colleagues has been a privilege and an honor. Nowhere but in America could a company like Nvidia be possible. I love our country, and I love Nvidia.

Trump may have learned as early as this spring that Huang was potentially the target of Chinese espionage, but the president claimed recently that he didn’t know Huang was potentially exposed to a Chinese spy as he was becoming a prominent part of Trump’s inner circle, CNN reported.

If Trump wasn’t adequately briefed, it raises question about whether the White House acted with “sufficient urgency” to address a serious espionage threat, CNN noted. But if Trump is deflecting, an even “graver question” will emerge about why Trump didn’t act on information and instead continued to keep Huang close, CNN said.

Instead of distancing himself from the Nvidia CEO, Trump invited Huang to sit at the head table during a state dinner with China’s president Xi Jinping, and last month, Trump awarded Huang the National Medal of Science, the nation’s highest scientiﬁc honor, for his AI achievements.

“Not only has he continued to do nothing, he’s invited that very CEO into the White House and on state visits to China with him,” Sacha Haworth, TOP’s founder and CEO, told Ars. “And the question we all have to ask is ‘why on Earth would he do that?’”.

Sources indicate that what Trump and Nvidia knew—and when—have become central questions for some Republicans, as well as top Democrats who initiative to investigate the scandal when they assume control of key committees in Congress next year. Andrew Yoon, head of research for a nonprofit called CivAI, which warns the public of dangers from AI, told Ars that Huang doesn’t need to be compromised by China to have endangered the US. Prioritizing boosting profits over national security alone is “pretty concerning.”.

Sources indicate that “I think the most notable thing here is that Jensen Huang has been at the absolute forefront of lobbying to be able to sell chips to China,” Yoon remarked. “He has leveraged his very deep relationships with government, especially President Trump, in order to be able to sell as many Nvidia chips to China as he can get away with. If he could be selling Blackwell chips to China, I’m sure he would be.”.

Yoon noted that evidence suggests some Chinese AI models have been trained on Blackwell chips. Even with China’s push to prioritize domestic chipmakers like Huawei, Nvidia chips remain “indispensable” to China’s frontier AI goals, Yoon remarked. And national security experts fear that access to high-end chips will help China advance its military AI or gain economic ground.

In a recent development, the White House isn’t commenting on Trump’s relationship with Huang. Among the lawmakers accusing the Trump administration of “corruption” is Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D–Conn.), The Wall Street Journal reported. He has already asked the White House to “preserve all communications” between Trump and Huang.

Recent findings suggest that other lawmakers are “seeking more information about what Trump knew about the relationship between Jensen Huang and Alice Huang,” the former Megaspeed CEO whom Huang admitted to having an affair with. They also want to know more about “the administration’s failure to stop artiﬁcial-intelligence chips from going to China,” WSJ reported, and Nvidia’s reportedly $25 million donation to the White House ballroom.

“It is not just an intelligence glitch; it’s also a potential corruption issue,” Blumenthal said. “There needs to be a really penetrating, comprehensive investigation of all the possible compromises of our intelligence that may have occurred, but also… that Donald Trump is so high on the head of Nvidia, and now is potentially linked to intelligence compromises.”.

Trump has profited off Nvidia’s success while in office, buying “millions of dollars worth of Nvidia shares in his personal brokerage accounts since returning to oﬃce in 2025,” the WSJ reported. The White House maintains that the president has no role in managing his stock portfolio.

It has been revealed that other officials advising Trump on AI also benefited from relaxing export curbs, TOP noted. On Tuesday, the group issued a report summarizing what the public knows about potential conflicts of interest of two Trump officials: former White House AI Czar David Sacks and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.

Haworth, TOP’s founder, told Ars she’s concerned that Sacks and Lutnick abused their power to convince Trump to let Nvidia sell cutting-edge technology to China, while standing to “gain astronomical amounts of money.”.

Industry observers note that “The public is outraged by Nvidia’s CEO for compromising our national security, but we didn’t get here because of Jensen Huang’s missteps alone,” Haworth mentioned. If “Nvidia was knowingly doing business with companies tied to illegal chip sales and smuggling with China,” then “the Trump administration was asleep at the wheel,” she said.

“White House AI Czar David Sacks and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick were protecting their own fortunes rather than looking out for Americans,” Haworth said.

If the US fails to hold them accountable for alleged conflicts of interest, “it would send a signal that powerful men once again cannot be held to account and that powerful men are protecting powerful men,” Haworth said.

Detailing Sacks’ alleged conflicts, the group cited New York Times reporting that the former AI Czar had long supported Huang’s stance that allowing Nvidia to sell more chips in China would help the US stay ahead in the AI race. After Sacks became Trump’s key AI advisor, Huang rapidly forged a “tight bond” with him, and “both stood to benefit” from their push to sell chips to China, TOP reported.

Industry observers note that also notably, while advising the White House, Sacks opposed policies such as the Trump administration’s recent move to cut off China’s remote access to US-made chips through now-restricted Southeast Asian data centers. Citing Sacks’ alignment with Huang, TOP reported that after just one meeting with the two men, Trump decided to allow Nvidia to sell AI chips to China that the Biden administration had previously blocked.

Huang valued his alliance with Sacks, TOP noted, citing reporting that showed Huang declaring last year that Nvidia was “lucky” to have Sacks advising Trump. But TOP is concerned that the alliance was built on mutual financial interest and that Sacks took a part-time role in the Trump administration to avoid divestments or disclosure requirements that might have revealed the extent of his possible conflicts of interest.

Industry observers note that TOP flagged two areas it believes warrant Congressional investigation. While advising Trump to embrace AI deregulation, Sacks and his firm, Craft Ventures, continued investing substantially in AI. Sacks also stood to benefit from pushing Trump to integrate AI into warfare and national security tools, TOP’s report mentioned. Craft Ventures has invested in Palantir, “a Peter Thiel-founded government contractor seen as a ‘rising star’ in the US Defense Tech World and holding hundreds of millions in US government defense contracts,” TOP said.

Even within the Trump administration, officials have turned on Commerce Secretary Lutnick, seemingly looking to blame him for mishandling the Nvidia spying investigation and allowing Trump to maintain close ties with Huang despite apparent intelligence risks.

Lutnick has bragged publicly that he promised Huang “really easy access to the President” after joining the administration, TOP reported. That alliance helped push Trump to approve the sale of Nvidia H200s to China, TOP mentioned. And that seemed to directly benefit Lutnick, TOP said, because before he was confirmed as secretary, he was CEO of Cantor Fitzgerald, which held $1.2 billion in Nvidia stock.

“In his role as Secretary, Lutnick is responsible for policing exports of sensitive technology to adversaries and global rivals, like China,” TOP reported. “Lutnick and the company he controls benefited greatly from opening sales of Nvidia chips to China.”.

After Lutnick was required to hand that firm over to his son, Cantor Fitzgerald “maintained extensive commercial ties to Nvidia, helping arrange billions of dollars in financing for companies in its AI business universe and repeatedly hosting its top executives,” TOP noted.

Those investments shifted substantially around the same time that Lutnick began facing pressure to “crack down on Megaspeed, a Singapore-based manufacturer suspected of funneling Nvidia’s advanced artificial intelligence chips to China,” TOP reported. Cantor Fitzgerald “reduced its Nvidia holdings by nearly 50 percent between September 2025 and March 2026,” TOP said.

However, when Trump was reportedly first told of the spying allegations this spring, Cantor Fitzgerald again continued to boost Nvidia. Most prominently, the tech giant helped “finance the expansion of one of Nvidia’s newest business partners, IREN,” facilitating a five-year, $3.4 billion deal between Nvidia and the Australia-based cloud computing provider.

According to recent reports, among those now criticizing Lutnick for weak enforcement of export controls that appeared designed to protect Huang’s bottom line is Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. But even with scrutiny coming from within the Trump administration, not everyone is convinced that Lutnick did anything wrong. Yoon suggested that, given Lutnick’s long career, there may be normal explanations for potential financial conflicts.

“Maybe a separate question is ‘should people with a long storied history in business and industry be working in government at this level?’” Yoon said.

Haworth remarked the next 48 hours will be critical in determining whether Huang succeeds in returning to Nvidia.

“I think it’s going to be incumbent on the incoming Democratic leadership to insist that whether or not he returns, investigations will follow,” Haworth mentioned.

But Yoon thinks Nvidia is unlikely to bring Huang back any time soon, despite current evidence suggesting that “Nvidia’s actions here have been perfectly legal” and that “influencing a political system for your financial interests is very much legal in the United States.”.

In a recent development, it seems “untenable for Nvidia to continue doing what they’re doing,” Yoon stated. Zooming out even more, he suggested the scandal showed that the entire AI industry can no longer afford to operate under a profit motive alone, so long as governments continue positioning the technology as a strategic asset.

It has been revealed that if US investigations expose corruption, bribery, treason, or other crimes, Nvidia risks losing some of its dominant stake in the GPU market. On Tuesday, the Information reported that some Nvidia customers had already lost trust and were looking to Google and AMD for more stability.

Nvidia has also been accused of intervening in a Commerce investigation, which Yoon said could also be “pretty consequential” if any evidence bears that out.

“I think it’s pretty unlikely that Huang makes it back to Nvidia any time soon,” Yoon stated. “Maybe he goes on a redemption tour and comes back in five years or something, but I’m not really seeing a path forward for him right now.”.
